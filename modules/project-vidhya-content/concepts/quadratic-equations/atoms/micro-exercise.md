For what value(s) of $k$ does the equation $x^2+kx+9=0$ have equal roots?

A) $k=6$ only
B) $k=-6$ only
C) $k=\pm 6$
D) $k=\pm 3$
E) no real value of $k$ works

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) $k=\pm 6$.**

Equal roots means the discriminant is exactly zero: $D=k^2-4(1)(9)=k^2-36=0$. Solving: $k^2=36 \Rightarrow k=\pm 6$ — both a positive and a negative value satisfy this, since squaring removes the sign.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and B) each keep only one sign, missing that both $k=6$ and $k=-6$ satisfy $k^2=36$.
- D) $k=\pm3$ comes from forgetting the factor of $4$: $k^2 = 9$ instead of $k^2=36$, i.e. computing $D=k^2-9$ by mistake.
- E) is wrong because $D=0$ absolutely does have real solutions here — this option is a trap for students who mis-simplify $D$ into something with no real roots.

</details>
