"A carbon-halogen bond means $S_N1$ or $S_N2$ is possible" misses a real exception: on an aryl (ring) carbon, essentially neither happens under ordinary conditions, whatever reagent is chosen.

Counterexample: chlorobenzene keeps its chlorine even when boiled with a strong, nucleophile-rich base — conditions that substitute an ordinary alkyl chloride almost instantly. Backside attack ($S_N2$) is blocked by the ring itself sitting in the way. Ionisation ($S_N1$) would need a stable, flat carbocation on that ring carbon, but the empty orbital left behind sits *in the ring's own plane*, at right angles to the ring's pi system — it gets no resonance help at all, and stays far too unstable to form.

A carbon-halogen bond only opens the *possibility* of either mechanism; whether one can actually reach that carbon at all — sterically or electronically — is a separate question entirely.
