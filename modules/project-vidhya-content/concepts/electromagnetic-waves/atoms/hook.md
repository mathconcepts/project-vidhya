A parallel-plate capacitor is being charged: a steady current of $2\text{ A}$ flows in the wire feeding one plate, and no wire at all connects the gap between the plates. Ampere's law, as Maxwell found it, relates a magnetic field around a loop to the current *through* any surface bounded by that loop — but a surface bulging into the gap between the plates has **zero conduction current** crossing it, while a flat surface just outside the plate has the full $2\text{ A}$.

Maxwell's fix: the *changing electric field* between the plates, as they charge up, counts as a current too — a **displacement current** — of exactly $2\text{ A}$ in this example, closing the gap so both surfaces agree. That one fix is also what predicts something new: a changing electric field always comes with a changing magnetic field beside it, and the two can regenerate each other outward through empty space as an **electromagnetic wave**.
