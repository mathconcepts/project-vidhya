A sealed gas cylinder left out in the sun on a hot afternoon can rupture, even though not one molecule was added or removed. Nothing about the *amount* of gas changed — so why does the pressure (force per unit area the gas pushes on the container wall, measured in pascals) rise enough to burst the metal?

Heating the gas does exactly one thing to it: it speeds up the gas molecules zipping around inside. Faster molecules slam into the walls **harder** and **more often** every second. Pressure, a number you read straight off a gauge, is really nothing more than billions of individual molecular collisions added up — count those collisions carefully enough, and the ideal gas equation $PV=nRT$ falls straight out of the counting, with no extra assumption needed.
