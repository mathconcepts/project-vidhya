**Newton's first law**: a body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by a net external force.

**Newton's second law**: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}=m\vec{a}$, where $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$ is the vector sum of every force acting on the body, $m$ is its mass, and $\vec{a}$ is its acceleration, in the same direction as $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$.

**Newton's third law**: for every action force one body exerts on a second body, the second body exerts an equal and opposite reaction force on the first — the two forces act on *different* bodies and never appear together in the same free-body diagram.

**Friction**: for two surfaces in contact with normal force $N$, static friction satisfies $f_s \le \mu_sN$ (it adjusts up to this maximum to prevent sliding), and kinetic friction (once sliding occurs) is $f_k=\mu_kN$, typically with $\mu_k \le \mu_s$.

**Connected bodies (Atwood-type system)**: masses $m_1$ (on a frictionless horizontal surface) and $m_2$ (hanging), linked by an inextensible light string over a frictionless, massless pulley, share the same magnitude of acceleration $a$ (since the string cannot stretch) and the same tension $T$ throughout the string:
$$T=m_1a, \qquad m_2g-T=m_2a \ \Longrightarrow\ a=\dfrac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}, \qquad T=\dfrac{m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}.$$

**Dynamics of circular motion**: the net force directed toward the centre of a circular path supplies the centripetal acceleration, $F_{\text{net,radial}}=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ — this is Newton's second law applied specifically to the radial direction, not a separate law.
