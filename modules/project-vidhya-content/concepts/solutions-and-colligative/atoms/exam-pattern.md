**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: molar mass from $\Delta T_f$ or $\Delta T_b$.** Given mass of solute, mass of solvent, and $\Delta T_f$ (or $\Delta T_b$) with $K_f$ (or $K_b$), find molar mass directly — check first whether the solute is stated to be a non-electrolyte (then $i=1$) or an electrolyte (then $i$ must be found or given).

- **MCQ: comparing colligative effects of different solutes at equal concentration.** The one with the LARGER van't Hoff factor always gives the larger colligative effect at the same molality/molarity — read off $i$ from how many ions the formula gives (or from a stated degree of dissociation), never from any other property of the solute.

- **Trap: mixing up which concentration unit a formula needs.** Relative lowering of vapour pressure wants mole fraction; boiling/freezing-point formulas want molality; osmotic pressure wants molarity — plugging the wrong one in gives a subtly wrong, not obviously wrong, answer.

- **Trap: an "apparent" molar mass from colligative data, for an electrolyte.** Skipping $i$ when the solute is known to ionise (or associate) gives a molar mass that is too low (or too high) — always check whether $i=1$ is actually justified before computing.

- **Time budget:** a direct molar-mass NAT (non-electrolyte, $i=1$) should take under $50$ seconds — one substitution, one division.
