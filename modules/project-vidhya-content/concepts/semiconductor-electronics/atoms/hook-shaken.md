Take one diode, wired into a circuit with a battery and a bulb one particular way round: the bulb lights up. Now flip only the battery's terminals — nothing else in the circuit changes — and the bulb goes dark, immediately.

The diode is a single **p-n junction**: a piece of **doped** silicon (silicon deliberately mixed with a trace of another element to change how it conducts) — one side donates extra electrons, the other side leaves extra "holes" (missing electrons that act like moving positive charge) — with the two sides joined at one junction.

Plain, undoped silicon does not do this trick at all; neither does an ordinary metal wire, which conducts equally well whichever way current is asked to flow. Only this specific two-sided doped structure conducts freely one way and blocks the other.
