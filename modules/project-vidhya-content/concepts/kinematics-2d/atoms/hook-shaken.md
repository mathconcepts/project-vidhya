A ball is launched at $u=20$ m/s, where $\sin\theta=0.6$ and $\cos\theta=0.8$. Launch point as origin, $x$ positive along the direction of travel, $y$ positive upward — settle that before splitting anything.

Split $u$ into two pieces. Horizontal: $u_x=u\cos\theta=20\times0.8=16$ m/s. Vertical: $u_y=u\sin\theta=20\times0.6=12$ m/s.

Now treat each piece on its own. Horizontally, $a_x=0$, so $u_x=16$ m/s the entire flight — it never changes. Vertically, $a_y=-10$ m/s$^2$, exactly the one-dimensional free-fall case already known.

Two numbers, $16$ and $12$, one shared clock $t$ between them — that is the whole method for two-dimensional motion.
