Compute the formal charge on nitrogen in the ammonium ion, $\text{NH}_4^+$.

Nitrogen's valence electrons (electrons in its own free atom, unbonded): 5.

Nitrogen has 0 lone-pair electrons in $\text{NH}_4^+$ — all its electrons are tied up in the 4 N–H bonds.

Nitrogen's bonding electrons: 4 bonds $\times$ 2 electrons per bond $= 8$ bonding electrons in total, shared between nitrogen and the 4 hydrogens.

Formal charge $= 5 - 0 - \dfrac{8}{2} = 5 - 4 = +1$.

This matches the ion's own overall charge, $+1$, exactly — a useful check that the electron count in the Lewis structure was done correctly, since a wrong bond count would have given a formal charge that does not match the known overall charge of the ion.
