**Which elements does "p-block" mean?** Groups 13 to 18 — every element whose last electron enters a $p$ subshell. Group 13 (boron family) through Group 17 (halogens) are non-metals or metalloids at the top, shading into metals lower down each group; Group 18 is the noble gases.

**What decides whether an oxide is basic, amphoteric or acidic?** Metallic character. A strongly electropositive element's oxide (e.g. $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$) is basic; a borderline metalloid's oxide (e.g. $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) is amphoteric; a non-metal's oxide (e.g. $\text{SO}_3$) is acidic. Metallic character falls left to right across a period and rises down a group, and oxide character tracks it directly.

**What is catenation, and which element does it best?** Catenation is an element bonding to itself in chains or rings. Carbon does it best of any element ($\text{C-C}$ bond is strong and stable); catenation ability falls sharply down Group 14 as bond strength between larger atoms drops. Sulfur also catenates well, forming $S_8$ rings.

**What is the inert pair effect, and what does it predict?** The outermost $s$-electron pair of a heavy $p$-block atom resists joining new bonds (poor shielding by inner $d$/$f$ electrons, poor orbital overlap of a large atom). It predicts that the (group minus two) oxidation state becomes progressively more stable than the full group oxidation state as you go down groups 13–15 — most visibly at thallium ($Tl^+$ favoured over $Tl^{3+}$) and lead ($Pb^{2+}$ favoured over $Pb^{4+}$).

**What is an interhalogen, and what decides its shape?** A compound of two different halogens, general formula $XX'_n$ with $n = 1, 3, 5, 7$, where $X$ is the larger, less electronegative halogen (central atom) and $X'$ is the smaller, more electronegative one (bonded around it). Shape follows VSEPR on the central atom's total electron domains: $ClF_3$ is T-shaped ($sp^3d$, 2 lone pairs), $BrF_5$ is square pyramidal ($sp^3d^2$, 1 lone pair), $IF_7$ is pentagonal bipyramidal ($sp^3d^3$, no lone pair).

**Why does xenon, a "noble" gas, form real compounds?** Xenon's outer electrons are shielded by many inner shells and held only loosely (low ionisation enthalpy for a noble gas), so a strongly electronegative partner like fluorine or oxygen can still pull enough electron density to force a bond. $XeF_2$ is linear ($sp^3d$, 3 lone pairs), $XeF_4$ is square planar ($sp^3d^2$, 2 lone pairs), $XeF_6$ is a distorted octahedron ($sp^3d^3$, 1 lone pair).

**What decides which oxyacid of a halogen is strongest?** The number of oxygen atoms on the central halogen (equivalently, its oxidation state). More oxygens pull electron density away from the $O-H$ bond, making the $H^+$ easier to release: for chlorine, $HOCl < HOClO < HOClO_2 < HOClO_3$ in acid strength.
