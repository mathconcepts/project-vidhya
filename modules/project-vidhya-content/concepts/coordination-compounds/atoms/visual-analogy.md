Picture a school committee (the metal ion) with 5 empty chairs arranged around it (the $d$ orbitals) at two slightly different table heights — 3 chairs a bit lower, 2 chairs a bit higher. When new members (electrons) arrive, they behave exactly like real students choosing seats: each one prefers an empty chair of its own before sitting next to someone already seated, unless the height difference between the two tables is annoying enough (a big energy gap) that squeezing in next to someone at the lower table is actually less trouble than climbing to the higher one.

A "weak" host committee (a weak-field ligand) barely raises the higher table at all, so new members happily spread out, one per chair, before anyone pairs up — this is the high-spin arrangement. A "strict" host committee (a strong-field ligand) raises the higher table so much that climbing up there is clearly worse than squeezing in below, so members pack into the lower chairs first, pairing up before anyone climbs — this is the low-spin arrangement. The seating pattern that results, not the committee's name, is exactly what crystal field theory is describing.
