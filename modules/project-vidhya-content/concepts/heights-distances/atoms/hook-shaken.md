Stand $30$ m from a tower's foot. Look up at its tip: the angle is $30°$. Walk $20$ m closer, to $10$ m away. Look up again: the angle is now $60°$. Two angles. Two distances. One unknown: the height. One angle by itself only gives a ratio, height divided by distance — not the height alone. This is what every heights-and-distances problem is: turn an angle into a length, using the ratio that connects an angle to the two legs of a right triangle. That ratio is tangent.
