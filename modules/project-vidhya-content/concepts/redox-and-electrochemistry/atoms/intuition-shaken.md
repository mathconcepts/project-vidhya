Take two half-reactions: $\text{Ag}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \text{Ag}$, with $E^\circ = +0.80\ \text{V}$, and $\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Zn}$, with $E^\circ = -0.76\ \text{V}$.

Compare the two numbers: $+0.80$ is more positive than $-0.76$, so silver's half-reaction pulls towards reduction more strongly. Silver gets reduced; zinc gets oxidised (reversed to $\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$).

Silver's electrode is the **cathode** (reduction). Zinc's electrode is the **anode** (oxidation).

$E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} = 0.80 - (-0.76) = 1.56\ \text{V}$.

Check: the sign is positive, so $2\text{Ag}^+ + \text{Zn} \rightarrow 2\text{Ag} + \text{Zn}^{2+}$ runs on its own, exactly as expected when the more-positive electrode is chosen as the cathode.
