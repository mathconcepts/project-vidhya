Take 11 g of carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$. Its molar mass is $12 + 2(16) = 44$ g/mol (one carbon at 12, two oxygens at 16 each).

Moles present: $\dfrac{11}{44} = 0.25$ mol.

Number of molecules: multiply by Avogadro's number, $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ per mole.

$0.25 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 1.5055 \times 10^{23}$ molecules.

Check the direction: 11 g is a quarter of 44 g (one full mole), so the molecule count should also be a quarter of $N_A$. A quarter of $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ is indeed $1.5055 \times 10^{23}$. The two routes agree, confirming the working mass $\to$ moles $\to$ particles direction was applied correctly.
