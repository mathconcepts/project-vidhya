**Paired concept: Quadratic Equations.**

A large share of JEE trigonometric equations are quadratic equations wearing a disguise — $2\sin^2\theta-\sin\theta-1=0$ is, underneath the substitution $s=\sin\theta$, exactly the same factoring problem as $2s^2-s-1=0$. Recognising the disguise fast means recognising the quadratic FIRST, before any trigonometry is applied.

**Drill**: Solve $2\cos^2\theta+\cos\theta-1=0$ for the general solution. Substitute $s=\cos\theta$, factor the resulting quadratic exactly as you would for $2s^2+s-1=0$, then translate each root of $s$ back into a general-solution formula for $\theta$ using $\cos\theta=\cos\alpha \Rightarrow \theta=2n\pi\pm\alpha$. Before trusting the factoring, check: does $s=-1$ or $s=1/2$ actually satisfy $2s^2+s-1=0$?
