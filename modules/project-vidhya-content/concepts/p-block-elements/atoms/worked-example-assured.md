**Problem:** State the shortcut for finding the central atom in any interhalogen, and confirm it against $IF_7$.

---

**Step 1 — The shortcut.** The central atom, $X$, is always the LARGER, less electronegative halogen — never assume the smaller atom sits in the middle, the way ordinary "least electronegative atom is usually central" intuition might suggest.

---

**Step 2 — Confirm it against $IF_7$.** Iodine (larger, less electronegative) sits central, surrounded by 7 fluorine atoms. Count domains around iodine: 7 valence electrons, each forming one $I-F$ bond, uses all 7 electrons with none left over — 7 bonding domains, 0 lone pairs, $sp^3d^3$ hybridisation, pentagonal bipyramidal shape. $\boxed{IF_7: \text{iodine central, } sp^3d^3, \text{pentagonal bipyramidal, 0 lone pairs}}$.

---

**Step 3 — Why the shortcut is not optional.** Fluorine has no accessible $d$ orbitals to expand its bonding beyond one bond per atom, and its own small size caps it there regardless. Putting fluorine central in $IF_7$ is not just unlikely, it is structurally impossible — fluorine simply cannot hold seven bonds. The rule "larger halogen is central" is a hard requirement enforced by fluorine's own limits, not a convenient guess.
