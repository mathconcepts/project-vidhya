**Problem:** Cyclopentadiene's $\text{CH}_2$ carbon can lose a proton (giving the anion $\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^-$) or lose a hydride (giving the cation $\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^+$). Which ion is aromatic?

---

**Step 1 — Count the anion's electrons.** Two double bonds: $4$ electrons. Losing a proton leaves a full lone pair in a p-orbital: $+2$ electrons. Total: $4+2=6$.

---

**Step 2 — Test the anion against $4n+2$.** $4n+2=6 \Rightarrow 4n=4 \Rightarrow n=1$. Whole number. Aromatic.

---

**Step 3 — Count the cation's electrons.** Same two double bonds: $4$ electrons. Losing a hydride takes both electrons of that bond away, leaving an empty p-orbital: $+0$ electrons. Total: $4+0=4$.

---

**Step 4 — Check the cation against $4n+2$.** $4n+2=4 \Rightarrow 4n=2 \Rightarrow n=0.5$. Not a whole number. Not aromatic — and since $4$ is exactly $4n$ with $n=1$, it is antiaromatic.

---

**Step 5 — Give the answer, then verify it.** $\boxed{\text{the anion } \text{C}_5\text{H}_5^-\text{ is aromatic; the cation } \text{C}_5\text{H}_5^+\text{ is antiaromatic}}$. Verify: $6=4(1)+2$ holds exactly; $4=4(1)+2$ does not.
