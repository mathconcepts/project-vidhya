A vending machine takes one coin at a time and needs a coin worth at least ₹$10$ before it dispenses anything at all. Feed it a ₹$5$ coin, over and over — nothing happens, ever, because the machine never adds up separate coins. Feed it one ₹$15$ coin instead, and it dispenses the item and hands back ₹$5$ change.

This is Einstein's photoelectric picture, coin for coin: one **photon** (a discrete packet of light energy, like one coin) meets one electron. If that photon's energy $hf$ is below the **work function** $\phi$ (the ₹$10$ minimum), nothing happens, however many such photons arrive per second. If a photon's energy clears $\phi$, the electron leaves with the leftover as kinetic energy, $KE_{max}=hf-\phi$ — exactly the machine's change. More coins per second (brighter light) means more successful transactions, never a bigger leftover from any one of them.
