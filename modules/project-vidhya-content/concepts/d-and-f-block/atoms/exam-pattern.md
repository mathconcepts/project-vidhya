**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ/NAT: electron configuration of $Cr$, $Cu$, or their ions.** The moment chromium or copper appears in a configuration question, assume the Aufbau exception ($3d^54s^1$, $3d^{10}4s^1$) is the point being tested, not the plain filling order.

- **NAT: spin-only magnetic moment calculation.** Find the ion's $d$-electron count first (remove $4s$ electrons before $3d$ ones), then fill orbitals by Hund's rule to get $n$, then apply $\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}$. If the question gives a ligand, check whether it is strong-field or weak-field before assuming high spin.

- **Trap: "d-block metal implies coloured compound".** Check the specific oxidation state's $d$-electron count before assuming colour — $Zn^{2+}$ ($d^{10}$) and $Sc^{3+}$ ($d^0$) are colourless, and $MnO_4^-$/$CrO_4^{2-}$ ($d^0$) are strongly coloured anyway, through charge transfer rather than a $d$-$d$ jump.

- **Assertion-reason: lanthanoid contraction and its consequence.** Expect a claim linking the steady shrinkage across $La$ to $Lu$ to the near-identical radii of $Zr$/$Hf$ or $Nb$/$Ta$ — check that the reasoning names poor $4f$ shielding, not just "they are in the same group".

- **Time budget:** a spin-only magnetic moment NAT should take under 60 seconds once the $d$-electron count and spin state are known — the arithmetic is just one square root.
