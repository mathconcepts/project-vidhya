Draw the two slits $S_1$, $S_2$, separated by $d=0.5\text{ mm}$, and a screen a distance $D=1\text{ m}$ away, much farther than $d$. Draw a line from each slit to the same point $P$ on the screen, height $y$ above the centre line. Because $D$ is so much larger than $d$ or $y$, these two lines are almost exactly parallel — and the small triangle they form with the centre line has a base angle whose tangent is $y/D$.

The extra distance the $S_2$-line travels compared to the $S_1$-line — the **path difference** — works out, from that triangle, to $\Delta \approx d\sin\theta \approx d\tan\theta = \dfrac{dy}{D}$, valid whenever $y$ is small compared to $D$ (true for practically every JEE Main YDSE question).

At $y=2\text{ mm}$: $\Delta = \dfrac{(0.5\times10^{-3})(2\times10^{-3})}{1} = 1000\text{ nm} = 2\lambda$ (using $\lambda=500\text{ nm}$) — a whole number of wavelengths, so $P$ is bright. At $y=1.5\text{ mm}$ instead: $\Delta = \dfrac{(0.5\times10^{-3})(1.5\times10^{-3})}{1} = 750\text{ nm} = 1.5\lambda$ — a half-integer number of wavelengths, so this closer point is dark. The same triangle, the same formula, two different points, two opposite outcomes — because path difference, not distance from the centre, is what interference actually tracks.
