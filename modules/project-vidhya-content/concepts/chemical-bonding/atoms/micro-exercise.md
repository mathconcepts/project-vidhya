A diatomic molecule has 8 bonding electrons and 6 antibonding electrons, according to molecular orbital theory. What is its bond order?

A) 0.5
B) 1
C) 1.5
D) 2
E) 7

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) 1.**

$\text{Bond order} = \dfrac{\text{bonding} - \text{antibonding}}{2} = \dfrac{8-6}{2} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $0.5$ comes from dividing by $4$ instead of $2$.
- C) $1.5$ comes from computing $\dfrac{8-6}{2}$ incorrectly as $\dfrac{3}{2}$ — a subtraction slip ($8-6=2$, not $3$).
- D) $2$ comes from swapping the numerator to $8-4$ (using the wrong antibonding count).
- E) $7$ is the *average* of $8$ and $6$, which is not what the bond order formula computes at all.

</details>
