Bohr's fix to Rutherford's nuclear atom: an electron cannot orbit the nucleus at just any distance, only at certain fixed distances called **energy levels**, numbered $n=1,2,3,\dots$. Each level has a fixed energy $E_n$ — for hydrogen, $E_n=-13.6/n^2$ eV, more negative (lower) for smaller $n$.

Light is only ever released when an electron **jumps down** from a higher level to a lower one, and its energy is fixed by the size of the gap it just crossed — not by which orbit it happens to land in afterwards: $E_{\text{photon}}=E_{n_{\text{high}}}-E_{n_{\text{low}}}$. A hydrogen atom can only ever emit a handful of exact colours (a **line spectrum**) because there are only so many level-to-level gaps to jump across — never a smooth rainbow of every colour in between.

This is the quantum picture's core claim: energy arrives in fixed steps, not a continuous slide. A spectral line's colour tells you which *two* levels an electron jumped between, never which single orbit it is sitting in at some instant — a single stationary orbit, with no jump happening, emits nothing at all.
