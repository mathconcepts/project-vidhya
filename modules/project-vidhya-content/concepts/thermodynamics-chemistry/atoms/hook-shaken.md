A reaction has $\Delta H = -58$ kJ/mol (enthalpy change — heat released, since it is negative) and $\Delta S = -175$ J/(mol$\cdot$K) (entropy change — negative means disorder decreases) at $T=300$ K.

Compute $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$. First convert units so both terms are in kJ: $T\Delta S = 300 \times (-175) = -52500$ J/mol $= -52.5$ kJ/mol.

Now substitute: $\Delta G = -58 - (-52.5) = -58 + 52.5 = -5.5$ kJ/mol.

$\Delta G$ is negative, so the reaction is spontaneous at $300$ K — even though its entropy term ($-T\Delta S = +52.5$ kJ/mol) actually works *against* spontaneity here. The negative $\Delta H$ term is large enough to overcome it.
