**Question:** State Kirchhoff's junction and loop rules, the sign convention for crossing a resistor and a cell, and the difference between emf and terminal voltage.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Junction rule:** $\sum I_{\text{in}}=\sum I_{\text{out}}$ at any junction.

**Loop rule:** $\sum \Delta V=0$ around any closed loop.

**Sign convention:** crossing a resistor *with* the assumed current direction is a drop ($-IR$), against it is a rise ($+IR$). Crossing a cell $-$ to $+$ is a rise ($+\text{emf}$), $+$ to $-$ is a drop ($-\text{emf}$).

**Emf vs terminal voltage:** emf is the cell's maximum possible push with no current flowing. Terminal voltage is what's actually measured across the cell once current flows: $\text{emf}-Ir$ while discharging, $\text{emf}+Ir$ while being charged.

</details>
