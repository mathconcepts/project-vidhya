Take $L_1: x+y-1=0$ and $L_2: 2x-y+3=0$.

Every equation $L_1+\lambda L_2=0$, for any number $\lambda$, is a straight line through the exact point where $L_1$ and $L_2$ cross — even though you never solved for that crossing point.

Check $\lambda=0$: you get $L_1$ back exactly. Check $\lambda=1$: $L_1+L_2=3x+2=0$ — a new line (just $x=-\tfrac{2}{3}$), still through the same crossing point.

That single fact — any $\lambda$ you pick, still the same crossing point — is the whole idea behind family of lines.
