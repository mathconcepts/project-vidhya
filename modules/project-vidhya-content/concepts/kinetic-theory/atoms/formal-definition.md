**System and boundary**: the system is a fixed number $N$ of identical, point-sized gas molecules of mass $m$ each, sealed inside a rigid container of volume $V$ (in $\text{m}^3$). The container's inner walls are the boundary — the only place the system interacts with the outside world, through elastic molecular collisions.

**Ideal gas equation**: $PV=nRT$, with $P$ = pressure (Pa), $V$ = volume ($\text{m}^3$), $n$ = amount of gas (mol), $R=8.314\ \text{J/(mol\cdot K)}$ (universal gas constant), $T$ = absolute temperature (K, never Celsius).

**Kinetic theory pressure equation**: $PV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle v^2\rangle$, where $N$ is the number of molecules and $\langle v^2\rangle$ (units $\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$) is the mean of the squared molecular speed.

**Temperature-energy link**: $\frac{1}{2}m\langle v^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2}kT$, with $k=1.38\times10^{-23}\ \text{J/K}$ (Boltzmann constant). This is the mean *translational* kinetic energy of one molecule.

**Degrees of freedom and equipartition theorem**: each independent way a molecule can store energy (a "degree of freedom") carries a mean energy of $\frac{1}{2}kT$ per molecule. Monatomic gas: $f=3$ (translation only), $U=\frac{3}{2}nRT$. Diatomic gas at ordinary temperatures: $f=5$ (3 translational + 2 rotational), $U=\frac{5}{2}nRT$.

**Typical speeds** (all in m/s, $M$ = molar mass in kg/mol): most probable speed $v_p=\sqrt{2RT/M}$; average speed $v_{avg}=\sqrt{8RT/(\pi M)}$; root-mean-square speed $v_{rms}=\sqrt{3RT/M}$. Always $v_p<v_{avg}<v_{rms}$.

**Mean free path**: $\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi d^2 n}$, with $d$ = molecular diameter (m), $n$ = number density of molecules ($\text{m}^{-3}$) — the average distance (m) a molecule travels between successive collisions.
