**Problem:** A block of mass $m_1=3$ kg rests on a frictionless horizontal table. It is connected by a light, inextensible string over a frictionless, massless pulley at the table's edge to a hanging block of mass $m_2=2$ kg. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

---

**Two diagrams, two equations.** $m_1$ (positive toward the pulley): $T=m_1a$. $m_2$ (positive downward): $m_2g-T=m_2a$. Adding them: $a=\dfrac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}=\dfrac{20}{5}=4$ m/s$^2$, so $T=m_1a=12$ N.

$$\boxed{a = 4\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad T = 12\ \text{N}}$$

---

**Where an examiner adds friction to catch a copied formula.** Suppose $m_1$'s table were not frictionless, with coefficient $\mu$. Copying $a=\dfrac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$ unchanged would be wrong: $m_1$'s equation now reads $T-\mu m_1g=m_1a$ (friction opposes $m_1$'s motion, toward the pulley being positive), which changes both the numerator and the structure of the combined result to $a=\dfrac{m_2g-\mu m_1g}{m_1+m_2}$. The "add the two equations" method still works — it always will, for any forces named on either diagram — but the *formula* memorised from the frictionless case does not transfer; only the *method* does.
