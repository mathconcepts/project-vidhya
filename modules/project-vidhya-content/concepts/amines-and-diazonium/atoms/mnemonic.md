**"Gas phase rewards the crowd; water rewards the grip."** In the gas phase, more alkyl groups on nitrogen simply wins (tertiary highest). In water, what counts is how many $\text{N-H}$ bonds are left for water to grip onto — a tertiary amine, with none of its original hydrogens left, loses that grip and drops down the order.

**"Aniline's lone pair ran away into the ring."** Aniline is a weak base not because it lacks a substituent, but because its lone pair delocalises into the aromatic ring instead of staying free to catch a proton.

**"Diazonium only shakes hands with a rich ring."** The diazonium ion is a weak electrophile — it only couples with a ring an electron-donating group ($\text{-OH}$, $\text{-NH}_2$) has already made electron-rich. No donation, no handshake.

**"Cold makes the diazonium salt; warmth kills it."** $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$ for diazotisation — above that, decomposition wins before any coupling reaction gets a chance.
