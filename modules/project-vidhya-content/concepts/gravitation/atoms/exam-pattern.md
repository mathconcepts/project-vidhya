**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: orbital speed or period, given $g$ and $R$ instead of $G$ and $M$.** Substitute $GM=gR^2$ immediately rather than looking up $G$ and a planetary mass separately — this avoids carrying a $10^{-11}$ and a $10^{24}$ through the same line of working.

- **MCQ: "gravity at height $h$" vs "gravity at depth $d$".** Read carefully whether the question says *above* or *below* the surface — height uses $g(R/(R+h))^2$, depth uses $g(1-d/R)$, and mixing them up is a deliberately common distractor pair.

- **Trap: escape velocity independent of the launch angle or the mass of the escaping object.** $v_e=\sqrt{2gR}$ depends only on the planet's own $g$ and $R$ — an option that scales $v_e$ with the projectile's mass, or claims a "steeper launch needs a higher escape speed", is a distractor built on a false dependency.

- **Trap: elliptical-orbit speed treated as constant.** If a question describes an elliptical path (even without the word "ellipse" — look for "closest approach" or "farthest point" language), speed varies with distance via conserved angular momentum; only a circular orbit has one fixed speed throughout.

- **Time budget:** an orbital-speed or escape-velocity NAT using $GM=gR^2$ should take under 40 seconds — one substitution, one square root, done.
