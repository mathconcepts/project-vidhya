**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: induced emf from rate of change.** Given a flux or field changing between two stated values over a stated time, compute $\varepsilon$ directly from $A\,|dB/dt|$ or $N\,|d\Phi/dt|$ — no need to know the circuit's resistance unless current or power is also asked.

- **MCQ: direction of induced current.** The moment a question describes a field "increasing" or "decreasing" and asks for a current's direction, decide first whether flux is going up or down — never look at the field's own direction. A decreasing field gets a current reinforcing it; an increasing field gets one opposing it.

- **Trap: back-emf questions dressed up as "current is large, so opposition is large".** A steady current, however big, produces zero back-emf. The question is really testing $dI/dt$, not $I$ — watch for the word "steady" or "constant" quietly making the answer zero.

- **Trap: motional emf with an angled rod or field.** JEE Main occasionally rotates the rod, or the field, away from the "all mutually perpendicular" setup. When that happens, only the perpendicular component of velocity (or field) contributes — using the raw speed without resolving it overstates the emf.

- **Time budget:** a straightforward $\varepsilon = A\,dB/dt$ or $BLv$ NAT question should take under $40$ seconds; the direction (Lenz's law) part of an MCQ should take under $20$ seconds once flux's rise-or-fall is identified.
