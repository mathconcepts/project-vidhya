**Travelling wave**: $y(x,t)=A\sin(kx-\omega t)$, with $A$ = amplitude (m), $k=2\pi/\lambda$ = wave number ($\text{m}^{-1}$), $\omega=2\pi f$ = angular frequency (rad/s).

**Wave equation**: $v=f\lambda$, with $v$ = wave speed (m/s), $f$ = frequency (Hz), $\lambda$ = wavelength (m).

**Speed of a wave on a string**: $v=\sqrt{T_s/\mu}$ ($T_s$ = tension, N; $\mu$ = mass per unit length, kg/m).

**Speed of sound in a gas** (Newton–Laplace): $v=\sqrt{\gamma P/\rho}$ ($\gamma=C_p/C_v$, $P$ = pressure in Pa, $\rho$ = density in $\text{kg/m}^3$).

**Particle velocity** (a point on the medium, NOT the wave itself): $v_{particle}=\partial y/\partial t=-A\omega\cos(kx-\omega t)$, in m/s — a different quantity from wave speed $v$, with a different value and different units of dependence.

**Standing wave** (superposition of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions): $y=2A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)$. Nodes (zero amplitude always) at $x=n\lambda/2$; antinodes (maximum amplitude) at $x=(2n+1)\lambda/4$.

**Beats**: two waves of close frequencies $f_1,f_2$ produce a beat frequency $f_{beat}=|f_1-f_2|$ (Hz).

**Organ pipes**: closed pipe (one end closed), $f_n=\dfrac{(2n-1)v}{4L}$, odd harmonics only. Open pipe (both ends open), $f_n=\dfrac{nv}{2L}$, all harmonics, $n=1,2,3,\dots$.

**Doppler effect** (sound, medium at rest): $f'=f\dfrac{v\pm v_o}{v\mp v_s}$, with $v_o$ = observer's speed (positive toward the source), $v_s$ = source's speed (positive toward the observer), $v$ = speed of sound. Numerator's sign is $+$ for an observer approaching, $-$ for receding; denominator's sign is $-$ for a source approaching, $+$ for receding.
