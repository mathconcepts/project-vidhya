- **Confusing particle speed with wave speed**: wave speed $v=f\lambda$ is how fast the overall *pattern* travels through the medium, and stays fixed by the medium's own properties. Particle speed $v_{particle}=\partial y/\partial t$ is how fast one point of the medium moves up and down, and it constantly changes — zero at the crest and trough, largest as the point crosses its resting position. A question asking for "the speed of the wave" and one asking for "the speed of a particle at $x=\dots,\ t=\dots$" want two entirely different numbers, from two entirely different formulas.

- **Getting the Doppler sign wrong for approach versus recede**: an approaching source raises the heard frequency, so the denominator of $f'=f\,v/(v\mp v_s)$ subtracts $v_s$; a receding source lowers it, so the denominator adds $v_s$. Reversing this swaps a rise in pitch for a drop, and the wrong-signed answer still looks like an ordinary number, not an obviously broken one.

- **Assuming frequency changes as a wave crosses into a new medium**: it does not. Wave speed and wavelength both change at a boundary, but frequency stays fixed — it is set by the source, once, at the very start.

- **Using the open-pipe harmonic formula for a closed pipe, or the reverse**: a closed pipe (one end sealed) allows only odd harmonics; treating it as an open pipe silently predicts frequencies (the even harmonics) that the closed pipe never actually produces.

- **Adding two waves' amplitudes to get the beat frequency**: beat frequency comes from the *difference* of the two frequencies, $|f_1-f_2|$ — amplitude has nothing to do with how many beats occur per second, only with how loud each beat sounds.
