**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: "which shape reaches the bottom first" on an incline.** Use the shape-factor form $v=\sqrt{2gh/(1+k^2)}$, where $k^2=I_{cm}/MR^2$ is a pure number ($\tfrac12$ disc, $\tfrac25$ solid sphere, $1$ ring) — a smaller $k^2$ wins the race, and $M$, $R$, $h$ cancel out entirely, so no numeric values for mass or radius are ever needed.

- **MCQ: parallel axis theorem with the axis unstated in words.** The question may describe the target axis only through a diagram (through an edge, through a marked point) — locate that axis and measure $d$ from the centre of mass to it *before* touching the formula, never assume the given $I_{cm}$ already matches the target axis.

- **Trap: rolling without slipping vs a wheel spinning in place.** A wheel spinning on ice with its centre fixed (like a car stuck in mud) has $v_{cm}=0$ while $\omega\ne0$ — the rolling condition $v=\omega R$ does not apply, because there is genuine sliding at the contact point. JEE distractors often reuse the rolling-without-slipping formula exactly where the question has ruled it out.

- **Trap: angular momentum conservation with a torque hiding in the setup.** A ball dropped onto a spinning disc, or a person jumping onto a rotating platform off-centre, involves friction/impact torques that are *internal* to the system as a whole — conserve $L$ for the whole system, not for one piece of it in isolation.

- **Time budget:** a parallel-axis-theorem NAT should take under 40 seconds once the axis and $d$ are correctly identified — the arithmetic itself is one multiplication and one addition.
