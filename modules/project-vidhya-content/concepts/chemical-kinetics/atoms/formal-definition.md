**Rate of reaction**: how fast concentration changes with time. For $aA+bB\rightarrow cC+dD$, $\text{rate}=-\dfrac{1}{a}\dfrac{d[A]}{dt}=-\dfrac{1}{b}\dfrac{d[B]}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{c}\dfrac{d[C]}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{d}\dfrac{d[D]}{dt}$ — dividing by each species' own stoichiometric coefficient so the rate is the same number, whichever species is tracked.

**Rate law and order**: $\text{rate}=k[A]^m[B]^n$, found by experiment. **Order** $=m+n$ (with respect to the whole reaction); the order with respect to $A$ alone is $m$. Order can be zero, a fraction, or negative, and is never assumed from the balanced equation's coefficients.

**Molecularity**: the number of reactant molecules (or atoms/ions) that collide together in one elementary (single-step) reaction. Always a small positive whole number ($1$, $2$, or rarely $3$); applies only to one elementary step, never to a multi-step overall reaction.

**Integrated rate law — zero order**: $[A]=[A]_0-kt$. Half-life $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{[A]_0}{2k}$ — DEPENDS on the starting concentration.

**Integrated rate law — first order**: $[A]=[A]_0e^{-kt}$, equivalently $\ln[A]=\ln[A]_0-kt$. Half-life $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln 2}{k}\approx\dfrac{0.693}{k}$ — INDEPENDENT of the starting concentration.

**Arrhenius equation**: $k=Ae^{-E_a/RT}$, where $E_a$ is the activation energy (the minimum extra energy colliding molecules need before they can actually react) and $A$ is a constant (the frequency factor). A higher temperature always increases $k$, since more molecules then have enough energy to cross $E_a$.
