Junction and loop rules are second nature by this stage. Here's a pair that keeps getting collapsed into one: **resistivity** ($\rho$, a pure property of the material — copper's $\rho$ is the same whether it's a thick cable or a thin wire) and **resistance** ($R$, a property of one specific piece of that material, depending on its length $L$ and cross-sectional area $A$ via $R=\rho L/A$) are not interchangeable, even though both get called "resistance" loosely in conversation.

Counterexample: take a wire of resistivity $\rho$, length $L$, area $A$, so $R_1=\rho L/A$. Stretch a second wire of the *same* material to twice the length **and** twice the cross-sectional area: $R_2=\rho(2L)/(2A)=\rho L/A=R_1$ — identical resistance, despite being a physically longer wire. "Longer wire → more resistance" only holds when area is held fixed; changing both length and area together can leave $R$ completely unchanged while $\rho$, correctly, never moved at all.
