A wave on a string has wave speed $v=200$ m/s and frequency $f=50$ Hz. A single particle on the string, at the instant it crosses its resting position, is moving at $8$ m/s. Which of these statements is correct?

A) The wave itself is travelling at $8$ m/s.
B) The wave's wavelength is $\lambda=v/f=4$ m, unrelated to the particle's $8$ m/s.
C) The particle's speed and the wave's speed must always be equal.
D) The wavelength depends on the particle's speed, so it cannot be found from $v$ and $f$ alone.
E) $8$ m/s is the wave's speed divided by its wavelength.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B).**

$\lambda=v/f=200/50=4$ m — this comes entirely from the wave speed and frequency, and has nothing to do with how fast any one particle happens to be moving at some instant.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and C) both confuse particle speed with wave speed — these are two different quantities with two different formulas, and there is no reason for them to be equal.
- D) wrongly assumes wavelength needs particle speed as an input; it does not.
- E) invents a formula that does not exist; wave speed divided by wavelength gives frequency, not a particle's speed.

</details>
