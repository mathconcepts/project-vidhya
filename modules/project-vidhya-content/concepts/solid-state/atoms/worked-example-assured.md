Density here came out to a specific number because $Z$, $M$, and $a$ were all given or found directly — a shortcut exists instead for a subtly different, more common comparison question: which of two STRUCTURES of the same element is denser, without computing either density fully.

For the SAME atomic radius, fcc ($74\%$ packing) is always denser than bcc ($68\%$), which is always denser than simple cubic ($52.4\%$) — packing efficiency and density move together, since $Z$ and $a$ both change together to keep the atoms genuinely touching.

**This shortcut assumes the atomic radius stays fixed across the comparison — nowhere near a safe assumption between two DIFFERENT elements.** A denser metal is not automatically the one with higher packing efficiency if its individual atoms are also heavier or larger; the packing-efficiency ordering above only settles a comparison of the SAME atom packed two different ways.
