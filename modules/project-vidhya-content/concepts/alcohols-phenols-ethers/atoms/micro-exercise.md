Rank these three compounds from **most** acidic to **least** acidic: ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$), phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), and p-nitrophenol (phenol with a $-\text{NO}_2$ group directly opposite the $-\text{OH}$).

A) ethanol $>$ phenol $>$ p-nitrophenol
B) p-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ ethanol
C) phenol $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ ethanol
D) All three are equally acidic, since all three carry an $-\text{OH}$ group
E) p-nitrophenol $>$ ethanol $>$ phenol

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) p-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ ethanol.**

Phenol beats ethanol because its phenoxide ion spreads the negative charge by resonance onto the ring, while ethanol's alkoxide has no such ring to spread into. p-Nitrophenol beats plain phenol because the $-\text{NO}_2$ group sits exactly at the para position, one of the phenoxide ion's own charge-bearing carbons, and pulls on that charge directly by resonance — stabilising the ion even further.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) reverses the whole order.
- C) puts phenol ahead of p-nitrophenol — backwards, since the extra electron-withdrawing group only adds stabilisation.
- D) is wrong: having the same functional group says nothing about acidity once resonance and substituent effects are considered.
- E) correctly ranks p-nitrophenol first but wrongly puts ethanol ahead of phenol.

</details>
