**Problem:** Find the binding energy per nucleon of helium-4. Given $m_H=1.007825$ u, $m_n=1.008665$ u, $M(\text{He-4})=4.002603$ u, $1$ u $=931.5$ MeV$/c^2$.

---

**Step 1 — The arithmetic, compressed.** $\Delta m=2m_H+2m_n-M=0.030377$ u $\Rightarrow BE=0.030377\times931.5\approx28.30$ MeV $\Rightarrow BE/A\approx7.07$ MeV/nucleon.

$$\boxed{BE/A\approx7.07\text{ MeV per nucleon}}$$

---

**Why this number matters more than the arithmetic.** $7.07$ MeV/nucleon sits on the steep *rising* side of the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve, well below iron's peak of about $8.79$ MeV/nucleon. Fusing light nuclei into something closer to iron moves every resulting nucleon *up* this curve — that released difference is exactly why fusing light nuclei releases energy.

The same logic runs from the far side too: uranium-235 sits at about $7.59$ MeV/nucleon, also below iron's peak, on the slowly *falling* heavy end. Splitting it into two medium-mass fragments moves those nucleons up toward the peak as well — this is why splitting very heavy nuclei also releases energy.

**The trap this closes:** assuming any combining or splitting releases energy. It never does moving *away* from the peak — fusing two iron nuclei into something heavier, or splitting helium into loose nucleons, both take energy in rather than giving it out.
