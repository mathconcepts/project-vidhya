- **Comparing masses instead of moles in a limiting-reagent question**: "16 g is more than 4 g, so $\text{O}_2$ can't be limiting" ignores that the equation's coefficients relate *moles*, not grams. Always convert every given mass to moles before comparing anything.

- **Molarity mistaken for molality**: molarity ($M$) is moles of solute per litre of *solution*; molality ($m$) is moles of solute per kilogram of *solvent*. The denominators are genuinely different substances (solution vs. solvent alone), so the two numbers are not interchangeable — swapping them in a dilution or colligative-property formula gives a wrong answer that still looks like a normal number.

- **Ignoring the balanced equation's coefficients**: reading $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ as a 1:1:1 ratio and dividing masses directly, instead of first balancing the equation and then applying the actual 2:1:2 mole ratio.

- **Confusing empirical formula with molecular formula**: stating the empirical formula ($\text{CH}_2\text{O}$) as if it were automatically the molecular formula, without checking whether the molecular mass demands a multiplier $n>1$.

- **Reporting a mass in kJ-style unit confusion elsewhere in the syllabus**: while this concept itself stays in grams and moles, the same "which unit does this number even belong to" carelessness — grams vs. moles, per-mole vs. per-molecule — is exactly what costs marks here, so re-check the unit attached to every number before substituting it.
