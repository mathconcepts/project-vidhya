Out of every $8000$ **alpha particles** (fast, positively-charged particles, each a helium nucleus: $2$ protons plus $2$ neutrons) fired at a thin gold foil, about $1$ bounces back at an angle bigger than $90°$ — sometimes almost straight back the way it came.

That one number breaks the old model completely. The old "plum pudding" model pictured an atom's positive charge smeared evenly through its whole volume, like a soft ball — nothing inside it solid enough to send a fast, heavy particle straight backward. A soft, spread-out target cannot do that, however many times you fire at it.

The only fix: pack almost all of an atom's mass, and its entire positive charge, into one tiny, dense **nucleus** at the centre. Most alpha particles miss this tiny target and sail straight through — $7999$ out of $8000$, with barely any bend at all. The rare $1$ that bounces straight back has hit the nucleus almost dead-on.
