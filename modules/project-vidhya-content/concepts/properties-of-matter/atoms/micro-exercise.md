A wire of cross-section $1\text{ mm}^2$ and length $1\text{ m}$ stretches by $0.5\text{ mm}$ under a $100\text{ N}$ load. What is its Young's modulus?

A) $1\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$
B) $2\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$
C) $5\times10^{10}\text{ Pa}$
D) $1\times10^{8}\text{ Pa}$
E) Cannot be found without knowing the material

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $2\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$.**

Stress: $\sigma = F/A = 100/(10^{-6}) = 1\times10^8\text{ Pa}$. Strain: $\epsilon = \Delta L/L_0 = 0.5\times10^{-3}/1 = 5\times10^{-4}$. Young's modulus: $Y=\sigma/\epsilon = (1\times10^8)/(5\times10^{-4}) = 2\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $1\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$ comes from forgetting to convert $0.5\text{ mm}$ to metres correctly, treating strain as $5\times10^{-3}$ instead of $5\times10^{-4}$.
- C) $5\times10^{10}\text{ Pa}$ divides the stress by $2$ by mistake instead of by $\epsilon$.
- D) $1\times10^{8}\text{ Pa}$ is just the stress value itself, mistaken for $Y$ directly.
- E) is wrong because $Y$ is computed here purely from the given measurements ($F$, $A$, $L_0$, $\Delta L$) — no separate material lookup is needed.

</details>
