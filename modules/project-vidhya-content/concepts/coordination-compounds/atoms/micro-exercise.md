Which type of isomerism do $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$ and $[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br$ show?

A) Linkage isomerism
B) Ionisation isomerism
C) Coordination isomerism
D) Geometrical isomerism
E) Optical isomerism

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) Ionisation isomerism.**

Both formulas have the same overall composition — one $Co$, five $NH_3$, one $Br$, one $SO_4$ — but they differ in which ion sits inside the coordination sphere (in brackets) and which sits outside as the free counter-ion. In $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$, $Br^-$ is a ligand and $SO_4^{2-}$ is the free ion. In $[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br$, $SO_4^{2-}$ is a ligand and $Br^-$ is the free ion. Different ions produced in solution from the same formula is exactly ionisation isomerism.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) Linkage isomerism needs one ambidentate ligand bonding through different atoms — neither $Br^-$ nor $SO_4^{2-}$ is doing that here.
- C) Coordination isomerism needs both a complex cation and a complex anion in the same compound, with ligands redistributed between them — this compound has only one complex ion plus a simple counter-ion.
- D) and E) both require a fixed set of ligands whose spatial arrangement changes — here, the actual ligand SET itself is different between the two compounds, not just its arrangement.
