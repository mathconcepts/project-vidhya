What are the hybridisation and molecular shape of $XeF_4$?

A) $sp^3d$, see-saw
B) $sp^3d^2$, square planar
C) $sp^3$, tetrahedral
D) $sp^3d^2$, octahedral
E) $sp^3d$, T-shaped

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $sp^3d^2$, square planar.**

Xenon has 8 valence electrons. Four $Xe-F$ bonds use 4 of them, leaving $8-4=4$ electrons, which pair up into 2 lone pairs. Total electron domains: $4$ bonding $+ 2$ lone pair $= 6$, giving $sp^3d^2$ hybridisation and an octahedral electron-domain arrangement. The two lone pairs sit directly opposite each other (minimising lone-pair repulsion), leaving the four fluorine atoms in one flat plane — a square planar molecular shape.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and E) belong to molecules with only 5 total domains (like $BrF_3$'s $sp^3d$), not $XeF_4$'s 6.
- C) ignores the 2 lone pairs entirely, as if xenon used only its bonding electrons.
- D) gets the hybridisation right but the molecular shape wrong — octahedral describes the electron-domain arrangement including the lone pairs, not the shape traced by the atoms once the two lone pairs are placed opposite each other.

</details>
