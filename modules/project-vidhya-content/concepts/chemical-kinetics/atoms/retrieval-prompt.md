**Question:** Define order and molecularity, and state one key difference between them.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Order**: the sum of the exponents in the experimentally-determined rate law $\text{rate}=k[A]^m[B]^n$; it is $m+n$, found by measurement, and can be zero, fractional, or negative.

**Molecularity**: the number of reactant species that collide together in one elementary (single) step of a reaction mechanism; always a small positive whole number.

**Key difference**: order is experimental and describes the whole reaction (or a whole elementary step); molecularity is mechanistic and applies only to a single elementary step — a multi-step reaction's overall order need not match any one step's molecularity.

</details>
