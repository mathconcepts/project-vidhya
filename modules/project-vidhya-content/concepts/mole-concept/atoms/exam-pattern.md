**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: mass-to-mole-to-particle chains.** A question rarely stops at "find the moles" — it usually asks for a mass, a volume at STP, or a particle count that is two conversion steps away. Write out mass $\to$ moles $\to$ (target) as separate lines instead of trying to do it in one jump.

- **MCQ: limiting reagent with masses given, not moles.** Both reactant amounts are given in grams specifically so that comparing them directly (without converting to moles first) produces a plausible-looking wrong option.

- **Trap: molarity vs. molality in the same question set.** A dilution question ("$M_1V_1=M_2V_2$") and a freezing-point question (needs molality) can appear back to back — read which one each question actually names before substituting a value.

- **Trap: empirical formula given as the final answer when molecular formula was asked.** Always check whether the molecular mass was given; if it was, the empirical formula alone is an incomplete answer.

- **Time budget:** a straightforward mole-mass-particle NAT should take under 45 seconds — one division, one multiplication, done. A limiting-reagent MCQ needs the extra step of comparing both reactants, so budget close to 90 seconds for that shape.
