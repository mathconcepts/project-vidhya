A solution contains $0.05\ \text{mol}$ of glucose in $1\ \text{L}$ of solution, at $300\ \text{K}$. Osmotic pressure: $\Pi = iCRT$, with $i=1$ (glucose does not ionise), $C=0.05\ \text{mol/L}$, $R=0.0821\ \text{L atm/mol K}$, $T=300\ \text{K}$.

$\Pi = 1 \times 0.05 \times 0.0821 \times 300 = 1.2315\ \text{atm}$.

Now dissolve the same $0.05\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{NaCl}$ instead, in the same $1\ \text{L}$, at the same $300\ \text{K}$. $\text{NaCl}$ gives $i=2$: $\Pi = 2 \times 0.05 \times 0.0821 \times 300 = 2.463\ \text{atm}$.

Check: doubling only the particle count (via $i$), with every other number identical, exactly doubled the osmotic pressure — confirming $\Pi$ depends on particle count, not on which solute was used.
