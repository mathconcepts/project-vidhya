Same arrow, two different addresses. The vector doesn't move — only the coordinates describing it change when you switch which basis you're reading it against. Take $x$ with coordinates $(2,1)$ in the basis $\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}$: read those same two numbers against the *standard* basis instead and you get $(3,1)$ — a different-looking pair of numbers for the exact same point in the plane. A change-of-basis matrix is nothing more than the dictionary that translates one address into the other, with no rotation or stretching of the vector itself involved.
