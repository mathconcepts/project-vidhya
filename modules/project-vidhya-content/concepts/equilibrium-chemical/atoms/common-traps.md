- **Forgetting the balanced equation's coefficients as exponents in $K_c$**: for $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2$, writing $K_c = [\text{NO}_2]/[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]$ (exponent $1$) instead of $[\text{NO}_2]^2/[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]$ (exponent $2$, matching $\text{NO}_2$'s coefficient) — an equation ignored is an exponent silently dropped.

- **Believing a catalyst shifts the equilibrium position**: a catalyst speeds up *reaching* equilibrium equally in both directions, but never changes the equilibrium concentrations or the value of $K_c$ itself.

- **Treating Le Chatelier's Principle as an explanation rather than a prediction rule**: it correctly predicts *which way* a stress shifts an equilibrium, but does not itself explain *why*, at the level of forward and reverse reaction rates — assuming it "proves" a mechanism overstates what the rule actually says.

- **Confusing $Q$ with $K$**: $K$ is the fixed value at equilibrium; $Q$ is the same expression computed at *any* moment, including well before equilibrium is reached. Reporting a mid-reaction $Q$ value as though it were $K$ (or vice versa) is a genuinely easy mix-up under time pressure.

- **Assuming temperature changes behave like concentration or pressure changes**: concentration, pressure, and volume changes shift the equilibrium *position* without changing $K$'s value; a temperature change is the one stress that changes $K$ itself, not just $Q$ relative to an unchanged $K$.
