**Cross-concept check: aldehydes-ketones-acids → amines-and-diazonium.**

**Question 1 (a carboxylic-acid derivative turns into an amine):** Acetamide, $\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2$ (an amide — one of the derivatives of carboxylic acids covered in this concept), is treated with $\text{Br}_2$ and NaOH. What is the product, and how many carbons does it have compared to acetamide?

*Answer:* This is the Hofmann bromamide degradation. It converts the amide directly into a primary amine with ONE FEWER carbon than the amide, releasing the carbonyl carbon as carbonate. Acetamide has two carbons; the product, methylamine ($\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$), has only one. The carbonyl group this concept studies — the electrophilic $\text{C=O}$ carbon in the amide — is exactly the carbon that leaves as carbonate here.

**Question 2 (a distinguishing test that only fires for one nitrogen count):** A primary amine, when warmed with chloroform and alcoholic KOH, produces an isocyanide with a distinctive foul smell (the carbylamine test). Would this concept's Tollens' reagent give the same kind of positive result for a primary amine?

*Answer:* No. Tollens' reagent tests for an easily oxidised aldehyde hydrogen — a primary amine has no carbonyl group and nothing for $\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2^+$ to oxidise, so it gives no silver mirror at all. The carbylamine test is a completely separate reagent built specifically to detect the $\text{-NH}_2$ group on a primary amine; the two tests target different functional groups and are never interchangeable.

**Why this drill exists:** the amide functional group this concept studies is one short step away from a primary amine — remove the carbonyl carbon via Hofmann degradation, and a carboxylic-acid derivative becomes exactly the amine the next concept builds on.
