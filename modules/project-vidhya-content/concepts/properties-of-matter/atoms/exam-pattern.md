**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: Young's modulus or stress/strain from four given numbers.** $F$, $A$, $L_0$, $\Delta L$ are almost always all given directly — compute $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ separately first, then divide, rather than trying to combine everything in one step.

- **MCQ: Bernoulli's equation with an unstated "horizontal pipe" assumption.** If a diagram shows both points at the same height, the $\rho gh$ terms cancel and are safe to drop — but check the diagram, since a tilted or vertical pipe keeps that term and skipping it there is a common wrong-answer trap.

- **Trap: soap bubble vs liquid drop excess pressure.** A bubble's two surfaces double the plain-drop formula ($4T/r$ instead of $2T/r$) — a distractor built for whichever formula the question is *not* actually describing.

- **Trap: capillary rise sign for a non-wetting liquid.** Mercury depresses rather than rises in a narrow tube; a question describing mercury and asking for "the rise" is testing whether the negative sign (from $\cos\theta<0$) is caught.

- **Time budget:** an elasticity or Pascal's-law NAT with all values given directly should take under 40 seconds — one substitution into the relevant formula, no derivation needed mid-exam.
