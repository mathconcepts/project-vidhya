**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: time of flight, height, or range from a launch angle and speed.** Almost always solvable directly from $T=2u_y/g$, $H=u_y^2/2g$, and $R=u_xT$ once $u_x,u_y$ are resolved — no need to derive the trajectory equation from scratch unless a specific point on the path is asked for.

- **MCQ: trajectory equation, or "at what height is the velocity purely horizontal".** The velocity is purely horizontal exactly at the highest point (where $v_y=0$), which is also where the trajectory's slope is zero — the same fact stated two different ways.

- **Trap: two launch angles give the same range.** $R=u^2\sin2\theta/g$ is the same for $\theta$ and $90°-\theta$ (since $\sin2\theta=\sin(180°-2\theta)$) — a question giving one angle and asking for "the other angle with the same range" wants $90°-\theta$, not a recomputation from scratch.

- **Trap: uniform circular motion questions test the direction of $a_c$, not just its magnitude.** A question describing a string breaking, or asking which way a car would skid, is testing whether the centripetal acceleration points inward (toward the centre) — losing that force removes the inward pull, so the object flies off *tangent* to the circle, not radially outward.

- **Time budget:** a standard projectile NAT (time of flight / height / range) should take under $60$ seconds once $u_x,u_y$ are resolved; resist the urge to re-derive the trajectory equation when the direct formulas already answer the question.
