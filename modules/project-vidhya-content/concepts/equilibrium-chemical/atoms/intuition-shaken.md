For $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2(g)$, suppose at equilibrium $[\text{NO}_2]=0.4$ M and $[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]=0.2$ M.

Write $K_c$'s expression: $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NO}_2]^2}{[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]}$. The exponent $2$ matches $\text{NO}_2$'s coefficient in the balanced equation — never left out.

Substitute: $K_c = \dfrac{(0.4)^2}{0.2} = \dfrac{0.16}{0.2} = 0.8$.

Now suppose more $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4$ is added, disturbing this balance. By Le Chatelier's Principle, adding a reactant pushes the reaction forward, making more $\text{NO}_2$, until $Q$ rises back to meet $K_c=0.8$ again — the *value* of $K_c$ itself never changes, only the concentrations shift until $Q$ catches up to it.
