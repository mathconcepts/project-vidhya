**"D needs C, C doesn't need D."** Differentiable always drags continuous along with it; continuous walks in alone, no derivative required. One arrow, not two.

**Micro-example:** $f(x)=|x|$ at $x=0$ — C holds (no gap, $f(0)=0$), D fails (left slope $-1$, right slope $+1$, no match). C without D, exactly as the mnemonic warns.

**Sanity-check reflex:** whenever a piecewise problem asks for constants "so that $f$ is differentiable," write down **two** equations — continuity first, matching one-sided derivatives second — before solving either. One equation alone under-determines the answer.
