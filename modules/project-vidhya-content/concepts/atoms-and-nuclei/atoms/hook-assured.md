The exam phrase to watch for is "large-angle scattering" or "back-scattering" — both point straight at Rutherford's experiment and the nuclear model, not at Bohr's orbits (a separate, later idea about where electrons sit once the nucleus already exists).

A shortcut that fails under pressure: reading "most alpha particles pass through undeflected" as the whole refutation of the plum-pudding model, while missing the specific fact that actually breaks it. It is not the *average* deflection that matters — it is the mere *existence* of any large-angle bounce at all. Charge smeared evenly through an atom's whole volume cannot produce even one $90°+$ deflection, however gently most particles happen to bend on average.
