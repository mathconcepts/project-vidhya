"A bigger piston area means a bigger output force, so bigger is always better" is the half-truth a hydraulic lift invites. Pascal's law transmits *pressure* undiminished, not force — the output force still scales with the output piston's own area, $F_2=P\times A_2$, and the trade-off is real: the small piston must travel a hundred times farther than the large one moves, since the fluid volume displaced on both sides must match.

**Properties of matter** bundles four separate rules under one heading — Pascal's law and buoyancy for still fluids, Bernoulli's principle for moving fluids, Hooke's law and Young's modulus for stretching solids, and surface tension for a liquid's surface — and the first real step is naming which of the four a question is even about.
