**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: "by what factor does X change" questions.** Given a change in temperature (or molar mass, or number of moles), find the factor by which rms speed, pressure, or internal energy changes. These never need the full formula solved twice — compute the ratio of the changed quantity directly, using the proportionality ($v_{rms}\propto\sqrt{T/M}$, $P\propto T$ at fixed $V,n$, $U\propto T$ for a fixed gas).

- **MCQ: monatomic vs diatomic internal energy.** The moment a question names a specific gas, check whether it is monatomic (helium, argon, neon) or diatomic ($N_2$, $O_2$, $H_2$) before writing $U=\frac{3}{2}nRT$ or $U=\frac{5}{2}nRT$ — using the wrong one is the single most common wrong-option trap in this concept.

- **Trap: unit mismatch in $M$.** A question may give molar mass in grams per mole to test whether the kilogram conversion is remembered. Convert before substituting, every time, without exception.

- **Trap: mixing up rms speed with average speed.** A distractor computed with the wrong one of the two formulas gives a numerically different but still "reasonable-looking" answer.

- **Time budget:** a "factor of change" NAT should take under 30 seconds — write the proportionality, plug in the ratio, done. There is no need to compute either absolute value.
