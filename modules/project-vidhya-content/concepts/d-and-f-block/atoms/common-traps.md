- **Trusting the Aufbau order blindly for chromium and copper**: the memorised filling order predicts $Cr$: $[Ar]3d^44s^2$ and $Cu$: $[Ar]3d^94s^2$ — and this is exactly the wrong answer the exam is testing. The real configurations are $Cr$: $[Ar]3d^54s^1$ and $Cu$: $[Ar]3d^{10}4s^1$, because a half-filled or fully filled $d$ subshell paired with a half-filled $4s$ is more stable than the "expected" arrangement. Any question naming chromium or copper's electron configuration is testing this specific exception, not the general filling rule.

- **Removing $3d$ electrons before $4s$ electrons when forming a cation**: transition metals always lose their $4s$ electrons first, regardless of which subshell was filled last while building up the atom. $Fe$ ($[Ar]3d^64s^2$) loses both $4s$ electrons to become $Fe^{2+}$ ($[Ar]3d^6$), not a $3d$ electron.

- **Treating "$d^0$/$d^{10}$ means colourless" as absolute**: $MnO_4^-$ ($Mn^{7+}$, $d^0$) is intensely purple and $CrO_4^{2-}$ ($Cr^{6+}$, $d^0$) is yellow, because charge transfer from the oxide ligand can produce colour even with no $d$ electrons at all — a completely different mechanism from the $d$-$d$ transitions that colour most transition-metal ions.

- **Assuming lanthanoid radii increase across the series, like an ordinary period**: they shrink steadily instead (lanthanoid contraction), because the poorly shielding $4f$ electrons let effective nuclear charge climb almost unopposed across the fourteen elements.

- **Computing magnetic moment from the metal ion alone, ignoring the ligand**: $\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}$ needs the right $n$, and $n$ depends on whether the complex is high-spin or low-spin — a fact set by the ligand's field strength, not by the metal identity by itself. The same ion, $Fe^{2+}$, gives $n=4$ with weak-field ligands and $n=0$ with strong-field ones like $CN^-$.
