**"Full house, half house — both hard to break into."** A completely filled subshell ($2s^2$) is a full house; a half-filled subshell ($2p^3$, one electron in each of three rooms) is a half house. Both are unusually settled, so the *very next* element — the one that has to add a new lone electron on a higher floor, or force two electrons to share one room — always has a *lower* ionisation enthalpy than the "steady climb" rule predicts.

**Where the two dips sit, in one line each.** Dip 1: $2s^2 \to 2s^22p^1$, i.e. Group 2 to Group 13 (Be $\to$ B, Mg $\to$ Al). Dip 2: $2p^3 \to 2p^4$, i.e. Group 15 to Group 16 (N $\to$ O, P $\to$ S). Same two group-boundaries, in every period that has a full $p$-block.

**For "which is bigger" ion-size questions, remember: "more protons pulling on the same crowd shrinks the crowd."** $Na^+$, $Mg^{2+}$, $Al^{3+}$ all have the exact same 10 electrons — the one with the most protons pulling on that crowd ($Al^{3+}$) is the smallest, not the same size as the rest.
