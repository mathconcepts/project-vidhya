**Problem:** Using Bohr's model, find the energy (in eV) of the photon emitted when an electron in a hydrogen atom falls from $n=2$ to $n=1$.

---

**Step 1 — Count what is being compared: two fixed energy levels, not a continuous range.** $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV applies at each level separately.

---

**Step 2 — Compute $E_1$ (the level the electron lands on).** $E_1 = -\dfrac{13.6}{1^2} = -13.6$ eV.

---

**Step 3 — Compute $E_2$ (the level the electron starts from).** $E_2 = -\dfrac{13.6}{2^2} = -\dfrac{13.6}{4} = -3.4$ eV.

---

**Step 4 — Energy released is the *starting* level minus the *landing* level.** $E_2 - E_1 = -3.4 - (-13.6) = 10.2$ eV.

$$\boxed{10.2 \text{ eV}}$$

---

**Why the order matters.** The electron falls to a *lower* (more negative) energy, releasing the difference as a photon. Computing $E_1 - E_2$ instead gives $-10.2$ eV — the correct magnitude, but the wrong sign convention for "energy released", a genuinely easy slip when both levels are negative numbers.
