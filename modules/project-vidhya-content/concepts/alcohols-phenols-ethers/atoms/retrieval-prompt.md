**Question:** In one or two sentences, explain why phenol is far more acidic than an ordinary alcohol, naming the specific structural feature responsible.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

When phenol loses its $-\text{OH}$ proton, the resulting phenoxide ion spreads its negative charge by resonance onto the ring's ortho and para carbons, not just the oxygen — lowering the ion's energy and making it easy to form. An ordinary alcohol's alkoxide ion has no ring to spread into, so its charge stays concentrated on one oxygen, keeping it high in energy and hard to form.

</details>
