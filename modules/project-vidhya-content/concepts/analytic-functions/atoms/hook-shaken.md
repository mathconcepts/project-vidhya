Take $f(z) = z^2$. Its derivative $f'(z) = 2z$ exists at every point — no bad points at all. That single fact, complex-differentiable on a whole disc, already forces something huge: $f$ turns out differentiable infinitely many times, and its values on that tiny disc fix its values across its whole connected domain. A real function differentiable once at a point promises none of that.
