An **orbital** is a probability region, not a path. It does not mean "the electron travels along this shape" — it means "if you looked right now, this is the region where the electron is most likely to be found." There is no fixed trajectory to draw at all, unlike a planet's orbit.

Four numbers, the **quantum numbers**, pin down which orbital an electron is in and its spin: $n$ (principal — which shell, sets the overall energy and size, values $1,2,3,\dots$), $l$ (azimuthal — which subshell within that shell, sets the orbital's shape, values $0$ to $n-1$, labelled $s,p,d,f$), $m_l$ (magnetic — which specific orientation of that shape, values $-l$ to $+l$), and $m_s$ (spin — the electron's own spin direction, $+\tfrac{1}{2}$ or $-\tfrac{1}{2}$).

Three rules then decide how electrons actually fill these orbitals in a real atom: **Aufbau** (fill the lowest-energy orbitals first), **Pauli exclusion** (no two electrons in one atom can share all four quantum numbers — at most 2 per orbital, with opposite spin), and **Hund's rule** (within orbitals of equal energy, fill each one singly before pairing any of them up).
