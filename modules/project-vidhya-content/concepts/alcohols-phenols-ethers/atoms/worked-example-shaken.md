**Problem:** tert-butyl methyl ether, $\text{CH}_3\text{-O-C(CH}_3)_3$, heated with excess $\text{HI}$. Which carbon-oxygen bond breaks?

---

**Step 1 — Name both candidates.** Methyl carbon: could be attacked directly by iodide ($S_N2$). Tertiary carbon: could ionise on its own first ($S_N1$).

---

**Step 2 — A first guess.** "Smaller carbon, easier to attack" points at the methyl carbon and $S_N2$.

---

**Step 3 — Test the tertiary route instead.** If the tertiary carbon ionises, it forms a tertiary carbocation: three neighbouring methyl groups, nine $\text{C-H}$ bonds available to donate by hyperconjugation. Very stable.

---

**Step 4 — Test the methyl route for comparison.** If the methyl carbon ionised instead, it would form a methyl cation: zero neighbouring carbons, zero donating $\text{C-H}$ bonds. Much too high in energy to appear at any real rate.

---

**Step 5 — Decide and confirm.** The tertiary route wins, since its carbocation is far easier to form. $\boxed{\text{tert-butyl iodide and methanol form, via } S_N1 \text{ at the tertiary carbon}}$. Confirm: this is the opposite pairing from the naive "attack the small carbon" guess.
