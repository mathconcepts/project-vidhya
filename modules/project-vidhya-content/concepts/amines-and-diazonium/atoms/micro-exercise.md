Which ring will undergo diazonium coupling with benzenediazonium chloride?

A) Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$)
B) Nitrobenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$)
C) Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$)
D) Chlorobenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl}$)

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) Phenol.**

The $\text{-OH}$ group is a strong electron-donor by resonance, pushing extra electron density into the ring and making it electron-rich enough for the weak diazonium electrophile to attack — coupling occurs mainly at the position para to $\text{-OH}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) Plain benzene has no activating group at all — no extra electron density for the diazonium ion to exploit.
- B) $\text{-NO}_2$ is a strong electron-WITHDRAWING group; it pulls electron density out of the ring, making it even less reactive toward the diazonium electrophile than plain benzene.
- D) Chlorine is only weakly activating overall (its resonance donation is outweighed by its inductive withdrawal) and is nowhere near strong enough to support coupling the way $\text{-OH}$ or $\text{-NH}_2$ does.

</details>
