Which of these undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction when treated with concentrated NaOH?

A) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}$ (propanal)
B) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO}$ (benzaldehyde)
C) $\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3$ (acetone)
D) $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ (acetaldehyde)

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO}$.**

Benzaldehyde's carbonyl carbon is bonded to the benzene ring on one side and a hydrogen on the other — the ring carbon has no hydrogen for the base to remove, so there is no alpha-hydrogen anywhere on this molecule. With the enolate route closed, concentrated base attacks the carbonyl carbon directly, and the molecule disproportionates: Cannizzaro.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) Propanal's $\text{CH}_2$ group, right next to the carbonyl carbon, carries two hydrogens — alpha-hydrogens are present, so base forms an enolate and this gives an aldol product instead.
- C) Acetone's two $\text{CH}_3$ groups each sit directly on the carbonyl carbon and each carries three hydrogens — plenty of alpha-hydrogens. It is not even a candidate for Cannizzaro.
- D) Acetaldehyde's $\text{CH}_3$ group carries three alpha-hydrogens — worked through in full in this concept's worked example, where it gives an aldol product, not Cannizzaro.

</details>
