- **Counting an $sp^3$ carbon's electrons as part of the pi system**: only atoms that each carry a p-orbital, lying flat in the ring's plane, can contribute to the conjugated count. A carbon still bonded to four separate groups (no p-orbital available) breaks the conjugation at that point and takes the whole ring out of the aromaticity question.

- **Forgetting planarity can be escaped on purpose**: a ring whose flat, planar form would be antiaromatic (like cyclooctatetraene) can pucker into a non-planar "tub" shape instead, breaking the continuous overlap of p-orbitals. Once it is not planar, Huckel's rule no longer applies to it at all — it is simply an ordinary alkene system.

- **Applying the peroxide (anti-Markovnikov) exception to the wrong acid**: this reversal is observed only for $\text{HBr}$. $\text{HCl}$ and $\text{HI}$ still add by the ordinary Markovnikov pathway even with peroxides present.

- **Mixing up ozonolysis products**: a $\text{=CH}_2$ end of a double bond becomes formaldehyde (a one-carbon aldehyde) on cleavage; a $\text{=CR}_2$ end (two other groups attached) becomes a ketone. Assuming every carbon gives the same type of carbonyl product misreads the structure.

- **Assuming any cyclic, conjugated ring is automatically aromatic**: cyclobutadiene is cyclic and conjugated and is *not* aromatic — it is antiaromatic, because its electron count is $4n$, not $4n+2$. The count, not the shape, is the deciding test.
