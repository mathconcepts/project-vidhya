**This concept covers four separate areas**, each with its own governing rule: elasticity (how solids stretch and recover), fluids at rest and in motion (pressure, buoyancy, and flow), surface tension and capillarity (how a liquid's surface behaves), and heat transfer (how thermal energy moves through materials).

**Elasticity.** Stress $\sigma=F/A$; strain $\epsilon=\Delta L/L_0$ (dimensionless); Hooke's law $\sigma=Y\epsilon$ within the elastic limit, where $Y$ (Young's modulus) is a material constant.

**Fluids at rest.** Pressure $P=F/A$. **Pascal's law**: pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished to every point. **Archimedes' principle** (buoyancy): the upward buoyant force on a submerged object equals the weight of fluid it displaces, $F_B=\rho_{fluid}\,V_{displaced}\,g$.

**Fluids in motion.** **Equation of continuity**: $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$ for an incompressible fluid in a pipe of varying cross-section. **Bernoulli's principle**: $P+\tfrac12\rho v^2+\rho gh = \text{constant}$ along a streamline, for steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow. **Stokes' law**: viscous drag force on a small sphere, $F=6\pi\eta rv$, where $\eta$ is the fluid's viscosity.

**Surface tension and capillarity.** Surface tension $T$ = force per unit length along a liquid surface. Excess pressure inside a drop: $\Delta P=2T/r$. Inside a soap bubble (two surfaces): $\Delta P=4T/r$. Capillary rise: $h=\dfrac{2T\cos\theta}{\rho gr}$, where $\theta$ is the contact angle and $r$ is the tube's radius.

**Heat transfer.** Conduction (rate of heat flow through a material): $\dfrac{dQ}{dt}=\dfrac{kA\,\Delta T}{L}$, where $k$ is thermal conductivity, $A$ is cross-sectional area, and $L$ is the material's thickness.
