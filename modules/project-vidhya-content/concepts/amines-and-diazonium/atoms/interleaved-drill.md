**Cross-concept check: amines-and-diazonium → biomolecules.**

**Question 1 (an amine group and an acid group on the same molecule):** An amino acid carries both a $\text{-NH}_2$ group and a $\text{-COOH}$ group on the same carbon. Which of these two groups is protonated, and which is deprotonated, at the pH found inside the human body?

*Answer:* The $\text{-NH}_2$ group is basic and picks up a proton, becoming $\text{-NH}_3^+$, the same protonation this concept covers for every amine. The $\text{-COOH}$ group is acidic and loses its proton, becoming $\text{-COO}^-$. The amino acid exists as a **zwitterion** — carrying both a positive and a negative charge on the same molecule at once, overall electrically neutral.

**Question 2 (peptide bonds are a condensation, not an addition):** Two amino acid molecules join through a peptide bond, releasing one molecule of water. Which functional group from this concept supplies the nitrogen in that new bond?

*Answer:* The $\text{-NH}_2$ (amine) group of one amino acid attacks the $\text{-COOH}$ (carboxylic acid) carbon of the other, and a molecule of water is lost as the new $\text{-CO-NH-}$ (peptide) bond forms — the same amine group whose basicity and reactions this concept has been building up.

**Why this drill exists:** amino acids are the one place an amine group and an acid group sit on the very same molecule — the protonation behaviour this concept teaches for amines in isolation is exactly what decides an amino acid's charge state in the next concept.
