**Acid dissociation constant**: for a weak acid $\text{HA} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{A}^-$, $K_a = \dfrac{[\text{H}^+][\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$ (concentrations at equilibrium).

**Base dissociation constant**: for a weak base $\text{BOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{B}^+ + \text{OH}^-$, $K_b = \dfrac{[\text{B}^+][\text{OH}^-]}{[\text{BOH}]}$.

**Ionic product of water**: $K_w = [\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-]$. At $25^\circ\text{C}$, $K_w = 1\times10^{-14}$ — this value changes at other temperatures, since water splitting into $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{OH}^-$ absorbs heat.

**pH and pOH**: $\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]$, $\text{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\text{OH}^-]$. At $25^\circ\text{C}$: $\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$.

**pKa, pKb, and a conjugate pair**: $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10}K_a$, $\text{p}K_b = -\log_{10}K_b$. For an acid and its own conjugate base, $\text{p}K_a + \text{p}K_b = 14$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$.

**Common-ion effect**: adding a salt that supplies an ion already present in a weak acid or base's equilibrium (e.g. $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$ into a $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ equilibrium) shifts that equilibrium back towards the un-ionised form, lowering its degree of ionisation.

**Henderson–Hasselbalch equation** (for an acidic buffer): $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}\right)$.

**Salt hydrolysis**: a salt formed from a weak acid and a strong base (like $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$) reacts partly with water to regenerate the weak acid, leaving the solution basic; a salt of a strong acid and a weak base leaves the solution acidic. Only a salt of a strong acid AND a strong base gives a neutral, $\text{pH}=7$ solution.

**Solubility product**: for a sparingly soluble salt $\text{A}_x\text{B}_y \rightleftharpoons x\text{A}^{y+} + y\text{B}^{x-}$, $K_{sp} = [\text{A}^{y+}]^x[\text{B}^{x-}]^y$ at equilibrium — if the ion product exceeds $K_{sp}$, the salt precipitates.
