Compute three terms of $a_n=\dfrac{2n+1}{n}$, in order. $n=1$: $\dfrac{3}{1}=3$. $n=2$: $\dfrac{5}{2}=2.5$. $n=3$: $\dfrac{7}{3}\approx 2.33$. Each term sits closer to $2$ than the last one did. Check whether any term can equal $2$ exactly: $\dfrac{2n+1}{n}=2$ needs $1/n=0$, which no finite $n$ gives. So the terms approach $2$ without ever touching it — that gap-that-never-closes-to-zero-but-shrinks-forever is what a limit means. Not every sequence behaves this way: some alternate forever, some grow without bound.
