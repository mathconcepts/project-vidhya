**Cross-concept check: equilibrium-chemical → equilibrium-ionic.**

**Question 1 (an acid dissociation is just a specific $K_c$):** Acetic acid dissociates as $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$. Write its equilibrium constant, $K_a$, using the same general form as $K_c$ for any reaction.

*Answer:* $K_a = \dfrac{[\text{H}^+][\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-]}{[\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}]}$ — exactly the general $K_c = \dfrac{\text{products}}{\text{reactants}}$ pattern, with a new name ($K_a$) attached only because the reaction happens to be an acid dissociation. No new rule is needed; it is the same law of mass action applied to this specific equilibrium.

**Question 2 (the $Q$-vs-$K$ comparison carries over unchanged):** If, at a given instant, $[\text{H}^+][\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-]/[\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}]$ works out smaller than the known $K_a$, which direction does the dissociation proceed?

*Answer:* Forward — more of the acid dissociates into $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$, exactly the same $Q<K$-means-forward-shift logic from general chemical equilibrium, just with $K_a$ standing in for $K_c$.

**Why this drill exists:** ionic equilibrium is not a separate set of rules to memorise — $K_a$, $K_b$, and solubility product are all just the general equilibrium constant expression applied to specific kinds of reactions (acid dissociation, base dissociation, dissolution).
