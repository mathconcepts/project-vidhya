Picture your own eye level as a perfectly horizontal line. Tilt your head up to look at something above it, and the angle between that horizontal and your line of sight is the angle of elevation. Tilt down instead, and the same kind of angle is called the angle of depression. One idea, two names, depending only on which way you look.

Here is the fact JEE leans on constantly: standing on a tower looking DOWN at a ground point, your angle of depression exactly equals the angle of elevation that same ground point would measure looking UP at you. Your horizontal and the ground's horizontal are parallel, and one line of sight cuts across both at the same slant — so the two angles are automatically equal, by nothing more than parallel lines and a transversal.

Tangent is the natural ratio here because these triangles almost always give you, or ask for, the two LEGS of a right triangle — never the slanted hypotenuse directly — and tangent is the one ratio built entirely out of the two legs.
