**Cross-concept check: electromagnetic-waves → ray-optics.**

**Question 1 (visible light is one thin band of a much bigger spectrum):** Red light has a wavelength of about $700\text{ nm}$ and violet light about $400\text{ nm}$. Both are electromagnetic waves. Which travels faster in vacuum?

*Answer:* Neither — both travel at $c \approx 3\times10^8\text{ m/s}$ in vacuum. Speed in vacuum does not depend on wavelength or frequency; every member of the electromagnetic spectrum shares the same vacuum speed.

**Question 2 (the same wavelength difference behaves very differently inside glass):** Inside a glass prism, does red light or violet light bend *more* on refraction, and what does this depend on?

*Answer:* Violet light bends more. A medium's refractive index is slightly different for different wavelengths (higher for shorter wavelengths like violet), a property called **dispersion** — exactly why a prism spreads white light into a spectrum, even though every colour arrived travelling at the identical speed before entering the glass.

**Why this drill exists:** it is easy to treat "the speed of light" as one fixed fact and stop there. The far more exam-relevant fact is that this shared vacuum speed is exactly what makes it surprising, and worth testing, that different wavelengths slow down by *different* amounts inside a real material — the whole basis of the next concept's dispersion questions.
