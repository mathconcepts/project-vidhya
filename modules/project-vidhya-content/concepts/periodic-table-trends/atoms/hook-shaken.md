Here are the real numbers for Period 2, in kilojoules per mole: lithium 520, beryllium 899, boron 801, carbon 1086, nitrogen 1402, oxygen 1314, fluorine 1681, neon 2081.

Read left to right: 520 to 899 is a rise, 899 to 801 is a fall. So the climb is not unbroken — there are exactly two dips in this row: beryllium to boron, and nitrogen to oxygen.

Both dips have a real cause, not random noise. Beryllium fills its 2s subshell completely (extra stable); boron puts its newest electron in 2p, easier to pull off. Nitrogen places three 2p electrons one per orbital (extra stable, called half-filled stability); oxygen forces a fourth electron to pair up in one orbital, and that pairing repulsion makes it easier to remove.
