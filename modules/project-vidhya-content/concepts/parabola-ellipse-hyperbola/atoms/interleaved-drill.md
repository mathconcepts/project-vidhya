**Cross-concept check: Conic Sections → Straight Lines.**

A line $y=mx+c$ is tangent to the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ exactly when $c^2=a^2m^2+b^2$ — the tangency condition every "form of a line" question secretly leans on the moment a conic gets involved.

**Question 1 (Straight Lines):** For the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1$ and slope $m=1$, what value of $c$ makes $y=x+c$ tangent to it?

*Answer:* $c^2=a^2m^2+b^2=25(1)+9=34$, so $c=\pm\sqrt{34}$.

**Question 2 (Conic Sections):** Confirm the tangency directly by substituting $y=x+\sqrt{34}$ into the ellipse and checking the resulting quadratic in $x$ has a repeated root.

*Answer:* Substituting gives a quadratic in $x$ whose discriminant works out to exactly $0$ (verified) — a repeated root means the line meets the ellipse at only one point, confirming tangency independently of the formula used in Question 1.

**Why this drill exists:** the discriminant-equals-zero idea behind "line tangent to a circle" from straight lines is the SAME test behind "line tangent to a conic" here — a formula like $c^2=a^2m^2+b^2$ is just that discriminant condition worked out once and packaged for reuse, not a separate fact to memorise per curve.
