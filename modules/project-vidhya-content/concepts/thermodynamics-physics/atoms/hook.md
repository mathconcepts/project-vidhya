A bicycle pump gets noticeably warm after a few fast strokes, even though nobody applied a flame to it. Squeeze the same trapped air slowly instead, over a long stretch of time, and it barely warms at all. Same gas, same final compression — so why does one method heat it up and the other does not?

The system here is the air trapped inside the pump's barrel; the barrel's inner wall is the boundary. Pushing the piston in does **work** on that trapped air. If the push happens fast enough that no heat has time to escape through the barrel wall, every joule of that work goes straight into raising the air's internal energy — and temperature rises sharply. Push slowly enough, and heat leaks out through the wall as fast as work goes in, so temperature barely moves. Same compression, two completely different outcomes, because heat flow is the variable that changed, not the mechanics of the squeeze.
