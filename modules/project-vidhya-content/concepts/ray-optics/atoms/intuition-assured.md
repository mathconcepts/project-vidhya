Reading "$v$ negative means virtual" as a universal rule is where this goes wrong — that reading is only valid once you already know whether you are looking at a mirror or a lens, because the two conventions assign the *far side* to opposite signs.

For a lens, the far side (where light continues after bending) is positive, so $v<0$ means virtual, same side as the object — exactly the $u=-10\text{ cm}$ case above. For a mirror, light reflects back the *same* way it came, so the far side (behind the mirror, where light never actually goes) is the *positive* side, and $v<0$ there means a **real** image, in front of the mirror. The bare statement "negative $v$ means virtual" is a lens-only fact smuggled in as if it were universal; for a mirror the correct reading is the reverse. Confirm which formula is in play before reading any sign at all.
