A hydraulic lift has a small piston of area $0.005\text{ m}^2$ and a large piston of area $0.5\text{ m}^2$.

Push down on the small piston with $100\text{ N}$.

Pressure created: $P = F/A = 100/0.005 = 20{,}000\text{ Pa}$.

**Pascal's law**: this pressure reaches every point of the confined fluid unchanged, including the large piston.

Force on the large piston: $F_2 = P \times A_2 = 20{,}000 \times 0.5 = 10{,}000\text{ N}$.

Taking $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$, that force can support a mass of $10{,}000/10 = 1000\text{ kg}$ — a small car, lifted using only $100\text{ N}$ of push. **Properties of matter** is this idea, plus how solids stretch, surfaces pull tight, and heat travels through materials.
