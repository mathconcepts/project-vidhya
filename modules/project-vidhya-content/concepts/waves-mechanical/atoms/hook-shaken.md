Flick a rope under tension $100$ N, with mass per unit length $\mu=0.01$ kg/m. The wave speed here is $v=\sqrt{T/\mu}=\sqrt{100/0.01}=100$ m/s — this is how fast the hump-shape itself travels down the rope.

Mark one point on the rope with a dot, $1$ m from your hand. At $100$ m/s, the hump reaches the dot in $1/100=0.01$ s. Now watch the dot itself as the hump passes through it: it moves up, then back down, ending up exactly where it started. It never travels toward the wall at all.

Two completely different speeds are hiding in this one flick: the wave's own speed ($100$ m/s, how fast the pattern moves) and the dot's own up-and-down speed (much smaller, and constantly changing) — never the same number.
