**Paired concept: Heights and Distances.**

A heights-and-distances problem hands you a height and a horizontal distance and asks for the ANGLE itself, not just a ratio — and finding an angle from $\tan\theta=h/d$ means applying $\tan^{-1}$, landing you straight back in this concept's own principal-value branch.

**Drill**: A pole $10$ m tall casts a shadow $10\sqrt3$ m long. Write the angle of elevation of the sun as $\theta=\tan^{-1}(h/d)$ with the correct numbers substituted, then state which of $\tan^{-1}$'s principal-range facts guarantees this expression gives a genuine angle between $0°$ and $90°$, not some other equivalent angle outside that window.
