**Cross-concept check: Straight Lines → Circles.**

A line touches (is tangent to) a circle exactly when the perpendicular distance from the circle's centre to the line equals the circle's radius — one number compared to another, nothing more.

**Question 1 (Straight Lines):** What is the perpendicular distance from the origin $(0,0)$ to the line $3x+4y-25=0$?

*Answer:* Distance $=\dfrac{|3(0)+4(0)-25|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}=\dfrac{25}{5}=5$ — the same perpendicular-distance formula used for angle bisectors, just measuring one point against one line instead of comparing two lines.

**Question 2 (Circles):** Is the line $3x+4y-25=0$ tangent to the circle $x^2+y^2=25$?

*Answer:* Yes. The circle has centre $(0,0)$ and radius $\sqrt{25}=5$. Question 1 already found that distance to be exactly $5$ — matching the radius exactly, so the line touches the circle at precisely one point.

**Why this drill exists:** the perpendicular-distance-from-a-point-to-a-line formula built for angle bisectors is the SAME formula that decides tangency to a circle — distance equals radius means tangent, distance less than radius means the line cuts through at two points, and distance more than radius means the line misses the circle entirely.
