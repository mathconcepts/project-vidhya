**Problem:** Nylon-6,6 is made from hexamethylenediamine ($\text{H}_2\text{N-(CH}_2)_6\text{-NH}_2$) and adipic acid ($\text{HOOC-(CH}_2)_4\text{-COOH}$). Is this addition or condensation polymerisation? Is polythene, made from ethylene, formed the same way?

---

**Step 1 — Check the monomers for a double bond to open, versus a reactive end group to react.** Hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid have no carbon-carbon double bond anywhere — instead, each carries a reactive end group ($\text{-NH}_2$ and $\text{-COOH}$) that can react with the other monomer's end group.

---

**Step 2 — Track what happens at each bond formed.** An amine end and an acid end react to form an amide bond, $\text{-CO-NH-}$, releasing one molecule of water every time this reaction happens. This repeats along the whole growing chain — one water molecule lost per amide bond.

$$\boxed{\text{Nylon-6,6 forms by condensation polymerisation.}}$$

**Step 3 — Now check ethylene, the competing mechanism, on the same two questions.** Ethylene, $\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2$, DOES have a carbon-carbon double bond. That double bond simply opens, and one ethylene monomer bonds directly to the next — no water, or anything else, is released at any step.

$$\boxed{\text{Polythene forms by addition polymerisation — a different mechanism from nylon-6,6.}}$$

**Why the two are not formed "the same way".** Both are called polymerisation, and both build a long repeating chain, but the test that sorts them is specific: does a small molecule leave at each join? Nylon-6,6's amide-bond formation always releases water; ethylene's double-bond opening never releases anything. Naming both "polymerisation" without checking this one detail would miss the entire mechanistic difference between them.
