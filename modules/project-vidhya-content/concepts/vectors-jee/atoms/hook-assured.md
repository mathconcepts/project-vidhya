Two vectors, two products, and they are not interchangeable tools for the same job.

$\vec a\cdot\vec b$ is a **scalar** — a plain number, no direction. $\vec a\times\vec b$ is a **vector**, perpendicular to both $\vec a$ and $\vec b$, with a direction fixed by the right-hand rule.

The trap: a question asks for "the component of a force perpendicular to a line," and it is tempting to reach for the cross product because "perpendicular" sounds like its territory. But the cross product gives a vector normal to the *plane* of $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ — not the perpendicular component of one vector *within* that plane. That perpendicular-component question is $\vec a - (\vec a\cdot\hat b)\hat b$, built from the dot product, not the cross product. Know which question you are answering before you pick the tool.
