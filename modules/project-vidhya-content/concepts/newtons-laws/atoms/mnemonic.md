**"One law per stubbornness, one law per push, one law per pair."** First law: an object is stubborn about changing its motion on its own. Second law: a push (net force) is what actually changes it, in proportion to $F=ma$. Third law: every push comes with an equal push back, but on someone else entirely.

**"Draw before you write."** A free-body diagram, drawn first, is what stops forces from being missed or invented — never write $F_{\text{net}}=ma$ before the diagram exists on paper for that specific object.

**"Same string, same pull number, same speed number."** For an inextensible string over an ideal (massless, frictionless) pulley: tension is identical on both sides, and every block tied to that string shares the same magnitude of acceleration — two free facts that turn a two-block problem into two simple one-block equations.
