Given: $\Delta H = -110.5$ kJ/mol for step 1 (carbon burning to carbon monoxide), and $\Delta H = -283.0$ kJ/mol for step 2 (carbon monoxide burning to carbon dioxide).

Add the two steps: $-110.5 + (-283.0) = -393.5$ kJ/mol.

This sum is Hess's Law in action: carbon burning *directly* to carbon dioxide, in one single step, has $\Delta H = -393.5$ kJ/mol — the exact same number as the two-step route added together.

The two paths (direct, or via carbon monoxide) start at the same reactants and end at the same products, so their total $\Delta H$ must match, no matter how many intermediate steps either path takes. Only the starting and ending points matter for $\Delta H$; the route between them does not change the total.
