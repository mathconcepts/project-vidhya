Every operation you can undo has a cost. Push sixteen arrows through $\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and they stay spread across the whole plane, just reshaped — that spread is what makes it invertible. The dashed grey arrows show a different matrix instead, one that crushes all sixteen onto a single line. Once a matrix flattens the plane like that, nothing can spread it back out — that collapse is exactly what $\det(A)=0$ means, and exactly when $A^{-1}$ fails to exist.
