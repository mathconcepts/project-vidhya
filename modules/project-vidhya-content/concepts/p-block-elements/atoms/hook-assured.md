The mechanics of the F2 anomaly are old ground now — the mark-costing trap is assuming "anomalous" means "weakest of all four halogens." It does not.

Counterexample: I-I bond energy is 151 kJ/mol, F-F is 159. Iodine's bond is weaker than fluorine's, even though fluorine is the one everyone calls anomalous. Fluorine's bond is weak relative to chlorine specifically (lone-pair repulsion between two very close, small atoms), but iodine's bond is weak for an unrelated reason: iodine's atoms are large enough that bonding-orbital overlap is poor regardless of lone pairs, which barely matter at that size. Two different structural causes weaken a bond at different ends of the same group, and "smallest atom, weakest bond" is the wrong generalisation to draw from either.
