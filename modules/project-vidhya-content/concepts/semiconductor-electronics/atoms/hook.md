An ordinary crystal of pure silicon barely conducts electricity — very little current flows whichever way you apply the voltage. Now take two differently **doped** pieces of silicon — silicon deliberately mixed with a trace of another element to change how it conducts — one mixed with an element that donates extra electrons, the other with one that leaves extra "holes" (missing electrons, which behave like moving positive charge). Join them at a single junction.

Apply voltage one way across that junction, and current flows easily. Flip the voltage the other way, and almost no current flows at all — the *same* piece of material, conducting freely in one direction and blocking the other. Nothing like this happens in a plain, undoped crystal, and nothing like it happens in an ordinary metal wire either, which conducts equally well both ways.

This is the **p-n junction**: two doped regions joined together, forming a one-way gate for current. It is the working part of every **diode**, and the reason a semiconductor is far more useful than either a perfect insulator or a plain conductor — its behaviour can be *designed*, just by choosing what to dope it with.
