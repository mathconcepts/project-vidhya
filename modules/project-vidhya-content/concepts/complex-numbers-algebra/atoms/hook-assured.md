"Distances multiply, angles add" under multiplication is standard by now. Where marks actually go missing: this rule belongs to **multiplication** alone, never addition. $|z_1+z_2| \ne |z_1|+|z_2|$ in general — take $z_1=1$, $z_2=i$: $|z_1+z_2|=|1+i|=\sqrt2$, while $|z_1|+|z_2|=1+1=2$. Not equal.

The argument behaves the same way: $\arg(z_1+z_2)$ is not $\arg(z_1)+\arg(z_2)$ either — addition on the Argand plane is plain vector addition (parallelogram rule), with no clean angle-adding property at all. Only multiplication gets the elegant "angles add" behaviour, because multiplication is a rotation-and-scale operation; addition is a translation. Reaching for De Moivre's angle-adding intuition on a *sum* of complex numbers misapplies a multiplication-only rule to a completely different operation.
