**Problem:** $500\ \text{mL}$ of a solution is prepared by dissolving $0.2$ mol of $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ and $0.1$ mol of $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ in it. Given $K_a(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}$, find the pH.

---

**Step 1 — Write down what is conserved.** The moles present are $n(\text{HA}) = 0.2$ mol of acid and $n(\text{A}^-) = 0.1$ mol of conjugate base. Both sit in the same $500\ \text{mL}$, so the ratio of moles will equal the ratio of concentrations — the volume can be ignored entirely.

---

**Step 2 — Take $-\log_{10}$ of $K_a$.** $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10}(1.8\times10^{-5})$. Split $1.8\times10^{-5}$ as $\log_{10}(1.8) + \log_{10}(10^{-5}) = 0.26 - 5 = -4.74$. So $\text{p}K_a = -(-4.74) = 4.74$.

---

**Step 3 — Find the mole ratio.** $\dfrac{n(\text{A}^-)}{n(\text{HA})} = \dfrac{0.1}{0.2} = 0.5$.

---

**Step 4 — Take $\log_{10}$ of that ratio.** $\log_{10}(0.5) = \log_{10}(1) - \log_{10}(2) = 0 - 0.30 = -0.30$.

---

**Step 5 — Add it to $\text{p}K_a$.** $\text{pH} = 4.74 + (-0.30) = 4.44$.

$$\boxed{\text{pH} = 4.44}$$

---

**Step 6 — Check.** More acid than salt is present, so the pH should land a little below $\text{p}K_a=4.74$. $4.44$ is indeed below $4.74$ — the size and direction of the shift both make sense.
