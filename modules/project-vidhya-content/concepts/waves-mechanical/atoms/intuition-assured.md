Knowing a pipe's fundamental-frequency formula settles nothing until the pipe's ends are correctly identified first. An open pipe (open at both ends) supports every harmonic, $f_n=nv/2L$ for $n=1,2,3,\dots$. A closed pipe (closed at one end) supports only ODD harmonics, $f_n=(2n-1)v/4L$ — the second harmonic, and every other even one, simply does not exist for it.

Counterexample: a closed pipe of length $L=0.5$ m in air ($v=340$ m/s) has fundamental $f_1=340/(4\times0.5)=170$ Hz. Its next allowed frequency is the *third* harmonic, $3\times170=510$ Hz — never $2\times170=340$ Hz, which an open-pipe assumption would wrongly predict.
