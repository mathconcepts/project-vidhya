Every line and plane in this concept can be written two ways, vector or Cartesian, and JEE will hand you either one without warning.

$\vec r=\vec a+\lambda\vec b$ and $\dfrac{x-x_1}{a}=\dfrac{y-y_1}{b}=\dfrac{z-z_1}{c}$ are the same line. $\vec r\cdot\hat n=d$ and $ax+by+cz=d$ are the same plane. Converting between them is not extra work laid on top of the topic — it **is** the topic, because a question routinely states one line in vector form and one plane in Cartesian form and expects you to combine them without translating either.

The distinction that costs marks: direction **ratios** are any scalar multiple of a direction; direction **cosines** are the one normalized triple, $l^2+m^2+n^2=1$. A formula built for cosines still works with ratios only when the ratios cancel out of it — check which kind a formula wants before plugging numbers in.
