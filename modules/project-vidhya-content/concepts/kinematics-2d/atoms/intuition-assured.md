Resolving a vector and splitting projectile motion into two independent 1D problems reads simple enough on paper. A sharper trap sits in circular motion: constant *speed* is not constant *velocity* — velocity is a vector, and its direction keeps changing every instant, which is itself an acceleration, even though the magnitude $v$ never varies.

A car going around a circular track at a rock-steady $20$ m/s reads zero on its speedometer's rate of change, yet it is *not* moving at constant velocity and *not* free of acceleration — the centripetal acceleration $a_c=v^2/r$ is continuously turning the velocity vector without ever changing its length. Calling this motion "unaccelerated" because the speedometer needle stays put is exactly the reasoning error to avoid: acceleration in mechanics tracks the *vector* velocity, never just its magnitude.
