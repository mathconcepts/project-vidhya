- **Trap**: JEE deliberately chooses $x,y$ values with $xy>1$ in a large share of $\tan^{-1}x+\tan^{-1}y$ questions specifically to catch students who apply the direct formula without checking the condition first.
- **MCQ**: when options are given as multiples of $\pi$ plus a $\tan^{-1}$ term (e.g. $\pi+\tan^{-1}(3)$ vs $\tan^{-1}(3)$), the condition check alone — without finishing the arithmetic — is often enough to eliminate two of the four options immediately.
- **Time**: simplifying an expression like $\sin^{-1}(\sin(7\pi/6))$ should take under $30$ seconds once you locate $7\pi/6$ relative to $\sin^{-1}$'s range $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ — do not evaluate $\sin(7\pi/6)$ numerically first if you can reason about the range directly.
- **NAT**: a question asking for the PRINCIPAL value expects the single answer inside the stated range, never a general solution with $+n\pi$ — reading "principal value" as "general solution" is a frequent, avoidable zero.
- **Trap**: composite expressions like $\tan^{-1}(\tan\theta)$ for $\theta$ outside $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ do NOT simplify to $\theta$ — they simplify to the equivalent angle inside that range, found by subtracting the right multiple of $\pi$.
