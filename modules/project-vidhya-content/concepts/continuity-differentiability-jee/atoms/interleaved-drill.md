**Cross-concept check: implicit differentiation → tangent line.**

The circle $x^2+y^2=25$ passes through the point $(3,4)$.

**Question 1 (continuity and differentiability — implicit differentiation):** Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at $(3,4)$.

*Answer:* Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$: $2x+2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{x}{y}$. At $(3,4)$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac34$.

**Question 2 (applications of derivatives — tangent line):** Using that slope, find the equation of the tangent to the circle at $(3,4)$.

*Answer:* $y-4=-\dfrac34(x-3)$, which clears to $3x+4y=25$.

**Why this drill exists:** finding a tangent line is not a separate skill from implicit differentiation — it is implicit differentiation's own output, fed straight into the point-slope formula. Students who can do each step in isolation sometimes stall when a problem asks for "the tangent" without first explicitly saying "find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$," because the two steps have never been practised back to back.
