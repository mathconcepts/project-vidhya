- **Switching sign convention mid-problem**: choosing upward as positive for the throw but then plugging $g=+10$ into $v=u+at$ instead of $a=-10$ (or the reverse, forgetting to flip $u$'s sign when the convention is downward-positive). The sign convention must be fixed once, at the start, and used identically in every equation for that problem — one flip halfway through silently makes every later answer wrong.

- **Using the wrong equation of motion for the unknown asked**: reaching for $v=u+at$ when time is not given at all, when $v^2=u^2+2as$ (which skips $t$ entirely) is the direct route. Each of the three equations of motion is missing exactly one quantity — pick the one that is already missing what you don't have.

- **Reading "return to the starting point" as $v=0$**: $v=0$ is the condition for the *highest point*, not for returning to the ground. Returning to the start means $s=0$ again (using the equation of motion for displacement), which gives a completely different time.

- **Confusing distance with displacement when the direction reverses**: for the ball thrown upward, the displacement over the whole flight is $0$ m (back to the start), but the *distance travelled* is $2\times20=40$ m (up and then back down) — average speed and average velocity over that flight are therefore different numbers, not the same thing measured two ways.

- **Reading a velocity-time graph's area as speed instead of displacement**: the *slope* of a $v$-$t$ graph gives acceleration; the *area* under it gives displacement. Swapping these two — for instance, reading a triangle's area as some kind of "average acceleration" — produces a quantity with the wrong physical meaning entirely, not just a wrong number.
