The exam-costing mix-up here is which level is *fixed* for a given series name. Lyman: transitions land on $n=1$ (ultraviolet). Balmer: land on $n=2$ (visible). Paschen: land on $n=3$ (infrared) — in every case, the *smaller* of the two levels is what the series name fixes, and the other level (always larger) is what varies across that series' different lines.

Counterexample: the "Balmer series limit" means $n=2$ fixed, the upper level running off to infinity (an electron barely bound at all, dropping to $n=2$): $E=13.6/4=3.4$ eV, giving $\lambda\approx1240/3.4\approx365$ nm. Confusing this with the Lyman limit ($n=1$ fixed instead) gives $E=13.6$ eV and $\lambda\approx91$ nm — a completely different wavelength, four times shorter, not a small rounding slip.
