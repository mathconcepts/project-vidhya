**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: missing coefficient or root from sum/product.** Given one root and either the sum or the product, find the other root or an unknown coefficient directly from $-b/a$ and $c/a$ — never re-form the quadratic and solve it again from scratch.

- **MCQ: common-root shortcut.** The moment you see "have a common root", check first whether either quadratic factors easily. If it does, substitute its roots into the other equation directly — reach for the general condition formula only when neither one factors.

- **Trap: location-of-roots questions have three checks, not one.** JEE distractors for "both roots exceed $k$" or "both roots lie in $(p,q)$" style questions are built by leaving out exactly one of the discriminant, boundary-sign, and vertex-position conditions. Missing any single one produces a wrong option that still looks reasonable.

- **Trap: quadratic inequalities with a negative leading coefficient.** When $a<0$, the "positive between the roots" rule flips — the expression is negative between the roots and positive outside them. Forgetting to flip the sign after multiplying through by $-1$ is a common source of wrong answers.

- **Time budget:** a nature-of-roots NAT should take under 30 seconds — compute $D$ once, read off its sign, done. There is no need to actually solve for the roots when only their nature is asked.
