Finding an **empirical formula** from percentage composition is exactly like splitting a mixed jar of coins by value: 100 g of a compound is broken down into how many grams belong to each element, and then each element's grams are converted to moles — the "count" that actually matters for a chemical ratio, since atoms of different elements have different masses.

For a compound that is 40% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen, and 53.3% oxygen by mass (in 100 g), the bars below show that split. Converting each to moles (dividing by 12, 1, and 16 respectively) and comparing the three counts gives a ratio close to 1 : 2 : 1 — the empirical formula $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$.
