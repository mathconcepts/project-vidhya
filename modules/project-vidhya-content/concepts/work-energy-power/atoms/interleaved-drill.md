**Cross-concept check: work-energy-power → newtons-laws.**

**Question 1 (energy method):** A $3\text{ kg}$ box starts at rest and is pushed by a constant $12\text{ N}$ force across $2\text{ m}$ of frictionless floor. Find its final speed using the work-energy theorem.

*Answer:* Work done: $W = Fd = 12 \times 2 = 24\text{ J}$. Since the floor is frictionless, $W_{net}=24\text{ J}=\Delta KE = \tfrac{1}{2}(3)v^2 - 0$. So $v^2 = 2(24)/3 = 16 \Rightarrow v = 4\text{ m/s}$.

**Question 2 (force method, same numbers):** Now find the same final speed using Newton's second law and kinematics.

*Answer:* Acceleration: $a = F/m = 12/3 = 4\text{ m/s}^2$. Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2ad = 0 + 2(4)(2) = 16 \Rightarrow v = 4\text{ m/s}$ — the same answer, reached by tracking force and time-independent distance instead of energy directly.

**Why this drill exists:** the work-energy theorem and Newton's second law are not two separate facts to memorise — the theorem is *derived* from $F=ma$ combined with $v^2=u^2+2ad$. Whenever a problem gives distance and force but not time, both routes must agree; if they don't, a sign or an arithmetic slip happened somewhere, and this is the fastest way to catch it.
