**"Q minus W, gas gives to get."** In the physics convention used here, $\Delta U=Q-W$: heat in ($Q$) minus work the gas gives out ($W$) equals the change in what the gas keeps for itself ($\Delta U$). If a formula sheet instead adds work, it has switched to the chemistry convention — check which one before substituting.

**"Iso stays, adia hides."** ISOthermal: temperature STAYS the same, because heat is free to flow. ADIAbatic: heat HIDES completely (zero heat exchange), so temperature is the one thing free to change instead. They are opposites in what they hold fixed, not synonyms for "nothing complicated."

**"One minus cold-over-hot" for Carnot.** $\eta=1-T_2/T_1$: cold temperature divides hot temperature, and that fraction gets subtracted from one. Both temperatures must already be in kelvin before this fraction means anything.
