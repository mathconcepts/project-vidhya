**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: compute $Q$ from given (not necessarily equilibrium) concentrations and compare to a given $K_c$.** Write both expressions with the same exponents (matching the balanced equation), compute $Q$, then compare — no need to solve for actual equilibrium concentrations unless separately asked.

- **MCQ: predicting the direction of shift after a stated stress.** Read carefully whether concentration, pressure/volume, or temperature is changing — only a temperature change also changes $K_c$'s value; the other two only move $Q$ away from an unchanged $K_c$.

- **Trap: "does a catalyst change the equilibrium yield" questions.** The answer is always no for yield/position and $K_c$'s value — a catalyst only changes how fast equilibrium is reached, a distinction JEE tests directly and often.

- **Trap: $K_p$ vs. $K_c$ conversion sign on $\Delta n$.** $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$, where $\Delta n$ = moles of gaseous products minus moles of gaseous reactants — using the reactants-minus-products order flips the exponent's sign.

- **Time budget:** a direct $Q$-vs-$K_c$ NAT should take under 40 seconds. A Le Chatelier direction MCQ, under 30 seconds once the type of stress is correctly identified.
