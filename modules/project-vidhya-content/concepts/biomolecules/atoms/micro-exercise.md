How many water molecules are released when four amino acids join to form one tetrapeptide?

A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) 3.**

A chain of $n$ amino acid units has exactly $n-1$ peptide bonds joining them, and each peptide bond releases one water molecule when it forms. For four amino acids, $n=4$, so there are $4-1=3$ peptide bonds, and $3$ water molecules are released.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and B) undercount the number of joins between four units.
- D) comes from the natural but wrong assumption that every one of the four units loses a water molecule of its own — the first amino acid's free amine end and the last amino acid's free acid end are never used in a bond with each other, so only the $n-1$ internal joins release water.

</details>
