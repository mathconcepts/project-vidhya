**Problem:** Work out the shape and hybridisation of $BrF_3$.

---

**Step 1 — Note bromine's electron count.** Bromine's group number is 17, so bromine has $7$ valence electrons.

---

**Step 2 — Subtract the bonding electrons.** $BrF_3$ has 3 bonds from bromine to fluorine, each using exactly 1 of bromine's electrons: $7 - 3 = 4$ electrons remain on bromine, unshared.

---

**Step 3 — Group those leftovers into pairs.** $4 \div 2 = 2$ lone pairs on bromine.

---

**Step 4 — Add up all the electron domains.** $3$ bonding domains $+ 2$ lone-pair domains $= 5$ domains. Five domains means $sp^3d$ hybridisation, arranged as a trigonal bipyramid.

---

**Step 5 — Decide where the lone pairs sit.** Place both lone pairs in the two equatorial positions of the bipyramid — equatorial spots have fewer close neighbours than axial spots, so putting the bulkier lone pairs there causes the least repulsion.

---

**Step 6 — Read off the shape.** With both equatorial spots taken by lone pairs, the three $F$ atoms are left in a bent, T-shaped pattern. $\boxed{BrF_3 \text{ is T-shaped}, sp^3d, 2 \text{ lone pairs}}$.
