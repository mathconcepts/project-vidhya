**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: plug straight into Coulomb's law or the inverse-square ratio.** Given two charges and a distance, or a "distance is doubled/halved" style ratio question, compute the number directly — never re-derive Coulomb's law from Gauss's law for a plain two-charge problem, since there's no symmetry to make Gauss's law worth invoking.

- **MCQ: "field inside a conductor" or "just outside a conductor's surface."** The moment a question mentions a conductor in equilibrium, the field inside it is zero and the field just outside is $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$, perpendicular to the surface — read off these two facts instead of trying to integrate anything.

- **Trap: symmetry recognition.** JEE distractors for "find the field due to..." questions often disguise a non-symmetric arrangement (two point charges, an asymmetric rod) as if Gauss's law would apply cleanly. Check for spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry *before* writing $\oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A$ — if none exists, switch to Coulomb's law and superposition immediately.

- **Trap: sign of the field near a negative charge.** A negative-charge field-direction question is a classic early distractor — the field points *towards* a negative charge, never away from it.

- **Time budget:** a direct Coulomb's-law NAT should take under 30 seconds — substitute and compute once, without re-deriving anything.
