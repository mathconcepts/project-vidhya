**Cross-concept check: electrostatics-coulomb → capacitance.**

**Question 1 (potential energy of two point charges):** Two charges $q_1=2\ \mu\text{C}$ and $q_2=3\ \mu\text{C}$ are $r=0.1\ \text{m}$ apart. Find the electric potential energy of this pair.

*Answer:* $U = \dfrac{kq_1q_2}{r} = \dfrac{9\times10^9 \times 2\times10^{-6} \times 3\times10^{-6}}{0.1} = 0.54\ \text{J}$. This is the work someone had to do, against the repulsive force, to bring the two charges to this separation from far apart.

**Question 2 (the same idea, stored on a capacitor):** A parallel-plate capacitor is really millions of tiny charges, all pushed onto one plate against their mutual repulsion. Does storing charge on a capacitor cost energy for the exact same reason as Question 1?

*Answer:* Yes. Charging a capacitor means pushing more and more like charge onto one plate, and each extra bit of charge has to fight the repulsion of all the charge already there — precisely the $kq_1q_2/r$ story from Question 1, repeated over and over. Working through that sum for a capacitor (rather than just two point charges) is exactly where the capacitor energy formula $U=\tfrac12 QV$ comes from.

**Why this drill exists:** electric potential energy between charges is often treated as a stand-alone formula to memorise and forget once electrostatics ends. It is not — it is the exact same repulsion-fighting idea that reappears, scaled up to millions of charges, the moment a capacitor enters the picture.
