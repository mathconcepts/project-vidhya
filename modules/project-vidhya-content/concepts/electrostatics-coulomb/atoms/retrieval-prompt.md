**Question:** State Coulomb's law and Gauss's law, and give the one condition that decides which is the faster tool for a given field problem.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Coulomb's law:** $F = \dfrac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}$, force between two point charges, along the line joining them.

**Gauss's law:** $\oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A = \dfrac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$, always true for any closed surface.

**The deciding condition:** Gauss's law is fast only when the charge arrangement has enough symmetry (spherical, cylindrical, or planar) to argue $E$ is constant over some closed surface. With no such symmetry, fall back to Coulomb's law and vector superposition instead.

</details>
