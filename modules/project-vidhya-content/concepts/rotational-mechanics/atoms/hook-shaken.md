A skater starts with moment of inertia $I_1 = 4\text{ kg·m}^2$ (the rotational version of mass), spinning at $\omega_1 = 2\text{ rad/s}$.

Angular momentum: $L = I_1\omega_1 = 4 \times 2 = 8\text{ kg·m}^2\text{/s}$.

The skater pulls both arms in. New moment of inertia: $I_2 = 2\text{ kg·m}^2$.

No outside torque touches the skater, so $L$ stays exactly $8\text{ kg·m}^2\text{/s}$. New spin rate: $L = I_2\omega_2 \Rightarrow 8 = 2 \times \omega_2 \Rightarrow \omega_2 = 4\text{ rad/s}$.

Spin rate doubled, purely from pulling the arms in — this is **conservation of angular momentum**: $L=I\omega$ stays constant whenever net external torque is zero.
