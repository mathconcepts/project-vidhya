The mark-costing confusion here is treating a semiconductor's energy **band** as if it were a single, sharp energy **level** — the way one isolated atom's electron sits at one exact allowed energy. It is not: a band is millions of extremely closely-spaced allowed levels, packed together so tightly they behave as one continuous range, never a single value.

Counterexample: one isolated silicon atom has sharp, discrete allowed energies, exactly like hydrogen's $E_n=-13.6/n^2$ eV levels. Bring roughly $10^{23}$ silicon atoms together into a crystal, and each of those sharp levels *splits* into that many extremely close sub-levels, merging into a band. "What is the exact energy of an electron in the conduction band" has no single answer — only a range does, bounded below by the band's edge — unlike a lone hydrogen electron's one exact $E_n$.
