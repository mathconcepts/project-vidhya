Take a block of mass $m=2$ kg on a frictionless surface, pushed by a single horizontal force $F=6$ N. Newton's second law: $a=F/m=6/2=3$ m/s$^2$, in the direction $F$ points.

Now put a second block, $m_2=3$ kg, right in front of it, in contact, and push the *pair* with the same $F=6$ N from behind. Total mass is now $2+3=5$ kg, so $a=6/5=1.2$ m/s$^2$ for both blocks together.

Find the contact force pushing the front block along. Only that contact force acts on $m_2$ (nothing else touches it horizontally): $N=m_2a=3(1.2)=3.6$ N.

Check it from the other block instead. On $m_1$, two forces act: $F$ forward, and the front block pushing back with $N$ (Newton's third law — equal, opposite, on a different object). So $F-N=m_1a \Rightarrow 6-N=2(1.2)=2.4 \Rightarrow N=3.6$ N. Both routes give the same $3.6$ N, confirming the two free-body diagrams agree.
