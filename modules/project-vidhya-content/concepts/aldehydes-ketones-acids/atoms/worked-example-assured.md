**Problem:** Acetaldehyde ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$) is treated with dilute NaOH. Name the product.

---

**Step 1 — Recognise the pattern directly.** Self-aldol of any carbonyl compound with an alpha-hydrogen always gives one product shape: two monomer units joined by a new carbon-carbon bond, with a hydroxyl sitting beta to the surviving carbonyl. For $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ that is $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH(OH)-CH}_2\text{-CHO}$ (3-hydroxybutanal); dehydration on heating always gives the conjugated enal, $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH=CH-CHO}$ (crotonaldehyde).

$$\boxed{\text{CH}_3\text{-CH=CH-CHO (crotonaldehyde)}}$$

**The condition that actually matters.** "Has an alpha-hydrogen" is necessary for aldol, but it is nowhere near sufficient for the CROSSED case examiners favour. Mix $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ with $\text{HCHO}$ (formaldehyde has no alpha-hydrogen and cannot form an enolate) and dilute NaOH: acetaldehyde's enolate can still attack formaldehyde's carbonyl carbon, giving a genuine crossed-aldol product. Meanwhile formaldehyde's own carbonyl, lacking any alpha-hydrogen, simultaneously undergoes a crossed Cannizzaro with any leftover acetaldehyde present. A mix of two carbonyl compounds is never automatically "one reaction or the other" — each compound's own alpha-hydrogen count decides its own available route, independently.
