Stacking oranges directly on top of each other, row above row, leaves large gaps between layers — this is simple cubic packing, wasting nearly half the crate's volume as empty space. Nudging each higher layer of oranges to sit in the DIPS of the layer below, instead of directly above, packs noticeably more fruit into the same crate — this is closer to how bcc and fcc structures pack their atoms.

The diagram on this card compares the actual packing efficiency of the three common structures, side by side: simple cubic wastes almost half its volume, bcc improves on that, and fcc (the tightest way to pack identical spheres, mathematically) leaves only about a quarter of the volume empty.
