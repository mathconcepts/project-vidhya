**System**: the part of the universe under study. **Surroundings**: everything else. **Boundary**: what separates them.

**First law of thermodynamics (chemistry/IUPAC convention)**: $\Delta U = q + w$, where $q$ = heat absorbed *by* the system (positive if absorbed), $w$ = work done *on* the system (positive if done on it, e.g. during compression). Note: the physics convention often instead writes $\Delta U = q - w$ with $w$ = work done *by* the system — the opposite sign for $w$.

**Enthalpy**: $H = U + PV$; at constant pressure, $\Delta H = q_p$ (heat exchanged).

**Hess's Law**: $\Delta H$ for an overall reaction is the same regardless of the path (number and choice of intermediate steps) taken, since enthalpy is a state function.

**Standard enthalpy of formation**, $\Delta H_f^\circ$: enthalpy change forming 1 mole of a compound from its elements in their standard states.

**Entropy**, $S$: a measure of disorder/randomness. Second law: for a spontaneous process, total entropy (system $+$ surroundings) increases.

**Gibbs free energy**: $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$ (at constant $T$, $P$). $\Delta G<0$: spontaneous. $\Delta G=0$: at equilibrium. $\Delta G>0$: non-spontaneous.
