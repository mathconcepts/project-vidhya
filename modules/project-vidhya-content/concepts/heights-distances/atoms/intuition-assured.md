Knowing $\tan\theta=h/d$ is necessary for most height-and-distance setups, but it is not automatically the RIGHT ratio — which ratio applies is decided by the side the question actually hands you. If a problem gives the horizontal distance $d$, tangent connects it to the height. If instead it gives the length of the LINE OF SIGHT itself — a ladder's length, a kite's string, a rope from an observer to the object — that length is the hypotenuse, not the adjacent side, and tangent no longer applies at all; sine is the ratio that uses a hypotenuse and the opposite side.

A student who reflexively writes $\tan\theta=h/d$ whenever an angle and a length appear together, without checking whether that length is the horizontal leg or the slanted line of sight, gets the wrong equation even though every algebraic step afterward is executed perfectly. The formula is only as reliable as the identification of which side is which — that identification is the actual skill, not the ratio itself.
