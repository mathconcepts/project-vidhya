Stand $30$ m from the foot of a mobile-signal tower and look up at its tip: the angle above the horizontal reads $30°$. Walk $20$ m closer, to $10$ m from the foot, and look up again — now it reads $60°$. Two angles, taken from two spots, and one number neither angle alone can give you: the tower's actual height. A single measured angle only fixes a RATIO between height and distance, never either one by itself. Heights-and-distances problems are exactly this: turning an angle your eyes can measure into a length a measuring tape cannot easily reach, using the one ratio that ties an angle to the two legs of a right triangle — tangent.
