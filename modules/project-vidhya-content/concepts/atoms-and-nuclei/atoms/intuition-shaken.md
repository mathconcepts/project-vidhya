Take hydrogen's electron sitting at level $n=3$: $E_3=-13.6/9\approx-1.51$ eV. It jumps down to level $n=2$: $E_2=-13.6/4=-3.4$ eV.

The photon released carries exactly the gap it crossed: $E_3-E_2=-1.51-(-3.4)=1.89$ eV. Converting to wavelength with $E\approx1240/\lambda(\text{nm})$: $\lambda=1240/1.89\approx656$ nm — one exact red line, not a spread of colours.

Now check: could this same red line come from an electron sitting still at $n=2$, with no jump happening at all? No — a level on its own, nothing crossing it, releases nothing. This colour exists only because of the specific jump $n=3\to n=2$; a different jump, say $n=4\to n=2$, would release a different, equally exact energy and a different colour entirely.
