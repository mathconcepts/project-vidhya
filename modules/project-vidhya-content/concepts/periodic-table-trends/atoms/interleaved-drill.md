**Cross-concept check: periodic-table-trends → p-block-elements.**

**Question 1 (predict oxide character from the electronegativity trend):** Arrange $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{SO}_3$ in increasing order of acidic character.

*Answer:* Electronegativity (and non-metallic character) rises left to right across Period 3: $Na < Al < S$. Oxide character tracks this directly — a strongly electropositive metal's oxide is basic, a borderline element's oxide is amphoteric, and a non-metal's oxide is acidic. So $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ (basic) $<$ $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (amphoteric) $<$ $\text{SO}_3$ (acidic). No new fact about oxides is needed here — this is the same period-wise trend from periodic-table-trends, just read off through a different property.

**Question 2 (a within-p-block comparison, same logic):** Between $\text{SiO}_2$ and $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10}$, which is the more strongly acidic oxide, and why?

*Answer:* $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10}$ is more strongly acidic. Phosphorus sits to the right of silicon in Period 3, so it is more electronegative — the same across-period trend that made $\text{SO}_3$ more acidic than $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ also makes phosphorus's oxide more acidic than silicon's. Silicon's oxide is acidic too, just less strongly so.

**Why this drill exists:** periodic-table-trends gives you electronegativity and metallic character as period-wise trends; p-block-elements immediately puts those same trends to work explaining a genuinely new fact — why oxides shift from basic to acidic across a row. The trend does not stop being useful once you leave this concept; it is the tool the next concept runs on.
