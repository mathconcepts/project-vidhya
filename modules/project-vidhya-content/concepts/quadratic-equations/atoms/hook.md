A shopkeeper's daily profit, in rupees, works out to $P(x) = -2x^2 + 40x - 150$ when the selling price is set $x$ rupees above cost. Before any calculus, one question decides the whole business: is there *any* price where profit is positive, or does this shop lose money at every price?

That question is answered by a **quadratic equation** — whether $-2x^2+40x-150=0$ even has real solutions, and if so, where they sit. The two roots are the exact prices where profit crosses zero; between them, profit is positive. One number, the **discriminant**, tells you instantly whether such crossing points exist at all, before solving anything.
