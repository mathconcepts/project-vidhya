A factory has three machines. Machine A makes $25\%$ of all items and gets $5\%$ of them wrong — the worst defect rate of the three. Machine B makes $35\%$ and gets $4\%$ wrong. Machine C makes the remaining $40\%$ and gets only $2\%$ wrong. An inspector pulls one item at random from the day's output and finds it defective. Which machine most likely made it? Guessing "Machine A, since its defect rate is the worst" ignores half the story — how MUCH each machine makes matters just as much as how OFTEN it slips up. Untangling "given that it is defective" from "the machine's own defect rate" is exactly what conditional probability and Bayes' theorem are for.
