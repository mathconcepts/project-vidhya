**"Multiply distances, add angles"** is the one line that carries De Moivre's theorem, polar-form multiplication, and cube roots of unity all at once. Everything else is that sentence applied to a specific number.

**For cube roots of unity, remember "three friends, evenly spaced, $120°$ apart."** $1$ sits at $0°$, $\omega$ at $120°$, $\omega^2$ at $240°$ — always $120°$ apart, never any other spacing, because $360°/3=120°$.

**"$1+\omega+\omega^2=0$" — a triangle that closes.** Three arrows of equal length, $120°$ apart, drawn tip-to-tail, form a closed equilateral triangle and land back exactly where they started. That is the geometric reason the sum is zero, not just an algebraic coincidence to memorise blind.

**Quadrant check before anything else**: sketch roughly where the point sits (which quadrant) before computing any inverse tangent — it takes five seconds and stops the single most common argument mistake on this topic.
