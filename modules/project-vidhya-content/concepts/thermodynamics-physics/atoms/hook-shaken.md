Push a bicycle pump's piston in fast, doing about $40$ J of work on the trapped air in under a second — too quick for heat to escape through the metal barrel. All $40$ J of that work turns straight into extra internal energy, so the air's temperature visibly rises.

Do the exact same $40$ J of compression slowly instead, over a full minute. Roughly $35$ J of heat now has time to leak out through the barrel wall while you push. Only $40-35=5$ J is left over to raise internal energy — so the slow compression barely warms the air, even though the piston moved the same distance both times.

The system is the trapped air; the barrel's inner wall is its boundary. Same mechanical squeeze, very different temperature outcome, because how fast heat could cross that boundary is what decided the result — not how far the piston moved.
