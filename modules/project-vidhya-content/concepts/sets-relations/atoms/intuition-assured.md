Equivalence relations and partial orders share two axioms — reflexive and transitive — and differ in exactly one: **symmetric** (equivalence) versus **antisymmetric** (partial order, where $aRb$ and $bRa$ together force $a=b$). That single axiom decides whether the relation groups elements into indifferent piles or ranks them.

The trap is assuming reflexive+transitive alone commits you to one or the other — it doesn't; that combination alone is only a **preorder**, and both stronger structures still need their third axiom checked explicitly, especially on a relation defined by "shares a property," which tempts symmetry but rarely earns antisymmetry for free.
