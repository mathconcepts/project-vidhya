**Problem:** You need propan-1-amine, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (three carbons). One student proposes Hofmann degradation starting from butanamide, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CONH}_2$ (four carbons). A second proposes Gabriel synthesis starting from 1-bromopropane, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$ (three carbons). Check both routes.

---

**Step 1 — Hofmann degradation removes the carbonyl carbon as carbonate.** Starting material: butanamide, four carbons. The reaction $\text{RCONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{RNH}_2 + \ldots$ replaces the whole $\text{-CONH}_2$ group with $\text{-NH}_2$ directly on the carbon that was attached to it — the carbonyl carbon itself leaves as $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$, taking one carbon out of the count.

---

**Step 2 — Count the product's carbons.** Butanamide's carbon chain, minus the carbonyl carbon, is $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{-}$, now bonded to $\text{NH}_2$: propan-1-amine, three carbons. Correct starting material.

---

**Step 3 — Gabriel synthesis keeps every carbon of the halide.** Potassium phthalimide displaces the bromide directly, and hydrolysis afterward releases the amine with the exact same carbon skeleton the halide brought in — no carbon is added or removed anywhere in this mechanism.

---

**Step 4 — Count the product's carbons for this route too.** 1-bromopropane has three carbons; the amine it produces keeps all three: propan-1-amine again.

$$\boxed{\text{Both routes correctly give propan-1-amine} — \text{but only because each starting material's carbon count already matched its own rule.}}$$

**Why the two routes need different starting materials for the same target.** Hofmann degradation always LOSES one carbon (the carbonyl carbon), so it needs an amide with ONE MORE carbon than the target amine — butanamide (4C) for propan-1-amine (3C). Gabriel synthesis loses NO carbon, so it needs a halide with the SAME carbon count as the target — 1-bromopropane (3C) for propan-1-amine (3C). Swap the two starting materials — propanamide (3C) into Hofmann, or 1-bromobutane (4C) into Gabriel — and both routes would silently drift one carbon away from the intended target. The choice of starting material is not interchangeable between the two mechanisms; each one's own carbon-counting rule has to be applied to the mechanism actually being used.
