Every square matrix has a signature equation, its characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda)$ — built so that plugging in each eigenvalue gives zero. Cayley-Hamilton says something stranger: plug in the *matrix itself*, and you still get zero. Not a coincidence — watch it happen on the same matrix's own eigen-directions below.

Rearranged, $p(A)=0$ turns every high power of $A$ into a combination of $A$ and $I$, and hands you $A^{-1}$ directly — no cofactors, no determinant expansion.
