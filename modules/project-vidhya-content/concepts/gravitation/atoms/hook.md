A satellite circles Earth at twice Earth's radius from the centre ($r=2R$, with $R=6400\text{ km}$). Using $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ at the surface, its orbital speed there works out to $v=\sqrt{gR^2/r} = 5600\text{ m/s}$.

Ground control wants it in a **higher** orbit instead, at $r=4R$. Does the satellite need to speed up or slow down to get there and stay? The number says slow down: at $r=4R$, the same formula gives $v=\sqrt{gR^2/(4R)}\approx3960\text{ m/s}$ — noticeably slower, even though *reaching* that higher orbit costs the satellite real energy along the way. **Gravitation** is the one topic where "farther out" and "moving faster" pull in opposite directions once an object has actually settled into a stable orbit.
