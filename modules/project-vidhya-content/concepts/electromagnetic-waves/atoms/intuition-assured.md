The two-surface picture is familiar. The distinction that costs marks: displacement current makes Ampere's law *self-consistent*, but that consistency does not automatically mean an electromagnetic wave is radiating in every situation with a changing electric field.

Counterexample: a capacitor charging slowly, over several seconds, on a lab bench has a genuine, calculable displacement current between its plates — Ampere's law needs it, and a real (tiny) magnetic field does curl around that gap. Yet nothing meaningfully "radiates away" as a wave here; the fields stay essentially confined near the capacitor. Radiation as a travelling electromagnetic wave additionally needs the fields to be changing fast enough, and in a geometry (like an oscillating antenna) that lets them detach from their source and propagate outward — a slowly charging capacitor satisfies Maxwell's equations perfectly without being a meaningful radio transmitter.
