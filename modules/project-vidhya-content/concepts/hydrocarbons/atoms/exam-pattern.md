**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: identify which of several given rings or ions is aromatic, antiaromatic, or non-aromatic.** Count pi electrons for each option separately, check $4n+2$ against $4n$, and separately confirm planarity and full conjugation before trusting any count.

- **MCQ: predict the product of $\text{HX}$ addition to an unsymmetrical alkene.** Check first whether peroxide is mentioned and whether the acid is specifically $\text{HBr}$ — that combination alone flips the addition to anti-Markovnikov; every other combination follows ordinary Markovnikov addition.

- **MCQ or NAT: identify ozonolysis products from a given alkene, or the reverse — identify the alkene from given ozonolysis products.** Match each carbonyl product back to one end of the original double bond; a $\text{=CH}_2$ end always gives formaldehyde.

- **Trap: an ion or ring is charged, so it "must" be classified a certain way.** Charge sign alone decides nothing — only the resulting pi electron count against $4n+2$ does. JEE distractors are built exactly around this assumption.

- **Time budget:** an aromaticity-check NAT or MCQ should take under 45 seconds — draw the ring, count contributing p-orbitals and electrons once, check the formula, done. Do not attempt to derive resonance structures unless specifically asked for them.
