Two narrow slits, $S_1$ and $S_2$, $0.5\text{ mm}$ apart, are lit by the same single source of light of wavelength $500\text{ nm}$, so both slits send out waves that stay in step with each other. A screen sits $1\text{ m}$ away. Pick a point $P$ on the screen, $2\text{ mm}$ above the centre line — light from $S_2$ has to travel very slightly farther to reach $P$ than light from $S_1$ does.

That tiny extra distance is the **path difference**, and here it works out to exactly $1000\text{ nm}$ — precisely twice the wavelength. Two whole wavelengths extra means the two waves arrive back in step, crest lined up with crest, and $P$ is bright. Move to a point where the extra distance is one-and-a-half wavelengths instead, and the two waves arrive crest-against-trough — $P$ goes dark. The exact distance between bright fringes, the **fringe width**, only comes after this path-difference idea is settled; without it, the fringe-width formula is just a formula with nothing behind it.
