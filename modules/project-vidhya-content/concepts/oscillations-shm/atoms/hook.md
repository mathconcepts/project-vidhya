Picture a child on a swing at the exact top of her arc, for one split second, hanging almost still before falling back. Now picture her at the lowest point of the swing, rushing through the middle as fast as she ever moves. Sketch her height above the ground against time, and it traces a smooth up-and-down curve — a **displacement-time graph** — before a single formula gets written.

That curve is **simple harmonic motion (SHM)**: motion where the restoring force pulling something back toward its centre is directly proportional to how far it has moved away from that centre. The swing is slowest exactly where it is farthest from centre, and fastest exactly where it passes through centre — displacement and speed are never both maximum at the same instant. This one relationship, restoring force proportional to displacement, is what makes every SHM system — a swing, a spring, a tuning fork — obey the exact same mathematics underneath.
