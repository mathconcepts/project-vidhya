What are the modulus and argument of $z = 1 - i\sqrt3$?

A) modulus $2$, argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
B) modulus $2$, argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
C) modulus $4$, argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
D) modulus $2$, argument $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
E) modulus $\sqrt2$, argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) modulus $2$, argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.**

**Modulus:** $|z| = \sqrt{1^2+(\sqrt3)^2} = \sqrt{1+3} = 2$.

**Argument:** the real part is positive ($1$) and the imaginary part is negative ($-\sqrt3$), so $z$ sits in the **fourth** quadrant. The reference angle is $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{1}\right)=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. In the fourth quadrant, the principal argument is the *negative* of the reference angle: $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) uses the reference angle's sign without adjusting for the fourth quadrant — the exact quadrant-checking trap this concept warns about.
- C) doubles the modulus by forgetting the square root: $1^2+(\sqrt3)^2=4$, and $\sqrt4=2$, not $4$.
- D) uses the second-quadrant adjustment ($\pi - \text{ref}$) instead of the fourth-quadrant one.
- E) drops the $3$ under the root: $\sqrt{1+3}\ne\sqrt2$.

</details>
