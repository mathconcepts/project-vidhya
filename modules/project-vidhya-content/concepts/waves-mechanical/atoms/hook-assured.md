"The listener hears a higher pitch because the source is moving" is true, but which sign goes where in the Doppler formula is not automatic — get it backwards and a rise in pitch turns into a drop.

A $500$ Hz source moving toward a stationary listener at $20$ m/s (sound speed $340$ m/s) is heard at $f'=500(340)/(340-20)\approx531$ Hz — higher. The identical source moving *away* at the same $20$ m/s is heard at $f'=500(340)/(340+20)\approx472$ Hz — lower. Same source, same speed, one sign flipped in the denominator, and a $59$ Hz gap between the two answers.
