**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: sum of $n$ terms of an AP or GP.** Direct formula application once $a$, the common difference or ratio, and $n$ are identified correctly from the problem's wording.

- **MCQ: optimisation using AM $\geq$ GM.** Phrased as "given $a+b=$ constant, find the maximum of $ab$" or the reverse ("given $ab=$ constant, find the minimum of $a+b$"). Recognise this shape immediately — it is always solved by AM-GM, never by calculus, at this level.

- **Trap: average-speed problems.** Equal distance at different speeds needs the harmonic mean. Equal TIME at different speeds needs the arithmetic mean. Reading which one the problem describes, before reaching for a formula, is the actual skill being tested.

- **NAT: telescoping sums.** Look for a denominator that factors into consecutive (or evenly-spaced) terms, like $r(r+1)$ or $(2r-1)(2r+1)$ — that shape signals a partial-fraction telescoping approach.

- **NAT: infinite GP sum,** always checking $|r|<1$ first before applying $S_\infty=\frac{a}{1-r}$.

- **Time budget:** under $60$ seconds for a direct AP/GP sum; up to $90$ seconds for a telescoping sum, since the partial-fraction split needs to be found before the cancellation can even start.
