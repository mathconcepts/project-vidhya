Draw the wire feeding the capacitor's top plate, current $2\text{ A}$. Draw a small loop around that wire.

Pick surface 1: a flat disc just above the plate. The wire pierces it. Current through it: $2\text{ A}$.

Pick surface 2: a sock-shaped surface that dips through the gap between the plates and comes back out. Nothing physical pierces it there. Current through it: $0\text{ A}$.

Same loop, two surfaces, two different numbers — that is a genuine contradiction, not a rounding error.

Fix: inside the gap, the electric field between the plates is changing as they charge. Count that changing field as a current, worth $2\text{ A}$ here, and surface 2 now reports $2\text{ A}$ too — matching surface 1 exactly, and removing the contradiction.
