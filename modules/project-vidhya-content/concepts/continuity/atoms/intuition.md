"No lifting the pen" is the honest first picture of continuity, but it hides three genuinely different ways a curve can force you to lift it — and telling them apart is most of what this topic tests.

A **hole**: the curve approaches a single height from both sides and simply doesn't mark a point there, or marks the wrong one. The surrounding curve gives you the missing value for free — plug it back in and the pen never has to leave the page. That's a **removable** discontinuity.

A **step**: the curve approaches one height from the left and a genuinely different height from the right. No single value could patch this — there is nowhere consistent to put the pen down, because the two sides disagree about where it should land. That's a **jump** discontinuity.

A **blow-up**: the curve races off toward $+\infty$ or $-\infty$ as you approach the point from one or both sides, never settling near any finite height at all. No patch is possible because there is no finite value to patch it with. That's an **infinite** discontinuity.

Same symptom — the pen lifts — three unrelated causes, and only one of the three can ever be fixed by redefining a single point.
