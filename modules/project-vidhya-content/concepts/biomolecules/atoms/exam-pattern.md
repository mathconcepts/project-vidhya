**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: reducing versus non-reducing sugar identification.** The moment a question names two or more sugars and a colour-change test (Fehling's or Tollens'), check each one's anomeric carbon count directly — free anomeric carbon means reducing, tied up in a glycosidic bond means non-reducing.

- **MCQ: matching a biomolecule to its monomer.** Starch and cellulose (glucose), proteins (amino acids), and nucleic acids (nucleotides) are a recurring "identify the building block" question shape — read the biomolecule named and recall its monomer directly, rather than the specific structure it forms.

- **Trap: same monomer, different molecule, questions.** A question naming two glucose-based polymers (starch vs. cellulose, or starch vs. glycogen) is testing whether linkage geometry or branching pattern — not the shared monomer — is what's actually being asked about.

- **Trap: DNA-versus-RNA base and sugar swaps.** A question describing RNA but naming thymine, or describing DNA but naming ribose sugar or uracil, has planted exactly one factual mismatch to catch a rushed reading.

- **Time budget:** these are direct-recall questions — under 20 seconds each, provided the fact (which sugar, which bond, which base) is genuinely memorised rather than half-remembered.
