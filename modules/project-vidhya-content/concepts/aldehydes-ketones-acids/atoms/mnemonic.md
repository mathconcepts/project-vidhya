**"No neighbour, no enolate — Cannizzaro instead."** If the carbon next to the carbonyl carbon has no hydrogen neighbour to hand over, the base can't build an enolate — so it has nowhere to go but straight at the carbonyl carbon itself, giving Cannizzaro.

**Reactivity, smallest crowd wins: $\text{HCHO} > \text{RCHO} > \text{R}_2\text{CO}$.** Count the alkyl groups sitting on the carbonyl carbon — zero, one, two — and reactivity runs the other way, largest crowd loses.

**Silver loves aldehydes only: "mirror means aldehyde."** Tollens' reagent gets reduced to a silver mirror by an aldehyde's easily removed hydrogen — a ketone has no such hydrogen on its carbonyl carbon to give up, so no mirror ever forms for a ketone.

**Acid strength: "spread the charge, keep the acid."** A carboxylate spreads its negative charge over two oxygens by resonance — the more that charge can spread further outward (an electron-withdrawing group nearby helps), the happier the ion is to exist, and the stronger the acid.
