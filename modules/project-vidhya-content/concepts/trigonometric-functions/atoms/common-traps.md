- **Dropping the alternating sign**: writing $\theta=n\pi+\pi/6$ for $\sin\theta=1/2$ loses the entire second family of solutions ($150°$, $510°$, and so on) that only the $(-1)^n$ term supplies. Without it, you have found half the answer.
- **Mixing the sine and cosine general-solution shapes**: $\cos\theta=\cos\alpha$ uses a plain $\pm\alpha$; $\sin\theta=\sin\alpha$ uses the alternating $(-1)^n\alpha$. Borrowing one equation's shape for the other throws away real solutions or invents ones that do not check.
- **Leaving the answer in degrees**: JEE general-solution answers are expected in radians, as multiples of $\pi$. Writing "$30$" instead of "$\pi/6$" costs the mark even when every step of the method was right.
- **Squaring introduces extra roots**: converting $\sin\theta+\cos\theta=1$ by squaring both sides picks up solutions where $\sin\theta\cos\theta=0$ that satisfy the SQUARED equation but not the original one. Every root produced by squaring must be substituted back into the original equation before it is accepted.
- **Treating a negative cosine value as a mistake**: in the cosine rule, $\cos A=(b^2+c^2-a^2)/(2bc)$ can come out negative — that is a completely valid answer meaning $A$ is obtuse, not a sign to go back and "fix" the arithmetic.
