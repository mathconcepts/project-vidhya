**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: wavelength or frequency from $c=f\lambda$.** A one-line substitution, as long as the powers of ten are tracked carefully — this is almost always the whole question.

- **MCQ: identifying a use or property of one spectrum member.** "Used to detect fake currency", "used in night-vision cameras", "absorbed by the ozone layer" — each phrase points to exactly one member (ultraviolet, infrared, ultraviolet again) and rarely needs any calculation at all.

- **Trap: momentum questions that don't specify absorption or reflection.** Read the question carefully for "absorbed" versus "reflected" before choosing between $p=U/c$ and $p=2U/c$ — this single word decides which formula applies.

- **Trap: a question describing $E$ and $B$ as "$90^\circ$ out of phase".** This is always a wrong-option distractor, borrowed from the unrelated AC voltage-current relationship — in a genuine electromagnetic wave, $E$ and $B$ are in phase.

- **Time budget:** a $c=f\lambda$ NAT should take under $20$ seconds; a spectrum-use MCQ should take under $15$ seconds once the use is recognised — there is no arithmetic to slow either one down.
