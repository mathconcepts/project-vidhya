A sealed bottle of soda stays fizzy for months on a shelf, yet the moment it is opened, it goes flat. Inside the sealed bottle, dissolved carbon dioxide gas is escaping into the space above the liquid *and* re-dissolving back into the liquid, at the same rate — nothing looks like it is changing, but both processes never stop.

This is **dynamic equilibrium**: the forward and reverse processes are both still happening, just at equal rates, so the observable amounts (how much gas is dissolved, how much sits above) stay constant. Opening the bottle lets the escaping gas leave permanently, breaking that balance.

For a reversible reaction $A \rightleftharpoons B$, the equilibrium constant $K_c$ is a fixed number (at a given temperature) that this dynamic balance always settles to. The **reaction quotient**, $Q$, uses the exact same expression as $K_c$ but can be evaluated at *any* point, not just at equilibrium — comparing $Q$ to $K_c$ says which way, if any, the reaction still needs to shift.
