**What is the general solution of $\cos\theta=-\dfrac12$?**

(A) $\theta=2n\pi\pm\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
(B) $\theta=2n\pi\pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
(C) $\theta=n\pi+(-1)^n\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
(D) $\theta=n\pi\pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
(E) $\theta=2n\pi\pm\dfrac{\pi}{6}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: (B).**

**Reasoning**: the general solution of $\cos\theta=\cos\alpha$ is always $\theta=2n\pi\pm\alpha$ — never the alternating $(-1)^n$ form, which belongs to sine. The reference angle $\alpha$ must itself satisfy $\cos\alpha=-1/2$ within $[0,\pi]$ (cosine's own principal range), and that angle is $2\pi/3$ ($120°$), since $\cos120°=-0.5$. Substituting gives option (B). Option (A) uses the wrong reference angle ($\pi/3$ gives $\cos=+0.5$, the wrong sign). Option (C) borrows sine's alternating shape, which does not apply here. Option (D) drops the "2" from $2n\pi$, silently discarding half the solution family.

</details>
