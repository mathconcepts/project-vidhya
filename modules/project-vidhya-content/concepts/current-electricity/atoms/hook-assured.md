"Terminal voltage is always less than emf" is true only while the cell is *discharging* (supplying current outward). Force current *into* the cell instead — while charging a battery from a stronger source — and the direction of the drop flips.

While being charged, $V_{\text{terminal}}=\text{emf}+Ir$, which is **greater** than the emf, not less. The same $12\ \text{V}$, $1\ \Omega$ cell, forced to accept $2\ \text{A}$ from a charger, shows $12+2=14\ \text{V}$ across its terminals — because now the current has to be pushed against the cell's own emf, and the internal resistance's drop adds on top of that instead of subtracting from it.
