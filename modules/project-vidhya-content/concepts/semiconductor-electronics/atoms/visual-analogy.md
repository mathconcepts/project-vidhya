Think of a one-way flyover ramp in a city. Traffic joining from the correct end merges smoothly and flows through, however heavy the traffic gets. Try entering from the *wrong* end, against the arrows, and barriers block nearly everyone — only a rare vehicle sneaks past.

A p-n junction diode behaves exactly like this ramp for electric current. In **forward bias** (the p-side connected to the higher potential), the majority carriers on each side — electrons from the n-side, "holes" (missing electrons that act like moving positive charge) from the p-side — are pushed toward the junction and merge into it easily, so current flows freely. In **reverse bias** (connected the other way round), those same carriers are pulled *away* from the junction instead, widening the carrier-free **depletion region** between the two sides — almost nothing gets through, except a tiny leakage current from the few minority carriers wandering the wrong way.

Unlike a real flyover, this ramp needs no gates, barriers, or moving parts at all — it is built entirely from where the donor and acceptor impurity atoms happen to sit inside the crystal.
