Director circle and auxiliary circle sound alike and are built from entirely different ideas — worth keeping apart on sight. The auxiliary circle, radius $a$ centred at the ellipse's own centre, is a construction device: every ellipse point $(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)$ has a matching auxiliary-circle point $(a\cos\theta,a\sin\theta)$ directly above or below it, sharing the SAME parameter $\theta$. It says nothing about tangents.

The director circle, $x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2$ for an ellipse, is the locus of a genuinely different fact: the point where two MUTUALLY PERPENDICULAR tangents to the ellipse happen to meet. For a hyperbola it is $x^2+y^2=a^2-b^2$ — real only when $a>b$; a rectangular hyperbola ($a=b$) has NO director circle at all, meaning no point in the plane admits two perpendicular tangents to it. The parabola has no director circle of either kind; instead, perpendicular tangents to a parabola always meet exactly on the directrix — a clean, provable fact, not a coincidence checked on one example.
