Bond energies, in kJ/mol: F-F = 159, Cl-Cl = 242. Subtract: 242 - 159 = 83. Chlorine's bond beats fluorine's by 83.

If size alone decided bond strength, smaller atoms sitting closer should give a stronger bond, and fluorine is smaller than chlorine. So F-F should beat Cl-Cl. It does not.

Reason: fluorine carries three lone pairs, and fluorine atoms are small enough that these lone pairs sit close to the neighbouring atom's lone pairs across the bond. Lone pairs repel. In F2 that repulsion weakens the bond; in Cl2 the atoms are bigger, so lone pairs sit farther apart. That extra repulsion drags F2's bond energy below Cl2's, despite fluorine's smaller size.
