**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: verify by substitution, don't derive from scratch.** When JEE gives you the differential equation AND a candidate solution as an option, substituting the candidate back in (differentiate it, plug into the equation, check both sides match) is almost always faster than solving the equation yourself from first principles. This is the "differentiate the options" trick from indefinite integration, applied one level up.

- **Trap: an option with the wrong number of arbitrary constants can be eliminated instantly.** A general solution to an order-$n$ equation needs exactly $n$ independent constants — not more, not fewer. An option offering two constants for a first-order equation, or one constant for a second-order equation, is wrong by structure alone, before checking whether it even satisfies the equation.

- **Order/degree MCQs are a radical-clearing test, not a definitions test.** Almost every JEE order/degree question hides a radical or fractional power on a derivative specifically to catch a student who reads degree off the unmodified equation. Clear it first, every time, before answering.

- **Time budget:** classify the equation type (separable / homogeneous / linear) within the first $20$ seconds using the three-question decision rule. An equation misclassified early costs the full remaining time budget on a method that was never going to reach the answer.
