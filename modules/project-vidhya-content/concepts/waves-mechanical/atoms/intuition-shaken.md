Take a string with tension $T_s=100$ N and mass per unit length $\mu=0.01$ kg/m.

**Step 1 — wave speed.** $v=\sqrt{T_s/\mu}=\sqrt{100/0.01}=100$ m/s.

**Step 2 — frequency, for wavelength $\lambda=0.5$ m.** $f=v/\lambda=100/0.5=200$ Hz — $200$ full up-down cycles pass any fixed point every second.

**Step 3 — shake the same string twice as fast (frequency $400$ Hz), tension and mass per length unchanged.** Speed stays $100$ m/s — it depends only on the string, never on how it is shaken — so wavelength must shrink: $\lambda=v/f=100/400=0.25$ m, exactly half of before.

**Step 4 — two tuning forks, $256$ Hz and $260$ Hz, sounded together.** Beat frequency is $|256-260|=4$ Hz — four loud-soft pulses heard every second.

**Step 5 — the $260$ Hz fork now moves toward the listener.** The Doppler effect raises the heard frequency above $260$ Hz, changing the beat rate too — even though the wave's true speed through the air never changed at all.
