**Newton's universal law of gravitation**: $F = \dfrac{GMm}{r^2}$, always attractive, along the line joining the two masses. $G=6.674\times10^{-11}\text{ N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2$ is the universal gravitational constant.

**Gravitational field**: $g = \dfrac{GM}{r^2}$ (force per unit mass at distance $r$ from mass $M$).

**Variation of $g$ with height** $h$ above the surface: $g' = g\left(\dfrac{R}{R+h}\right)^2$. **Variation of $g$ with depth** $d$ below the surface: $g' = g\left(1-\dfrac{d}{R}\right)$ — a different formula, since below the surface only the mass *inside* radius $R-d$ contributes.

**Gravitational potential energy**: $U = -\dfrac{GMm}{r}$, taking $U=0$ at $r=\infty$. Always negative for any finite $r$.

**Orbital velocity** (circular orbit, radius $r$): $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$, obtained by equating gravitational force to the required centripetal force.

**Time period of a circular orbit**: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$ — Kepler's third law, $T^2 \propto r^3$.

**Escape velocity** (minimum speed to reach $r=\infty$ with zero final kinetic energy, from the surface): $v_e = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2}\,v_{orbital,surface}$.

**Kepler's laws**: (1) planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus; (2) the line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times (equivalent to conservation of angular momentum, since gravity's pull is always radial and exerts zero torque about the Sun); (3) $T^2 \propto r^3$ for the semi-major axis $r$.
