**"Bigger halogen, bigger house — always sits in the middle."** In any interhalogen $XX'_n$, the larger halogen $X$ gets the central "house," with the smaller ones $X'$ arranged around it. Never let electronegativity fool you into placing the smaller atom centrally.

**"Small and crowded fights itself."** Fluorine and oxygen, the smallest members of their groups, pack their lone pairs too close for comfort — that is why $F_2$'s bond is weaker than $Cl_2$'s, and why $F$ and $O$ have less negative electron gain enthalpy than the element directly below them.

**"The heaviest sibling gets lazy about the last pair."** Going down groups 13-15, the heaviest common member (Tl, Pb, Bi) increasingly prefers to leave its outermost $s$-pair alone rather than drag it into bonding — the inert pair effect, strongest at the bottom of each group.
