**"Shape from VBT, shade from CFT."** Valence bond theory tells you the shape (hybridisation); crystal field theory tells you the shade (colour) and the exact magnetism. Neither one answers the other's question.

**Naming order, in one line: "Ligands (A to Z), then metal, then Roman numeral, then the outsider."** Alphabetise ligands first (ignoring prefixes like di-/tri-), name the metal next with its oxidation state in brackets, and only then name whatever counter-ion sits outside the brackets.

**"Two chairs, two tables — pack low if the gap is big."** A strong-field ligand raises the energy gap between the lower and higher $d$-orbital sets so much that pairing up in the lower set beats climbing to the higher one — low spin. A weak-field ligand barely raises the gap at all, so electrons spread out, one per orbital, before any pairing — high spin.
