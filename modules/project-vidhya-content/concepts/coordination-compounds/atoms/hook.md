Ask why $[Ni(NH_3)_6]^{2+}$ is octahedral in shape, and valence bond theory answers cleanly: nickel uses $sp^3d^2$ hybrid orbitals, six equivalent hybrids pointing to six corners of an octahedron, one ligand pair donated into each. Ask why the same ion is blue-violet, and valence bond theory has nothing more to say — it named a hybridisation, not a colour.

Ask crystal field theory the colour question instead, and it answers cleanly: the six ligands split nickel's five $d$ orbitals into two energy levels, and the gap between them happens to match the energy of visible light, so the compound absorbs one colour and shows its complement. But ask crystal field theory to derive the octahedral shape from first principles, and it cannot — it starts by assuming six ligands are already arranged octahedrally, and only then computes what happens to the orbitals. Two real theories for the same molecule, each answering a question the other was never built to answer.
