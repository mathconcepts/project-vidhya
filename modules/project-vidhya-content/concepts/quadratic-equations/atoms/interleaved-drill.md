**Cross-concept check: quadratic-equations → complex-numbers-algebra.**

**Question 1 (negative discriminant, find the complex roots):** Solve $x^2+2x+5=0$ and write the roots in $a+bi$ form.

*Answer:* $D = 4-20=-16 < 0$, so the roots are a complex-conjugate pair. Using the quadratic formula, $x = \dfrac{-2 \pm \sqrt{-16}}{2} = \dfrac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2i$. So the roots are $-1+2i$ and $-1-2i$ — a genuine complex number in $a+bi$ form, not a "no solution" answer.

**Question 2 (Vieta's formulas still hold, even for complex roots):** Verify sum and product of these two roots against $-b/a$ and $c/a$.

*Answer:* Sum: $(-1+2i)+(-1-2i) = -2$. Compare to $-b/a = -2/1=-2$. Matches. Product: $(-1+2i)(-1-2i) = (-1)^2 - (2i)^2 = 1-(-4) = 5$. Compare to $c/a=5/1=5$. Matches. Sum and product of roots work exactly the same way whether the roots are real or complex — the formulas never cared which case they were in.

**Why this drill exists:** $D<0$ is often treated as a dead end ("no real roots, question over"), when it is actually the doorway into complex numbers — the very topic this concept feeds into next. Every negative-discriminant quadratic is a fully worked complex-numbers problem in disguise.
