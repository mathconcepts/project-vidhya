A hostel allots rooms to students for the term. Every student gets exactly one room — that allotment is a **function** from students to rooms. It is well-defined precisely because no student is ever told two different room numbers.

Now compare two different hostels. In Hostel A, no two students share a room, and every room has someone in it — that allotment is **one-one and onto**: a perfect bijection between students and rooms. In Hostel B, several students are packed two-to-a-room (not one-one), and a few rooms at the end of the corridor sit empty (not onto). Same idea — "assign each student exactly one room" — but the pattern of arrows decides whether it is a bijection, and only counting the actual arrows tells you which.

The moment two different students are told the *same* room and one of them shows up to find it occupied, that is not a many-one function working correctly — it is the allotment failing to be well-defined in the first place, the same way $y^2=x$ fails to be a function until it is split in two.
