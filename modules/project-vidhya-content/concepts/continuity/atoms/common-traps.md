**Trap 1 — Stopping at "the limit exists."** A limit existing is only condition (2) of three. Forgetting to compare it against $f(a)$ — condition (3) — misses exactly the failure mode a removable discontinuity is built around.

**Trap 2 — Calling every discontinuity "removable."** Only a discontinuity with a finite, agreeing two-sided limit can be patched. A jump (one-sided limits disagree) or an infinite discontinuity (a one-sided limit is $\pm\infty$) cannot be fixed by redefining a single point, no matter what value is chosen.

**Trap 3 — Misreading what the IVT actually proves.** The IVT guarantees a value is *attained somewhere* on the interval — it says nothing about *where*, and nothing about *uniqueness*. "The IVT gives $c=1.5$" is a category error; the theorem never names a location.
