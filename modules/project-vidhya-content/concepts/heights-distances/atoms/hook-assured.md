Two vertical-plane problems look identical on the surface — two ground points, two elevation angles to the same tower top — but only one lets you use $c^2=p^2+q^2$, and the difference is a condition the mechanics alone will not warn you about. That formula needs the two ground directions to be PERPENDICULAR; two elevation angles alone never guarantee that. A tower observed from $30°$ due east and $45°$ due north satisfies it — the compass directions are given, not assumed. A tower observed from two arbitrary, unstated directions at the same two angles does not; without a right angle between the two lines of sight, $p^2+q^2$ is not even the right expression for the ground distance between them, however correct $p$ and $q$ individually are.
