Two cities report the exact same average rainfall over five years: $100$ mm. City A's yearly totals are $100,102,98,101,99$ — barely moving. City B's are $100,80,120,90,110$ — swinging wildly. A farmer choosing where to plant a rain-fed crop cannot see this difference from the average alone; both cities say "$100$ mm." What tells them apart is spread: how far, on average, each year's rainfall strays from that shared mean. That "how far, on average" idea is exactly what mean deviation, variance, and standard deviation measure — three different ways of turning "spread out" into one honest number, once you already know two datasets can share a mean and still be nothing alike.
