Take 2.4 g of magnesium ribbon. Its molar mass (the mass of one mole, in grams) is about 24 g/mol. So the moles of magnesium present are $2.4 \div 24 = 0.1$ mol — a small, exact number, worked out before touching the equation.

Now the equation: $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$. It says 2 moles of magnesium need 1 mole of oxygen. So 0.1 mol magnesium needs half of that: $0.1 \div 2 = 0.05$ mol of oxygen.

Oxygen's molar mass is 32 g/mol, so the mass of oxygen used is $0.05 \times 32 = 1.6$ g.

Check: magnesium oxide formed is also 0.1 mol (same ratio as magnesium, 2:2). Its molar mass is $24+16=40$ g/mol, so mass formed is $0.1 \times 40 = 4.0$ g. Mass in ($2.4+1.6=4.0$ g) equals mass out ($4.0$ g). The numbers agree.
