Treating "ionisation enthalpy rises across a period" as an unbroken rule is exactly the trap examiners build on — the two named exceptions are the tested part, not the general trend.

Counterexample: the raw rule predicts oxygen's first ionisation enthalpy exceeds nitrogen's, since oxygen sits to nitrogen's right. It does not — nitrogen (1402 kJ/mol) beats oxygen (1314 kJ/mol), because nitrogen's three 2p electrons sit one per orbital (half-filled stability), while oxygen must pair two into one orbital, and that repulsion makes oxygen's fourth electron easier to remove. The same reasoning, reversed direction, also puts beryllium above boron. Every period repeats the same two anomaly points at the same two places: 2s-to-2p, and half-filled-to-paired-p.
