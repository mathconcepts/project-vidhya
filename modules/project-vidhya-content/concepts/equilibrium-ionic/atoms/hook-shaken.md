Mix 0.1 mol/L acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) with 0.1 mol/L sodium acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$, which supplies the conjugate base ion $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$). Acetic acid's dissociation constant is $K_a = 1.8\times10^{-5}$.

Take $-\log_{10}$ of $K_a$: $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10}(1.8\times10^{-5}) = 4.74$.

The mixture's pH follows $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}\right)$. Here $[\text{A}^-]=[\text{HA}]=0.1$, so the ratio is $1$, and $\log_{10}(1)=0$.

So $\text{pH} = 4.74 + 0 = 4.74$.

Check: equal amounts of acid and its conjugate base always give $\text{pH}=\text{p}K_a$, whatever their common concentration actually is — it is the ratio, not the amount, that decides it. This mixture is a **buffer**, and $4.74$ is the pH it holds close to, even as small amounts of acid or base are added.
