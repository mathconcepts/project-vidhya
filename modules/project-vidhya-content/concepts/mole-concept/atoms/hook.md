A biscuit factory's recipe needs 2 kg of flour for every 500 g of sugar. Scale the batch up ten times, and every ingredient must scale by the same factor — 20 kg of flour, 5 kg of sugar — or the biscuits come out wrong.

Chemical reactions follow the same rule, except the recipe is a balanced equation and the ingredients are counted not in kilograms but in **moles** — a fixed, enormous count of particles, the same way "a dozen" is a fixed count of eggs.

Burn 2.4 g of magnesium ribbon completely in oxygen: $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$. The equation fixes the *ratio* of magnesium to oxygen to magnesium oxide — but that ratio is a ratio of moles, not of grams. Convert the mass to moles first, and the exact mass of oxygen used and product formed both drop out directly.
