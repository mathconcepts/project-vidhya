Switching from Cartesian to polar coordinates is worth it exactly when the REGION is naturally circular — a disk, a ring, a sector. The level curves of $x^2+y^2$ are concentric circles; a region bounded by one of them (say $x^2+y^2\le4$) has Cartesian bounds full of $\sqrt{\cdot}$ expressions, while in polar coordinates the same disk is simply $0\le r\le2$, $0\le\theta\le2\pi$ — the region's own natural shape becomes the bounds, instead of fighting against them.
