A diver leaving a springboard has almost no external torque acting once airborne — gravity pulls straight through the diver's own centre of mass, so it cannot twist the body at all. Tucking into a ball pulls the mass close to the spin axis, shrinking the moment of inertia sharply, and to keep angular momentum $L=I\omega$ fixed, the spin rate $\omega$ must shoot up. Straightening out again just before entering the water spreads the mass back out, growing $I$ back and slowing the spin down to something safe and controlled.

Nothing about the diver's total angular momentum ever changed mid-air — only the *shape* changed, and shape decides how that fixed $L$ splits between "spread out and slow" or "compact and fast". A rotating office chair, pulled feet in versus stretched feet out, does exactly the same trick on the ground: same idea, same law, no springboard required.
