"The ball slows down as it rises, so its speed at the top is less" sounds right but is imprecise — the *vertical* speed drops to zero at the top, while the *horizontal* speed $u_x=16$ m/s never changes at all, so the ball's overall speed at the peak is exactly $16$ m/s, not zero.

Counterexample worth remembering: launch the same $u=20$ m/s straight up instead ($\theta=90°$, so $u_x=0$, $u_y=20$). Now the speed at the top genuinely *is* zero, because there is no horizontal component left to keep it moving. The angle of launch, not the act of "going up," decides whether the peak speed is zero or something else entirely.
