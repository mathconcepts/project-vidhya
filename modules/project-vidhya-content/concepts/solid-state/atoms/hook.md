Stack oranges in a crate the "simple" way — directly on top of each other in neat rows — and there is a lot of wasted empty space between them. Nudge each higher layer into the dips of the layer below instead, and suddenly far more oranges fit into the exact same crate, with far less wasted space.

Atoms in a solid metal do exactly this. How tightly they pack together — called **packing efficiency**, the fraction of a unit cell's (the smallest repeating box of the crystal) volume actually filled by atoms rather than empty space — decides real, measurable properties: how dense the metal is, and even how easily its atoms can slide past each other. A metal packed less efficiently is measurably less dense than one packed more tightly, for the exact same atom.
