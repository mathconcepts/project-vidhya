The rigidity people quote — analytic on a disc pins down the function everywhere connected — needs the domain to actually be connected; "everywhere" only reaches where an analytic continuation exists, not automatically the whole plane. $z^{1/2}$ is complex-differentiable off a branch cut but nowhere near entire; $1/z$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, not on all of $\mathbb{C}$. One differentiable point does nothing; a whole neighborhood does everything — that gap, not "complex numbers are somehow stronger," is the actual content of the claim.
