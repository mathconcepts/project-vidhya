**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: half-life arithmetic in whole half-lives.** When the time given is a clean multiple of the half-life, skip $\lambda$ entirely — just halve the count that many times, $N=N_0(1/2)^n$ where $n$ is the number of half-lives elapsed.

- **NAT: wavelength of a named spectral line.** Given the series name and which transition (e.g. "second line of the Balmer series" means $n_1=2$, $n_2=4$), apply the Rydberg formula directly — the series name only fixes $n_1$, never $n_2$.

- **Trap: "line spectrum" questions testing the transition-vs-orbit distinction.** A stem describing "light emitted while the electron is in the $n=3$ orbit" is testing whether you know no light is emitted from sitting in an orbit at all — only from a jump between two orbits.

- **Trap: half-life word problems with "how much is left after $k$ half-lives".** JEE distractors routinely include the starting amount divided by $k$ (a straight-line guess) alongside the correct $(1/2)^k$ answer — always check whether the option matches repeated halving, not linear division.

- **Time budget:** a whole-half-lives NAT should take under $30$ seconds — count how many half-lives have passed, halve that many times, done.
