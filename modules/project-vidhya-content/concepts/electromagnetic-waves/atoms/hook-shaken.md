A capacitor is charging. Current in the wire: $2\text{ A}$. Nothing physically crosses the gap between the plates.

Take a flat surface just outside one plate: conduction current through it $= 2\text{ A}$.

Take a bulging surface that dips into the gap instead: conduction current through it $= 0\text{ A}$, since no wire is there.

Same loop, two different surfaces, two different answers — Ampere's law, as originally written, cannot be consistent here.

Maxwell's fix: count the *changing electric field* in the gap as a current too, called the **displacement current**, worth exactly $2\text{ A}$ in this example. Add it to the bulging surface's $0\text{ A}$ and both surfaces now agree: $2\text{ A}$ either way.
