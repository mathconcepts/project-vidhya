Think of a tide gauge on a harbour wall, recording water height through the day. The water rises, falls, rises again — a repeating wave, exactly the shape of $y=\sin\theta$ plotted against time.

Now mark a single horizontal line on that gauge at the "half-tide" height. The water crosses that line more than once a day: once on the way up, once on the way down, and then again tomorrow, and the day after. A harbour master asking "at what time is the water at half-tide?" is asking exactly the question a trigonometric equation asks — and the honest answer is never one timestamp, it is a repeating schedule.

That schedule is what the general solution formula writes down. The "$n\pi$" or "$2n\pi$" part says "and again every cycle after this one." The $(-1)^n$ or $\pm$ part says "once on the rise, once on the fall" — the two crossings within a single cycle that a repeating wave, unlike a straight line, always produces.
