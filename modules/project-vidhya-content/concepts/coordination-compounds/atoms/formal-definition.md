**Coordination compound**: a compound built around a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands, each attached through a coordinate (dative) bond, that stays intact as one unit whether dissolved or solid.

**Ligand**: an ion or molecule that donates an electron pair to the central metal. Monodentate (one donor atom, e.g. $NH_3$, $Cl^-$), bidentate (two donor atoms, e.g. ethylenediamine, $en$), or polydentate (several donor atoms, e.g. $EDTA^{4-}$, six donor atoms).

**Coordination number**: the number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal — usually 4 or 6.

**Werner's postulates (the core idea)**: a metal shows two kinds of valency — primary (ionisable, satisfied by anions, corresponds to oxidation state) and secondary (non-ionisable, a fixed number, satisfied by ligands, and this is what decides geometry: secondary valency 6 gives octahedral, 4 gives tetrahedral or square planar).

**IUPAC naming, the load-bearing rules**: name ligands first, in alphabetical order, before the metal; anionic ligands end in $-o$ (chloro, cyano, sulphato); neutral ligands mostly keep their molecule name, with exceptions ($H_2O$ = aqua, $NH_3$ = ammine, $CO$ = carbonyl); ligand count uses di-/tri-/tetra- for simple names or bis-/tris- for already-complex ligand names; the metal's oxidation state is written as a Roman numeral in brackets right after the metal name; an anionic complex's metal name ends in $-ate$.

**Isomerism in complexes**: ionisation isomers differ in which ion sits inside versus outside the coordination sphere; linkage isomers differ in which atom of an ambidentate ligand ($NO_2^-/ONO^-$, $SCN^-/NCS^-$) bonds to the metal; coordination isomers differ in how ligands are split between a complex cation and a complex anion in the same compound; hydrate isomers differ in whether water sits inside or outside the coordination sphere; geometrical (cis-trans) isomerism occurs in square planar $MA_2B_2$ and octahedral $MA_4B_2$ complexes; optical isomerism (non-superimposable mirror images) is common in octahedral complexes with bidentate ligands, e.g. $[Co(en)_3]^{3+}$.

**VBT hybridisation by geometry**: coordination number 4 gives $sp^3$ (tetrahedral) or $dsp^2$ (square planar); coordination number 6 gives $sp^3d^2$ (outer orbital, usually higher spin) or $d^2sp^3$ (inner orbital, usually lower spin).

**CFT splitting**: an octahedral field splits the 5 $d$ orbitals into a lower $t_{2g}$ set (3 orbitals) and a higher $e_g$ set (2 orbitals), gap $\Delta_o$. A tetrahedral field splits them into a lower $e$ set and a higher $t_2$ set, gap $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$ for the same metal and ligands — small enough that tetrahedral complexes are essentially always high spin.
