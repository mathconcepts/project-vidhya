**"P is for Position, C is for Choice."** Permutation cares about **P**osition — who goes where, in what order. Combination is just a **C**hoice — a group picked, with no ranking inside it.

**"Divide out every twin."** For each letter or item that repeats, divide the total by that repeat-count's own factorial — one division per distinct repeated item, not one division overall.

**"Together means glue; never-together means gaps."** A "must be together" restriction glues the group into one block before counting. A "must never be adjacent" restriction instead arranges everything else first, then slots the restricted items into the gaps between them — two different pictures, two different techniques, never interchangeable.

**"At least means subtract."** Whenever a question says "at least one", count the total and subtract the one case that fails the condition entirely — it is almost always the shortest path.
