The shortcut "current and voltage in an AC circuit peak together" only survives for a pure resistor. The instant a coil or a capacitor is in the circuit, that shortcut fails: a pure inductor's current peaks a quarter-cycle *after* voltage, a pure capacitor's current peaks a quarter-cycle *before* it.

Students who memorise "$90^\circ$ phase difference" without fixing which quantity leads which frequently apply it backwards on the one component that isn't a resistor. What actually decides the answer is not the size of that angle — it is which of voltage or current arrives first, and that ordering flips between an inductor and a capacitor.
