**Cross-concept check: newtons-laws → work-energy-power.**

**Question 1 (use the acceleration already found to get a speed):** For this concept's own connected-bodies system ($m_1=3$ kg on a frictionless table, $m_2=2$ kg hanging, acceleration $a=4$ m/s$^2$ found from Newton's second law), find the speed of the system after it has moved $d=2$ m from rest.

*Answer:* Using the kinematics equation $v^2=u^2+2ad$ (starting from rest, $u=0$): $v^2=0+2(4)(2)=16$, so $v=4$ m/s.

**Question 2 (check it against energy, without using Newton's second law again):** Confirm this speed using energy instead of force. Only $m_2$ has gravity doing work on it as it falls $d=2$ m ($m_1$ moves horizontally, so gravity does no work on it). Does the work done by gravity match the total kinetic energy gained by both blocks?

*Answer:* Work done by gravity on $m_2$: $W=m_2gd=2(10)(2)=40$ J. Total kinetic energy gained by both blocks together (moving at the same speed $v=4$ m/s, total mass $m_1+m_2=5$ kg): $KE=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)v^2=\tfrac{1}{2}(5)(16)=40$ J. They match exactly.

**Why this drill exists:** Newton's second law and the work-energy theorem are two different routes to the *same* answer for how fast a system ends up moving — force-and-acceleration is often faster for finding acceleration itself, while energy is often faster for finding a final speed directly, skipping acceleration altogether. Neither method replaces the other; checking one against the other is exactly what this drill just did.
