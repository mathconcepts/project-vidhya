Formula: $F=qvB\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between velocity $v$ and field $B$.

Case 1: proton's velocity is *along* the field, so $\theta=0°$. $\sin 0°=0$. $F=q\times v\times B\times 0=0$, whatever numbers $q$, $v$, $B$ actually are.

Case 2: same proton, now fired *across* the field instead, so $\theta=90°$. $\sin 90°=1$. With $q=1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$, $v=2\times10^6\ \text{m/s}$, $B=0.5\ \text{T}$: $F=1.6\times10^{-19}\times2\times10^6\times0.5\times1=1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}$.

Same charge, same speed, same field strength — only $\theta$ changed, from $0°$ to $90°$, and the force went from exactly zero to a real, nonzero number. The angle is not a detail to skip; it decides whether there is any force at all.
