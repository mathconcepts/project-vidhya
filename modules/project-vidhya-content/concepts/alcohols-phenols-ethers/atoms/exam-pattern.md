**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: rank a set of alcohols and/or substituted phenols by acidity.** Check resonance stabilisation of the ion (phenol beats alcohol) before checking substituent position (para/ortho electron-withdrawing groups beat meta ones); checking substituent identity alone, without position, gives the wrong order.

- **MCQ: identify the product of Reimer-Tiemann versus Kolbe's reaction from the reagents given.** Chloroform $+$ $\text{NaOH}$ signals $-\text{CHO}$ ortho to $-\text{OH}$; sodium phenoxide $+$ $\text{CO}_2$ signals $-\text{COOH}$ ortho to $-\text{OH}$. Match the reagent, not the memory of "some ortho product."

- **MCQ or NAT: predict the product of unsymmetrical ether cleavage by excess $\text{HI}$.** Check for a tertiary (or benzylic/allylic) carbon on either side first — its presence switches the whole mechanism to $S_N1$ at that carbon, reversing which product pairs with which.

- **Trap: assuming Williamson ether synthesis works the same regardless of which alkyl halide is chosen.** A secondary or tertiary $\text{R}'\text{X}$ invites elimination from the strongly basic alkoxide; only a primary $\text{R}'\text{X}$ reliably gives the ether.

- **Time budget:** an acidity-ranking MCQ should take under 40 seconds — check resonance first, substituent position second, done. Save full mechanism-drawing for ether cleavage questions specifically.
