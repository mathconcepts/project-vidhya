**Problem:** Acetaldehyde ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$) is treated with dilute NaOH. Name the reaction that occurs and the final product, and explain why this is not the Cannizzaro reaction.

---

**Step 1 — Check for an alpha-hydrogen first, before picking a reaction.** The carbon next to the carbonyl carbon in $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ is the $\text{CH}_3$ carbon, and it carries three hydrogens. So this molecule has alpha-hydrogens available — the enolate route is open, and Cannizzaro (which needs the *absence* of an alpha-hydrogen) is ruled out immediately, before any mechanism is drawn.

---

**Step 2 — Dilute base removes one alpha-hydrogen.** The hydroxide ion pulls off one hydrogen from the $\text{CH}_3$ group. The electron pair left behind delocalises onto the carbonyl oxygen, since it sits right next to the carbonyl group — this gives a resonance-stabilised **enolate** ion.

---

**Step 3 — The enolate attacks a second acetaldehyde molecule.** The enolate's electron pair forms a new carbon-carbon bond to the carbonyl carbon of a second, untouched $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$ molecule. As that bond forms, the second molecule's own carbonyl pi electrons shift fully onto its oxygen, turning that oxygen into an alkoxide.

---

**Step 4 — Protonation gives the aldol product.** The alkoxide picks up a proton from water, giving 3-hydroxybutanal: $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH(OH)-CH}_2\text{-CHO}$ — a beta-hydroxy aldehyde, one new carbon-carbon bond, four carbons total from two two-carbon starting molecules.

---

**Step 5 — On mild heating, the aldol loses water.** The $\text{-OH}$ and an adjacent hydrogen leave together as $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, forming a new carbon-carbon double bond conjugated with the carbonyl.

$$\boxed{\text{CH}_3\text{-CH=CH-CHO (crotonaldehyde)}}$$

**Why this is aldol, not Cannizzaro.** Cannizzaro needs a carbonyl compound with *no* alpha-hydrogen at all, so that the base has nothing to remove and attacks the carbonyl carbon directly instead. Acetaldehyde's $\text{CH}_3$ group hands the base three alpha-hydrogens to choose from, so the enolate route opens immediately and out-competes any direct attack on the carbonyl carbon. The alpha-hydrogen count is not a minor detail here — it is the one fact that rules Cannizzaro out completely before the mechanism even starts.
