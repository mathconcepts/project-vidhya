A block of mass $m=5$ kg rests on a horizontal surface where the coefficient of static friction is $\mu_s=0.4$. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. A horizontal force of $F=15$ N is applied to the block. Does the block move, and what is the friction force acting on it?

A) The block moves; friction $=20$ N.
B) The block stays at rest; friction $=15$ N.
C) The block stays at rest; friction $=20$ N.
D) The block moves; friction $=0$ N.
E) The block stays at rest; friction $=6$ N.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) The block stays at rest; friction $=15$ N.**

Normal force $N=mg=5(10)=50$ N. Maximum possible static friction: $f_{s,\max}=\mu_sN=0.4(50)=20$ N. Since the applied force ($15$ N) is *less than* $f_{s,\max}$, the block does not move, and static friction adjusts to exactly balance the applied force — friction $=15$ N, not its maximum value.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and C) both wrongly assume static friction is automatically equal to its maximum value $\mu_sN=20$ N, even though the applied force never got that large.
- D) wrongly assumes the block moves the instant any force is applied, ignoring friction's role in preventing motion until the applied force exceeds $f_{s,\max}$.
- E) $6$ N comes from an unrelated, incorrect combination of the given numbers.

</details>
