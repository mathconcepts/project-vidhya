A weather map does not label each point with one number — it attaches an arrow: wind speed and wind direction together. Walk inland from the coast and the arrows swing from northeast toward due north, shrinking as they go. That rule, one vector attached to every point of a region, is a **vector field**, unlike the temperature map beside it, which attaches only a number to each point.

Predict this before the formal definition: at two points ten kilometres apart on the same field, can the arrows point in completely opposite directions? Yes — nothing forces a vector field to vary smoothly in direction, only however the underlying flow demands.
