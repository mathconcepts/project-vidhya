**Problem:** A sugar gives a positive Fehling's test (a brick-red precipitate). Glucose or sucrose?

---

**Step 1 — State the requirement.** Fehling's test needs a free anomeric carbon that can open to expose an aldehyde group.

---

**Step 2 — Check glucose.** One anomeric carbon, free. It can open into its chain form and expose the aldehyde. Positive test expected.

---

**Step 3 — Check sucrose.** Its glycosidic bond ties up BOTH anomeric carbons — glucose's and fructose's. Zero free anomeric carbons left. No aldehyde available anywhere.

$$\boxed{\text{Glucose gives the positive test; sucrose cannot.}}$$

**Check:** count free anomeric carbons in each. Glucose: one. Sucrose: zero. Only glucose has one to give a positive result — matches the observed brick-red precipitate.
