**"All Students Take Calculus."** Going anticlockwise through the four quadrants, this sentence gives you which ratios are POSITIVE in each: quadrant I — **A**ll (sine, cosine, tangent all positive); quadrant II — **S**ine (only sine, and its reciprocal cosecant); quadrant III — **T**an (only tangent, and cotangent); quadrant IV — **C**os (only cosine, and secant). Everything else in that quadrant is negative.

**For the general-solution shape, remember which function "mirrors" and which does not**: sine and cosine each hit a value twice per period (needing $(-1)^n$ or $\pm$); tangent hits a value only once per its own shorter period (needing neither). If a problem's function repeats every $\pi$ instead of $2\pi$, it is tangent-shaped, and the formula is just $n\pi+\alpha$ — no correction term at all.

**One check that catches most alternating-sign slips**: after writing $\theta=n\pi+(-1)^n\alpha$, substitute $n=0$ and $n=1$ and confirm both give the SAME sine value as the original equation. If either fails, the sign or the reference angle is wrong — fix it before moving on, not after.
