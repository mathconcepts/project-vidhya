**Ohm's law**: $V=IR$, where $V$ is potential difference across a resistor, $I$ is current through it, and $R$ is its resistance.

**Resistivity and resistance**: $R=\rho\dfrac{L}{A}$, where $\rho$ (resistivity) is a fixed material property, $L$ is length, and $A$ is cross-sectional area.

**Series combination**: $R_s=R_1+R_2+\cdots$ — same current through every resistor.

**Parallel combination**: $\dfrac{1}{R_p}=\dfrac{1}{R_1}+\dfrac{1}{R_2}+\cdots$ — same voltage across every resistor.

**Kirchhoff's junction rule**: at any junction, $\sum I_{\text{in}}=\sum I_{\text{out}}$.

**Kirchhoff's loop rule**: around any closed loop, $\sum \Delta V = 0$. Sign convention: walking through a resistor in the assumed direction of current is a drop ($-IR$), against it is a rise ($+IR$); walking through a cell from $-$ to $+$ terminal is a rise ($+\text{emf}$), from $+$ to $-$ is a drop ($-\text{emf}$).

**Terminal voltage of a cell**: $V_{\text{terminal}}=\text{emf}-Ir$ while the cell discharges (supplies current outward), or $V_{\text{terminal}}=\text{emf}+Ir$ while it is being charged (current forced into it), where $r$ is the cell's internal resistance.

**Wheatstone bridge balance condition**: for the four arms $P,Q,R,S$ arranged so the galvanometer carries zero current, $\dfrac{P}{Q}=\dfrac{R}{S}$.
