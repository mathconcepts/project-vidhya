Two real salts, both $+2$, from neighbouring elements: $CuSO_4$ solution is blue. $ZnSO_4$ solution is colourless. Write each ion's configuration. $Cu^{2+}$: $[Ar]3d^9$. $Zn^{2+}$: $[Ar]3d^{10}$.

Count the difference: $10-9=1$. Copper's $d$ subshell is missing exactly one electron out of ten; zinc's is completely full. That one missing electron is the whole reason copper's compound has colour.

A full $d$ subshell, like zinc's, has no empty spot for an electron to jump into when light hits it, so no visible light gets absorbed and the compound looks colourless. A partly filled subshell, like copper's, has empty and filled $d$ orbitals close enough in energy that an electron CAN jump between them, absorbing one colour of visible light — what you see is the colour left over after that absorption.
