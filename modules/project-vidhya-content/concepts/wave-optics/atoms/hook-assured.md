Jumping straight to the fringe-width formula, $\beta=\lambda D/d$, without first checking path difference at a specific point skips the one question a JEE Main stem is actually built around most often: is *this particular point* bright, dark, or neither.

$\beta$ only tells you the *spacing* between successive bright fringes — it says nothing about what sits at any one named point unless that point's own path difference, $\Delta = dy/D$, is checked against $n\lambda$ or $(n+\tfrac12)\lambda$ separately. A question naming a specific $y$ is asking for $\Delta$, not $\beta$, even when both numbers happen to use the same $d$, $D$, and $\lambda$.
