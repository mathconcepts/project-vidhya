**Angle of elevation**: the angle between the horizontal line at the observer's eye and the line of sight to a point ABOVE the horizontal.

**Angle of depression**: the angle between the horizontal line at the observer's eye and the line of sight to a point BELOW the horizontal.

**Depression-elevation equality**: because the observer's horizontal at the top and the ground's horizontal are parallel lines cut by the same line of sight, the angle of depression measured from the top equals the angle of elevation measured from the point on the ground below (alternate interior angles).

**Core relation, one vertical plane**: if $h$ is the height of the object and $d$ is the horizontal distance from the observer to its foot, then $\tan\theta=h/d$, where $\theta$ is the angle of elevation.

**Core relation when the line of sight length is given**: if $\ell$ is the length of the line of sight itself (the hypotenuse) rather than the horizontal distance, then $\sin\theta=h/\ell$ instead.

**Two vertical planes (perpendicular horizontal directions)**: if the foot of the object is $O$, and two ground observation points lie along two PERPENDICULAR directions from $O$ at distances $p=h\cot\alpha$ and $q=h\cot\beta$ (where $\alpha,\beta$ are the two elevation angles), and $c$ is the straight-line ground distance between the two observation points, then $c^2=p^2+q^2=h^2(\cot^2\alpha+\cot^2\beta)$, giving $h=\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{\cot^2\alpha+\cot^2\beta}}$.
