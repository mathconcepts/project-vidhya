A lender offers to compound a 100% annual interest rate more and more often — twice a year, then monthly, then daily. Each compounding step multiplies your money by $(1+x)^{1/x}$, where $x$ is the size of the gap between compoundings, as a fraction of a year. As $x$ shrinks toward $0$, the base $(1+x)$ drifts toward $1$. Predict before tracing the curve: does the whole expression drift toward $1$ too, or does something else happen? A base heading to $1$ and an exponent racing toward infinity are pulling in opposite directions — a limit is exactly what settles which one wins.
