At a critical point $c$ with $f'(c)=0$, the second derivative test finds $f''(c)=0$ too. What should you conclude?

- **(A)** $c$ is a local maximum
- **(B)** $c$ is a local minimum
- **(C)** $c$ is definitely not an extremum
- **(D)** The test is inconclusive — check the sign of $f'$ around $c$ instead

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**. $f''(c)=0$ tells the second derivative test nothing — it is not evidence for a maximum, a minimum, or neither. $f(x)=x^3$ at $x=0$ has $f'(0)=f''(0)=0$ and is neither a max nor a min (the trail keeps climbing straight through). $f(x)=x^4$ at $x=0$ also has $f'(0)=f''(0)=0$, but *is* a minimum. The two cases are indistinguishable by the second derivative test alone — only checking whether $f'$ actually changes sign around $c$ settles it.

</details>
