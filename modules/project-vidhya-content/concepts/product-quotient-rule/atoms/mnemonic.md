**Product rule, said out loud:** "this one's derivative times that one, plus that one's derivative times this one" — $u'v + uv'$, no minus sign anywhere, and the two halves are symmetric.

**Quotient rule, remembered as Lo-D-Hi minus Hi-D-Lo, over Lo-Lo.** Call the denominator "Lo" and the numerator "Hi." The recipe is: Lo times D(Hi), minus Hi times D(Lo), all over Lo squared.

**Worked micro-example:** $y = \dfrac{x}{x+1}$. Lo $= x+1$, Hi $= x$. Lo·D(Hi) $= (x+1)\cdot 1$; Hi·D(Lo) $= x \cdot 1$. Numerator: $(x+1) - x = 1$. Denominator: $(x+1)^2$. So $y' = \dfrac{1}{(x+1)^2}$.

**Sanity-check reflex:** the quotient rule's numerator is always a *subtraction* — if your working shows a plus sign there, one of the two derivative terms has its sign flipped.
