**Path difference in Young's double-slit experiment**: for coherent sources $S_1$, $S_2$ separated by $d$, screen distance $D\gg d$, at a point $y$ above the centre line, $\Delta \approx \dfrac{dy}{D}$.

**Constructive interference (bright fringe)**: $\Delta = n\lambda$, $n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots$

**Destructive interference (dark fringe)**: $\Delta = (n+\tfrac12)\lambda$, $n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots$

**Fringe width**: the distance between two consecutive bright (or two consecutive dark) fringes, $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$ — a *consequence* of the bright/dark conditions above, not a separate idea.

**Thin-film interference**: for a film of thickness $t$, refractive index $\mu$, viewed near-normal incidence, the effective path difference is $2\mu t$, *plus* an extra $\lambda/2$ if reflection at one surface occurs at a boundary going into a denser medium (a phase reversal) and not at the other.

**Single-slit diffraction**: for a slit of width $a$, minima (dark bands) occur at $a\sin\theta = n\lambda$, $n=\pm1,\pm2,\ldots$ — note $n=0$ is excluded here; it is the middle of the bright central maximum, not a minimum.

**Resolving power**: the smallest angle two point sources can be told apart through an aperture of diameter $D$ is $\Delta\theta = \dfrac{1.22\lambda}{D}$ (the diffraction limit).

**Malus's law**: light of intensity $I_0$, already linearly polarised, passing through a polariser whose transmission axis is at angle $\theta$ to the light's own polarisation direction, emerges with intensity $I = I_0\cos^2\theta$.

**Brewster's law**: at the specific angle of incidence $\theta_B$ where $\tan\theta_B = n$ ($n$ = refractive index of the second medium relative to the first), the reflected ray is completely polarised, perpendicular to the plane of incidence.
