**Find the acute angle bisector of $L_1: 3x+4y-6=0$ and $L_2: 4x+3y-4=0$.**

$c_1=-6$, $c_2=-4$. Both negative — same sign already, nothing to flip.

$\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$. $\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$. Both normalisers are $5$.

$$\frac{3x+4y-6}{5}=\pm\frac{4x+3y-4}{5}$$

$+$ sign: $3x+4y-6=4x+3y-4 \Rightarrow x-y+2=0$.

$-$ sign: $3x+4y-6=-(4x+3y-4) \Rightarrow 7x+7y-10=0$.

Now compute $a_1a_2+b_1b_2=(3)(4)+(4)(3)=24$. Positive, and $c_1,c_2$ were already same sign, so the $-$ sign result is acute: $7x+7y-10=0$.

Check: angle between $L_1,L_2$ is $\theta\approx16.26°$. Angle between $L_1$ and $7x+7y-10=0$ is $\approx8.13°$ — exactly half. Angle between $L_1$ and $x-y+2=0$ is $\approx81.87°$ — half of the obtuse $163.74°$ instead.

**Answer: $7x+7y-10=0$.**
