Flick one end of a long rope tied to a wall, and a single hump travels down its length, reaching the wall a moment later. Watch closely, though: no single bit of rope actually travelled from your hand to the wall — each little segment of rope only ever moved up and then back down, in place. What travelled the whole distance was the *pattern* — the hump-shape itself — while the rope's own material stayed roughly where it started.

Draw this as a picture before any formula: a snapshot of the rope at one instant looks like a hump sitting somewhere along its length; a moment later, the same-shaped hump has shifted a little further along. This shifting picture is a **travelling wave**, and everything about waves — speed, wavelength, frequency — is really just a description of how fast, and how often, this repeating shape moves past a fixed point.
