**Cross-concept check: solutions-and-colligative → mole-concept.**

**Question 1 (mole fraction from moles):** A solution is made from $2\ \text{mol}$ of water and $0.5\ \text{mol}$ of a non-volatile, non-electrolyte solute. Find the mole fraction of the solute.

*Answer:* $x_{\text{solute}}=\dfrac{n_{\text{solute}}}{n_{\text{solute}}+n_{\text{solvent}}}=\dfrac{0.5}{0.5+2}=\dfrac{0.5}{2.5}=0.2$.

**Question 2 (using it in the colligative formula):** If pure water's vapour pressure at this temperature is $p^\circ=24\ \text{mmHg}$, find the solution's vapour pressure.

*Answer:* $\dfrac{p^\circ-p_s}{p^\circ}=i\,x_{\text{solute}}=1\times0.2=0.2$ (non-electrolyte, $i=1$). So $p^\circ-p_s=0.2\times24=4.8\ \text{mmHg}$, giving $p_s=24-4.8=19.2\ \text{mmHg}$.

**Why this drill exists:** every colligative-property calculation starts from a mole count — moles of solute, moles of solvent, or both — the exact bookkeeping mole-concept already teaches. Getting the mole count wrong (say, from a wrong molar mass) breaks every colligative-property answer built on top of it, however carefully the rest of the formula is applied.
