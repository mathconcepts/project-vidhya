"Cross-linking gives elasticity" is not the same claim as "cross-linking gives strength", and mixing the two up costs marks. Vulcanised rubber's cross-links give it the ability to spring back after stretching (elasticity), which is about how the network responds to being deformed and released. A far more heavily cross-linked material, like a thermosetting plastic (e.g. Bakelite), is rigid and brittle rather than elastic — its dense cross-linking prevents chains from moving relative to each other AT ALL, so instead of stretching and recoiling, it simply resists deformation and then cracks. The amount and pattern of cross-linking, not cross-linking's mere presence, decides whether the result is a bouncy elastic material or a rigid, unyielding one.
