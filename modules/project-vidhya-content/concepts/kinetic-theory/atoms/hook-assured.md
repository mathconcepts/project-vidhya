"Hot gas has faster molecules, so pressure rises" is true but incomplete — it hides that pressure depends on *two* separate things going up together: how hard each collision hits, and how often collisions happen per second, and both scale with molecular speed.

Double the temperature and the rms speed rises by only $\sqrt{2}\approx1.41$, not by a factor of 2 — yet at constant volume, pressure itself does double, because each of the two effects (harder hits, and more frequent hits) individually scales with speed, and they multiply together. Assuming pressure and rms speed scale by the same factor is a genuine trap: rms speed goes as $\sqrt{T}$, pressure at fixed volume goes as $T$ itself.
