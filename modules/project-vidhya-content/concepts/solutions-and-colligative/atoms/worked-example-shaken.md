**Problem:** $1.05\ \text{g}$ of a non-electrolyte is dissolved in $26\ \text{g}$ of benzene. The freezing point drops by $1.2\ \text{K}$. Given $K_f(\text{benzene})=5.12\ \text{K kg/mol}$, find the molar mass of the solute.

---

**Step 1 — Note $i=1$.** The solute is a non-electrolyte, so it does not split into ions or associate — $i=1$, and every formula below can drop it.

---

**Step 2 — Find molality.** $m=\dfrac{\Delta T_f}{K_f}=\dfrac{1.2}{5.12}$. Dividing: $1.2\div5.12=0.234\ \text{mol/kg}$.

---

**Step 3 — Convert $26\ \text{g}$ of benzene to kilograms.** $26\ \text{g}=0.026\ \text{kg}$.

---

**Step 4 — Multiply molality by the mass of solvent, in kilograms, to get moles of solute.** $\text{moles}=0.234\times0.026=6.09\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$.

---

**Step 5 — Divide mass of solute by moles of solute.** $M=\dfrac{1.05}{6.09\times10^{-3}}$.

$$\boxed{M \approx 172.3\ \text{g/mol}}$$

---

**Step 6 — Check.** g/mol is the correct unit for a molar mass, and $172.3$ is a plausible size for a real molecule — no units slip anywhere in the chain.
