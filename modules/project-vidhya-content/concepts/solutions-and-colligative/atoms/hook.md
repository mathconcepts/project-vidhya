On a freezing morning, salt is spread on an icy road, and the ice starts melting even though the air is still below $0^\circ\text{C}$ — plain water would stay frozen solid at that same temperature.

Salt does not "attack" ice chemically. Dissolved into the thin film of water on the ice's surface, it simply makes that water's own freezing point drop BELOW the outside temperature — so what would normally be solid ice becomes liquid again. This is a **colligative property**: a property of a solution that depends only on HOW MANY dissolved particles are present, never on what those particles actually are. Sugar dissolved in the same amount would drop the freezing point too — just by a different amount, since sugar and salt do not release the same number of particles per unit dissolved.
