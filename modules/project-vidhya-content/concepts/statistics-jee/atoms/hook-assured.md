Two cities, identical mean rainfall $100$ mm, wildly different reliability — City A's standard deviation is $\sqrt2\approx1.41$, City B's is $10\sqrt2\approx14.14$, exactly $10\times$ bigger for exactly $10\times$ bigger swings. The sharper question this sets up: standard deviation alone cannot compare two datasets with DIFFERENT means or different units — a rainfall SD of $14$ mm and a temperature SD of $14\degree$C are not "equally variable" just because the numbers match. That is precisely why coefficient of variation exists: $\text{CV}=\dfrac{\text{SD}}{\text{mean}}\times100$ strips the units away, turning spread into a percentage of the mean, which is the only fair way to compare variability across two datasets that don't share a common scale.
