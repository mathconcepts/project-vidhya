**"ZMS" for standardizing:** **Z**ero the mean (subtract $\mu$), then **S**cale by the spread (divide by $\sigma$): $z=(x-\mu)/\sigma$. Do it in that order — subtract first, divide second — and the result is always in the same universal units, however wild the original $\mu$ and $\sigma$ were.

Worked micro-example: $x=70$, $\mu=50$, $\sigma=10$. Zero the mean: $70-50=20$. Scale by spread: $20/10=2$. So $z=2$.

**Sanity-check reflex:** after standardizing, ask whether the sign of $z$ matches which side of the mean $x$ sits on — $x$ above the mean must give a positive $z$, below must give negative. A flipped sign here silently reverses which tail of the table you read.
