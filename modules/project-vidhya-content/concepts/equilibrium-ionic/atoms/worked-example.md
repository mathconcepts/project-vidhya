**Problem:** $500\ \text{mL}$ of a solution is prepared by dissolving $0.2$ mol of $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ and $0.1$ mol of $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ in it. Given $K_a(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}$, find the pH.

---

**Step 1 — Count what matters before reaching for any formula.** What is conserved here is moles of acid and moles of conjugate-base salt, both in the SAME $500\ \text{mL}$ of solution. Because both species share one volume, the volume itself cancels in a ratio — $\dfrac{n(\text{A}^-)}{n(\text{HA})}$ equals $\dfrac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$ exactly, so there is no need to convert either amount to a concentration first.

---

**Step 2 — Convert $K_a$ to $\text{p}K_a$.** $\text{p}K_a = -\log_{10}(1.8\times10^{-5}) = 4.74$.

---

**Step 3 — Apply Henderson–Hasselbalch using the mole ratio directly.** $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{n(\text{A}^-)}{n(\text{HA})}\right) = 4.74 + \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{0.1}{0.2}\right) = 4.74 + \log_{10}(0.5) = 4.74 - 0.30$.

$$\boxed{\text{pH} = 4.44}$$

---

**Step 4 — Check the direction.** There is more acid than salt ($0.2 > 0.1$), so the solution should sit slightly more acidic than $\text{p}K_a=4.74$ — and $4.44 < 4.74$, confirming the answer's direction is right before trusting the number.
