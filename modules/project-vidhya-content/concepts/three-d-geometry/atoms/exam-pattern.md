**Where this sits relative to your board syllabus.** The Tamil Nadu board's vector chapter covers scalar and vector triple products, and planes, but only in vector form. The full Cartesian machinery here — direction cosines and ratios, converting a line or plane between vector and Cartesian form, skew lines and the shortest distance between them, angle between two planes, angle between a line and a plane — is not part of that syllabus at all. Treat this whole Cartesian half as genuinely new material worth its own deliberate practice, not a quick refresher.

**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: shortest distance between two skew lines**, given in vector or Cartesian form, sometimes mixed — one line in each. Read both, convert nothing, just extract $(\vec a,\vec b)$ pairs directly.

- **NAT: distance of a point from a plane.** Pure substitution into one formula — the risk is entirely arithmetic, not concept.

- **MCQ: angle between two planes, or between a line and a plane.** The plane-normal formula (cosine) and the line-plane formula (sine) look almost identical; mixing them up is the single most common error type here.

- **Trap: skew requires two separate checks, not one.** Not parallel is necessary but not sufficient — the scalar triple product must also be nonzero.

- **Time budget:** under 90 seconds to read $(\vec a,\vec b)$ off a Cartesian line; under 150 seconds for a full shortest-distance-between-skew-lines computation, cross product included.
