**Question:** State the basicity order of methylamine, dimethylamine, and trimethylamine in the gas phase, and separately in water. Then state why the two orders differ.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Gas phase:** trimethylamine $>$ dimethylamine $>$ methylamine. Only electron donation by the methyl groups matters — no solvent to interfere.

**Water:** dimethylamine $>$ methylamine $>$ trimethylamine. The protonated amine is stabilised by hydrogen bonding through its remaining $\text{N-H}$ bonds; trimethylamine's ammonium ion has only one such bond, so it is poorly solvated and its basicity drops below the other two.

**Why they differ:** electron donation is the only effect in the gas phase, but water adds a second, competing effect — solvation of the ammonium ion through its $\text{N-H}$ bonds — that outweighs electron donation for the most heavily substituted amine.

</details>
