If $y=f^{-1}(x)$, what is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$?

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{f'(x)}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{f'(y)}$
- **(C)** $f'(y)$
- **(D)** $-\dfrac{1}{f'(y)}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. The derivative of an inverse function is expressed in terms of $y$ (the inverse function's own output), not $x$ — because $f'(y)$ measures how fast the *original* function $f$ changes at the point $y$, and the inverse function's slope is the reciprocal of that. **(A)** is the reflex mistake of plugging $x$ in instead of $y$.

</details>
