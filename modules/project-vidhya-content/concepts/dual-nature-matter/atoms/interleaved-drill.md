**Cross-concept check: wave-optics → dual-nature-matter.**

**Question 1 (wave picture vs particle picture of the same light):** A wave's intensity (brightness) is proportional to the square of its amplitude. Violet light and red light are shone on the same metal, both above the threshold frequency, with the red beam carrying far more total power. Which produces the faster photoelectrons?

*Answer:* Violet. Total power (intensity) only fixes how many photons arrive per second — it never enters $KE_{max}=hf-\phi$. Violet's higher **frequency** $f$ gives each of its photons more energy, however weak the violet beam's total power is next to the red one.

**Question 2 (frequency-wavelength relation feeds photon energy directly):** Visible light of wavelength $500$ nm travels at $c=3\times10^8$ m/s. Find its frequency, then its photon energy in eV, and check against the shortcut $E\approx1240/\lambda(\text{nm})$.

*Answer:* $f=c/\lambda=\dfrac{3\times10^8}{500\times10^{-9}}=6\times10^{14}$ Hz. Photon energy $E=hf=(6.626\times10^{-34})(6\times10^{14})\approx3.98\times10^{-19}$ J $\approx2.48$ eV. Shortcut check: $1240/500=2.48$ eV — matches exactly.

**Why this drill exists:** the wave picture's $v=f\lambda$ (speed equals frequency times wavelength) and the particle picture's $E=hf$ are not rival theories fighting over the same experiment — they describe the same light, and computing frequency from one feeds directly into the other.
