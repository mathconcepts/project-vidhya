**"Ratio Rules, Amount Doesn't."** For a buffer, only the RATIO of conjugate base to acid decides the pH — not how concentrated the mixture actually is. Equal amounts always give $\text{pH}=\text{p}K_a$, whatever number "equal" happens to be.

**Sum-of-14 pairs, only at $25^\circ\text{C}$.** $\text{pH}+\text{pOH}=14$ and $\text{p}K_a+\text{p}K_b=14$ (for a conjugate pair) are both "sum to $14$" facts — and both hold only at $25^\circ\text{C}$, since $K_w$ itself changes with temperature.

**"Common ion pushes back."** Adding a product the equilibrium already makes (a common ion) always pushes that equilibrium back towards the reactant side — less ionisation, not more. This is Le Chatelier's principle applied to one specific case.
