**Cross-concept check: coordination-compounds → d-and-f-block.**

**Question 1 (same ion, two different framings):** $Fe^{2+}$ is $3d^6$. In $d$-and-$f$-block terms, state its spin-only magnetic moment as a free ion (all 5 orbitals available, maximally unpaired). In coordination-compounds terms, state what would have to be true of the ligand field for that same free-ion spin state to survive once $Fe^{2+}$ is placed in an octahedral complex.

*Answer:* As a free ion, $Fe^{2+}$ spreads its $6$ electrons across all $5$ orbitals as far as possible: $5$ orbitals get one electron each, the $6$th pairs up, giving $n=4$ unpaired and $\mu=\sqrt{4 \times 6}=\sqrt{24}\approx4.90$ BM. For that same $n=4$ arrangement to survive inside an octahedral complex, the ligand must be weak-field — strong enough splitting (as with $CN^-$) would instead force all $6$ electrons into the lower $t_{2g}$ set, giving $n=0$.

**Question 2 (colour, traced back to its cause):** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ is pale green. Using the $d$-and-$f$-block idea that colour needs a partly filled $d$ subshell, confirm this ion is capable of colour at all, then state which coordination-compounds idea (VBT or CFT) actually computes the colour.

*Answer:* $Fe^{2+}$ is $3d^6$ — neither $d^0$ nor $d^{10}$, so a $d$-to-$d$ electron jump is possible, and colour is expected. Crystal field theory is the one that computes it: the energy gap $\Delta_o$ that $H_2O$'s field opens between the $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ sets matches a specific visible wavelength, and the colour seen is the complement of the colour absorbed. VBT would only tell you this complex is $sp^3d^2$, an outer orbital complex — a shape, not a colour.

**Why this drill exists:** d-and-f-block hands you the raw fact (a partly filled $d$ subshell); coordination-compounds is where that fact gets split into two separate, theory-specific questions — shape from VBT, colour and exact spin state from CFT — depending on which ligand is actually attached.
