**Problem:** tert-butyl methyl ether $+$ excess $\text{HI}$. Which bond breaks?

**Step 1.** The tertiary carbon can form a stable carbocation (nine hyperconjugating $\text{C-H}$ bonds); the methyl carbon, ionised the same way, would form the least stable carbocation possible.

$$\boxed{S_N1 \text{ at the tertiary carbon gives tert-butyl iodide and methanol}}$$

---

**"Excess $\text{HX}$ always cleaves via $S_N1$" overgeneralises this one result.** $S_N1$ won here specifically because a tertiary (or benzylic, or allylic) carbon was available to ionise comfortably. Cleave a symmetrical ether made of two primary or secondary alkyl groups instead — say, diethyl ether — and neither side can form a carbocation worth the trouble. There, ordinary $S_N2$ takes over: iodide simply attacks the less hindered carbon directly, exactly the pathway that lost the competition in the tertiary case above.

The mechanism is never fixed to the reagent alone. It is decided fresh each time by whether a genuinely stable carbocation is reachable from either side of that particular ether — present, $S_N1$ wins; absent, ordinary $S_N2$ takes over instead.
