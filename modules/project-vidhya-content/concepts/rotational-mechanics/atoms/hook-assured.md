"Angular momentum is conserved, so kinetic energy is conserved too" is the trap the skater example silently invites — it is false. When the skater pulls arms in, $L=I\omega$ stays fixed, but rotational kinetic energy $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$ actually **increases**: with $I_1=4,\omega_1=2$, $KE_1=\tfrac12(4)(4)=8\text{ J}$; with $I_2=2,\omega_2=4$, $KE_2=\tfrac12(2)(16)=16\text{ J}$.

The extra $8\text{ J}$ comes from the skater's own muscles doing work pulling the arms inward against the outward-flinging tendency of the spin — angular momentum conservation is a statement about zero *external torque*, and it says nothing at all about internal work done by the body itself. Conserved $L$, changed $KE$: two independent facts, not one.
