**Displacement current**: $I_d = \varepsilon_0\dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$, where $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux through the surface in question. Added to the conduction current $I_c$, the Ampere–Maxwell law becomes $\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0(I_c + I_d)$ — now the same total regardless of which surface bounds the loop.

**Transverse nature**: in a plane electromagnetic wave, the electric field $\vec{E}$, the magnetic field $\vec{B}$, and the direction of propagation are all mutually perpendicular, with $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ oscillating **in phase** with each other (both peak, and both cross zero, at the same instant).

**Speed**: $c = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} \approx 3\times10^{8}\text{ m/s}$ in vacuum, the same for every member of the spectrum. Related to frequency and wavelength by $c = f\lambda$.

**Energy transported**: average intensity $I = \dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 c E_0^2$, where $E_0$ is the peak electric field.

**Momentum transported**: for energy $U$ falling on a surface, momentum delivered is $p = U/c$ if fully absorbed, or $p = 2U/c$ if fully reflected back the way it came.

**Electromagnetic spectrum**, in order of increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength): radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. Common uses: radio — broadcasting and communication; microwaves — radar and satellite links; infrared — remote controls and thermal imaging; ultraviolet — sterilisation; X-rays — medical imaging; gamma rays — treating cancerous cells.
