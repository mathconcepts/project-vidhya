**Problem:** A sodium surface (work function $\phi=2.3$ eV, the least energy needed to free one electron) is lit by light of wavelength $400$ nm. Find (a) the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons, (b) the stopping potential, and (c) sodium's threshold wavelength.

---

**Step 1 — Find the photon's energy.** Using $E\approx\dfrac{1240}{\lambda(\text{nm})}$ eV (a rounded form of $hc/e$ that gives energy in electron volts, eV, directly): $E=\dfrac{1240}{400}=3.1$ eV.

---

**Step 2 — Apply Einstein's photoelectric equation.** $KE_{max}=E-\phi=3.1-2.3=0.8$ eV. This is the fastest a photoelectron (an electron knocked out by the light) can leave the surface.

---

**Step 3 — Convert to stopping potential.** Stopping potential $V_0$ is the reverse voltage that just halts the fastest photoelectrons, so $eV_0=KE_{max}$; numerically, $V_0=0.8$ V.

---

**Step 4 — Find the threshold wavelength.** At the **threshold frequency** $f_0$ (the lowest frequency that can eject any electron at all), the photon's whole energy equals the work function, so $\phi=\dfrac{1240}{\lambda_0(\text{nm})}$. Solving: $\lambda_0=\dfrac{1240}{2.3}\approx539$ nm.

---

**Step 5 — Check the answer makes physical sense.** $400$ nm $<539$ nm, so this light's frequency exceeds the threshold — photoemission should indeed happen, matching a positive $KE_{max}$ in Step 2.

$$\boxed{KE_{max}=0.8\text{ eV},\quad V_0=0.8\text{ V},\quad \lambda_0\approx539\text{ nm}}$$

Answer: $KE_{max}=0.8$ eV, $V_0=0.8$ V, $\lambda_0\approx539$ nm.
