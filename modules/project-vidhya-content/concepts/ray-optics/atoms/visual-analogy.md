A photographer's zoom lens is one convex lens doing exactly this job at three different distances. Walk far away from a subject, and the lens forms a small, upside-down image on the sensor — real, and easy to capture on film. Walk closer, staying beyond the focal length, and the same lens throws a *bigger* upside-down image, still real. Walk closer still, past the focal length itself (the way a magnifying glass is normally held), and there is no longer any real image on the far side at all — only a large, upright, "phantom" image that seems to sit behind the object itself, the kind an eye can look through but a sensor cannot capture directly.

The three panels below are the same convex lens, drawn to scale, for these three object positions — the ray straight through the lens's centre is undeviated in every panel, and it alone is enough to fix where the image sits and which way up it is.
