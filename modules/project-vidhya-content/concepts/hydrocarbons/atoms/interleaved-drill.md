**Cross-concept check: hydrocarbons → haloalkanes-haloarenes.**

**Question 1 (why an aryl carbon-halogen bond behaves differently):** Chlorobenzene has chlorine attached directly to an aromatic ring carbon. Its lone pairs on chlorine can delocalise into the ring, the same way an aromatic ring's own pi electrons stay delocalised around it. What does that delocalisation do to the carbon-chlorine bond?

*Answer:* It gives the carbon-chlorine bond partial double-bond character — the lone pair spends part of its time shared into the ring instead of sitting entirely on chlorine. A partial double bond is shorter and stronger than an ordinary single bond, which is exactly why that bond is much harder to break than the carbon-halogen bond in an ordinary alkyl halide.

**Question 2 (predict the consequence for reactivity):** Given that the bond is shorter and stronger, would you expect chlorobenzene to undergo nucleophilic substitution as readily as an alkyl chloride does?

*Answer:* No — much less readily. Nucleophilic substitution needs that carbon-halogen bond to break; a bond made stronger by resonance delocalisation resists breaking, so aryl halides are far less reactive toward nucleophilic substitution than alkyl halides with an otherwise similar structure.

**Why this drill exists:** the same delocalisation reasoning that decides whether a ring is aromatic here also decides, in the very next concept, why a halogen sitting on an aromatic ring behaves nothing like the same halogen sitting on a plain alkyl chain.
