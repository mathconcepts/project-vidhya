$\theta=90°$ gives the *maximum* force, $F=qvB$ — but "maximum force" does not mean "the field speeds the charge up." $\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B$ is a cross product, so the force is always perpendicular to $\vec v$ itself, at every instant, for every angle that gives a nonzero force.

A force perpendicular to velocity can only bend the path — it can never have a component along the direction of motion, so it does zero work and never changes the charge's speed, only its direction. This is why a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field moves in a circle at constant speed, not a spiral that speeds up or slows down: the magnetic force is doing work of exactly zero, always, on the moving charge.
