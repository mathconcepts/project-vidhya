**Frozen Variable First.** Before differentiating for $\partial f/\partial x$, mentally paint every OTHER variable as frozen ice — only $x$ is allowed to move. $\partial/\partial x\,(x^3y^2)$: treat $y^2$ as a constant multiplier, giving $3x^2y^2$.

**Worked micro-example.** $\partial/\partial x\,(x^3y^2)=3x^2y^2$; at $(1,2)$: $3(1)(4)=12$.

**Sanity-check reflex.** Count the variables in the function. A function of two variables needs two partials — $\partial f/\partial x$ AND $\partial f/\partial y$ — before the job of finding the gradient is actually done.
