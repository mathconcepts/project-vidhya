**Work done by a constant force**: $W = \vec F \cdot \vec d = Fd\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the force $\vec F$ and displacement $\vec d$. Work is measured in joules ($\text{J}$).

**Work done by a variable force**: $W = \displaystyle\int_{x_1}^{x_2} F(x)\,dx$ — the area under the force-versus-displacement graph, between the start and end positions.

**Kinetic energy**: $KE = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

**Work-energy theorem**: $W_{net} = \Delta KE = KE_{final} - KE_{initial}$, where $W_{net}$ is the total work done by *every* force acting on the object.

**Potential energy** (gravitational, near Earth's surface): $PE = mgh$, where $h$ is height above a chosen reference level.

**Conservation of mechanical energy**: $KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f$, valid **only** when the net work done by non-conservative forces (friction, air drag, an applied push) is zero.

**Elastic collision**: both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. **Inelastic collision**: momentum is conserved, kinetic energy is not (some converts to heat, sound, or deformation). **Perfectly inelastic collision**: the colliding bodies stick together and move with a common final velocity.

**Power**: $P_{avg} = \dfrac{W}{t}$ (average power); $P = \vec F \cdot \vec v = Fv\cos\theta$ (instantaneous power). Power is measured in watts ($\text{W}$, $1\text{ W} = 1\text{ J/s}$).
