- **Classifying a copolymer as condensation just because it has two different monomers**: Buna-S and Buna-N are copolymers of two different monomers, both formed by addition polymerisation, since neither pair of monomers releases anything when they join. Monomer count is not the test — check whether a small molecule leaves at each bond.

- **Confusing elasticity with strength when describing vulcanisation**: cross-linking from vulcanisation gives rubber the ability to stretch and recoil (elasticity). A far more heavily cross-linked material like Bakelite is rigid and brittle instead — more cross-linking does not automatically mean "more elastic"; past a point it means "less able to move at all".

- **Assuming natural polymers are always addition polymers**: proteins (via peptide bonds) and starch or cellulose (via glycosidic linkages) are natural CONDENSATION polymers, releasing water at each bond formed, exactly like nylon does. "Natural" and "addition" are unrelated facts.

- **Treating "commercially important" polymer names as interchangeable with their monomers**: knowing "nylon-6,6" is a name is not the same as knowing it comes from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid specifically — a question naming the polymer expects the monomer pair to be recalled directly, not guessed from the name alone.
