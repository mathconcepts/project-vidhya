Supply frequency: $\omega = 100\text{ rad/s}$. One full cycle: $T = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi/100 \approx 0.063\text{ s}$.

A pure inductor's voltage peaks a quarter-cycle in: $t_1 = T/4 \approx 0.0157\text{ s}$.

Its current peaks a further quarter-cycle after that: $t_2 = t_1 + T/4 \approx 0.0314\text{ s}$.

Gap between the two peaks: $t_2 - t_1 = T/4 \approx 0.0157\text{ s}$ — exactly one quarter of the full cycle, no more, no less.

This quarter-cycle gap is the **phase difference** between voltage and current for a pure inductor: current is always $90^\circ$ behind voltage, every single cycle, however fast or slow the supply runs.
