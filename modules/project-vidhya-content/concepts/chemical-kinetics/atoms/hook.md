A painkiller tablet puts a dose of medicine into the bloodstream. Doctors need to know exactly how fast the body clears it, so the next dose is timed correctly — too soon, and the drug builds up to a harmful level; too late, and pain returns before the next dose.

Measuring the concentration every so often shows a clear pattern: it drops by roughly HALF every fixed stretch of time, whatever the concentration happens to be at that moment — the same fixed time takes it from a high value down to half of it, and later takes it from a much smaller value down to half of THAT. This constant "halving time" is the signature of **first-order kinetics** — and the number that decides how fast a reaction runs, in general, is called its **rate**.
