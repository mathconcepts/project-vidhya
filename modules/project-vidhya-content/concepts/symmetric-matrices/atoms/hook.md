Push sixteen arrows through the matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$ and most of them swing off to a new angle. But flip $A$ across its own diagonal, swapping rows for columns, and you get $A$ right back — that is what "symmetric" means. For a symmetric matrix, the two arrows that refuse to turn always land exactly 90° apart, guaranteed by that flip-and-match property, never a coincidence. Watch that right angle survive the push.
