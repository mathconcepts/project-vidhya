A ship's navigator does not describe a destination as "go $4$ km east and $3$ km north." Instead, the navigator gives a **distance and a bearing**: "go $5$ km, on a compass heading of $53°$ from north." Same destination, described two completely different ways.

The Argand plane offers exactly this same choice for any complex number. Writing $z=a+bi$ is the "$4$ km east, $3$ km north" description — the Cartesian form. Writing $z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$ is the "$5$ km, bearing $53°$" description — the **polar form**, using distance ($|z|$) and angle ($\arg z$) instead. Neither is more "correct" than the other; Cartesian form is easier for adding complex numbers (just add the coordinates, like adding east-north offsets), while polar form is by far the easier one for multiplying, exactly because multiplying two navigational instructions by combining a distance-scale-factor with an angle-turn is a far more natural operation than combining two pairs of east-north numbers.
