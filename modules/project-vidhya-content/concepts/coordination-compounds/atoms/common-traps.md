- **Assuming any $MA_2B_2$ complex must show cis-trans isomerism**: the memorised rule holds for square planar $MA_2B_2$, but a TETRAHEDRAL $MA_2B_2$ complex shows no cis-trans isomerism at all — every position around a tetrahedron is equally adjacent to every other, so there is no "opposite corner" for a trans arrangement to exist. Check the geometry before applying the rule, not just the ligand pattern.

- **Confusing ambidentate with polydentate ligands**: an ambidentate ligand ($NO_2^-$, $SCN^-$) binds through only ONE of two possible donor atoms at a time, producing linkage isomers. A polydentate ligand (ethylenediamine, $EDTA^{4-}$) binds through SEVERAL donor atoms simultaneously, on the same metal, in the same molecule. "Two possible binding atoms" and "many binding atoms at once" are different ideas, not two names for the same thing.

- **Using the plain element or molecule name instead of the ligand name**: writing "ammonia" or "water" inside a complex's name instead of "ammine" or "aqua" is an automatic naming error — these neutral ligands have special names that do not match their free-molecule names.

- **Alphabetising by the multiplying prefix, not the ligand name**: "dichlorido" is alphabetised under "c" (chlorido), not "d" (di-), when deciding ligand order in a name. Ignoring this can place ligands in the wrong order even when every other part of the name is correct.

- **Assuming optical activity is guaranteed wherever bidentate ligands appear**: $cis$-$[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+$ is optically active, but its own geometrical isomer, $trans$-$[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+$, is not — the trans form has a plane of symmetry that the cis form lacks. Bidentate ligands make optical activity *possible*, not automatic; the actual test is whether the specific geometrical isomer has a plane of symmetry at all.
