Count every electron pair around oxygen in water, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, using its Lewis structure.

Total valence electrons available: oxygen contributes 6, each hydrogen contributes 1, giving $6+1+1=8$ electrons in total.

Two O–H bonds use 2 shared pairs — 4 of those 8 electrons. The remaining 4 electrons sit entirely on oxygen, forming 2 **lone pairs** (a lone pair is 2 electrons not shared with any other atom).

So oxygen ends up surrounded by 4 electron pairs in total: 2 bonding pairs (the O–H bonds) and 2 lone pairs. **VSEPR** spreads all 4 pairs as far apart as possible in space (roughly tetrahedrally), but only the 2 bonded hydrogen atoms are actually drawn into the final shape — the 2 lone pairs stay invisible on paper while still squeezing the two O–H bonds together, giving a bent molecule, not a straight one.
