Take one monomer: ethylene, $\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2$. It has a double bond.

That double bond opens up, and one ethylene links directly to the next, over and over. Nothing is released along the way. Count the atoms in the monomer and in one repeat unit of the final chain — they match exactly. This is **addition polymerisation**, and polythene is the result.

Now take two different monomers instead: a diamine and a diacid. Each has a reactive end group. They react at each step, and every single time they do, one water molecule is released.

Count what's released this time: one water molecule per bond formed, all the way along the growing chain. This is **condensation polymerisation**, and nylon-6,6 is the result.

Same question both times — is anything lost when the chain forms? — with two opposite, concrete answers.
