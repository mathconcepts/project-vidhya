If bond strength depended only on how close two atoms sit to each other, fluorine's F-F bond should be the strongest bond among the halogens — its atoms are the smallest, so they should pack in the tightest. The real numbers say otherwise.

Real bond dissociation energies, in kJ/mol: F-F is 159, Cl-Cl is 242, Br-Br is 193, I-I is 151. Chlorine's bond beats fluorine's by more than 80 kJ/mol, even though chlorine's atoms are noticeably bigger. Something about being small is working against fluorine here, not for it — and finding out what forces three lone pairs on two tiny neighbouring atoms to actively fight each other is where this concept starts.
