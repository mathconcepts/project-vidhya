**Problem:** Arrange beryllium, boron and carbon in order of increasing first ionisation enthalpy, and identify the one pair that does not follow the naive left-to-right rule.

---

**Step 1 — Start with the naive rule.** Be, B and C sit left to right in Period 2, so the naive guess is $IE(Be) < IE(B) < IE(C)$.

---

**Step 2 — Write out each electron configuration.** Be: $1s^22s^2$. B: $1s^22s^22p^1$. C: $1s^22s^22p^2$.

---

**Step 3 — Compare Be against B specifically.** Be's outer subshell $2s^2$ is completely filled — extra stable. B's newest electron sits alone in $2p$, at higher energy and only lightly shielded by the $2s^2$ pair underneath it. So B's outer electron is actually easier to remove than Be's, not harder.

---

**Step 4 — Confirm against real values (kJ/mol).** Be = 899, B = 801, C = 1086. Indeed $801 < 899 < 1086$ — B is lowest, not Be.

---

**Step 5 — State the corrected order.** $\boxed{IE(B) < IE(Be) < IE(C)}$ — the naive rule holds for Be-to-C and B-to-C, but flips exactly at Be-to-B, because that one step crosses from a filled $2s$ subshell into a new, higher-energy $2p$ subshell.
