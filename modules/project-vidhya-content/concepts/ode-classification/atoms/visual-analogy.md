Think of order as **how many photographs you need to fix one path in a family of curves** — a first-order equation's solution family is pinned down by a single snapshot $y(x_0)=y_0$ (one photo: where you are), while a second-order family needs two (where you are, and which direction you're currently moving). This is why order equals the count of arbitrary constants in the general solution: each constant is exactly one more "photograph" the initial conditions must supply before a unique member of the family is selected.

Degree and linearity don't fit this same picture at all — they're properties of the equation's *shape on the page*, not of the solution family's size. That's the honest reason this concept resists a single plot: order describes a family of curves, while degree and linearity describe the algebra that generates them, and there's no one honest 2-D animation that shows all three at once without misleading about what each one actually measures. A worked classification (see the worked example) is the more truthful way to see the distinction in action.
