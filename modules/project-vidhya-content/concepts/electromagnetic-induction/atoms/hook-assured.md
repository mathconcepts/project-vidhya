The shortcut students reach for under time pressure — "induced current opposes the field, so point the induced field opposite to the given field" — fails the instant the given field is *decreasing*, exactly like a fading $0.6\text{ T}\to 0.2\text{ T}$ field through a coil.

Lenz's law opposes the **change**, not the field's instantaneous direction. A decreasing field gets an induced current that reinforces it, not one that cancels it. The shortcut only happens to give the right answer when the field is increasing — which is why it survives unnoticed until an exam question quietly flips that assumption.
