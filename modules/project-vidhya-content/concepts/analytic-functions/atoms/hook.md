Know a real function on a tiny interval and you know almost nothing about it anywhere else. Know that a complex function is analytic on a tiny disc, and you have pinned it down across its entire connected domain. That rigidity — differentiable once in the complex sense forces infinitely differentiable — is why analytic functions get a theory of their own. Below, watch $w=z^2$ trace the image of a point circling the origin once — the same doubling rule holds at every point on the loop, all at once.
