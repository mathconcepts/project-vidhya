**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: "find the distance/speed" with friction, no time given.** The moment a question gives distance or speed but never asks about time, reach for the work-energy theorem first — it skips the acceleration step kinematics needs.

- **MCQ: "which of these is conserved" for a given collision.** Momentum is conserved in *every* collision (elastic or not); kinetic energy is conserved *only* in an elastic collision. A perfectly inelastic collision conserves momentum but always loses some kinetic energy — check which quantity the question actually names before answering.

- **Trap: variable-force work problems disguised as constant-force ones.** If a question gives $F$ as a function of $x$ (a spring, for instance) and a distractor option uses plain $W=Fd$ with one force value, that option is built for students who forgot the force changes — the real answer needs the area under the F-x graph (an integral).

- **Trap: "mechanical energy is conserved" assumed by default.** Any mention of friction, air resistance, or an external push in the problem statement means mechanical energy conservation does *not* apply — only the work-energy theorem does.

- **Time budget:** a work-energy-theorem NAT with one force and no time dependence should take under 45 seconds — write $W_{net}=\Delta KE$, plug in, solve for the one unknown. There is no need to find acceleration or time at all.
