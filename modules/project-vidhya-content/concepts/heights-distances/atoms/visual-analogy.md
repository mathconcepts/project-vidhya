Think of angle of elevation like tilting your neck back to watch a kite climbing. For a kite at a FIXED height, the closer you stand to directly beneath it, the more steeply your neck has to tilt — the angle grows. Stand farther away for that same height, and your neck barely tilts at all — the angle shrinks. Tangent is just a number that captures exactly how much "tilt" a given height-to-distance pair produces, nothing more mysterious than that.

Now picture walking towards the kite's launch point, the way the worked example does with the tower. As you get closer, the same fixed height demands more and more tilt, until directly underneath, your neck would have to tilt all the way to $90°$ — straight up. Two measurements of that tilt, taken from two known distances, is enough information to pin down a height you could never measure with a tape, precisely because tangent turns "how much I had to tilt" into a number you can do algebra with.
