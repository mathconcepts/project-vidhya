Take one solid disc, mass $M=2\text{ kg}$, radius $R=0.3\text{ m}$, spinning about the axis through its own centre.

Its moment of inertia there: $I_{cm} = \tfrac12 MR^2 = \tfrac12(2)(0.3)^2 = 0.09\text{ kg·m}^2$.

Now shift the axis to the disc's rim, a distance $d=R=0.3\text{ m}$ away from the centre.

Parallel axis theorem: $I = I_{cm} + Md^2 = 0.09 + (2)(0.3)^2 = 0.09 + 0.18 = 0.27\text{ kg·m}^2$.

Moving the axis away from the centre made the disc harder to spin — $0.27 > 0.09$, always an increase, never a decrease.

Now check angular momentum. If nothing external pushes or twists this disc, $L=I\omega$ stays fixed even while it changes shape or axis in a problem — but the moment torque from friction or a support touches it, $L$ is free to change.
