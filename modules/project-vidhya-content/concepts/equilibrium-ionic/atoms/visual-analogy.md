A car's shock absorber does not stop the wheel from feeling every bump in the road — it cushions the jolt, so the cabin moves far less than the wheel does. Hit a big enough pothole, though, and the shock absorber bottoms out; past that point, the cabin feels the jolt almost directly.

A buffer plays the exact same role for pH. Adding acid or base to a buffered solution still moves its pH — the buffer just cushions that move, holding it to a small fraction of what the same addition would cause in plain water. And exactly like the shock absorber, a buffer has a limit: add enough acid to use up all of its conjugate base, and the cushioning stops. Past that point, the pH swings freely, just like an unbuffered solution.
