Take a velocity of magnitude $u=10$ m/s at $\theta=30°$ from the horizontal, where $\sin30°=0.5$ and $\cos30°=0.866$.

Resolve it: $u_x=u\cos\theta=10\times0.866=8.66$ m/s. $u_y=u\sin\theta=10\times0.5=5$ m/s.

Check the split makes sense: the horizontal piece ($8.66$) is bigger than the vertical piece ($5$), which matches a shallow $30°$ angle — most of the motion should be sideways, only a little of it upward. The two pieces are found completely separately, using only $u$ and $\theta$, before either one is used in any further equation.

Now take uniform circular motion at speed $v=6$ m/s around a circle of radius $r=2$ m. The centripetal acceleration is $a_c=v^2/r=36/2=18$ m/s$^2$, and this acceleration always points from the object straight toward the centre — not forward along the circle, even though the object itself is moving forward the whole time.
