A hydraulic car lift has a small piston of area $0.005\text{ m}^2$ ($50\text{ cm}^2$) and a large piston of area $0.5\text{ m}^2$ ($5000\text{ cm}^2$) — one hundred times bigger. Pressing down with just $100\text{ N}$ on the small piston creates a pressure of $100/0.005 = 20{,}000\text{ Pa}$ (pascals, the SI unit of pressure — force per unit area).

**Pascal's law** says this pressure is transmitted *undiminished* to every point in the confined fluid, including the large piston. So the large piston feels the same $20{,}000\text{ Pa}$, over its much bigger area: force $= 20{,}000 \times 0.5 = 10{,}000\text{ N}$ — enough to lift a $1000\text{ kg}$ car (taking $g=10\text{ m/s}^2$) with barely more effort than pushing a door open. **Properties of matter** covers this and three other big ideas — how solids stretch, how surfaces pull themselves tight, and how heat moves through materials — each with its own governing rule.
