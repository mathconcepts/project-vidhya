Two slits, $0.5\text{ mm}$ apart, screen $1\text{ m}$ away, wavelength $500\text{ nm}$. Point $P$ sits $2\text{ mm}$ above the centre line.

Path difference at $P$: $\Delta = \dfrac{dy}{D} = \dfrac{(0.5\times10^{-3})(2\times10^{-3})}{1} = 1\times10^{-6}\text{ m} = 1000\text{ nm}$.

Divide by the wavelength: $1000/500 = 2$ — a whole number.

Whole number of wavelengths means the two waves arrive crest-to-crest: bright fringe.

Had that division come out $1.5$ instead, the waves would arrive crest-against-trough: dark fringe. The **fringe width** formula, which spaces these bright and dark points evenly apart, only makes sense once this whole-number-versus-half-integer check is understood first.
