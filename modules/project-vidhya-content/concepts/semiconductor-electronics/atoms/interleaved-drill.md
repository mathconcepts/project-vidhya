**Cross-concept check: atoms-and-nuclei → semiconductor-electronics.**

**Question 1 (one sharp number vs one edge of a range):** Hydrogen's ionization energy (the energy needed to remove its electron entirely) is exactly $13.6$ eV — one sharp number. Silicon's band gap is "about $1.1$ eV" — the *edge* of a whole band, not one sharp electron energy. Why does one concept give a single exact value and the other give a boundary?

*Answer:* A lone hydrogen atom has discrete, well-separated energy levels, so removing its one electron crosses one exact gap: $13.6$ eV, no range about it. A silicon crystal's conduction band is millions of extremely closely-packed levels merged into a continuous range; $1.1$ eV names only where that whole range *begins*, not a single electron's exact jump.

**Question 2 (same photon formula, two very different answers):** Find the photon wavelength needed for (a) ionizing a hydrogen atom, (b) just barely lifting an electron across silicon's band gap.

*Answer:* (a) $\lambda=1240/13.6\approx91$ nm — deep ultraviolet. (b) $\lambda=1240/1.1\approx1127$ nm — near-infrared. Same formula, $\lambda=1240/E$, but a more than $12$-times difference in required energy gives wavelengths in two completely different parts of the spectrum.

**Why this drill exists:** both problems are "how much energy does it take to free an electron", but atoms-and-nuclei asks it of one isolated atom's exact levels, while semiconductor-electronics asks it of a whole crystal's merged bands — same question, two very different kinds of answer.
