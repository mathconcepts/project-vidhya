**Problem:** A satellite orbits at $r=2R$ from Earth's centre ($R=6400\text{ km}$, $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ at the surface). Find its orbital speed and time period.

---

**Step 0 — Set up before any formula.** A stable circular orbit, gravity alone providing the centripetal force. Frame: Earth's centre as origin, outward is positive. Only magnitudes are needed here.

---

**Step 1 — Write the balance.** Gravity supplies the centripetal force: $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

---

**Step 2 — Rearrange for speed, before touching any numbers.** Cancel one $m$ and one $r$: $v^2=\dfrac{GM}{r} \Rightarrow v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.

---

**Step 3 — Replace $GM$.** At the surface, $g=GM/R^2$, so $GM=gR^2$. Now $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{gR^2}{r}}$.

---

**Step 4 — Substitute $r=2R$ and the numbers.** $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{gR}{2}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{9.8\times6400000}{2}}=\sqrt{31360000}=5600\text{ m/s}$.

---

**Step 5 — Find $T$.** $T=\dfrac{2\pi r}{v}=\dfrac{2\pi\times12800000}{5600}\approx14362\text{ s}$.

---

**Step 6 — Sanity-check the period.** Convert: $14362/3600\approx3.99\text{ hours}$, under four hours — a reasonable low-orbit period, much shorter than the Moon's month-long orbit far higher up. $\boxed{v=5600\text{ m/s},\ T\approx3.99\text{ hours}}$.
