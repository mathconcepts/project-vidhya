The rule "partly filled $d$ subshell gives colour" is assumed known — the mark-costing distinction is that BOTH $d^0$ and $d^{10}$ are colourless, not just $d^{10}$, and the same metal can sit on either side depending only on its oxidation state.

Counterexample: $Cu^+$ has configuration $3d^{10}$ and is colourless — solid $CuCl$ is white. $Cu^{2+}$ has configuration $3d^9$ and is blue. Same element, one oxidation-state step apart, and colour appears only once the $d$ subshell stops being completely full. "This is a $d$-block metal, so its compounds are coloured" is false whenever the specific ion in question happens to land on $d^0$ or $d^{10}$ — $Sc^{3+}$ ($d^0$) and $Zn^{2+}$ ($d^{10}$) are both colourless for exactly this reason, on opposite ends of the same subshell.
