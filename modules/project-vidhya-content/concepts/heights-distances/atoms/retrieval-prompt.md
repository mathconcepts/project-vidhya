**Recall**: A man standing on top of a cliff of height $h$ metres observes a boat at an angle of depression $\theta$. What is the horizontal distance from the base of the cliff to the boat, in terms of $h$ and $\theta$ — and why is the angle of depression usable directly in a formula built around the angle of elevation?

<!-- answer -->

**Answer**: $d=h\cot\theta=\dfrac{h}{\tan\theta}$. The angle of depression from the top, by the alternate-angle equality, exactly equals the angle of elevation the boat would measure looking up at the cliff-top — so the same right triangle, and the same $\tan\theta=h/d$ relation, applies with $\theta$ plugged in directly, no extra conversion needed.
