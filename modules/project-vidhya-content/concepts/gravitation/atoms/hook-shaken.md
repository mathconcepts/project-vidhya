Take $R=6400\text{ km}$ (Earth's radius) and $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ at the surface.

A satellite orbits at $r=2R$. Orbital speed there: $v=\sqrt{gR^2/r} = \sqrt{(9.8)(6400000)^2/(12800000)} = 5600\text{ m/s}$.

Now move it to $r=4R$, twice as far. New speed: $v=\sqrt{gR^2/(4R)}=\sqrt{gR/4}\approx3960\text{ m/s}$.

$3960 < 5600$ — the satellite is **slower** at the higher orbit, not faster.

This is **gravitation**: an orbiting satellite that goes higher settles into a *slower* stable speed, even though climbing there in the first place used up real energy.
