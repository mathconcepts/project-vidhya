**"RST" for equivalence relations.** Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive — say all three letters out loud before ticking any box. Missing even one and it is not an equivalence relation, no matter how "obviously fair" it looks.

**"One in, one out, no exceptions" for functions.** If you can find even one input with two outputs, or one input with zero outputs, it is not a function — full stop.

**One-one vs onto, remembered by what goes wrong.** One-one breaks when two different inputs *collide* into the same output — think of two students crashing into one hostel room. Onto breaks when some output is left *empty* — a room nobody was ever assigned to. Collision breaks one-one; an empty seat breaks onto. Bijective means neither failure happens: no collisions, no empty seats.

**Composition domain, remembered as "the inner function's output must fit the outer function's door".** Before writing $f(g(x))$, check that $g(x)$ can actually walk through $f$'s door — that it lands inside $f$'s domain. If it can't, that $x$ is not allowed in, no matter how clean the algebra of $f(g(x))$ looks afterward.
