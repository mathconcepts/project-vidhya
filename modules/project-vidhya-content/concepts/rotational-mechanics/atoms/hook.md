An ice skater spinning with arms stretched out has a **moment of inertia** (the rotational version of mass — how much an object resists a change in spin) of $I_1 = 4\text{ kg·m}^2$ and spins at $\omega_1 = 2\text{ rad/s}$. Pulling the arms in tight drops that moment of inertia to $I_2 = 2\text{ kg·m}^2$ — with no external push, no motor, nothing touching the ice differently.

**Angular momentum** $L = I\omega$ stays fixed at $L = 4 \times 2 = 8\text{ kg·m}^2\text{/s}$ throughout, since no outside torque acts on the skater. So the new spin rate must satisfy $8 = 2 \times \omega_2$, giving $\omega_2 = 4\text{ rad/s}$ — the skater spins twice as fast purely by changing shape. That is **conservation of angular momentum**, the rotational sibling of conservation of linear momentum, and it works whenever net external torque is zero.
