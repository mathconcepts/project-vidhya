The inverse-square drop is nothing new by this point. Here's the part that quietly changes the exponent: "$F \propto 1/r^2$" is true for **point charges** (or spheres, measured centre to centre) — that same power of $r$ does not automatically survive once a charge distribution has size or shape.

For an electric dipole, the force on a distant test charge falls off faster than $1/r^2$ once you're far enough away that the two opposite charges nearly cancel. Treating a dipole like a single point charge and applying $F \propto 1/r^2$ directly gives the wrong power of $r$ — the correct field decays as $1/r^3$ at large distance along the axis.
