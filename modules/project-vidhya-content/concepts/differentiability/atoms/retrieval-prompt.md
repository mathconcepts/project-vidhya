Before checking, try to recall from memory: is "differentiable $\Rightarrow$ continuous" true, and is its converse ("continuous $\Rightarrow$ differentiable") also true?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The first is true — differentiability always implies continuity. The converse is **false**: $f(x)=|x|$ is continuous at $x=0$ but not differentiable there. Continuity is necessary for differentiability, never sufficient.</details>
