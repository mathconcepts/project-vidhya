Draw two small fields before naming either operator. First, $\mathbf F=(x,y)$: arrows point straight outward from the origin, longer further out — water spraying from a point. Second, $\mathbf G=(-y,x)$: arrows circle counterclockwise, all the same length on any circle centred at the origin — water swirling with no net outflow.

Drop a tiny balloon at a point in each field. In $\mathbf F$, the balloon inflates — more is leaving its neighbourhood than entering. That inflating-or-shrinking measurement is **divergence**. Drop a tiny paddle wheel instead, in $\mathbf G$: it spins, even though nothing is piling up or draining away nearby. That spin is **curl**.

$$\operatorname{div}\mathbf F=2,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\mathbf G=2$$

Neither operator can substitute for the other: $\mathbf F$ has divergence but no spin ($\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F=0$), and $\mathbf G$ spins but never inflates ($\operatorname{div}\mathbf G=0$). A field is free to do both, either, or neither at a given point — the two questions, source-or-sink and spin-or-still, are genuinely independent of each other.
