Two facts explain the whole formula. Fact one: sine and cosine repeat every $2\pi$. So if one angle works, adding a full turn, $2n\pi$, gives another one. That is the "$n\pi$" part.

Fact two: $\sin(150°)=\sin(30°)$, because $150°=180°-30°$. Check it: both equal $0.5$. So $30°$ and $150°$ both solve $\sin\theta=0.5$. Cosine mirrors differently: $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, so cosine's formula uses $\pm\alpha$ instead of an alternating sign.

That alternating sign, $(-1)^n$, only switches the formula between the reference angle and its mirror image. Put $n=0$: you get $\alpha$. Put $n=1$: you get $\pi-\alpha$, the mirror. Nothing more than that.
