Knowing $\dfrac{1}{v}-\dfrac{1}{u}=\dfrac{1}{f}$ by heart is not the same as applying its sign convention correctly under pressure. The shortcut that fails: treating a mirror's magnification formula, $m=-v/u$, as if it also applied to a lens, where it is $m=+v/u$ — no minus sign at all.

Same numbers, wrong formula, wrong sign on the answer: a $20\text{ cm}$-focal-length lens with $u=-30\text{ cm}$ gives $m=-2$ correctly by $m=v/u$, but the mirror formula would wrongly flip that to $+2$ — upright instead of inverted, the one detail an exam distractor is built to exploit.
