"Carbocations always rearrange" is a dangerous over-generalisation — a shift happens only when the destination carbon ends up genuinely *more* stable than the one currently holding the charge, never automatically.

Counterexample: if the carbon next to a secondary carbocation is only **primary** (joined to just one other carbon), no shift happens. Moving the charge there would trade a secondary carbocation for a *less* stable primary one — a step nothing in the molecule favours. The reaction proceeds straight from the original secondary cation instead.

Compare the stabilities of the current carbon and its neighbour — via hyperconjugation and the inductive effect — before assuming any migration at all.
