**Bohr energy levels** (hydrogen-like atom, atomic number $Z$): $E_n=-\dfrac{13.6\,Z^2}{n^2}$ eV, $n=1,2,3,\dots$

**Bohr radius**: $r_n=\dfrac{0.529\,n^2}{Z}$ angstrom (one angstrom $=10^{-10}$ m).

**Rydberg formula** for a spectral line: $\dfrac{1}{\lambda}=R\left(\dfrac{1}{n_1^2}-\dfrac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$, $n_2>n_1$, where $R=1.097\times10^7\text{ m}^{-1}$ is the Rydberg constant and $\lambda$ is the emitted wavelength.

**Nuclear radius**: $R=R_0A^{1/3}$, where $A$ is the **mass number** (protons plus neutrons together) and $R_0\approx1.2$ fm ($1$ fm $=10^{-15}$ m).

**Mass-energy equivalence**: $E=\Delta m\,c^2$, with $1$ **u** (atomic mass unit) $=931.5$ MeV/$c^2$.

**Binding energy**: the energy needed to break a nucleus completely apart into separate protons and neutrons, $BE=[Zm_p+(A-Z)m_n-M]c^2$, where $M$ is the nucleus's actual mass — always slightly less than the sum of its separate parts (the **mass defect**).

**Radioactive decay law**: $N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$, where $N_0$ is the number of undecayed nuclei at $t=0$ and $\lambda$ is the **decay constant**.

**Half-life**: $T_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln 2}{\lambda}\approx\dfrac{0.693}{\lambda}$ — the time for exactly half the sample to decay, however much is left at the start.

**Activity**: $A=\lambda N$, the number of decays happening per second.
