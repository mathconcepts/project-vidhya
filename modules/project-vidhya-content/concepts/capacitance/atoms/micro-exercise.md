Two capacitors, $C_1=6\ \mu\text{F}$ and $C_2=3\ \mu\text{F}$, are connected in **series**. What is the equivalent capacitance?

A) $9\ \mu\text{F}$
B) $2\ \mu\text{F}$
C) $18\ \mu\text{F}$
D) $4.5\ \mu\text{F}$
E) $3\ \mu\text{F}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $2\ \mu\text{F}$.**

$\dfrac{1}{C_s}=\dfrac{1}{C_1}+\dfrac{1}{C_2}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $C_s=2\ \mu\text{F}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $9\ \mu\text{F}$ is $C_1+C_2$ — the **parallel** formula, applied to a series combination (or the resistor-series rule, applied where it doesn't belong).
- C) $18\ \mu\text{F}$ is $C_1\times C_2$ with the "over the sum" part of the series shortcut forgotten.
- D) $4.5\ \mu\text{F}$ is the plain average $(C_1+C_2)/2$, which has no physical meaning here.
- E) $3\ \mu\text{F}$ wrongly assumes the series combination just takes the smaller of the two values.

</details>
