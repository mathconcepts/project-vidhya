**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: half-life or rate constant from first-order data.** Given $k$ (or a half-life) and two concentrations, or a fraction remaining, apply $t=\dfrac{2.303}{k}\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{[A]_0}{[A]}\right)$ directly — no need to derive the integrated rate law from the differential form under time pressure.

- **MCQ: order vs. molecularity, or order vs. stoichiometric coefficient.** The moment a question quotes a balanced equation's coefficients and asks for "the order," treat that as a trap unless it explicitly says the reaction is a single elementary step — order is stated or measured, never assumed from the equation.

- **Trap: half-life dependence on concentration.** A question stating "half-life stays the same as concentration falls" is describing first order specifically — reusing that fact for a zero-order or second-order reaction gives a wrong numeric answer.

- **Trap: forgetting the stoichiometric coefficient when relating rates of different species.** For $2A\rightarrow B$, the rate of consumption of $A$ is twice the rate of formation of $B$ — a question asking for "the rate of the reaction" from a given species' rate needs that factor applied.

- **Time budget:** a first-order half-life or rate-constant NAT should take under $40$ seconds — one substitution into $t_{1/2}=0.693/k$ (or its rearrangement), nothing more.
