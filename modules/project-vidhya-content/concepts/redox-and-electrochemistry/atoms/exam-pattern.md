**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: cell potential from the Nernst equation.** Given $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ and concentrations that are not $1\ \text{M}$, compute $E_{\text{cell}}$ directly using $E_{\text{cell}}=E^\circ_{\text{cell}}-\dfrac{0.0591}{n}\log_{10}Q$ at $298\ \text{K}$ — the constant $0.0591$ is worth memorising outright, since deriving it from $2.303RT/F$ under time pressure wastes seconds better spent elsewhere.

- **MCQ: galvanic vs. electrolytic electrode polarity.** The moment a question says "external battery" or "electrolysis," the anode/cathode polarity flips relative to a plain galvanic cell — read for that one word before assigning any sign.

- **Trap: forgetting solids and pure liquids in $Q$.** A reaction quotient $Q$ only includes dissolved ions and gases; a solid electrode's own "concentration" never appears in it.

- **Trap: mismatched $n$ between the half-reactions and the overall cell reaction.** Multiply a half-reaction to balance electrons before combining it — but $E^\circ$ values themselves are NEVER multiplied, only the $n$ used afterward in $\Delta G^\circ=-nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ or the Nernst equation changes.

- **Time budget:** a direct Nernst NAT should take under $60$ seconds once $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ and $n$ are identified — the arithmetic is a single logarithm and one subtraction.
