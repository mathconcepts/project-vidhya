**Problem:** A proton moves at $v=2\times10^6\ \text{m/s}$ along $+x$. Field $B=0.5\ \text{T}$ points along $+y$. Find the force's magnitude and direction, and what changes if $\vec B$ instead points along $+x$.

---

**Step 1 — Identify the angle between $\vec v$ and $\vec B$.** $\vec v$ is along $+x$, $\vec B$ is along $+y$. These two directions are $90°$ apart.

---

**Step 2 — Plug into the magnitude formula.** $F=qvB\sin\theta=(1.6\times10^{-19})(2\times10^6)(0.5)\sin90°$.

---

**Step 3 — Compute.** $\sin90°=1$, so $F=1.6\times10^{-13}\times1$.

$$\boxed{F = 1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}}$$

---

**Step 4 — Find the direction: apply the right-hand rule from $\vec v$ toward $\vec B$.** From $+x$ curling towards $+y$ gives a thumb direction of $+z$.

$$\boxed{\vec F \text{ points along } +z \text{ (positive charge)}}$$

---

**Step 5 — Now redo with $\vec B$ along $+x$ instead.** $\vec v$ and $\vec B$ are now both along $+x$ — the same direction, so $\theta=0°$.

---

**Step 6 — Compute this new case.** $F=qvB\sin0°=qvB\times0=0$.

$$\boxed{F = 0}$$

---

**Step 7 — Compare.** Same charge, same speed, same field strength in both cases — only the angle changed, and the force went from a real nonzero value to exactly zero.
