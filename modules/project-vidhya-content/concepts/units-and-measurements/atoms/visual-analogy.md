A tailor's measuring tape is marked only in centimetres — no finer lines at all. Every length the tailor reads off it is honestly good only to the nearest centimetre, whatever the tailor's eyesight. If the tailor stitches three pieces of cloth end to end, each measured on that same tape, the total length's uncertainty is the *sum* of the three individual uncertainties — errors from separate measurements add when the measurements themselves are added.

Now suppose the tailor instead needs the *area* of a single rectangular piece, multiplying a measured length by a measured breadth. Here the two individual uncertainties do not simply add; each one contributes as a *fraction* of its own measurement, and those fractions add. A tape that is honest to a whole centimetre out of a $30$ cm length carries a much smaller fractional uncertainty than the same one-centimetre gap out of a $3$ cm breadth — so the shorter measurement, not the longer one, ends up dominating how trustworthy the final area actually is.
