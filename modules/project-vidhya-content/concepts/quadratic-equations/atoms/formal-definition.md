**Quadratic equation**: $ax^2+bx+c=0$, with $a,b,c$ real and $a \ne 0$.

**Discriminant**: $D = b^2-4ac$.

**Nature of roots** (for real $a,b,c$): $D>0$ gives two real, distinct roots; $D=0$ gives one real root repeated twice; $D<0$ gives two complex-conjugate roots (no real roots).

**Sum and product of roots**: for roots $\alpha,\beta$ of $ax^2+bx+c=0$, $\alpha+\beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}$ and $\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a}$.

**Forming an equation from its roots**: $x^2 - (\alpha+\beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$.

**Common root condition**: two quadratics $a_1x^2+b_1x+c_1=0$ and $a_2x^2+b_2x+c_2=0$ share exactly one root when $(c_1a_2-c_2a_1)^2 = (a_1b_2-a_2b_1)(b_1c_2-b_2c_1)$.

**Quadratic inequality**: for $a>0$, $ax^2+bx+c$ is negative *between* the real roots and positive *outside* them; for $a<0$, the sign flips.

**Location of roots relative to a number $k$**: for both roots to lie on one side of $k$ (for a quadratic with $a>0$), three conditions must all hold together: $D \ge 0$ (real roots exist), $a \cdot f(k) > 0$ (the curve is on the correct side at $k$), and the vertex $x=-b/2a$ lies on the same side of $k$ as the roots.
