Think of the gap between a capacitor's two plates as a hill with a steady, even slope from one plate to the other — never a cliff on one side and flat ground on the other. Walking from the positive plate towards the negative plate, the "height" (electric potential) drops by equal amounts for equal steps, because the electric field pushing you down the slope is the same strength everywhere between the plates.

The line on this card traces exactly that: potential falling steadily and evenly from one plate (left) to the other (right), a straight line rather than a curve. This uniform drop is what "uniform field between the plates" actually means in a picture — the field strength itself never changes as you cross the gap, so the potential can only fall at a constant rate.
