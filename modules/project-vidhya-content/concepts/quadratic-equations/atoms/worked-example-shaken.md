**Problem:** Find all $k$ for which $2x^2+kx-5=0$ and $x^2-3x-4=0$ have a common root.

---

**Step 1 — Solve the equation with no $k$ in it.** $x^2-3x-4=0$. Two numbers multiplying to $-4$, adding to $-3$: $-4$ and $1$. So $x^2-3x-4=(x-4)(x+1)$, giving roots $x=4$ and $x=-1$.

---

**Step 2 — The common root must be one of these two.** No third possibility — write both cases down first.

---

**Step 3 — Case $x=4$.** Substitute into $2x^2+kx-5=0$: $2(16)+4k-5=0 \Rightarrow 32+4k-5=0 \Rightarrow 4k=-27 \Rightarrow k=-\dfrac{27}{4}$.

---

**Step 4 — Case $x=-1$.** Substitute: $2(1)-k-5=0 \Rightarrow 2-k-5=0 \Rightarrow -k-3=0 \Rightarrow k=-3$.

---

**Step 5 — Both are valid.** $\boxed{k=-3 \text{ or } k=-\dfrac{27}{4}}$. Check $k=-3$: the second equation becomes $2x^2-3x-5=0=(2x-5)(x+1)$ — root $x=-1$ is present, confirming the common root.
