A quadratic form is just a formula like $Q(x,y)=5x^2+4xy+2y^2$ — no plain $x$ or $y$ term, only squares and a cross term — packed into the symmetric matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}5&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}$ from the hook. Its *level curve* — trace every $(x,y)$ that gives $Q$ one fixed value — turns out to be exactly the ellipse the hook already drew. See why below.

Positive eigenvalues everywhere keep $Q$ from ever going negative: a closed ellipse (an ellipsoid, in higher dimensions) — the level-curve picture of a *positive definite* form. Flip the sign of even one eigenvalue and $Q$ turns positive in some directions, negative in others — an open saddle shape instead of a closed bowl, since nothing stops it dropping below zero anymore.
