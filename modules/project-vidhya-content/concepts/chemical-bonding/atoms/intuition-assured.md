MO theory's real payoff over VSEPR and hybridisation: it predicts **magnetism**, and it can explain a molecule VSEPR and hybridisation get equally, deceptively "right" together.

Take $\text{O}_2$. VSEPR (trivially, a diatomic molecule) and a simple Lewis dot structure both suggest a normal double bond with all electrons paired — nothing there predicts anything unusual. But $\text{O}_2$'s bond order via MO theory, $\dfrac{8-4}{2}=2$, comes with 2 electrons left genuinely *unpaired* in its antibonding $\pi^*$ orbitals. This is not a detail MO theory adds for completeness — it is the *only* one of the three theories that correctly predicts liquid oxygen is attracted to a magnet (paramagnetic), a real, measurable property that a plain Lewis structure's "all electrons paired" picture flatly contradicts.

VSEPR and hybridisation being self-consistent with each other is not evidence that the bonding picture is complete — bond order and unpaired-electron count need MO theory specifically, every time.
