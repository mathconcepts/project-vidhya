A car drives the first half of a $100$ km trip at $60$ km/h and the second half at $40$ km/h. What was its average speed for the whole trip? The tempting answer is $(60+40)/2 = 50$ km/h — but that is wrong, and the reason is worth pinning down exactly. Average speed over EQUAL DISTANCES is not the arithmetic mean of the two speeds; it is their harmonic mean, $\dfrac{2\times60\times40}{60+40} = 48$ km/h. The car spends MORE time at the slower speed, since covering the same distance takes longer, so the true average leans toward $40$, not sits exactly between the two. Every progression you meet in this concept — arithmetic, geometric, harmonic — has its own mean, and mixing them up costs marks in exactly this way.
