"More hydrogens in the molecule means more hyperconjugation" overreaches badly. Only the carbon-hydrogen bonds sitting on carbons **directly attached** to the charged (or radical) centre can line up with its empty orbital and donate into it — a $\text{C-H}$ bond two carbons further away has no geometric path to reach it at all.

Counterexample: in $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH}_2\text{-CH}_2^+$ (a primary cation with a long tail), the three $\text{C-H}$ bonds on the far methyl group contribute nothing — they sit two carbons removed from the positive centre. Only the two $\text{C-H}$ bonds on the carbon immediately next to the charge count, leaving this cation barely more stabilised than a plain primary one despite the extra chain sitting right there, visible but irrelevant to the count.
