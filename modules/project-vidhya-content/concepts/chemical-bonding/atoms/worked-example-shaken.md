**Problem:** Using molecular orbital theory, find the bond order of the nitrogen molecule, $\text{N}_2$, given that it has 10 bonding electrons and 4 antibonding electrons. State whether this matches a single, double, or triple bond.

---

**Step 1 — Write down the two given numbers.** Bonding electrons $=10$. Antibonding electrons $=4$.

---

**Step 2 — Subtract antibonding from bonding.** $10 - 4 = 6$.

---

**Step 3 — Divide by 2.** $6 \div 2 = 3$.

$$\boxed{\text{Bond order} = 3}$$

---

**Step 4 — Match the number to a bond type.** Bond order $1 \to$ single bond. Bond order $2 \to$ double bond. Bond order $3 \to$ triple bond. Here, bond order $3$ means $\text{N}_2$ has a triple bond, $\text{N}\equiv\text{N}$.

**Check:** a triple bond should be the strongest, shortest kind — and $\text{N}_2$ is indeed known for being very hard to break apart, which fits a triple bond, not a weaker single or double one.
