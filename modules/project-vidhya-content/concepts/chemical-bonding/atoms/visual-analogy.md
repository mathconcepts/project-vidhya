Tie several balloons together by their necks at one central knot. Left alone, the balloons push each other apart and spread out as evenly as space allows — two balloons end up opposite each other in a straight line, three spread out flat at $120°$ apart, four arrange themselves into a tetrahedron.

This is exactly what **VSEPR** predicts for electron pairs around a central atom: each pair (bonding or lone) behaves like one balloon, pushing every other pair as far away as possible, purely because same-charge electron clouds repel each other. Four electron pairs on oxygen in water settle into that same tetrahedral spread — but only two of those four "balloons" have a hydrogen atom tied to their tip. The molecule's drawn shape shows only the balloons with something visible attached, which is why water's shape reads as bent even though the underlying electron-pair arrangement is roughly tetrahedral, not bent at all.

A 3D shape like this cannot be honestly reduced to a flat, two-variable animated curve, so no scene is attached here — the balloon picture above, held in the mind, does the real work.
