**Set-up:** a ball is launched from the ground at $u=20$ m/s, at an angle where $\sin\theta=0.6$ and $\cos\theta=0.8$ (a common JEE $37°$-style angle). Take the launch point as origin, horizontal (direction of travel) as the positive $x$-axis, and vertical (upward) as the positive $y$-axis — this **frame of reference and sign convention** is fixed before any formula appears.

The trick that makes this problem tractable: split $u$ into a horizontal piece $u_x=u\cos\theta=20(0.8)=16$ m/s and a vertical piece $u_y=u\sin\theta=20(0.6)=12$ m/s. Horizontally, nothing pushes or pulls the ball — $u_x$ stays $16$ m/s the whole flight. Vertically, gravity acts exactly as it did in one dimension, with $a_y=-10$ m/s$^2$. Two independent one-dimensional problems, running on the same clock — that is all two-dimensional motion under gravity ever is.
