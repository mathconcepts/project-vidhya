**Cross-concept check: haloalkanes-haloarenes → alcohols-phenols-ethers.**

**Question 1 (predicting the product of a substitution reaction):** 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, a tertiary halide, is heated with water (a weak nucleophile, no base present). By the mechanism this concept establishes for a tertiary substrate under these conditions, what functional group does the product carry, and how did it get there?

*Answer:* The bromide leaves on its own first, forming a stable tertiary carbocation (stabilised by hyperconjugation and the inductive effect from three neighbouring methyl groups). A water molecule then attacks that carbocation, and after losing a proton, the oxygen becomes an $-\text{OH}$ group. The product is 2-methylpropan-2-ol, a tertiary alcohol.

**Question 2 (connecting the mechanism to the next concept's central question):** Given that this alcohol's $-\text{OH}$-bearing carbon is tertiary (bonded to three other carbons, with no hydrogen on it), predict whether it can be oxidised by a strong oxidising agent the way a primary alcohol can.

*Answer:* No. Oxidation at this carbon needs a hydrogen attached to the carbon bearing $-\text{OH}$ to remove, and a tertiary carbon has none — its other three bonds are all to carbon. This alcohol resists oxidation entirely, unlike a primary or secondary alcohol.

**Why this drill exists:** the very carbocation mechanism used here to predict a substitution product also determines the structure of the alcohol formed — and that structure, in turn, decides everything about how the next concept's oxidation reactions behave on it.
