# Concept Anchors + the content-rendering agenda (2026-09-18)

`/investigate` + `/design-review` on two asks:

1. "The content for e.g. Trace of a matrix cannot explain intuitively,
   connecting theoretical concepts to practical application for
   understanding. Do research for every subtopic and regenerate intuitive
   content."
2. "Be aware of the constraints (lack of space on screen, poor attention
   span of students, poor competency, ELI5 preference) and run a detailed
   agenda for effective content rendering. The goal is any person of any
   competency shall immediately grasp the concept."

Root-caused before any fix, per the skill's Iron Law. Both asks turned out
to share one structural cause.

---

## 1. Root cause

### The symptom is real and it is not an authoring lapse

Read `trace`'s eleven atoms end to end and a student learns: trace is the
diagonal sum; it equals the sum of eigenvalues; it survives a change of
basis; `tr(AB) = tr(BA)`; here is how GATE asks it. Every one of those
sentences is *about matrices*. Nothing anywhere says what a trace is **for**
— what job it does in a system somebody actually built.

That is not one author's miss. It is the shape of the pipeline:

| Question a learner asks | Atom type that answers it |
|---|---|
| What is it? | `formal_definition` |
| What does it feel like? | `hook`, `intuition`, `visual_analogy` |
| How do I compute it? | `worked_example`, `micro_exercise` |
| What goes wrong? | `common_traps` |
| How do I remember it? | `mnemonic` |
| How will the exam ask it? | `exam_pattern` |
| How do I keep it? | `retrieval_prompt`, `interleaved_drill` |
| **What is it FOR?** | **— nothing —** |

`AtomType` (`src/content/content-types.ts:167`) has exactly eleven members
and no twelfth.

### Every framing registry is exam-framed by construction

Four per-concept prose registries exist. None carries a real-world
application string, and the gap is systematic rather than incidental:

| Registry | Path | Covers | Rendered? | Frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain points | `src/registry/pain-points.ts` + `data/registry/pain-points/*.yml` | 24 concepts | No — `review_status: draft`, steers nothing | student pain |
| Atomic catalogue → DPS | `data/curriculum/gate-em/atomic-catalogue.json` → `frontend/src/generated/intent-slices.gen.ts` → `ProblemStatementBlock.tsx` | ~39 concepts | **Yes** | `gate_examination_intent` + `primary_pain_point` (the latter **module-level** — all 26 LA atoms share one identical string) |
| Attention hypothesis | `docs/content-spec/atomic-content-structure-map.csv` → `resonance-strategy.ts` | 100/116 atomic ids | No — prompt-time, hook/intuition only | attention design |
| `ConceptNode.description` | `data/curriculum/gate-ma.yml` | 82 concepts | No — builds an embedding query only | terse index line |

The smoking gun is in the Pedagogy Pattern Library:
`ped_property_first` already carries the directive *"End with a one-line
'Why it matters in GATE' callout."* The platform has a "why it matters"
concept — and it is scoped to the exam.

### Measured, not assumed

Grepping all 101 concepts for any real-world signal (`in practice`,
`real-world`, `engineers use`, `PageRank`, `signal processing`,
`machine learning`, `circuit`, …) returns **18 concepts**. Reading the
matched lines, nearly every one is the figure of speech *"in practice"*
meaning *"in computational practice"*:

> "Row reduction dominates **in practice** — $O(n^3)$ …"
> "**In practice**, for rational $X(z)$, we use partial fractions …"

Genuine real-world bridges: **about two** (`conformal-mapping`'s airfoil
lift/drag line, `z-transform`'s signal-processing mention).

**Root cause:** the pipeline has no slot — no atom type, no registry field,
no prompt directive, no gate — for "what this is for in the world," so
authors had nowhere to put it and no check noticed it was missing.

### One concrete content bug found in the reported concept

`trace/atoms/visual-analogy.md` claims trace answers:

> "By how much does this transformation expand or shrink the volume of a
> tiny box, overall?"

That is the **determinant**, not the trace. For the concept's own matrix
$A=\begin{pmatrix}5&1\\2&4\end{pmatrix}$: $\det A = 18$ (Wolfram-verified),
$\operatorname{tr} A = 9$. The follow-on justification — "you're pumping the
same total volume expansion through in a chain, so $\operatorname{tr}(AB) =
\operatorname{tr}(BA)$" — is a fabricated mechanism; volume reasoning gives
$\det(AB)=\det(BA)$, which is a different theorem. The atom's `gif-scene`
plots `sqrt(x*x + 4*x*x)` $= \sqrt5\,|x|$, a V-shape with no relationship to
trace at all.

Trace *is* related to volume, but as a **rate**: $\frac{d}{dt}\det(e^{tA})
\big|_{t=0} = \operatorname{tr}(A)$. "How much does A scale a box" is 18;
"how fast does the flow generated by A grow volume" is 9.

## 2. The rendering constraints, measured

The second ask names four constraints. Here is what the corpus actually
costs against them (measured with the repo's own `countTotalReadingLoad`,
base atoms only — what one student is served, excluding stance variants):

```
concepts                      101
cards per concept            11.0
reading load per concept    1,752 words   =  8.8 min @200wpm
TOTAL corpus              176,931 words   = 14.7 hours @200wpm

heaviest:  euler-hamilton   3,040 w (15.2 min)
           shortest-paths   3,038 w (15.2 min)
           trees            2,789 w (13.9 min)
lightest:  multiple-integrals 1,135 w
```

Two gates are blind to part of this:

- **Beat text is invisible to the prose budget.** `countProseWords` strips
  fenced blocks, but since resonance beats a hook's `narration_steps[].text`
  — what the student reads while the scene plays — lives inside that fence.
  Worst undercount: `ode-bernoulli/hook-shaken.md`, gate sees 27 words,
  real load 252 (**9.3x**).
- **`common_traps` has no budget at all.** No `ASSURED_PROSE_BUDGET` entry,
  no `stances:` guidance block, no density check. Average 146 words;
  **18 of 101 exceed 220** (every *other* type's widest ceiling); worst is
  `symmetric-matrices` at **406**. This is the atom type `pedagogy-engine`
  force-injects after three consecutive wrong answers — i.e. it is longest
  exactly when the student is most overloaded.

### The trap this creates for ask #1

The obvious fix for "no practical application" is "add a practical
application atom." That would make it **12 cards and 9.5 minutes** per
concept. It makes every single named constraint worse. **Adding content is
not available as a fix here.** Whatever closes the gap has to cost
approximately zero cards and approximately zero words.

## 3. The agenda

Six rules, ordered by how much they move the stated goal ("any person of
any competency shall immediately grasp"). R1 is built in this pass; R2–R6
are the standing doctrine and are scoped below.

### R1 — One non-mathematical sentence, first, before any math

A low-competency student decides whether to engage in the first few
seconds, and the first thing they currently meet is mathematics. Give the
concept an **anchor**: one plain sentence, at the top of the first card,
naming something real the maths does a job inside.

Cost: ≤30 words × 101 concepts = ≤3,030 words = **+1.7%** reading load, and
**zero new cards**. This is the only budget-positive way to close ask #1.

### R2 — Never a new card when a line will do

Card count is the binding constraint on attention, not word count. Any
future "students also need X" is answered inside an existing card until
someone proves a line cannot carry it. Eleven cards is already past what a
tired student finishes.

### R3 — Budget what you force on a struggling student

`common_traps` gets a real ceiling before it gets more content. An atom
that appears *because* the student just failed three times must be the
shortest thing on the page, not the longest. (Editorial ceiling decision
still open — see §5.)

### R4 — Measure the load the student actually carries

The prose-budget gate must count beat text. A gate that reads 27 words on a
252-word atom is not protecting anything. `countTotalReadingLoad` already
exists and is already report-only; the work is promoting it.

### R5 — Personalise register, not facts

The platform already serves `shaken` / `assured` stance variants. The anchor
does **not** get variants: a sentence clear enough for the least confident
reader is not worse for the most confident one, and 202 strings cost twice
what 101 do while doubling drift risk. Personalise *how* something is said
where it already happens (stance prose, pacing); keep *what is true* single-
sourced.

### R6 — One home per kind of fact

Four framing registries already exist and none of them holds this fact.
A fifth is only justified because "what it is for" is a genuinely new *kind*
of fact, per-concept, covering all 101 — not because the existing four are
inconvenient. The anchor is never duplicated into catalogue copy or atom
prose; that is the "parallel truths that drift" bug this repo keeps paying
for (v4.25.0's model-id drift, and others since).

## 4. The Concept Anchor contract (v1)

Locked. Future shape changes ship as `v: 2`, never by mutating this.

```yaml
version: 1
concepts:
  trace:
    anchor: "A positive diagonal sum means a vibrating structure's wobble
      grows instead of dying out — one glance rules a design out before
      anyone computes a single frequency."
  ode-classification:
    anchor: null
    reason: "A classification skill, not a thing built into a system —
      no honest real-world artifact to point at."
```

**Rules every anchor must pass:**

1. **≤ 30 words.** Hard cap, gate-enforced.
2. **Names something concrete** — a bridge, an MRI scanner, a recommender,
   a route planner. Never "engineering", "science", "many fields".
3. **Carries the bridge, not a name-drop.** "Eigenvalues are used in
   PageRank" is a name-drop. What the maths *does* inside the system is the
   whole point — that is precisely the "connecting theoretical concepts to
   practical application" the report asked for.
4. **No mathematical notation.** No LaTeX, no symbols. This is the one
   surface in the corpus that must be readable with zero prerequisites.
5. **No exam framing.** No GATE, no marks, no "commonly asked". Those are
   `exam_pattern`'s and the DPS's job. This slot is the only non-exam thing
   on the page; diluting it back to exam framing recreates
   `ped_property_first`'s exact failure.
6. **True, and verified.** Mathematical claims checked (Wolfram/SymPy);
   application claims standard and checkable. No invented products, no
   invented company behaviour. A concept with no honest anchor takes
   `null` + `reason` — the honest-absence discipline used throughout this
   repo, never a fabricated application.

**Worked example of the contract biting.** A first draft for `trace` read
"tells engineers instantly whether vibrations die down or grow." That
overstates in one direction: $\operatorname{tr}(A) > 0$ forces at least one
eigenvalue with positive real part (unstable), but a negative trace does
**not** guarantee stability. The shipped sentence claims only the direction
that is true.

## 5. Scope

**Built in this pass:** the contract above; a per-concept registry covering
all 101; codegen to the client; the renderer (one line, top of the hook
card, no new card); a CI coverage + contract gate; anchors authored for all
101 concepts; the `trace` visual-analogy bug fixed.

**Named, not done** (each needs an editorial decision this pass should not
make unilaterally):

- **R3's ceiling for `common_traps`.** Requires deciding what a trap atom
  *should* cost. The measurement is in hand (146 avg, 18 over 220, 406
  worst); the number is a call for whoever owns content standards.
- **R4's promotion of the beat-aware count to a blocking gate.** Same
  shape: the function exists and is honest, the ceiling is an editorial
  call, and turning it on today fails 255 atoms at once.
- The remaining ~99 `visual_analogy` atoms are **not** audited here for
  errors of the class found in `trace`. One confirmed instance is not
  evidence of a pattern, but it is not evidence of its absence either; a
  corpus sweep is tracked separately.
