An electron ($q=1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$) moves at $v=4\times10^6\ \text{m/s}$, at an angle of $30°$ to a magnetic field of magnitude $B=0.25\ \text{T}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on it?

A) $1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}$
B) $8.0\times10^{-14}\ \text{N}$
C) $1.4\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}$
D) $0\ \text{N}$
E) $1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{N}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $8.0\times10^{-14}\ \text{N}$.**

$F=qvB\sin\theta=(1.6\times10^{-19})(4\times10^6)(0.25)\sin30°=(1.6\times10^{-19})(4\times10^6)(0.25)(0.5)=8.0\times10^{-14}\ \text{N}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}$ comes from using $\sin\theta=1$ — treating the field as perpendicular to the velocity when it's actually at $30°$.
- C) $1.4\times10^{-13}\ \text{N}$ comes from using $\cos30°\approx0.866$ instead of $\sin30°=0.5$.
- D) $0\ \text{N}$ wrongly treats a small-looking angle as if it were $0°$ (parallel) — the force is zero only exactly at $0°$ or $180°$, not merely at a small nonzero angle.
- E) $1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{N}$ forgets to multiply by $v$ and $B$ at all, leaving only the charge itself.

</details>
