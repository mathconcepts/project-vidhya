Light of wavelength $248$ nm falls on a zinc surface, whose work function (the least energy needed to free one electron) is $\phi=4.3$ eV. What is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons?

A) $0.7$ eV
B) $5.0$ eV
C) $4.3$ eV
D) $9.3$ eV
E) No photoelectrons are emitted

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $0.7$ eV.**

Photon energy: $E=\dfrac{1240}{248}=5.0$ eV. Einstein's photoelectric equation: $KE_{max}=E-\phi=5.0-4.3=0.7$ eV.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) $5.0$ eV is the *photon's* energy, not what is left over after freeing the electron — the work function was never subtracted.
- C) $4.3$ eV is just the work function itself, restated without using the light at all.
- D) $9.3$ eV comes from *adding* the work function instead of subtracting it — a sign error in Einstein's equation.
- E) is wrong because $E=5.0$ eV $>\phi=4.3$ eV, so this light is well above zinc's threshold — photoemission does happen.

</details>
