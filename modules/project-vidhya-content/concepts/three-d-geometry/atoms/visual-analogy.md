Picture two metal pipes bolted to the ceiling of a workshop, running off at different tilts, neither one parallel to the other, and set at different heights so they never actually cross. You are asked: what is the shortest steel rod you could weld between them, if the rod has to touch both pipes?

You would not measure along either pipe's own length — sliding a rod along one pipe's direction never gets you closer to the other pipe. The rod has to run perpendicular to *both* pipes at once, like a single crossbar wedged in exactly one orientation between them. Once you find that one direction, the rod's length is the actual shortest distance — everything else, any rod at any other angle, is longer.

That crossbar direction is $\vec b_1\times\vec b_2$, and the rod's length is exactly the shortest-distance formula.
