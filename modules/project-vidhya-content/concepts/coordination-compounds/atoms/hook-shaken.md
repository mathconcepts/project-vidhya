Take one real ion: $[Ni(NH_3)_6]^{2+}$. Fact 1: it is octahedral in shape. Fact 2: it is blue-violet in colour. Two separate facts, two separate theories.

Valence bond theory (VBT) answers the shape question. Nickel mixes $s$, $p$ and $d$ orbitals into $6$ equivalent $sp^3d^2$ hybrids, pointing at the $6$ corners of an octahedron, and each of the $6$ ammonia ligands donates a lone pair into one hybrid. That is the whole answer VBT gives — a shape, from a hybridisation label. It says nothing about why the compound is blue.

Crystal field theory (CFT) answers the colour question instead. It splits nickel's $5$ $d$ orbitals into $2$ energy groups once the $6$ ligands are placed around it, and the size of that gap decides which colour of light gets absorbed. But CFT never derives the octahedral shape on its own — it starts by assuming $6$ ligands sit octahedrally, then works out the splitting from that assumption.
