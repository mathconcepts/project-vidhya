Two charges, $q_1=2\ \mu\text{C}$ and $q_2=2\ \mu\text{C}$, are $r=0.1\ \text{m}$ apart. Coulomb's law: $F = \dfrac{k q_1 q_2}{r^2}$, with $k = 9\times10^9\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$.

$F = \dfrac{9\times10^9 \times 2\times10^{-6} \times 2\times10^{-6}}{(0.1)^2} = 3.6\ \text{N}$.

Now double $r$ to $0.2\ \text{m}$: $F = \dfrac{9\times10^9 \times 2\times10^{-6} \times 2\times10^{-6}}{(0.2)^2} = 0.9\ \text{N}$.

$3.6 \div 0.9 = 4$. Doubling $r$ divided the force by **4**, not by $2$ — because $r$ is squared in the formula. Next time distance doubles in a Coulomb's-law question, square that factor before touching the force.
