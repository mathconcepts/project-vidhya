A reaction has $\Delta H = +30$ kJ/mol and $\Delta S = +100$ J/(mol$\cdot$K) at $T=250$ K. Is it spontaneous at this temperature?

A) Yes, since $\Delta S>0$
B) Yes, since $\Delta H>0$
C) No, $\Delta G = +5$ kJ/mol
D) No, $\Delta G = -5$ kJ/mol
E) Cannot be determined without more information

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) No, $\Delta G = +5$ kJ/mol.**

Convert $\Delta S$ to kJ: $100$ J $= 0.1$ kJ. $T\Delta S = 250 \times 0.1 = 25$ kJ/mol. $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S = 30 - 25 = 5$ kJ/mol — positive, so non-spontaneous at $250$ K.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $\Delta S>0$ alone does not guarantee spontaneity — $\Delta H$ still matters, and here it wins at this temperature.
- B) $\Delta H>0$ alone would suggest non-spontaneous, but stopping there skips the actual $\Delta G$ calculation.
- D) $-5$ comes from a sign slip: computing $\Delta H - T\Delta S$ as $30 - 25$ but writing the answer with the wrong sign, or computing $-30+25$ instead.
- E) The problem gives every value needed — nothing more is required.

</details>
