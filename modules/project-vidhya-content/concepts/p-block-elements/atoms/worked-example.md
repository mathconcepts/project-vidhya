**Problem:** Work out the shape and hybridisation of the interhalogen compound $BrF_3$.

---

**Step 1 — Count bromine's valence electrons.** Bromine is in Group 17, so it has 7 valence electrons.

---

**Step 2 — Use 1 electron per $Br-F$ bond.** Three bonds to three fluorine atoms use 3 of bromine's 7 electrons, leaving $7-3=4$ electrons on bromine, unshared.

---

**Step 3 — Group the leftover electrons into lone pairs.** $4$ electrons make 2 lone pairs on bromine.

---

**Step 4 — Count total electron domains around bromine.** 3 bonding domains + 2 lone-pair domains = 5 domains total. Five domains correspond to $sp^3d$ hybridisation and a trigonal bipyramidal electron arrangement.

---

**Step 5 — Place the lone pairs to minimise repulsion.** Lone pairs push hardest against neighbours at $90°$, so both lone pairs sit in the equatorial positions of the trigonal bipyramid, where they only have to repel each other and two, not three, bonding pairs at close range.

---

**Step 6 — Read off the molecular shape.** With two equatorial positions taken by lone pairs, the three fluorine atoms sit in a bent, T-shaped arrangement. $\boxed{BrF_3 \text{ is T-shaped}, sp^3d \text{ hybridised, with 2 lone pairs on Br}}$.
