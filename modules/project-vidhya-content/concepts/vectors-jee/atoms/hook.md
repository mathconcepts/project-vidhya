A worker drags a loaded crate with a rope pulled at an angle to the ground. Only part of that pull actually drags the crate forward — the rest presses it down into the ground, wasted. To find the useful part, you need one number built from the rope's direction and its strength: that is the **dot product**.

Now the same rope is tied off to one corner of the crate instead of the middle, and yanked hard. The crate does not just slide — it swings, one edge lifting off the ground. That turning effect needs a completely different number, built from the exact same two pieces of information: the **cross product**.

Same two vectors. Two ways to combine them. Two unrelated answers.
