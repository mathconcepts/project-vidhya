A car moving at $u=30$ m/s decelerates uniformly and comes to rest in $t=6$ s. Take the direction of motion as positive. Find the distance it travels before stopping.

A) $180$ m
B) $90$ m
C) $45$ m
D) $15$ m
E) $270$ m

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $90$ m.**

First find the (signed) acceleration: $a=\dfrac{v-u}{t}=\dfrac{0-30}{6}=-5$ m/s$^2$ — negative because it is a deceleration, opposing the positive direction of motion. Then $s=ut+\tfrac{1}{2}at^2 = 30(6)+\tfrac{1}{2}(-5)(6)^2 = 180-90=90$ m.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $180$ m treats the car as moving at constant speed the whole time ($s=u\times t$), ignoring that it is decelerating throughout.
- C) $45$ m comes from a division slip while solving $0=900-10s$ for $s$ (dividing by $20$ instead of $10$).
- D) $15$ m uses $s=\tfrac{1}{2}at$, forgetting to square $t$.
- E) $270$ m uses the acceleration's magnitude, $a=+5$, instead of its correct signed value $a=-5$, in $s=ut+\tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ — adding the second term instead of subtracting it.

</details>
