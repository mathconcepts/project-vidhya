Counting to $4n+2$ is necessary, but on its own it settles nothing — **planarity** is the condition that quietly overrides a passing electron count.

Counterexample: cyclooctatetraene, $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$, has $8$ pi electrons. Solved flat, $4n+2=8$ gives $n=1.5$, not aromatic on the electron count anyway — but even a ring whose electron count *does* satisfy $4n+2$ can escape both aromaticity and antiaromaticity by simply refusing to stay flat. A ring under strain from an unfavourable electron count tends to buckle into a non-planar, "tub" shape precisely to break the continuous overlap of p-orbitals that aromaticity or antiaromaticity would require. Once the ring is no longer planar, the p-orbitals no longer form one connected system, and Huckel's rule has nothing left to apply to — the ring behaves as an ordinary, non-aromatic polyene instead.

Always check planarity and full conjugation before trusting an electron count at all.
