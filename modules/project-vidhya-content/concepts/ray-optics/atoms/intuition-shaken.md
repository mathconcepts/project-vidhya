Same convex lens, $f=20\text{ cm}$. Object now at $u=-10\text{ cm}$, inside the focal length.

$\dfrac{1}{v}-\dfrac{1}{u}=\dfrac{1}{f} \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20}+\dfrac{1}{-10} = \dfrac{1}{20}-\dfrac{2}{20} = -\dfrac{1}{20}$.

So $v=-20\text{ cm}$. Negative — the image is on the *same* side as the object, not the far side.

Draw the two rays: parallel-then-through-focus, and straight-through-centre. On the far side, they spread apart and never cross. Only extending them backwards, behind the object, makes them meet — exactly what "virtual" means.

Magnification: $m=v/u=-20/-10=+2$. Positive: upright. Size $2$: magnified.

Compare to the object-at-$30\text{ cm}$ case from before: there, $v$ was positive (real) and $m$ was negative (inverted) — the opposite pattern, both flips coming from the same one formula.
