**Problem:** Water ($\rho=1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) flows through a horizontal pipe narrowing from $A_1=4\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$ to $A_2=1\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$. At the wide part: $v_1=2\text{ m/s}$, $P_1=2\times10^5\text{ Pa}$. Find $v_2$ and $P_2$.

---

**Step 0 — Set up before any formula.** Horizontal pipe (height terms cancel), flow assumed steady, incompressible, non-viscous. Label wide section 1, narrow section 2 — both speed and pressure are positive numbers at each point.

---

**Step 1 — Continuity equation.** $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$. Numbers: $(4\times10^{-4})(2)=8\times10^{-4}$.

---

**Step 2 — Compute the narrow-section speed.** $v_2 = 8\times10^{-4}/(1\times10^{-4}) = 8\text{ m/s}$.

---

**Step 3 — Write Bernoulli's equation.** $P_1+\tfrac12\rho v_1^2 = P_2+\tfrac12\rho v_2^2$ (height terms drop out, pipe is horizontal).

---

**Step 4 — Compute each kinetic term.** $\tfrac12\rho v_1^2 = \tfrac12(1000)(4)=2000$. $\tfrac12\rho v_2^2=\tfrac12(1000)(64)=32{,}000$.

---

**Step 5 — Compute the downstream pressure.** $P_2 = P_1+2000-32{,}000 = 2\times10^5 - 30{,}000 = 1.7\times10^5\text{ Pa}$.

---

**Step 6 — Confirm the pressure trend.** Speed increased ($2\to8\text{ m/s}$), so pressure should decrease — it did, from $2\times10^5$ down to $1.7\times10^5\text{ Pa}$. $\boxed{v_2=8\text{ m/s},\ P_2=1.7\times10^5\text{ Pa}}$.
