**"1, spin, spin-back."** The cube roots of unity — the three solutions of $z^3=1$ — sit on the unit circle spaced $120°$ apart: $1$, then $\omega=e^{i2\pi/3}=-\tfrac12+i\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, then $\omega^2=e^{i4\pi/3}=-\tfrac12-i\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$. One real root, and its partner spinning forward mirrored by its partner spinning back — same real part, opposite imaginary part.

**Worked micro-example.** Check $\omega^3=1$: $\omega=e^{i2\pi/3}$, so $\omega^3=e^{i2\pi}=1$ ✓. Check the sum: $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$ (the three points balance around the origin, so their vector sum cancels).

**Sanity-check reflex.** Any $n$th roots of a number should be equally spaced around a circle of radius $|{\cdot}|^{1/n}$ — if your $n$ computed roots aren't evenly spaced by $360°/n$, an arithmetic slip happened somewhere in the argument.
