**Cross-concept check: Circles → Conic Sections.**

The circle's chord-of-contact argument — "both touch points satisfy the same equation, so that equation is their connecting line" — is not special to circles. It carries over unchanged to the parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola, with only the tangent formula swapped out.

**Question 1 (Circles):** For circle $x^2+y^2=9$, what is the chord of contact from the external point $(6,3)$?

*Answer:* $T=0$ at $(6,3)$: $6x+3y=9$, i.e. $2x+y-3=0$. (Check outside first: $36+9=45>9$, yes.)

**Question 2 (Conic Sections):** For the parabola $y^2=8x$, tangent at a point $(x_2,y_2)$ on it is $yy_2=4(x+x_2)$. Using the SAME argument as Question 1, write down the chord of contact from an external point $(x_1,y_1)$, without deriving it again from scratch.

*Answer:* $yy_1=4(x+x_1)$ — swap $(x_2,y_2)$ for $(x_1,y_1)$ in the tangent formula, exactly the move that worked for the circle.

**Why this drill exists:** the "evaluate the tangent formula at the outside point instead of the touch point" trick is one idea reused across every conic in this exam, not four separate formulas to memorise — recognising the pattern here is what makes the parabola/ellipse/hyperbola versions feel familiar on sight instead of new.
