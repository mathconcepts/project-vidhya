Picture two ways of building a paper chain. In the first, each paper strip already has a slit cut into both ends, and you simply hook one strip's slit through the next — nothing is thrown away, every bit of paper you started with is still part of the finished chain. That's **addition polymerisation**: the monomer's double bond is the slit, already there and ready to link, with nothing lost.

In the second way, each strip needs its overlapping ends glued together, and gluing means trimming a small overlapping tab off both strips first before they can be pressed together. Every single join costs you a small scrap of paper. That's **condensation polymerisation**: every amide or ester bond formed costs one small molecule, usually water, thrown away.

Now picture the finished rubber-band chain being tied off with extra cross-threads linking one loop of the chain to loops on nearby strands, at scattered points — that's vulcanisation's sulfur cross-links. Stretch the whole tangled structure, and the cross-threads pull everything back into shape once released; without them, the loops would simply slide apart and stay stretched.
