An instant cold pack, squeezed once, turns cold within seconds — no ice, no battery, no external cooling. Breaking a seal inside lets a salt dissolve in water, and that dissolving step *absorbs* heat straight out of your hand.

This is an **endothermic** process: the **system** (the salt and water) takes in heat from the **surroundings** (your hand, the air), so the surroundings feel colder. The reverse — a reaction that releases heat into the surroundings, like burning fuel — is **exothermic**.

What decides whether a process actually *happens on its own* is not just this heat exchange (measured by **enthalpy**, $H$) but a combination of it with **entropy** ($S$, a measure of disorder) — combined into one number, the **Gibbs free energy** change, $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$. A process is spontaneous only when $\Delta G < 0$, even an endothermic one like this cold pack.
