**Cross-concept check: redox-and-electrochemistry → thermodynamics-chemistry.**

**Question 1 (cell potential to Gibbs free energy):** For the Daniell cell ($\text{Zn}+\text{Cu}^{2+}\rightarrow\text{Zn}^{2+}+\text{Cu}$, $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}=1.10\ \text{V}$, $n=2$), find $\Delta G^\circ$ at $298\ \text{K}$.

*Answer:* $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}} = -(2)(96500)(1.10) = -212300\ \text{J} = -212.3\ \text{kJ}$. The large negative value says this reaction releases a substantial amount of usable energy.

**Question 2 (Gibbs free energy to the equilibrium constant):** Using $\Delta G^\circ=-2.303RT\log_{10}K$, estimate $\log_{10}K$ for this same reaction.

*Answer:* Combining $\Delta G^\circ=-nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ with $\Delta G^\circ=-2.303RT\log_{10}K$ gives the direct shortcut $\log_{10}K = \dfrac{nE^\circ_{\text{cell}}}{0.0591} = \dfrac{2(1.10)}{0.0591} \approx 37.2$. $K \approx 10^{37}$ — an almost unimaginably large number, meaning this reaction runs essentially to completion, with virtually no zinc or copper ions left unreacted at equilibrium.

**Why this drill exists:** a cell potential is not an isolated electrochemistry fact — it is a direct, computable statement about $\Delta G^\circ$ and $K$, the same two ideas chemical thermodynamics and chemical equilibrium already built. A large $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ always means a large $K$, connecting three topics through one formula.
