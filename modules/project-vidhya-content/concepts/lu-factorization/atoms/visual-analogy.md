Factoring an integer is familiar: $12=3\times4$ tells you everything about 12's structure at a glance. Factoring a matrix works the same way. $A=LU$ splits one opaque object into two transparent ones: $L$ is the record of every row operation elimination performed, $U$ is the triangular shape those operations leave behind. Multiply the two factors back together and $A$ reappears exactly — just as $3\times4$ reappears as 12.

The factors are more useful than $A$ itself for the same reason prime factors are more useful than the plain number: a triangular system solves in one direct pass, the way a prime factorization tells you divisibility instantly. There is no honest single 2D curve to trace for this idea — the payoff is structural, not geometric, so no animation is attached here.
