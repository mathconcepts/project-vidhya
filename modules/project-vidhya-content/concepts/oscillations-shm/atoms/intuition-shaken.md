Take a block of mass $m=0.5$ kg on a spring of stiffness $k=200$ N/m, pulled out to amplitude $A=0.05$ m and released.

**Step 1 — angular frequency.** $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}=\sqrt{200/0.5}=\sqrt{400}=20$ rad/s.

**Step 2 — time period.** $T=2\pi/\omega=2\pi/20\approx0.314$ s — just under a third of a second per full swing.

**Step 3 — maximum speed, at the centre.** $v_{max}=A\omega=(0.05)(20)=1$ m/s.

**Step 4 — maximum acceleration, at the extremes.** $a_{max}=A\omega^2=(0.05)(400)=20\ \text{m/s}^2$.

**Step 5 — total mechanical energy, constant throughout.** $E=\frac{1}{2}kA^2=\frac{1}{2}(200)(0.05)^2=\frac{1}{2}(200)(0.0025)=0.25$ J.

**Step 6 — check the two extremes agree.** At the centre ($x=0$): all $0.25$ J is kinetic, $\frac{1}{2}mv_{max}^2=\frac{1}{2}(0.5)(1)^2=0.25$ J — matches. At the extreme ($x=A$, $v=0$): all $0.25$ J is potential, $\frac{1}{2}kA^2=0.25$ J — matches too. Energy never leaves this closed loop between kinetic and potential.
