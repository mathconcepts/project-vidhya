Two people stand at fixed spots, $S_1$ and $S_2$, and shout the same word at exactly the same moment, over and over, in step. A listener standing exactly midway between them, facing both equally, hears both shouts arrive at once every single time — the two sounds are always in step, however long the listener stays there. A listener standing off to one side hears $S_2$'s shout a little later than $S_1$'s each time, because the sound has slightly farther to travel — a fixed lag, repeated every shout.

Light from two coherent slits behaves exactly the same way: it is not the listener's *distance* from either slit that matters, it is the *difference* in how far the two paths are. On the centre line, that difference is exactly zero — an automatic bright fringe. Off the centre, the two paths are genuinely unequal, and whether that gap works out to a whole number or a half-integer number of wavelengths is what decides bright or dark there — never the raw distance travelled.
