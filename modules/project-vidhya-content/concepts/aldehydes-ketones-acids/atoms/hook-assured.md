"More alkyl groups on the carbonyl carbon means slower nucleophilic addition" is the rule everyone remembers — aldehyde faster than ketone, because fewer/smaller groups crowd it. It is not the whole story once resonance is added: an aryl ketone like acetophenone ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3$) is slower than acetone even though both carry the same number of substituents on the carbonyl carbon.

The ring's electron cloud delocalises into the carbonyl group (conjugation), spreading out — and so partly cancelling — the carbon's electron shortage. Less shortage means less pull for a nucleophile to respond to. Steric bulk alone predicts acetophenone and acetone should react at comparable rates; only accounting for this extra electronic effect from the ring explains why the aromatic ketone is markedly slower.
