**Problem:** In a Young's double-slit setup, $d=0.5\text{ mm}$, $D=1\text{ m}$, $\lambda=500\text{ nm}$. Determine whether the point $y=2\text{ mm}$ above the centre is bright or dark, and separately find the fringe width.

---

**Step 1 — Path difference at the named point.** $\Delta = \dfrac{dy}{D} = \dfrac{(0.5\times10^{-3})(2\times10^{-3})}{1} = 1\times10^{-6}\text{ m} = 1000\text{ nm}$.

---

**Step 2 — Compare to the wavelength.** $\dfrac{\Delta}{\lambda} = \dfrac{1000}{500} = 2$, a whole number.

---

**Step 3 — Classify the point.** A whole-number multiple of $\lambda$ means constructive interference: $y=2\text{ mm}$ is **bright** (the second-order bright fringe, $n=2$).

---

**Step 4 — Fringe width, as a separate quantity.** $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d} = \dfrac{(500\times10^{-9})(1)}{0.5\times10^{-3}} = 1\times10^{-3}\text{ m} = 1\text{ mm}$.

$$\boxed{y=2\text{ mm}: \text{bright } (n=2), \qquad \beta = 1\text{ mm}}$$

---

**Step 5 — Cross-check.** A bright fringe of order $n$ should sit at $y_n = n\beta$ (from the centre). Here, $y_2 = 2\times1\text{ mm} = 2\text{ mm}$ — exactly the point given in the problem, confirming Steps 1–4 are consistent with each other.
