**Problem:** A rigid, sealed cylinder (the system; its inner wall is the boundary) holds $n=2$ mol of helium gas (monatomic, molar mass $M=0.004$ kg/mol) at $T=300$ K in a volume $V=0.05\ \text{m}^3$. Find (a) the rms speed of a helium atom, (b) the total internal energy of the gas, and (c) the pressure inside the cylinder.

---

**Step 1 — Identify what "monatomic" fixes.** Helium atoms have no rotational degrees of freedom worth counting — only $f=3$ translational ones. This tells us directly that internal energy will be $U=\frac{3}{2}nRT$, with no extra rotational term to add.

---

**Step 2 — rms speed.** $v_{rms}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3(8.314)(300)}{0.004}}\approx1368\ \text{m/s}$.

---

**Step 3 — Total internal energy.** $U=\frac{3}{2}nRT=\frac{3}{2}(2)(8.314)(300)=7482.6\ \text{J}$.

---

**Step 4 — Pressure, from the macroscopic gas law.** $P=\dfrac{nRT}{V}=\dfrac{(2)(8.314)(300)}{0.05}=99768\ \text{Pa}\approx99.8\ \text{kPa}$.

---

**Step 5 — State all three answers together.** $\boxed{v_{rms}\approx1368\ \text{m/s},\quad U=7482.6\ \text{J},\quad P\approx99.8\ \text{kPa}}$. Notice $U$ came from counting degrees of freedom, while $P$ came straight from $PV=nRT$ — two separate routes into the same sealed gas, both valid, neither substitutable for the other.
