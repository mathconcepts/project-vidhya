**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: plug straight into $F=qvB\sin\theta$ or $B=\mu_0I/2\pi r$.** Given the angle, speed, field, and charge (or a wire's current and distance), compute the number directly — check the angle first, since it decides whether the answer is even nonzero.

- **MCQ: "moves along the field" or "moves parallel to the field" phrasing.** The instant a question describes motion along, parallel to, or in the same line as the field, the magnetic force on that charge is zero — no further calculation needed.

- **Trap: which hand rule applies.** A question about a single moving charge needs the right-hand rule ($\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B$); a question about a current-carrying wire or coil needs Fleming's Left-Hand Rule ($\vec F=I\vec L\times\vec B$, or torque on a loop). Reading which setup the question describes, before reaching for either hand, is the actual skill.

- **Trap: torque's $\theta$ is measured from the loop's normal, not its plane.** A coil "with its plane parallel to $B$" has $\theta=90°$ (maximum torque), not $\theta=0°$ — a very common misread under time pressure.

- **Time budget:** a direct force or field NAT should take under 30 seconds once the angle is identified correctly; spend that first moment checking the geometry, not the arithmetic.
