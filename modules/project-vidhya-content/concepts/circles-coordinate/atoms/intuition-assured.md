The "two points satisfy the same equation, so that equation is their line" step generalises well beyond circles: it is the exact same argument behind chord of contact for a parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola, and behind why "pole" and "polar" are inverse operations everywhere they are defined. Once you have seen it work for the circle, the conic sections' versions are not new theorems — they are the identical proof with a different tangent formula plugged in.

One invariant worth carrying forward: for circle $x^2+y^2=a^2$, if $d_1$ is the distance of the outside point from the centre and $d_2$ is the distance of its chord of contact FROM the centre, then $d_1d_2=a^2$ exactly — always, regardless of where the point sits outside the circle. This is the algebraic fingerprint of pole–polar duality: swap the point for its polar line, and the product of the two distances from the centre stays fixed at $r^2$. Radical axis, met later in this same concept, is a genuinely different construction — a line built from TWO circles, not a point and one circle — so do not reach for $T=0$ there.
