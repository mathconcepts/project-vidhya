**Problem:** Using molecular orbital theory, find the bond order of the nitrogen molecule, $\text{N}_2$, given that it has 10 bonding electrons and 4 antibonding electrons. State whether this matches a single, double, or triple bond.

---

**Step 1 — Apply the formula directly.** $\dfrac{10-4}{2}=3$. $\text{N}_2$ has a triple bond.

$$\boxed{\text{Bond order} = 3}$$

---

**The genuinely useful contrast: this agrees with a Lewis-structure count here, but MO theory earns its keep on $\text{O}_2$, not $\text{N}_2$.** $\text{O}_2$'s Lewis structure alone (all electrons paired by construction) predicts bond order 2 and zero unpaired electrons — a normal double bond, nothing more. MO theory, applied the same way, also gives bond order $\dfrac{8-4}{2}=2$, but with those 4 antibonding electrons distributed as 2 pairs in one $\pi^*$ orbital *and* 1 electron each in two separate $\pi^*$ orbitals (Hund's rule, applied to antibonding orbitals exactly as it applies to ordinary ones) — leaving 2 electrons genuinely unpaired.

A student who treats "bond order matches, so the two theories always agree" as a general rule will correctly predict $\text{N}_2$'s bond type and then wrongly conclude $\text{O}_2$ has no unpaired electrons, missing its real, measurable magnetism entirely.
