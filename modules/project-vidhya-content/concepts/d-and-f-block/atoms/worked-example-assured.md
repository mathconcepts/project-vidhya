**Problem:** State why $Fe^{2+}$'s magnetic moment is not one fixed number, and compute it for the strong-field complex $[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}$.

---

**Step 1 — Name the hidden variable.** $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ only works once $n$ is known, and $n$ depends on the ligand's field strength, not on the metal ion alone. The same ion, $Fe^{2+}$ ($3d^6$), gives completely different values of $n$ in a weak-field versus a strong-field octahedral complex.

---

**Step 2 — Recall the weak-field case.** In a weak field (e.g. $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$), electrons spread across all 5 $d$ orbitals before any pairing — high spin, $n=4$, $\mu \approx 4.90$ BM, worked out in the base example.

---

**Step 3 — Work out the strong-field case.** In a strong field like $CN^-$, the energy cost of moving an electron to a higher orbital exceeds the cost of pairing it up in a lower one, so all 6 electrons crowd into the 3 lower ($t_{2g}$) orbitals first: $3 \times 2 = 6$, filling them completely, leaving the 2 higher ($e_g$) orbitals empty.

---

**Step 4 — Count unpaired electrons.** All 6 electrons are paired; $n=0$.

---

**Step 5 — Final answer.** $\boxed{\mu([Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}) = \sqrt{0 \times 2} = 0 \text{ BM — diamagnetic}}$, despite being the exact same $Fe^{2+}$, $3d^6$ ion as the 4.90 BM example above.
