**Reversible reaction**: a reaction that can proceed in both the forward and reverse directions, written $A \rightleftharpoons B$.

**Dynamic equilibrium**: the state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, so concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time (though both reactions keep occurring).

**Law of mass action / equilibrium constant**: for $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$, $K_c = \dfrac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$ (concentrations, at equilibrium). $K_p$ uses partial pressures in the same form. Relation: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$, where $\Delta n$ = (moles of gaseous products) $-$ (moles of gaseous reactants).

**Reaction quotient**, $Q$: the same expression as $K_c$ (or $K_p$), evaluated at *any* point, not necessarily equilibrium. $Q<K$: reaction shifts forward. $Q>K$: reaction shifts in reverse. $Q=K$: at equilibrium.

**Le Chatelier's Principle**: if a system at equilibrium is disturbed (change in concentration, pressure/volume, or temperature), the equilibrium shifts in the direction that partially opposes the disturbance. A prediction rule, not a mechanism.

**Catalyst**: lowers the activation energy of both the forward and reverse reactions equally; changes the rate of *reaching* equilibrium, never the value of $K$.

**Relation to Gibbs free energy**: $\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K$.
