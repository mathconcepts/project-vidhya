Follow the charge after phenol loses its proton.

Before: phenol, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$, neutral, the oxygen holding two lone pairs.

After losing $\text{H}^+$: the phenoxide ion, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$. One of oxygen's lone pairs joins the ring's connected pi system. The negative charge is no longer stuck on oxygen alone — it spreads onto three ring carbons too, the ones directly across the ring from oxygen and one position further round on each side.

Count the result: one charge, now sitting across four positions instead of one. Spreading it out this way lowers the ion's energy, making it form more easily.

Now do the same for an ordinary alcohol's alkoxide, $\text{RO}^-$. There is no ring here — nothing for the charge to spread into. It sits entirely on one oxygen, high in energy, and the attached carbon group pushes *more* electron density onto that same oxygen through the inductive effect, making things worse, not better.
