**Problem:** A sodium surface (work function $\phi=2.3$ eV) is lit by light of wavelength $400$ nm. Find (a) maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons, (b) stopping potential, (c) sodium's threshold wavelength.

---

**Step 1 — Photon energy.** $E=\dfrac{1240}{400}=3.1$ eV.

---

**Step 2 — Subtract the work function.** $KE_{max}=3.1-2.3=0.8$ eV.

---

**Step 3 — Read off the stopping potential.** $eV_0=KE_{max}$, so $V_0=0.8$ V — same number, different unit.

---

**Step 4 — Threshold wavelength.** $\phi=\dfrac{1240}{\lambda_0}\Rightarrow\lambda_0=\dfrac{1240}{2.3}\approx539$ nm.

---

**Step 5 — Check.** $400<539$, so this light's frequency is above threshold — a photoelectron should be emitted, matching the positive $KE_{max}=0.8$ eV found in Step 2.

$$\boxed{KE_{max}=0.8\text{ eV},\quad V_0=0.8\text{ V},\quad \lambda_0\approx539\text{ nm}}$$

Answer: $KE_{max}=0.8$ eV, $V_0=0.8$ V, $\lambda_0\approx539$ nm.
