A germanium photodiode has band gap $E_g\approx0.7$ eV. What is its maximum detectable wavelength?

A) $1771$ nm
B) $1127$ nm
C) $868$ nm
D) $700$ nm
E) $0.7$ nm

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $1771$ nm.**

$\lambda_{max}=\dfrac{1240}{E_g}=\dfrac{1240}{0.7}\approx1771$ nm.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) $1127$ nm is *silicon's* cutoff wavelength ($1240/1.1$), computed with the wrong band gap.
- C) $868$ nm comes from *multiplying* $1240\times0.7$ instead of dividing.
- D) $700$ nm mistakes the band-gap number itself for a wavelength in nanometres.
- E) $0.7$ nm confuses the band gap's numerical value (in eV) with a distance (in nm).

</details>
