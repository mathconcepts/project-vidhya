**Paired concept: Inverse Trigonometric Functions.**

Every heights-and-distances problem that asks for the ANGLE itself, rather than the height or distance, hands you a ratio like $h/d$ and expects you to recover $\theta=\tan^{-1}(h/d)$ — pulling you straight back into $\tan^{-1}$'s own principal-value branch.

**Drill**: A flagpole $5\sqrt3$ m tall stands $5$ m from an observer's feet. Write the angle of elevation as $\theta=\tan^{-1}(h/d)$ with the numbers substituted, evaluate it, and state why the result is guaranteed to land inside $\tan^{-1}$'s principal range $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ without any correction being needed.
