**Problem:** For a reaction, $\Delta H = -58$ kJ/mol and $\Delta S = -175$ J/(mol$\cdot$K). Is the reaction spontaneous at $T=300$ K?

---

**Step 1 — Write both given values down, with units.** $\Delta H = -58$ kJ/mol. $\Delta S = -175$ J/(mol$\cdot$K).

---

**Step 2 — Convert $\Delta S$ into kJ, to match $\Delta H$'s unit.** $-175$ J $= -0.175$ kJ. So $\Delta S = -0.175$ kJ/(mol$\cdot$K).

---

**Step 3 — Multiply by $T$.** $T\Delta S = 300 \times (-0.175) = -52.5$ kJ/mol.

---

**Step 4 — Substitute into $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$.** $\Delta G = -58 - (-52.5)$.

---

**Step 5 — Simplify the subtraction of a negative.** $-58 - (-52.5) = -58 + 52.5 = -5.5$ kJ/mol.

$$\boxed{\Delta G = -5.5 \text{ kJ/mol}}$$

**Step 6 — Check the sign and conclude.** $-5.5$ is negative, so $\Delta G<0$: the reaction is spontaneous at $300$ K.
