The $d^0$-is-colourless rule is assumed known — the loud counter-case sits in $MnO_4^-$ (permanganate), which is intensely purple despite manganese there being $Mn^{7+}$, configuration $3d^0$: no $d$ electrons at all, so no $d$-to-$d$ electron jump is even possible.

The colour survives through a different mechanism entirely: charge transfer. An electron jumps not between two $d$ orbitals on manganese, but from an oxide ligand's filled orbital straight into one of manganese's empty $d$ orbitals — ligand-to-metal charge transfer, not the $d$-$d$ transition every earlier example in this concept relied on. $d^0$ blocks $d$-$d$ colour specifically, not colour in general, whenever a strongly electron-donating ligand like oxide is attached. The same reasoning explains why $CrO_4^{2-}$ ($Cr^{6+}$, also $d^0$) is yellow, and why treating "$d^0$ or $d^{10}$ means colourless, full stop" as an absolute rule fails on exactly the ions ($MnO_4^-$, $Cr_2O_7^{2-}$) most likely to appear in a question.
