**Problem:** Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of $Fe^{2+}$ in $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ (weak-field, high-spin).

---

**Step 1 — Write the neutral configuration.** Iron's neutral configuration is $[Ar]3d^64s^2$ — that is $6$ electrons in $3d$ and $2$ in $4s$, $8$ outer electrons total.

---

**Step 2 — Remove 2 electrons to form the ion.** Transition metals always lose $4s$ electrons before $3d$ ones, so both $4s$ electrons go: $Fe^{2+}$ is $[Ar]3d^6$ — $6$ electrons, $0$ left in $4s$.

---

**Step 3 — Fill the 5 orbitals, one at a time.** Put those $6$ electrons into $5$ orbitals, high-spin. First $5$ electrons: one each into all $5$ orbitals, none paired yet. That leaves $6-5=1$ electron with no empty orbital left, so it pairs up in whichever orbital it lands in.

---

**Step 4 — Count what is unpaired.** $5$ orbitals total, $1$ now holds a pair, so $5-1=4$ orbitals still hold a single, unpaired electron. $n=4$.

---

**Step 5 — Compute the moment.** $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{4 \times 6} = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.90$ BM.

---

**Step 6 — Final answer.** $\boxed{\mu(Fe^{2+}) \approx 4.90 \text{ BM}}$.
