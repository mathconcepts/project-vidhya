- **Assuming speed is greatest where displacement is greatest**: it is exactly the opposite. Speed is zero at maximum displacement and greatest at zero displacement — the two never coincide.

- **Writing acceleration as $a=+\omega^2x$**: the minus sign in $a=-\omega^2x$ is not optional. It is what makes the force a *restoring* force, always pointing back toward the centre; dropping it describes a force that pushes an object further away instead.

- **Applying the spring-mass angular frequency formula to a pendulum, or the reverse**: a spring-mass system uses $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$; a simple pendulum uses $\omega=\sqrt{g/L}$. These involve entirely different physical quantities and are not interchangeable.

- **Assuming a larger amplitude changes the time period**: for ideal SHM, $T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$ (or $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$) does not depend on amplitude at all. This independence breaks down only once a pendulum's swing angle grows too large for $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ to hold.

- **Mixing up time period and frequency**: $T$ (seconds, time for one full cycle) and $f=1/T$ (hertz, cycles per second) are reciprocals, not the same number. Reading a graph's period off directly and then using it in a formula that expects frequency (or the reverse) silently inverts every dependent answer.
