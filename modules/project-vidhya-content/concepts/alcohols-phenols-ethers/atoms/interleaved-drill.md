**Cross-concept check: alcohols-phenols-ethers → aldehydes-ketones-acids.**

**Question 1 (which product forms, and why one stops early):** Propan-1-ol, a primary alcohol, is oxidised with a strong oxidising agent like acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$. What is the final product, and what does 2-methylpropan-2-ol (a tertiary alcohol) give under the same conditions?

*Answer:* Propan-1-ol oxidises all the way to propanoic acid, a carboxylic acid — a strong oxidant removes hydrogens from the carbon bearing $-\text{OH}$ twice in succession, first to an aldehyde, then again to the acid. 2-methylpropan-2-ol resists oxidation entirely under the same conditions, because its carbon bearing $-\text{OH}$ has no hydrogen attached to it at all — there is nothing for the oxidant to remove.

**Question 2 (name the general rule this illustrates):** State, in general terms, which class of alcohol resists oxidation and why.

*Answer:* A **tertiary alcohol** resists oxidation, because oxidation at this carbon always works by removing a hydrogen attached directly to the carbon-bearing $-\text{OH}$, and a tertiary carbon has none — its other three bonds are all to carbon.

**Why this drill exists:** the same carbon that carried the acidic $-\text{OH}$ in this concept becomes the exact carbon whose remaining hydrogens (or lack of them) decide everything in the next concept — how far oxidation can go is read straight off that one carbon's structure.
