Treating VBT and CFT as rival theories, where one must be "more correct" than the other, is exactly backwards — they are not competitors, and one needs outside help the other does not.

Counterexample: $[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}$ is diamagnetic and square planar ($dsp^2$); $[NiCl_4]^{2-}$ is paramagnetic and tetrahedral ($sp^3$), for the same $Ni^{2+}$ ion. VBT can describe both hybridisations once told which is which, but VBT alone gives no reason to expect one ligand to force pairing and the other not to — that fact has to be fed in from experiment first. CFT predicts it directly: $CN^-$ is strong-field, splitting orbitals enough to favour pairing and square planar geometry; $Cl^-$ is weak-field, leaving electrons unpaired, geometry unforced. CFT explains what VBT can only describe after the fact.
