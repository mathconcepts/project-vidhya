- **Using the gas-phase basicity order in an aqueous question, or vice versa**: "tertiary is most basic" is the gas-phase order; in water, for simple alkyl amines, secondary usually beats both primary and tertiary because of how well each protonated amine's remaining $\text{N-H}$ bonds hydrogen-bond with water. Reading a question that says "in aqueous solution" and reaching for the gas-phase order anyway is a frequent, easy-to-miss error.

- **Treating aniline like any other amine with one substituent**: aniline's nitrogen lone pair delocalises into the aromatic ring by resonance, making aniline markedly LESS basic than ammonia — not comparable to methylamine just because both carry one substituent on nitrogen.

- **Trying diazonium coupling on an unactivated or deactivated ring**: coupling only proceeds on a ring carrying a strong electron-donating group ($\text{-OH}$, $\text{-NH}_2$). Assuming it will work on plain benzene, or on a ring bearing an electron-withdrawing group like $\text{-NO}_2$, silently predicts a reaction that does not happen.

- **Forgetting the temperature window for diazotisation**: the diazonium salt is only stable at $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$. Above that range it decomposes (often to a phenol, releasing nitrogen gas) before it can be used in any subsequent reaction — a question describing diazotisation "at room temperature" is describing decomposition, not a usable diazonium salt.

- **Mixing up which Hinsberg-test layer is which**: the primary amine's sulfonamide product is soluble in excess KOH (it has an acidic $\text{N-H}$); the secondary amine's product is insoluble (no $\text{N-H}$ left to ionise). Swapping "soluble" and "insoluble" between the two flips the whole identification.

- **Assuming Gabriel synthesis works for any amine, aromatic included**: it reliably gives pure primary ALIPHATIC amines with no over-alkylation, but it cannot make an aromatic amine like aniline — the mechanism needs a halide that a phthalimide anion can displace, and an aryl carbon does not offer that under Gabriel's conditions.
