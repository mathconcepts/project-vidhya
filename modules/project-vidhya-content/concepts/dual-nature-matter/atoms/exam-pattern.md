**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: stopping potential or threshold wavelength from a single photon-energy calculation.** Given wavelength and work function, compute $E=1240/\lambda(\text{nm})$ eV first, then subtract $\phi$ — do this in one pass rather than solving for frequency in SI units and converting back.

- **MCQ: "which quantity changes when intensity is doubled".** The moment a question mentions changing brightness/intensity while holding wavelength fixed, the only things that can change are photocurrent (electrons per second) and the number of photons — never $KE_{max}$ or the stopping potential.

- **Trap: de Broglie questions swap the particle without warning.** A question may give an accelerating voltage and ask for a wavelength, but for a proton or an alpha particle (not an electron) — using the electron-specific $12.27/\sqrt{V}$ shortcut here is a guaranteed wrong answer; recompute $p=\sqrt{2mqV}$ with the given particle's own mass and charge.

- **Trap: "no photoelectric effect" is read as "small effect".** Below the threshold frequency, JEE distractors sometimes offer a small nonzero kinetic energy — the correct answer is that no photoelectron is emitted at all, not a reduced one.

- **Time budget:** a single-wavelength stopping-potential NAT should take under $40$ seconds — one photon-energy calculation, one subtraction, done.
