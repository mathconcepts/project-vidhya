Look at one fact first: benzene, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$, barely reacts with bromine water. Cyclohexene, which really does have one plain double bond, decolourises bromine water within seconds.

Both molecules are drawn with double bonds. Only one of them acts like it has any. Something extra is stabilising benzene's ring, over and above whatever a plain double bond gives.

That extra stability has a name — **aromaticity** — and a rule that predicts it before you even need to test a bottle of bromine water: **Huckel's rule**, based purely on counting electrons in the ring.
