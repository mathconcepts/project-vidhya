**Problem:** Find the binding energy per nucleon of a helium-4 nucleus ($_2^4\text{He}$). Given: mass of a hydrogen atom $m_H=1.007825$ u, mass of a neutron $m_n=1.008665$ u, mass of a helium-4 atom $M=4.002603$ u, and $1$ u $=931.5$ MeV$/c^2$.

---

**Step 1 — Count the constituents.** Mass number $A=4$, atomic number $Z=2$ (helium has $2$ protons), so **neutron number** $A-Z=2$. Using the hydrogen atom's mass in place of a bare proton's is deliberate: both $m_H$ and $M$ already include their orbiting electrons, so the electron count matches on both sides and cancels automatically.

---

**Step 2 — Add up the separate pieces' mass.** $2m_H+2m_n=2(1.007825)+2(1.008665)=4.03298$ u.

---

**Step 3 — Find the mass defect.** $\Delta m=4.03298-4.002603=0.030377$ u — the tiny bit of mass "missing" once the pieces are bound together into one nucleus.

---

**Step 4 — Convert to binding energy.** $BE=\Delta m\times931.5=0.030377\times931.5\approx28.30$ MeV — the energy that would be needed to pull this nucleus completely apart.

---

**Step 5 — Divide by the nucleon count.** $\dfrac{BE}{A}=\dfrac{28.30}{4}\approx7.07$ MeV per nucleon.

$$\boxed{BE\approx28.30\text{ MeV},\quad BE/A\approx7.07\text{ MeV per nucleon}}$$

Answer: $BE\approx28.30$ MeV, binding energy per nucleon $\approx7.07$ MeV.
