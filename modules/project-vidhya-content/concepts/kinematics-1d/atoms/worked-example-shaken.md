**Problem:** A ball is thrown vertically upward from the ground with initial speed $u=20$ m/s. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. Find (a) the time to reach maximum height, (b) the maximum height, (c) the total time of flight, and (d) the velocity when it returns to the ground.

---

**Step 1 — Origin, direction, sign — settle these before any equation.** Origin at the ground. Take upward as positive. Then $u=+20$ m/s and $a=-g=-10$ m/s$^2$, since gravity always points down, opposite to whichever way is positive here.

---

**Step 2 — Time to the top, where $v=0$.** $v=u+at \Rightarrow 0=20+(-10)t \Rightarrow 10t=20 \Rightarrow t=2$ s.

---

**Step 3 — Height reached in that time.** $s=ut+\tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \Rightarrow s=20(2)+\tfrac{1}{2}(-10)(2)^2$. Compute each piece: $20(2)=40$. $\tfrac{1}{2}(-10)(4)=-20$. Add them: $40-20=20$ m.

---

**Step 4 — Time to return to the ground, where $s=0$ again.** $0=20t-5t^2 = t(20-5t)$. So $t=0$ (start) or $20-5t=0 \Rightarrow t=4$ s. Time of flight $=4$ s.

---

**Step 5 — Velocity on return.** $v=u+at=20+(-10)(4)$. Compute: $(-10)(4)=-40$. So $v=20-40=-20$ m/s, the value carried into the last step below.

---

**Step 6 — Check the sign makes sense.** Magnitude $20$ m/s matches the launch speed exactly (expected, by the up-down symmetry of this motion), and the negative sign, under our upward-positive convention, correctly says the ball is now moving downward.

$$\boxed{t_{\text{up}}=2\text{ s},\ \ h_{\max}=20\text{ m},\ \ T=4\text{ s},\ \ v_{\text{return}}=-20\text{ m/s}}$$
