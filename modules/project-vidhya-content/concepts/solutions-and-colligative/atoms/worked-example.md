**Problem:** $1.05\ \text{g}$ of a non-electrolyte is dissolved in $26\ \text{g}$ of benzene. The freezing point drops by $1.2\ \text{K}$. Given $K_f(\text{benzene})=5.12\ \text{K kg/mol}$, find the molar mass of the solute.

---

**Step 1 — Count what is conserved before reaching for a formula.** The moles of solute dissolved is a fixed, single number — everything else in this problem (mass, molality, molar mass) is just a different way of describing that same amount. The non-electrolyte does not dissociate, so $i=1$ and can be dropped from every formula below.

---

**Step 2 — Find the molality from $\Delta T_f$.** $\Delta T_f = K_f\,m \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{\Delta T_f}{K_f} = \dfrac{1.2}{5.12} = 0.234\ \text{mol/kg}$.

---

**Step 3 — Convert molality to moles of solute.** Molality is moles PER KILOGRAM of solvent, and there is $26\ \text{g}=0.026\ \text{kg}$ of benzene here: $\text{moles} = 0.234 \times 0.026 = 6.09\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$.

---

**Step 4 — Molar mass from mass and moles.** $M = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{moles}} = \dfrac{1.05}{6.09\times10^{-3}}$.

$$\boxed{M \approx 172.3\ \text{g/mol}}$$

---

**Step 5 — Check the units survived correctly.** Grams divided by moles gives g/mol, exactly what a molar mass should be — and $172.3$ is a physically reasonable size for a real organic molecule, not an implausible number a units slip would have produced.
