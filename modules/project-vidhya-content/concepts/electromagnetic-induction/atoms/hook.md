Picture a flat circular coil lying on a table, its area $0.02\text{ m}^2$ (about the size of a small dinner plate). A magnet held just above it points its field straight down through the coil, at $0.6\text{ T}$. Over the next $0.4$ seconds, the magnet is slowly pulled away, and the field through the coil fades to $0.2\text{ T}$.

Nothing in the coil moved. No wire touched anything new. Yet for those $0.4$ seconds, a real current flows in the coil — induced purely because the field is *changing*, not because anything is in motion. How strong that current is comes from **Faraday's law**. Which way it flows comes from **Lenz's law**, and that direction is exactly where marks are lost: the induced current opposes the *change* in the field, not the field's own direction — and here, since the field is fading, the current actually flows to try to keep the old field alive, not to cancel it.
