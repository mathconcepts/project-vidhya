**Problem:** A sugar gives a positive Fehling's test. Glucose or sucrose?

---

**Step 1 — Apply the free-anomeric-carbon rule directly.** Glucose keeps one anomeric carbon free; sucrose's glycosidic bond consumes both anomeric carbons at once, leaving none.

$$\boxed{\text{Glucose — sucrose has no free anomeric carbon to react.}}$$

**The condition that actually matters.** "It's a disaccharide" does not by itself mean "non-reducing" — maltose is a disaccharide of two glucose units, but its glycosidic bond ties up only ONE of the two anomeric carbons, leaving the other free. Maltose is therefore a reducing sugar and does give a positive Fehling's test, unlike sucrose. Whether a sugar reduces Fehling's reagent depends on counting free anomeric carbons directly, not on whether the molecule happens to be a monosaccharide or a disaccharide.
