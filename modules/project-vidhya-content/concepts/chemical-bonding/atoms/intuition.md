A **covalent bond** is a shared pair of electrons holding two atoms together; an **ionic bond** is a complete electron transfer, so the two resulting ions attract each other simply because they carry opposite charge.

**Formal charge** checks whether a proposed Lewis structure is a reasonable way to distribute a molecule's electrons: $\text{FC} = (\text{valence electrons}) - (\text{lone-pair electrons}) - \dfrac{\text{bonding electrons}}{2}$. It is a bookkeeping check, not the atom's actual charge.

**VSEPR**, **hybridisation**, and **molecular orbital (MO) theory** are three separate tools answering three separate questions, in this order of what each one is even trying to do: VSEPR predicts the molecule's overall *shape* by treating every electron pair (bonding and lone) as repelling every other pair equally; hybridisation names *which atomic orbitals mix* on the central atom to actually produce that shape's bond angles; MO theory goes further still, combining atomic orbitals into bonding and antibonding molecular orbitals to predict *bond order and stability* — including whether the molecule has unpaired electrons (making it magnetic), something neither VSEPR nor hybridisation can predict at all.
