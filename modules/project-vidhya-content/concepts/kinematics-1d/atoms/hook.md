**Set-up:** a ball is thrown straight up from the ground with an initial speed $u=20$ m/s. Take upward as the positive direction, so the acceleration due to gravity acts as $a=-10$ m/s$^2$ (it points downward, opposite to the direction we chose as positive). This choice of positive direction and origin is the **frame of reference and sign convention** — fixed once, before any formula is used.

With that in place, the velocity at any time $t$ is $v(t) = u+at = 20-10t$ m/s. At $t=0$, $v=20$ (moving up fast). At $t=2$, $v=0$ (momentarily at rest, the highest point). At $t=4$, $v=-20$ (moving down, back at the ground) — the same speed as it started with, only the sign has flipped, because a *negative* velocity in this frame means "moving downward," not "an error."
