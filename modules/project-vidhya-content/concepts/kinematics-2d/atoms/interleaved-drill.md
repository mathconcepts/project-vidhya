**Cross-concept check: kinematics-2d → newtons-laws.**

**Question 1 (a kinematics quantity becomes a force):** A stone of mass $m=0.2$ kg is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius $r=0.5$ m at constant speed $v=4$ m/s, using this concept's own centripetal-acceleration result $a_c=v^2/r=32$ m/s$^2$. What force must the string provide to keep the stone moving in this circle?

*Answer:* By Newton's second law, $F=ma_c=0.2\times32=6.4$ N, directed along the string, toward the centre — this is the **tension** the string must supply. Circular motion is not a new force by itself; it is a description of *acceleration*, and Newton's second law is what turns that acceleration into a required force.

**Question 2 (what happens without the force):** If the string suddenly snaps, which direction does the stone fly off in — toward the centre, away from the centre, or along the tangent to the circle at that instant?

*Answer:* Along the tangent. With no more centripetal force acting, Newton's first law takes over: the stone continues in a straight line in whatever direction it was moving the instant the string broke — which is tangent to the circle, not radially outward and not toward the centre.

**Why this drill exists:** every "find the tension/force needed for circular motion" problem is this concept's $a_c=v^2/r$ result, fed straight into $F=ma$ — nothing new about circular motion needs to be learned for the dynamics half, only Newton's second law applied to an acceleration already known.
