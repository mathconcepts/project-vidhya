$0.01\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$ (which fully dissociates into $3$ ions: $2\text{K}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-}$) is dissolved in $1\ \text{kg}$ of water, alongside a separate $0.01\ \text{mol}$ sample of glucose in another $1\ \text{kg}$ of water. Which solution has the LARGER freezing-point depression, and by roughly what factor?

A) $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$'s, by a factor of about $3$
B) $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$'s, by a factor of about $2$
C) Glucose's, since it is a "stronger" molecule
D) They are equal, since the moles dissolved are equal
E) Cannot be determined without knowing molar masses

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$'s, by a factor of about $3$.**

$\Delta T_f=iK_fm$. Glucose: $i=1$. $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$: $i=3$ (three ions per formula unit, fully dissociating). Same $m$, same $K_f$ for both — so $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$'s $\Delta T_f$ is exactly $3$ times glucose's.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) $2$ undercounts $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$'s ions — it gives THREE particles per formula unit ($2\text{K}^++\text{SO}_4^{2-}$), not two.
- C) is wrong — colligative properties do not care which solute is "stronger" in any chemical sense, only how many particles it produces.
- D) ignores that colligative properties depend on particle count, not moles of FORMULA UNITS dissolved.
- E) is wrong — molar mass never enters this comparison, since both are already given in moles, not mass.

</details>
