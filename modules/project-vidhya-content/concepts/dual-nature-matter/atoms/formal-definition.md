**Photon energy**: $E=hf=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, where $h$ is Planck's constant, $f$ is frequency, $c$ is the speed of light, and $\lambda$ is wavelength. In electron volts (eV — the energy one electron gains crossing a $1$-volt potential difference) with $\lambda$ in nanometres, $E\approx\dfrac{1240}{\lambda(\text{nm})}$ eV.

**Work function** $\phi$: the least energy needed to free one electron from a metal's surface. Equals $hf_0$, where $f_0$ is the **threshold frequency** — the lowest frequency that can eject any electron at all.

**Einstein's photoelectric equation**: $KE_{max}=hf-\phi$, valid only for $f\ge f_0$. For $f<f_0$, no photoelectron is emitted, however bright the light.

**Stopping potential** $V_0$: the reverse voltage that just stops the fastest photoelectrons from reaching the collector plate; $eV_0=KE_{max}=hf-\phi$, so $V_0=\dfrac{h}{e}f-\dfrac{\phi}{e}$ — a straight line in $f$ with slope $h/e$, the same for every metal.

**Photon momentum**: $p=\dfrac{h}{\lambda}=\dfrac{E}{c}$.

**De Broglie wavelength**: for any moving particle of momentum $p=mv$ (mass times velocity), $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$. For an electron accelerated from rest through potential $V$, $p=\sqrt{2meV}$, giving $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}\approx\dfrac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}}$ angstrom (one angstrom $=10^{-10}$ m) — valid only for an electron, since $m$ and $e$ are its own mass and charge.
