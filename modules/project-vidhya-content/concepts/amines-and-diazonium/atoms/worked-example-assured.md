**Problem:** Make propan-1-amine, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$. Pick the correct starting material for Hofmann degradation, and separately for Gabriel synthesis.

---

**Step 1 — Apply each rule directly.** Hofmann loses one carbon (carbonyl carbon leaves as carbonate): start from butanamide (4C). Gabriel loses no carbon: start from 1-bromopropane (3C).

$$\boxed{\text{Hofmann: butanamide. Gabriel: 1-bromopropane. Both give propan-1-amine.}}$$

**The trap in treating "same target" as "same starting carbon count".** "Same target amine" does not mean "same starting material carbon count" — the two mechanisms remove a carbon differently, one (Hofmann) and zero (Gabriel), and treating the two routes as interchangeable in their carbon bookkeeping is the error. A sharper trap: Gabriel synthesis cannot make an AROMATIC primary amine like aniline at all, whatever carbon count is chosen — the phthalimide anion does not displace a halide from an aryl carbon the way it does from an alkyl one. Aniline has to come from reducing nitrobenzene instead. "Which mechanism, on which class of halide" decides the route far more than carbon-counting alone.
