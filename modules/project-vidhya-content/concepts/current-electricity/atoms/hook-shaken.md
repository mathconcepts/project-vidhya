A cell has $\text{emf}=12\ \text{V}$ and internal resistance $r=1\ \Omega$, connected to an external resistor $R=5\ \Omega$.

Find the current: $I=\dfrac{\text{emf}}{R+r}=\dfrac{12}{5+1}=2\ \text{A}$.

Now find the terminal voltage — what a voltmeter across the cell actually shows: $V_{\text{terminal}}=\text{emf}-Ir=12-(2)(1)=10\ \text{V}$.

$12\ \text{V}$ was written on the cell. $10\ \text{V}$ is what the voltmeter shows. The $2\ \text{V}$ difference is exactly $Ir$ — the voltage used up pushing current through the cell's own internal resistance, before any of it ever reaches the external circuit.
