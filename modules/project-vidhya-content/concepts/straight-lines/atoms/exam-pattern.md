**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **Family of lines, angle bisectors, and the pair-of-straight-lines equation are not part of the Tamil Nadu Class 11 syllabus.** Samacheer Kalvi's coordinate geometry chapter covers the standard forms and the angle-between-two-lines formula, and stops there. Everything from family of lines onward is genuinely new material here, worth its own dedicated practice rather than a quick revision pass.

- **MCQ family-of-lines questions always hide the extra condition in the wording.** "passes through $(2,3)$", "parallel to...", "perpendicular to..." — find that phrase first; it is the one thing that fixes $\lambda$, and reading the two given lines alone will not.

- **NAT questions on angle bisectors usually want one coefficient, not the full equation.** "The coefficient of $x$ in the acute angle bisector" is a single number pulled out after you have already decided which bisector is acute — do the acute/obtuse test before hunting for that number, not after.

- **Trap: forgetting the same-sign check on $c_1,c_2$ before applying the $a_1a_2+b_1b_2$ rule.** Skip it and the acute/obtuse labels come out swapped, with no arithmetic error anywhere to catch it.

- **MCQ "does this equation represent a pair of straight lines" tests two conditions, not one.** $\Delta=0$ alone is not enough — a set with $\Delta=0$ but $h^2<ab$ is a standard wrong-option trap, since it describes two imaginary lines, not two you can draw.

- **Time budget:** an acute-bisector question with clean, equal normalisers (like $5$ and $5$ here) runs $2$–$3$ minutes end to end, including the acute/obtuse check. If the normalisers come out as messy surds, budget closer to $4$ minutes and expect the final answer to carry a surd too — a suspiciously clean final answer from surd normalisers is worth a re-check.
