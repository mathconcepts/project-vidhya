A catalyst added to $A \rightleftharpoons B$ makes the bottle reach its fizzy balance *faster* — it never changes where that balance actually sits.

A catalyst lowers the activation energy for the forward *and* reverse reactions by the same amount, so both rates speed up equally. Since $K_c$ is just the ratio of those two rate constants at equilibrium, an equal speed-up on both sides leaves that ratio, and so $K_c$ itself, completely unchanged. Only the *time* to reach equilibrium shrinks — the equilibrium concentrations, and $K_c$'s value, are identical with or without the catalyst.

**Le Chatelier's Principle** predicts *which direction* a stress (concentration, pressure, temperature change) shifts the position — it is a prediction rule read off the stress, not an explanation of the molecular mechanism causing that shift.
