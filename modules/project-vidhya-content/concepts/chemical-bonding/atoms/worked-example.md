**Problem:** Using molecular orbital theory, find the bond order of the nitrogen molecule, $\text{N}_2$, given that it has 10 bonding electrons and 4 antibonding electrons. State whether this matches a single, double, or triple bond.

---

**Step 1 — Count what the formula actually needs: bonding electrons and antibonding electrons, separately.** Given: bonding electrons $=10$, antibonding electrons $=4$.

---

**Step 2 — Apply the bond order formula.** $\text{Bond order} = \dfrac{\text{bonding} - \text{antibonding}}{2} = \dfrac{10-4}{2} = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3$.

$$\boxed{\text{Bond order} = 3}$$

---

**Step 3 — Interpret the number.** A bond order of 1 means a single bond, 2 a double bond, 3 a triple bond. Bond order 3 here matches $\text{N}\equiv\text{N}$, a triple bond — consistent with nitrogen gas's own very high bond enthalpy and its general unreactiveness at room temperature.

---

**Why this needed MO theory and not just a Lewis structure count.** A Lewis structure alone gives the bond order by counting shared electron pairs directly — for $\text{N}_2$ it also gives 3, so the two methods agree here. The real value of MO theory shows up in a different case, $\text{O}_2$ (bond order 2 from MO theory, but with 2 electrons left unpaired) — a fact a plain Lewis structure, which pairs every electron by default, cannot predict at all.
