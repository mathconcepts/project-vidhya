# Group Theory — Visual Analogy

## Rotations of a Square: The Symmetry Group

Consider a square lying flat. You can rotate it by multiples of 90° and it looks the same. These rotations form a group $(\{R_0, R_{90}, R_{180}, R_{270}\}, \circ)$ where $\circ$ is "apply one rotation after another."

```
     R0:    R90:    R180:   R270:
     ┌─┐    ┌─┐     ┌─┐     ┌─┐
     │ │ →  │ │ →   │ │ →   │ │
     └─┘    └─┘     └─┘     └─┘
     (0°)  (90°)  (180°)  (270°)
```

**Cayley table for this group (add angles mod 360°):**

```
  ∘   | R0   R90  R180  R270
------+------------------------
R0   | R0   R90  R180  R270
R90  | R90  R180 R270  R0
R180 | R180 R270 R0    R90
R270 | R270 R0   R90   R180
```

**Checking the four axioms:**

- **Closure:** Rotating twice always gives another rotation in the table.
- **Associativity:** Rotate by (90° then 90°) then 180° = rotate by 90° then (90° then 180°). Yes — rotation composition is always associative.
- **Identity:** $R_0$ does nothing — rotating by 0°.
- **Inverse:** $R_{90}$'s inverse is $R_{270}$ (they cancel to $R_0 = 360°$).

This group is **isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_4$** — a cyclic group of order 4, generated by $R_{90}$.

## Lagrange's Theorem: Slicing the Group Evenly

Think of the group as a pizza with $|G|$ slices. Subgroups are ways to cut the pizza where every cut gives the same-sized piece.

```
   Group G: pizza with 12 slices
   Possible subgroup sizes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (divisors of 12)
   
   |H|=4 gives 12/4 = 3 equal cosets (three 4-slice pieces)
   |H|=6 gives 12/6 = 2 equal cosets (two 6-slice halves)
```

**Lagrange's theorem says:** you can ONLY cut a group into equal-sized cosets if the subgroup size **divides** the group order. You cannot have a subgroup of size 5 in a group of size 12.

## Cosets: Parallel Rows in a Grid

The cosets of $H$ in $G$ look like **parallel parking lanes**. If $H = \{0, 3\}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_6$:

```
H   = {0, 3}   ← the "home lane" (subgroup)
1+H = {1, 4}   ← shift every element by 1
2+H = {2, 5}   ← shift every element by 2
```

The three lanes together tile all of $\mathbb{Z}_6 = \{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$:
- Each lane has 2 elements (= $|H|$).
- No element appears in two lanes.
- All elements are covered.

This is Lagrange's theorem made visible: 3 cosets × 2 elements each = 6 = $|\mathbb{Z}_6|$.

## Cyclic Groups: Clock Arithmetic

$\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ under addition mod 12 is the **clock group** — generated by 1 (one tick at a time):

```
 12 (=0)
11      1
 10    2
   9  3
    8 4
     7 5
      6
```

Starting from 0, repeatedly adding 1 visits every hour: $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to \cdots \to 11 \to 0$. One generator, all 12 positions reached — **cyclic**.

**Order of an element:** How many steps to get back to 12 o'clock?
- Start at 4, step by 4: $4 \to 8 \to 0$. Three steps → $\text{ord}(4) = 3$.
- Start at 6, step by 6: $6 \to 0$. Two steps → $\text{ord}(6) = 2$.

The pattern: $\text{ord}(k)$ in $\mathbb{Z}_n$ equals $n / \gcd(n, k)$.
