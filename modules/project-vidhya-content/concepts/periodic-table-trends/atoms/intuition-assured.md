A dip in ionisation enthalpy does NOT mean the atom got bigger, or that the general period trend broke down everywhere — atomic radius keeps shrinking right through the dip, unaffected. Missing this is exactly how marks get lost here.

Counterexample: beryllium's covalent radius is about 111 pm, boron's is about 88 pm — boron is smaller, exactly as the period trend for radius predicts, right where ionisation enthalpy goes the other way (899 down to 801 kJ/mol). Smaller radius should mean a tighter hold and a higher enthalpy; it does not here, because radius and ionisation enthalpy are driven by different structural facts. Radius depends on effective nuclear charge and shell number alone. Ionisation enthalpy additionally depends on which specific subshell the outermost electron sits in — filled $2s^2$ versus lone $2p^1$, or half-filled $2p^3$ versus paired $2p^4$. Two trends that usually move together can decouple at exactly the two points where subshell occupancy changes character, and only there.
