The coefficients in $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$ are a **mole** ratio, not a mass ratio — 2 mol Mg to 1 mol O$_2$, never 2 g to 1 g.

Read the equation as "2 g of Mg reacts with 1 g of O$_2$" and every downstream number is wrong, even though nothing about the equation itself looks incorrect. Burning 2.4 g of Mg genuinely needs 1.6 g of O$_2$ (via 0.1 mol Mg $\to$ 0.05 mol O$_2$), not 1.2 g from a naive $2.4 \div 2$ mass-ratio guess.

The fix is always the same: convert every given mass to moles first, apply the equation's mole ratio, then convert the answer's moles back to mass — mass never gets compared to mass directly across a chemical equation.
