**Problem:** A ball is thrown vertically upward from the ground with initial speed $u=20$ m/s. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. Find (a) the time to reach maximum height, (b) the maximum height, (c) the total time of flight, and (d) the velocity when it returns to the ground.

---

**Step 0 — Set up the physical situation, frame, and sign convention before any formula.** Origin at the ground, where the ball is thrown from. Take **upward as positive**. Then the initial velocity is $u=+20$ m/s, and the acceleration due to gravity is $a=-g=-10$ m/s$^2$ (it always points downward, opposite to our chosen positive direction, whichever way the ball is moving).

---

**Step 1 — Time to maximum height.** At the highest point the ball is momentarily at rest, $v=0$. Using $v=u+at$: $0 = 20 + (-10)t \Rightarrow t = 2$ s.

---

**Step 2 — Maximum height.** Using $s=ut+\tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ at $t=2$ s: $s = 20(2) + \tfrac{1}{2}(-10)(2)^2 = 40-20=20$ m.

---

**Step 3 — Total time of flight.** The ball returns to the ground when $s=0$ again (same origin): $0 = 20t - 5t^2 = t(20-5t)$, so $t=0$ (the start) or $t=4$ s (the return). Time of flight $=4$ s.

---

**Step 4 — Velocity on return.** Using $v=u+at$ at $t=4$ s: $v = 20 + (-10)(4) = 20-40=-20$ m/s.

$$\boxed{t_{\text{up}}=2\text{ s},\ \ h_{\max}=20\text{ m},\ \ T=4\text{ s},\ \ v_{\text{return}}=-20\text{ m/s}}$$

---

**Reading the sign, not just the number.** $v_{\text{return}}=-20$ m/s is not a mistake — the negative sign, under our upward-positive convention, means the ball is moving *downward* at $20$ m/s, exactly the speed it started with. Its magnitude matches $u$ by the up-down symmetry of motion under constant gravity; the sign is what tells you the direction has reversed.
