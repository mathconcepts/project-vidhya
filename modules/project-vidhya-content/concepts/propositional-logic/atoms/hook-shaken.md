Take one rule: if it rains, the ground is wet.

Today it did not rain. Is the rule broken?

No. The rule only promises something for rainy days. On a dry day the rule is automatically kept — true, by definition, whether or not the ground happens to be wet anyway.

That is the hardest row of the implication table: $P$ false, $Q$ anything, result is always **true**. Two of the four rows work this way.
