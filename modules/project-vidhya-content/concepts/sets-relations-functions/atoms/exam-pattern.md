**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: the domain of a composition.** JEE hands you $f$ and $g$ with a restricted domain on at least one of them, and asks for the domain of $f \circ g$ or $g \circ f$. The formula for $f(g(x))$ is usually the easy part; the actual test is remembering to intersect it with where $g(x)$ is allowed inside $f$'s domain.

- **NAT: number of onto functions.** "Number of onto functions from a set of $m$ elements to a set of $n$ elements" is a fixed cue phrase for inclusion-exclusion counting, not for listing functions by hand. Recognising the phrase is most of the work.

- **Trap: equivalence class counting.** Count classes by finding one representative pattern per class (a remainder, a ratio, a sign) rather than checking every pair for reflexive/symmetric/transitive by brute force — brute force is correct but far too slow under time pressure.

- **Trap: relation given as a graph or a table.** JEE sometimes gives a relation as a set of dots on a grid rather than a formula. Read off the ordered pairs directly from the picture before testing reflexive/symmetric/transitive — do not try to guess a formula that was never intended.

- **Time budget:** a straightforward domain-or-range identification question should take under 45 seconds — read off the natural restrictions (denominator $\ne 0$, expression under a root $\ge 0$, argument of a log $> 0$) directly, rather than re-deriving them from first principles.
