"A negative velocity means the object is slowing down" is a common but wrong reading — sign tells you *direction* under the chosen convention, never speeding up or slowing down. For the ball thrown at $u=20$ m/s (upward positive, $a=-10$ m/s$^2$), $v=-20$ m/s at $t=4$ s is moving *downward*, and it is *speeding up* on the way down, exactly as it was slowing down on the way up.

Counterexample that separates the two ideas cleanly: an object with velocity $v=-20$ m/s and acceleration $a=-10$ m/s$^2$ (both negative — same direction) is speeding up, not slowing down. Slowing down happens only when velocity and acceleration have *opposite* signs, whatever those signs individually are.
