**Oxidation and reduction**: oxidation is loss of electrons (oxidation number increases); reduction is gain of electrons (oxidation number decreases). A redox reaction is balanced by ensuring electrons lost equal electrons gained.

**Electrochemical cell**: converts chemical energy to electrical energy (galvanic/voltaic cell, spontaneous reaction) or electrical energy to chemical energy (electrolytic cell, non-spontaneous reaction driven by an external source).

**Anode and cathode**: the anode is always where oxidation happens; the cathode is always where reduction happens. In a galvanic cell the anode is negative and the cathode is positive; in an electrolytic cell (driven by an external battery) the anode is positive and the cathode is negative.

**Standard electrode potential $E^\circ$**: the potential of a half-cell measured against the standard hydrogen electrode (defined as exactly $0\ \text{V}$), under standard conditions ($1\ \text{M}$ concentration, $1\ \text{atm}$ pressure, $298\ \text{K}$).

**Standard cell potential**: $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}$, using both as reduction potentials.

**Nernst equation** (relates cell potential to non-standard concentrations): $E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \dfrac{RT}{nF}\ln Q$, where $n$ is moles of electrons transferred in the balanced cell reaction and $Q$ is the reaction quotient. At $298\ \text{K}$, this becomes $E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \dfrac{0.0591}{n}\log_{10}Q$.

**Gibbs free energy link**: $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ — a positive $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ always corresponds to a negative $\Delta G^\circ$ (spontaneous).

**Faraday's laws of electrolysis**: the mass deposited (or liberated) at an electrode is directly proportional to the charge passed, $Q=It$; the amount is also given by $\text{moles of substance} = \dfrac{Q}{nF}$, where $n$ here is electrons needed per ion (e.g. $n=2$ for depositing $\text{Cu}$ from $\text{Cu}^{2+}$).
