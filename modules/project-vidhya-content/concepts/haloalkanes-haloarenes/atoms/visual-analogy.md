Picture a revolving door with only one open side, and someone trying to walk straight through from the opposite direction while a person is already exiting through it. If there is empty space directly behind the exiting person, the newcomer can walk in exactly as the first person walks out — one clean, single motion. That is $S_N2$: the nucleophile walks in from directly behind, exactly as the leaving group walks out, in one step.

Now picture a crowded doorway with three people already standing shoulder to shoulder blocking the space behind whoever is about to leave. Nobody can walk straight through from behind at all. Instead, the person leaving has to step out on their own first, opening up a gap — and only afterwards can someone new step into that gap, from whichever side happens to be free. That is $S_N1$: the leaving group departs alone first, and the nucleophile only arrives afterwards, from either side.

Which situation a given doorway is in depends entirely on how crowded it already is — exactly the way a carbon's substitution mechanism depends on how many groups already crowd around it.
