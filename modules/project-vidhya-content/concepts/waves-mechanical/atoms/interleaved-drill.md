**Cross-concept check: waves-mechanical → oscillations-shm.**

**Question 1 (every particle in a wave is secretly doing SHM):** A transverse wave $y(x,t)=A\sin(kx-\omega t)$ has amplitude $A=0.02$ m and frequency $f=50$ Hz. Find the maximum speed of any one particle of the medium as the wave passes through it.

*Answer:* Each particle of the medium oscillates in SHM with amplitude $A$ and angular frequency $\omega=2\pi f=2\pi(50)\approx314.16$ rad/s — exactly the oscillations-shm formula, borrowed directly. Maximum particle speed is $v_{max}=A\omega=(0.02)(314.16)\approx6.28$ m/s. This is a particle speed, not the wave's own travel speed — the two remain unrelated numbers even inside this single calculation.

**Question 2 (maximum acceleration, same particle):** Find the maximum acceleration of that same particle.

*Answer:* $a_{max}=A\omega^2=(0.02)(314.16)^2\approx1973.9\ \text{m/s}^2$ — again the plain SHM formula, using the wave's own $\omega$ as the SHM system's angular frequency.

**Why this drill exists:** a wave is not a separate kind of motion from SHM — each particle the wave passes through genuinely performs SHM, with the wave's own $\omega$ standing in directly for the oscillator's angular frequency. Treating wave motion and SHM as unrelated chapters misses that one is built entirely out of the other.
