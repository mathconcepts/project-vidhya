Ten people walk into a room and shake hands with some — but not all — of the others. Add up how many hands each person shook. That total always comes out even, no matter who shook whose hand, no matter how the party unfolds. This isn't luck; it's a genuine mathematical certainty, provable in three lines, and it applies to every graph, not just parties. Vertices are people, edges are handshakes, and degree is how many hands each person shook. The pattern this reveals — the handshaking lemma — feeds directly into nearly every result graph theory builds afterward.
