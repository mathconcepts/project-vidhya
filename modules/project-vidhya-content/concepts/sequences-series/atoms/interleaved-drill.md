**Cross-concept check: sequences and series → quadratic equations.**

**Question 1 (sequences and series):** The roots of $x^2-12x+a=0$ are in the ratio $1:2$. Using the ratio, write the two roots as a single unknown multiple of each other, and find their sum and product in terms of that unknown.

*Answer:* Let the roots be $p$ and $2p$ (ratio $1:2$). Sum $=3p$, product $=2p^2$.

**Question 2 (quadratic equations):** Using the sum and product from Question 1, find $a$.

*Answer:* For $x^2-12x+a=0$, sum of roots $=12$ and product of roots $=a$ (from the standard relations sum $=-\frac{\text{coeff of }x}{\text{coeff of }x^2}$, product $=\frac{\text{constant}}{\text{coeff of }x^2}$).

From Question 1: $3p=12\Rightarrow p=4$, so the roots are $4$ and $8$.

$$a = pq = 4\times8 = 32$$

**Check:** $x^2-12x+32=0$ has discriminant $144-128=16$, roots $\dfrac{12\pm4}{2}=8,4$ — matches exactly.

**Why this drill exists.** "The roots are in a given ratio" is a sequences-flavoured way of describing two numbers, and it converts a quadratic-equations problem into simple algebra the moment you write the roots as $p$ and $kp$ instead of two separate unknowns — one variable instead of two.
