A neon sign glows a fixed, bright colour — not a mix of every colour, one specific one. That single colour is direct evidence for something odd about atoms: their electrons cannot sit at just any energy, only at specific, fixed levels — like a ladder with rungs at fixed heights, never a ramp.

Bohr's model gives those rungs a formula for hydrogen: $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV, where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ is the **principal quantum number** (which shell the electron sits in). An electron falling from $n=2$ down to $n=1$ releases a photon carrying *exactly* $E_1 - E_2$, no more and no less — and that fixed energy shows up as a fixed colour of light, never a smear across the spectrum.

This is the first real clue that an atom is not the tiny solar system it is often drawn as — it is something quantised, rung by rung.
