Walk across Period 2 from lithium to neon and one number should climb steadily: how much energy it takes to pull the outermost electron away, called ionisation enthalpy (in kilojoules per mole). More protons are pulling on the same shell at every step, so the number should just keep rising.

It does not. Between beryllium and boron, and again between nitrogen and oxygen, that steady climb dips down before climbing again. The periodic table is not breaking its own rule here — it is telling you something sharper: the rising trend comes from nuclear pull, but the arrangement of electrons inside a shell can win a short, local fight against that pull. Finding the exact structural reason behind each dip is what this topic is really about.
