**Enthalpy** ($H$) tracks heat exchanged at constant pressure — a negative $\Delta H$ (exothermic) is not, by itself, proof that a reaction happens on its own. Plenty of exothermic reactions still need a spark to start, and some endothermic ones (like the cold pack dissolving) happen instantly, unaided.

**Hess's Law** says $\Delta H$ for an overall reaction is the same no matter which path (which sequence of intermediate steps) is taken to get from reactants to products — because enthalpy is a **state function**, depending only on the starting and ending states, not the route between them. This lets $\Delta H$ for a hard-to-measure reaction be built from the $\Delta H$ of easier-to-measure steps that add up to it.

**Entropy** ($S$) measures disorder; the second law says total entropy (system plus surroundings) increases for any spontaneous process. Combining both effects into one system-only number, **Gibbs free energy**, $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$, gives the actual spontaneity test: $\Delta G<0$ spontaneous, $\Delta G=0$ at equilibrium, $\Delta G>0$ non-spontaneous — at constant temperature and pressure.
