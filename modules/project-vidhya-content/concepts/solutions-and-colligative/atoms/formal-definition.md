**Colligative properties**: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, and osmotic pressure — properties that depend only on the NUMBER of solute particles present, not their chemical identity.

**Raoult's law** (ideal solution): $p_{\text{solution}} = x_{\text{solvent}}\,p^\circ_{\text{solvent}}$, where $x_{\text{solvent}}$ is the mole fraction of solvent and $p^\circ$ is the pure solvent's vapour pressure.

**Relative lowering of vapour pressure**: $\dfrac{p^\circ-p_s}{p^\circ} = i\,x_{\text{solute}}$, where $x_{\text{solute}}$ is the solute's mole fraction.

**Boiling-point elevation**: $\Delta T_b = i\,K_b\,m$, where $K_b$ is the solvent's molal boiling-point elevation constant and $m$ is molality (moles of solute per kilogram of solvent).

**Freezing-point depression**: $\Delta T_f = i\,K_f\,m$, where $K_f$ is the solvent's molal freezing-point depression constant.

**Osmotic pressure**: $\Pi = iCRT$, where $C$ is molarity (moles of solute per litre of SOLUTION).

**Van't Hoff factor**: $i = \dfrac{\text{actual number of particles in solution}}{\text{number of formula units dissolved}}$. For a non-electrolyte, $i=1$. For a strong electrolyte fully dissociating into $n$ ions, $i=n$. For a weak electrolyte dissociating to degree $\alpha$ into $n$ ions, $i=1+(n-1)\alpha$. For a solute that associates (e.g. dimerises) with degree $\alpha$ into groups of size $p$, $i=1-\alpha\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p}\right)$.
