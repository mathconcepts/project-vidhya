Treating "$-\text{OH}$" as one fixed acidity, adjusted a little by whatever sits nearby, badly understates how far apart these compounds actually sit.

Two comparisons pull in opposite directions. Among ordinary alcohols, adding *more* carbon around the $-\text{OH}$ makes it *less* acidic — tert-butanol gives up its proton far less readily than methanol does. Attach that same $-\text{OH}$ to an aromatic ring instead, and acidity jumps the other way entirely: phenol is roughly a million times more acidic than either alcohol.

Two different neighbours, two completely different mechanisms behind the shift — one from where extra carbon pushes electron density, the other from where a ring can spread a negative charge out.
