$\text{Zn}^{2+}/\text{Zn}$ has a standard electrode potential $E^\circ = -0.76\ \text{V}$. $\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}$ has $E^\circ = +0.34\ \text{V}$.

In the cell $\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}$, copper is reduced (gains electrons) — copper is the **cathode**. Zinc is oxidised (loses electrons) — zinc is the **anode**.

The cell potential is $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} = 0.34 - (-0.76) = 1.10\ \text{V}$.

Check: a positive $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ means this reaction runs on its own, releasing electrical energy — exactly what is observed when zinc metal is dropped straight into copper sulfate solution, with electrons flowing spontaneously from zinc to copper.
