**Paired concept: Binomial Theorem.**

The $\binom{n}{r}$ in the binomial distribution's formula is the exact same coefficient the binomial theorem produces when expanding $(a+b)^n$ — counting "how many ways can $r$ successes be arranged among $n$ trials" is the same counting problem as "what is the coefficient of $a^r b^{n-r}$."

**Drill**: Without computing either from scratch, explain why the coefficient of $x^3$ in $(1+x)^5$ and the value $\binom{5}{3}$ appearing in $P(X=3)$ for a binomial distribution with $n=5$ trials must be the same number. Then compute that shared number and verify it equals $10$.
