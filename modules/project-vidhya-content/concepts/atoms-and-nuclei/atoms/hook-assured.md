This concept's exam giveaway is the phrase "large-angle scattering" or "back-scattering" — both name Rutherford's experiment and the nuclear model, never Bohr's orbits (a separate, later idea about where electrons sit once the nucleus already exists).

One reading trips up otherwise-solid preparation: taking "most alpha particles pass through undeflected" as the whole refutation of the plum-pudding model, missing the specific fact that actually breaks it. It is not the average deflection that matters — it is the mere existence of any large-angle bounce at all. Charge smeared evenly through an atom's whole volume cannot produce even one $90°+$ deflection, however gently most particles happen to bend on average.
