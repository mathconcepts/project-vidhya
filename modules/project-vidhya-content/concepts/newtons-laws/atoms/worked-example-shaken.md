**Problem:** A block of mass $m_1=3$ kg rests on a frictionless horizontal table. It is connected by a light, inextensible string over a frictionless, massless pulley at the table's edge to a hanging block of mass $m_2=2$ kg. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

---

**Step 1 — Draw two separate free-body diagrams, one per block.** For $m_1$: positive direction is "toward the pulley." The only horizontal force on it is tension $T$, pulling it toward the pulley. For $m_2$: positive direction is "downward." Two forces act on it: gravity $m_2g$ (downward) and tension $T$ (upward). Because the string cannot stretch, both blocks move with the same magnitude of acceleration $a$, each in its own positive direction.

---

**Step 2 — Write Newton's second law for $m_1$.** $T = m_1a$.

---

**Step 3 — Write Newton's second law for $m_2$.** $m_2g - T = m_2a$.

---

**Step 4 — Add both equations together to remove $T$.** $T + (m_2g-T) = m_1a+m_2a$, which simplifies to $m_2g = (m_1+m_2)a$.

---

**Step 5 — Solve for $a$.** $a = \dfrac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2} = \dfrac{2(10)}{3+2} = \dfrac{20}{5}=4$ m/s$^2$.

---

**Step 6 — Substitute back into Step 2 to find $T$.** $T=m_1a=3(4)=12$ N.

$$\boxed{a = 4\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad T = 12\ \text{N}}$$

---

**Step 7 — Confirm with the other block's equation.** $m_2g-T=2(10)-12=20-12=8$ N. Separately, $m_2a=2(4)=8$ N. Both sides land on $8$, so the answer is consistent with *both* free-body diagrams, not only the one used to solve for $a$.
