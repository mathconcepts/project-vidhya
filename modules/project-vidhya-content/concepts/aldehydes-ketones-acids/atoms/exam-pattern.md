**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: "which of these gives Cannizzaro" style.** The moment you see a list of carbonyl compounds and a strong base, check each one's alpha-hydrogen count first, before reading the answer choices. Only the ones with zero alpha-hydrogens are even candidates.

- **MCQ: distinguishing tests.** A question naming Tollens', Fehling's, or the iodoform test is really asking "aldehyde or ketone?" or "does this carbon carry a $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH(OH)-}$ or $\text{CH}_3\text{-CO-}$ fragment?" (the iodoform test) — read the reagent name as a direct question about structure, not a fact to memorise in isolation.

- **Trap: acidity-ordering questions mix up which effect wins.** A question ranking several substituted carboxylic acids by acidity is testing whether an electron-withdrawing group (raises acidity) is being confused with an electron-donating one (lowers it) — and whether the effect is closer to or farther from the $\text{-COOH}$ group, since inductive effects weaken quickly with distance along the chain.

- **Trap: assuming ketones behave like aldehydes in oxidation questions.** Ketones resist mild oxidising agents (Tollens', Fehling's) precisely because there is no hydrogen left on the carbonyl carbon to remove during oxidation — an option claiming a ketone gives a positive silver-mirror test is built to catch this.

- **Time budget:** an alpha-hydrogen check is a 10-15 second glance at the structure — do it before reading any answer option, never after.
