**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: rank the stability of given carbocations, radicals or carbanions.** Count neighbouring donating groups for the first two; remember carbanions reverse the order entirely, since alkyl groups destabilise an already electron-rich centre instead of helping it.

- **MCQ: identify the type of isomerism between two given structures.** Check the carbon skeleton first (same or different — chain isomerism), then the functional group (same or different — functional isomerism), then where the group sits on an otherwise identical skeleton (position isomerism). Working through this order in the given sequence avoids picking the wrong label.

- **Trap: hyperconjugation and resonance get mixed up under time pressure.** Hyperconjugation always involves a $\text{C-H}$ (or $\text{C-C}$) sigma bond overlapping into an empty p-orbital; resonance always moves through an already-existing, connected pi system or lone pair. If no pi system is drawn anywhere near the effect being asked about, it is hyperconjugation, not resonance.

- **Trap: assuming a carbocation rearrangement is optional or always happens.** It happens if and only if the shift leads to a genuinely more stable carbocation. JEE distractors are built by offering the un-rearranged product as one option and the rearranged one as another — checking the destination's stability, every time, decides between them.

- **Time budget:** a stability-ranking NAT or MCQ should take under 30 seconds — count neighbouring carbons once, rank, done. Do not attempt to draw out full resonance structures unless the question is specifically about resonance stabilisation.
