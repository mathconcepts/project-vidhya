Take a steel wire, cross-section $A=1\text{ mm}^2=10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$, original length $L_0=1\text{ m}$.

Hang a $200\text{ N}$ load on it. It stretches by $\Delta L=1\text{ mm}=10^{-3}\text{ m}$.

Stress: $\sigma = F/A = 200/10^{-6} = 2\times10^8\text{ Pa}$.

Strain: $\epsilon = \Delta L/L_0 = 10^{-3}/1 = 10^{-3}$ (a pure number, no unit).

**Young's modulus**: $Y = \sigma/\epsilon = (2\times10^8)/(10^{-3}) = 2\times10^{11}\text{ Pa}$ — matching steel's real value.

This whole formula, $\sigma=Y\epsilon$, only works up to the **elastic limit**. Load the wire far more heavily and it stops springing back at all — the straight-line relationship between stress and strain simply ends.
