Two small charged spheres, each carrying $+2\ \mu\text{C}$ ($\mu\text{C}$ is a microcoulomb — one-millionth of a coulomb, the unit of electric charge), sit $10\ \text{cm}$ apart in air. They push each other apart with a force of $3.6\ \text{N}$ — roughly the weight of a large mango.

Now pull them apart to $20\ \text{cm}$ — double the distance. Guess before reading on: does the push become half as strong, at $1.8\ \text{N}$?

It does not. The force drops to $0.9\ \text{N}$ — a **quarter** of the original, not a half. Coulomb's law says the force between two point charges falls as the **square** of the distance between them, not the distance itself. Double the gap, and the push weakens four times over.
