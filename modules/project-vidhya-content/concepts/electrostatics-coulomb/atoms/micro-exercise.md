Two point charges, $q_1=3\ \mu\text{C}$ and $q_2=6\ \mu\text{C}$, are placed $0.3\ \text{m}$ apart in air. What is the force of repulsion between them? (Take $k=9\times10^9\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$.)

A) $0.54\ \text{N}$
B) $1.8\ \text{N}$
C) $18\ \text{N}$
D) $5.4\ \text{N}$
E) $0.18\ \text{N}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $1.8\ \text{N}$.**

$F = \dfrac{k q_1 q_2}{r^2} = \dfrac{9\times10^9 \times 3\times10^{-6} \times 6\times10^{-6}}{(0.3)^2} = \dfrac{9\times10^9 \times 18\times10^{-12}}{0.09} = 1.8\ \text{N}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $0.54\ \text{N}$ comes from dividing by $r=0.3$ directly instead of $r^2=0.09$ — forgetting to square the distance at all.
- C) $18\ \text{N}$ comes from misplacing a power of ten while multiplying the two microcoulomb charges together ($10^{-11}$ used instead of the correct $10^{-12}$).
- D) $5.4\ \text{N}$ comes from computing $r^2$ as $0.3\times0.1=0.03$ instead of $0.3\times0.3=0.09$.
- E) $0.18\ \text{N}$ comes from misplacing the decimal point while squaring $r$, using $r^2=0.9$ instead of $0.09$.

</details>
