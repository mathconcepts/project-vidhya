- **MCQ**: general-solution questions are almost always presented with the answer already in one of the standard $n\pi$/$2n\pi$ forms as an option — matching the SHAPE of your final answer to an option is often faster than simplifying it further by hand.
- **Time**: a quadratic-in-$\sin\theta$ or quadratic-in-$\cos\theta$ problem should take under $90$ seconds once you spot the substitution. If you are still expanding brackets after two minutes, you likely picked the wrong identity to start from.
- **Trap**: JEE frequently hides the quadratic, e.g. $2\cos^2\theta+3\sin\theta=3$, forcing you to rewrite $\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta$ before the quadratic in $\sin\theta$ becomes visible at all.
- **NAT**: when the answer is a count of solutions (for example, "how many solutions in $[0,2\pi]$"), generate them one at a time from the general formula by substituting consecutive integers for $n$, rather than trying to visualise the whole graph at once.
- **Trap**: sine and cosine rule questions sometimes give two sides and a non-included angle (the "SSA" case). This is the ambiguous case and can produce two valid triangles — check whether both are geometrically possible before picking one answer.
