Take $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ at Earth's surface, radius $R=6400\text{ km}$.

At height $h=R$ above the surface (so distance from centre is $r=2R$), the field is $g'=g(R/r)^2 = 9.8(1/2)^2 = 9.8/4 = 2.45\text{ m/s}^2$ — a quarter of the surface value, exactly matching the inverse-square rule.

Now check potential energy. $U=-GMm/r$ is always **negative**, becoming less negative (closer to zero) as $r$ grows, and reaching exactly zero only at $r=\infty$. This is different from the near-surface formula $U=mgh$, which is always positive and only approximates the real formula for small $h$.

For a satellite in circular orbit at this same $r=2R$: gravity provides the centripetal force, $GMm/r^2 = mv^2/r$, giving $v=\sqrt{GM/r}$. Plugging in $r=2R$ and using $GM=gR^2$ gives $v=\sqrt{gR^2/(2R)}=\sqrt{gR/2}=5600\text{ m/s}$.
