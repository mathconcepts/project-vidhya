**Problem:** Make propan-1-amine ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$, three carbons) by Hofmann degradation. Which amide do you start from?

---

**Step 1 — Know the rule: Hofmann degradation loses one carbon.** The carbonyl carbon of the amide leaves as $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$. So the starting amide needs ONE MORE carbon than the target.

---

**Step 2 — Apply it.** Target has 3 carbons. Amide needed: 4 carbons. That is butanamide, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CONH}_2$.

---

**Step 3 — Check by counting the product.** Remove the carbonyl carbon from butanamide's chain: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{-}$ is left, now bonded to $\text{NH}_2$.

$$\boxed{\text{Butanamide} \rightarrow \text{propan-1-amine, 3 carbons — matches the target.}}$$

**Now compare Gabriel synthesis for the same target.** Gabriel keeps every carbon of the starting halide — no carbon is lost. So the halide needed is 1-bromopropane (3 carbons, same as the target), not 1-bromobutane. The two methods need different starting-material carbon counts for the identical target amine.
