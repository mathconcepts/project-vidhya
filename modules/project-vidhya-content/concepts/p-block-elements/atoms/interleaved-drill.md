**Cross-concept check: p-block-elements → d-and-f-block.**

**Question 1 (variable oxidation states, two different causes):** Both thallium (p-block) and manganese (d-block) show more than one common oxidation state. State which subshell is responsible for each, and why the two situations are not the same phenomenon.

*Answer:* Thallium's variable states ($+1$ and $+3$) come from whether its outermost $s$-electron pair joins bonding or stays put — the inert pair effect, a $p$-block phenomenon tied to poor shielding of an $s$-pair by inner $d$/$f$ electrons. Manganese's variable states ($+2$ up to $+7$) come from how many of its partly filled $3d$ electrons actually take part in bonding — a completely different subshell, with no inert-pair-style reluctance involved. Two elements, two multi-valued oxidation stories, two unrelated structural causes.

**Question 2 (predict which compound is the stronger oxidiser):** Between $PbO_2$ (lead, $+4$) and $KMnO_4$ (manganese, $+7$), which behaves as an oxidising agent for the same underlying reason — "wanting" to fall to a more comfortable lower state?

*Answer:* Both are strong oxidisers, but only $PbO_2$'s reason is the inert pair effect — lead's $+4$ state is uncomfortable because its $s$-pair would rather stay unbonded, so $PbO_2$ readily grabs electrons and drops to $Pb^{2+}$. $KMnO_4$'s $+7$ manganese is a strong oxidiser for an unrelated reason: a $d^0$ configuration with no remaining $d$-electrons to stabilise, not any reluctant $s$-pair. Two oxidisers, both wanting to be reduced, for two different structural reasons.

**Why this drill exists:** "variable oxidation state" sounds like one idea, but p-block-elements and d-and-f-block reach it through completely different subshells. Confusing the two — quoting the inert pair effect to explain manganese, say — is a genuine, exam-relevant mix-up this drill exists to head off.
