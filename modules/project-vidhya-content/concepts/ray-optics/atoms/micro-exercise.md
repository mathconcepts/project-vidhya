A convex mirror has $f=15\text{ cm}$. An object is placed $30\text{ cm}$ in front of it. What is the image distance, and is the image real or virtual?

A) $v=10\text{ cm}$, virtual
B) $v=-10\text{ cm}$, real
C) $v=10\text{ cm}$, real
D) $v=30\text{ cm}$, virtual
E) $v=-30\text{ cm}$, real

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $v=10\text{ cm}$, virtual.**

Convex mirror: $f=+15\text{ cm}$. Object: $u=-30\text{ cm}$. $\dfrac{1}{v}=\dfrac{1}{f}-\dfrac{1}{u}=\dfrac{1}{15}-\dfrac{1}{-30}=\dfrac{1}{15}+\dfrac{1}{30}=\dfrac{3}{30}=\dfrac{1}{10}$, so $v=+10\text{ cm}$. Positive $v$ for a mirror means the image forms *behind* the mirror — virtual, exactly as expected for a convex mirror with any real object.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) has the right magnitude but the wrong sign, and wrongly labels it "real" — a positive $v$ for a mirror is never real.
- C) has the right magnitude and sign but the wrong label — positive $v$ for a mirror is virtual, not real.
- D) and E) both use $u$'s own magnitude, $30\text{ cm}$, as if it were the answer, skipping the formula entirely.

</details>
