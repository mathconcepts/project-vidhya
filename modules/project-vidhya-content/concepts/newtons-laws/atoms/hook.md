**Set-up:** a block of mass $m_1=3$ kg sits on a frictionless table, connected by a light string over a frictionless pulley at the table's edge to a hanging block of mass $m_2=2$ kg. Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$. Before writing a single equation, fix the **frame and sign convention**: for $m_1$, positive is "toward the pulley"; for $m_2$, positive is "downward" — the two blocks are linked by the same string, so whatever speed one gains, the other gains too, in its own positive direction.

Two free-body diagrams, one equation each: for $m_1$, only the string's tension $T$ pulls it (positive direction), giving $T=m_1a$. For $m_2$, gravity $m_2g$ pulls down while the string's tension $T$ pulls up, giving $m_2g-T=m_2a$. Two equations, two unknowns ($T$ and $a$) — solvable together without needing anything beyond Newton's second law applied once to each block.
