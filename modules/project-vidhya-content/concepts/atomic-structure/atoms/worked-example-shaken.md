**Problem:** Using Bohr's model, find the energy (in eV) of the photon emitted when an electron in a hydrogen atom falls from $n=2$ to $n=1$.

---

**Step 1 — Write the formula down first.** $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV. It needs one number, $n$, plugged in at a time.

---

**Step 2 — Plug in $n=1$.** $E_1 = -\dfrac{13.6}{(1)^2} = -\dfrac{13.6}{1} = -13.6$ eV.

---

**Step 3 — Plug in $n=2$.** $E_2 = -\dfrac{13.6}{(2)^2} = -\dfrac{13.6}{4} = -3.4$ eV.

---

**Step 4 — Subtract: starting level minus landing level.** The electron starts at $n=2$ and lands at $n=1$, so compute $E_2 - E_1$: $-3.4 - (-13.6) = -3.4 + 13.6 = 10.2$ eV.

$$\boxed{10.2 \text{ eV}}$$

**Check:** the answer should be positive, since energy is *released* (given off), and $10.2$ is indeed positive. If the subtraction had come out negative, the order was done backwards.
