Two lighthouses shine circular beams of different reach over the same stretch of sea — one beam described by $S_1=0$, the other by $S_2=0$. A ship somewhere on the water can measure a "power" number for each lighthouse: how far outside (or inside) that lighthouse's own circular beam it currently sits, which is exactly what $S_1$ and $S_2$ compute at the ship's coordinates.

The line where the ship's power reading is IDENTICAL for both lighthouses — $S_1=S_2$, i.e. $S_1-S_2=0$ — is the radical axis. It is always a straight line, and it always runs perpendicular to the line joining the two lighthouses, no matter how differently sized their beams are. Where the two beams genuinely overlap, this same line passes right through both crossing points — the common chord is just the radical axis, viewed on the stretch of sea where both beams actually reach.
