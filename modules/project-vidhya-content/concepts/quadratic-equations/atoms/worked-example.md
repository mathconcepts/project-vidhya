**Problem:** Find all values of $k$ for which $2x^2+kx-5=0$ and $x^2-3x-4=0$ have a common root.

---

**Step 1 — Solve the equation without $k$ first.** $x^2-3x-4=0$ factors as $(x-4)(x+1)=0$, so its roots are $x=4$ and $x=-1$. Any common root must be one of these two specific numbers — there is nothing else it could be.

---

**Step 2 — Check both possibilities separately.** A common root means *one* of these two numbers also satisfies the first equation. There are two genuinely separate cases to check, not one.

---

**Step 3 — Case $x=4$.** Substitute into $2x^2+kx-5=0$: $2(16)+4k-5=0 \Rightarrow 32+4k-5=0 \Rightarrow 4k=-27 \Rightarrow k=-\dfrac{27}{4}$.

---

**Step 4 — Case $x=-1$.** Substitute: $2(1)+k(-1)-5=0 \Rightarrow 2-k-5=0 \Rightarrow -k-3=0 \Rightarrow k=-3$.

---

**Step 5 — State both answers.** $\boxed{k = -3 \text{ or } k = -\dfrac{27}{4}}$ — two separate values, one for each way the equations can share a root. Neither case makes the two equations identical to each other, so both are genuine, distinct common-root situations.
