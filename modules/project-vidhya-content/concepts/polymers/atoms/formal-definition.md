**Polymer**: a large molecule built from many small, repeating units (monomers) joined together.

**Addition polymerisation**: monomers with a double bond join directly, with no small molecule lost. Example: ethylene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2$) monomers give polythene, $-(\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2)_n-$.

**Condensation polymerisation**: monomers join with the loss of a small molecule (commonly water) at each bond formed. Example: hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid give nylon-6,6, releasing one water molecule per amide bond formed.

**Copolymer**: a polymer built from two or more different monomers. Buna-S (butadiene + styrene) and Buna-N (butadiene + acrylonitrile) are addition copolymers; nylon-6,6 (a diamine + a diacid) is a condensation copolymer.

**Natural rubber**: cis-polyisoprene, obtained from the latex of the rubber tree (Hevea brasiliensis).

**Vulcanisation**: heating natural rubber with sulfur, which forms cross-links (sulfur bridges) between neighbouring polymer chains. Cross-linked rubber can stretch and recoil elastically because no single chain can permanently slide past its neighbours.

**Biodegradable polymer**: a polymer broken down by microorganisms in the environment. Examples: PHBV (a copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid) and nylon-2-nylon-6 (an alternating polyamide copolymer of glycine and amino caproic acid).
