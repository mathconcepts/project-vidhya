**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: predict the mechanism and stereochemical outcome given a substrate and a reagent.** Check the substrate class first (rules out one mechanism outright for a primary or tertiary carbon), then check the reagent (strong nucleophile versus strong base, protic versus aprotic solvent) to pick between what remains.

- **MCQ: predict major and minor alkene products with a bulky base.** A bulky base cannot reach a crowded hydrogen easily, so it takes the more accessible one, giving the less substituted (Hofmann) alkene as major — the opposite of the ordinary Zaitsev outcome with a small base.

- **MCQ: rank the reactivity of alkyl versus aryl halides toward nucleophilic substitution.** Aryl halides sit far below any alkyl halide, regardless of substrate class, because of resonance and the ring's steric block — never rank them alongside alkyl halides using substrate class alone.

- **Trap: assuming Grignard reagent formation works on any halide containing other functional groups.** Any $-\text{OH}$, $-\text{NH}$, or $-\text{COOH}$ group on the same molecule destroys the Grignard reagent the instant it forms, well before it can react with anything else.

- **Time budget:** a mechanism-identification MCQ should take under 40 seconds — classify the substrate, check the reagent, done. Save full mechanism-drawing for questions that explicitly ask for the stereochemical outcome.
