Take oxygen, atomic number 8 — 8 electrons to place.

Fill the lowest energy orbitals first (Aufbau): $1s$ holds 2, $2s$ holds 2. That is 4 electrons placed, 4 left, going into $2p$.

$2p$ has three orbitals of equal energy ($2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$). Hund's rule says: put one electron in each before pairing any. So the first 3 of the remaining 4 electrons go in one per orbital — $2p_x{\uparrow}$, $2p_y{\uparrow}$, $2p_z{\uparrow}$.

The 4th and final electron has no empty $2p$ orbital left, so it must pair up with one of the three, spin flipped by the Pauli exclusion rule (opposite spin from its partner): $2p_x{\uparrow\downarrow}$, $2p_y{\uparrow}$, $2p_z{\uparrow}$.

Full configuration: $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$. Two electrons ($2p_y, 2p_z$) remain unpaired — this is exactly why oxygen is paramagnetic.
