Capacitor A ($2\ \mu\text{F}$, $\mu\text{F}$ is a microfarad, the unit of capacitance) is charged to $6\ \text{V}$ and then disconnected from everything. Capacitor B ($4\ \mu\text{F}$) is charged separately to $12\ \text{V}$ and disconnected too. Now their positive plates are joined together, and their negative plates are joined together.

Guess before reading on: does the shared voltage end up somewhere between $6\ \text{V}$ and $12\ \text{V}$, closer to the middle, like an average?

It does not land at the simple average of $9\ \text{V}$. The actual shared voltage comes out to $10\ \text{V}$ — because charge, not voltage, is what stays conserved when two charged capacitors are joined. The bigger capacitor was carrying far more charge at its own voltage, and that charge has to be accounted for, not the voltages averaged.
