**Problem:** Arrange beryllium, boron and carbon in order of increasing first ionisation enthalpy.

---

**Step 1 — Write down the real numbers first, in kJ/mol.** Be = 899. B = 801. C = 1086. Compare these three numbers directly: $801 < 899 < 1086$.

---

**Step 2 — Read off the order.** So the order is B first, then Be, then C — Be comes after B, even though Be sits to Boron's left in the period.

---

**Step 3 — Check why, using electron configuration.** Be: $1s^22s^2$, the $2s$ subshell completely full. B: $1s^22s^22p^1$, the extra electron moved into a new $2p$ subshell.

---

**Step 4 — Compare stability.** A filled subshell resists losing an electron more than one holding a single new electron in a higher orbital. So B's electron comes off more easily than Be's, confirming $IE(B) < IE(Be)$.

---

**Step 5 — Final order and check.** $\boxed{IE(B) < IE(Be) < IE(C)}$. $B \to C$ and $Be \to C$ go the expected direction; only $Be \to B$ flips, crossing $2s^2$ into a fresh $2p$ subshell.
