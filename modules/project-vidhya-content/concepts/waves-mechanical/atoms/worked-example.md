**Problem:** An ambulance siren emits sound at $f=500$ Hz. It approaches a stationary bystander at $v_s=20$ m/s. Speed of sound in air is $v=340$ m/s. Find the frequency the bystander hears (a) while the ambulance approaches, and (b) immediately after it passes and moves away at the same speed.

---

**Step 1 — Picture it first.** The bystander is fixed; only the source moves. Approaching squeezes the wavefronts closer together ahead of the ambulance, raising the frequency heard; receding stretches them apart, lowering it.

---

**Step 2 — Approaching: source moving toward a stationary observer, so $v_o=0$ and the denominator uses the minus sign.** $f'=f\dfrac{v}{v-v_s}=500\times\dfrac{340}{340-20}=500\times\dfrac{340}{320}\approx531.25$ Hz.

---

**Step 3 — Receding: source moving away, denominator now uses the plus sign.** $f'=f\dfrac{v}{v+v_s}=500\times\dfrac{340}{360}\approx472.22$ Hz.

---

**Step 4 — State both together.** $\boxed{f'_{approach}\approx531.25\ \text{Hz},\quad f'_{recede}\approx472.22\ \text{Hz}}$. The pitch drops noticeably the instant the ambulance passes — this sudden fall, not a rise, is the well-known "ambulance siren" effect, and it happens because the sign in the denominator flips the moment the source switches from approaching to receding.
