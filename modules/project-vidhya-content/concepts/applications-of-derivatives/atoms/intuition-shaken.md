Picture a graph as a hiking trail. Going left to right: climbing means positive slope, descending means negative slope. At the very top of a hill, for one instant, you are neither climbing nor descending — slope exactly zero. Check this with $h(t)=20t-5t^2$ from the hook: $h'(t)=20-10t$. At $t=2$ (the peak), $h'(2)=20-20=0$ — flat, exactly as the picture predicts. Before $t=2$, $h'(t)>0$ (climbing); after, $h'(t)<0$ (descending). That sign change, positive to negative, is what marks a maximum.

The tangent line is the trail's straight-line stand-in at one exact point; its slope is the derivative there, by definition. The normal line is that same stand-in rotated a quarter turn — perpendicular to the trail's direction, so its slope is the tangent's negative reciprocal, not the same number.
