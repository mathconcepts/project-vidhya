A simple pendulum of length $L=1$ m swings with a small angle on Earth ($g=9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$). If the length is instead quadrupled to $4$ m, by what factor does the time period change?

A) $4$
B) $2$
C) $\sqrt{2}$
D) unchanged
E) $16$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) $2$.**

$T=2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, so $T\propto\sqrt{L}$. Quadrupling $L$ means the period scales by $\sqrt{4}=2$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $4$ mistakes $T\propto L$ (a direct proportion) for the real relation $T\propto\sqrt{L}$.
- C) $\sqrt{2}$ would be the factor if $L$ had only doubled, not quadrupled.
- D) is wrong because period clearly depends on length here; only amplitude, not length, leaves period unchanged.
- E) $16$ squares the length ratio instead of taking its square root.

</details>
