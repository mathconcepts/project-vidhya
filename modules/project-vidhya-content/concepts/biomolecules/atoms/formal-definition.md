**Carbohydrate classification**: **monosaccharides** (glucose, fructose — cannot be broken into smaller sugar units by hydrolysis), **oligosaccharides** (2 to 10 monosaccharide units, e.g. sucrose and maltose are disaccharides), and **polysaccharides** (many units, e.g. starch, cellulose, glycogen).

**Glycosidic linkage**: the $\text{C-O-C}$ bond joining two monosaccharide units, formed with the loss of one water molecule per linkage.

**Reducing vs non-reducing sugar**: a sugar with a free anomeric carbon (able to open into a chain with a free aldehyde or ketone group) reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents — glucose and maltose are reducing sugars. Sucrose has both monosaccharide units' anomeric carbons tied up in the glycosidic bond, leaving no free reducing group, so it is a non-reducing sugar.

**Amino acid**: carries both an amine group ($\text{-NH}_2$) and a carboxylic acid group ($\text{-COOH}$) on the same (alpha) carbon. Exists as a **zwitterion** at physiological pH — the amine group protonated to $\text{-NH}_3^+$, the acid group deprotonated to $\text{-COO}^-$, overall neutral.

**Peptide bond**: $\text{-CO-NH-}$, formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amine group of another, with loss of one water molecule per bond. A chain of $n$ amino acids has $n-1$ peptide bonds.

**Protein structure, four levels**: primary (the amino acid sequence), secondary (local folding held by hydrogen bonds, e.g. the alpha helix and beta sheet), tertiary (the full three-dimensional folded shape), and quaternary (multiple folded chains, called subunits, assembled together — haemoglobin has four).

**Enzyme**: a biological catalyst, almost always a protein, that lowers the activation energy of a specific reaction without being consumed by it.

**Nucleic acids**: DNA uses deoxyribose sugar and the bases adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine, arranged as a double helix; RNA uses ribose sugar and the base uracil in place of thymine, and is usually single-stranded. Base pairing is by hydrogen bonds: adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, guanine pairs with cytosine through three.
