Capacitor A: $C_1=2\ \mu\text{F}$, charged to $V_1=6\ \text{V}$. Its charge: $Q_1=C_1V_1=2\times6=12\ \mu\text{C}$ ($\mu\text{C}$ is a microcoulomb).

Capacitor B: $C_2=4\ \mu\text{F}$, charged to $V_2=12\ \text{V}$. Its charge: $Q_2=C_2V_2=4\times12=48\ \mu\text{C}$.

Join positive plate to positive plate. Total charge is conserved: $Q_{\text{total}}=Q_1+Q_2=12+48=60\ \mu\text{C}$. Total capacitance once joined this way: $C_{\text{total}}=C_1+C_2=6\ \mu\text{F}$.

Shared voltage: $V=\dfrac{Q_{\text{total}}}{C_{\text{total}}}=\dfrac{60}{6}=10\ \text{V}$ — not the average of $6$ and $12$, which would have been $9\ \text{V}$.
