"A ring with alternating single and double bonds is aromatic" sounds like the whole story, but it leaves out the one condition that actually decides it: the electron *count* around that ring.

Counterexample: cyclobutadiene, $\text{C}_4\text{H}_4$, is drawn with alternating bonds exactly like benzene, in a ring exactly like benzene. It is not aromatic — it is **antiaromatic**, unusually *unstable*, because it carries only four pi electrons where the rule demands $4n+2$.

Alternating bonds only earn you a *conjugated* ring — a candidate. Whether that candidate is aromatic, antiaromatic, or neither still comes down to one count: pi electrons against $4n+2$.
