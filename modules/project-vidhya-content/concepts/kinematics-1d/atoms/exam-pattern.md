**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: pick the equation of motion that already skips the unknown you don't need.** JEE numericals on 1D motion almost always give exactly three of the five quantities ($s,u,v,a,t$) and ask for a fourth — scanning for which equation is missing the one quantity you were never given avoids an unnecessary extra step of solving for it first.

- **MCQ: velocity-time graph reading.** Given a $v$-$t$ graph (often piecewise-linear), find displacement over an interval (area under the graph) or acceleration at an instant (slope). The trap options are usually built by silently swapping which one — slope or area — the question is actually asking for.

- **Trap: sign convention must survive the whole problem.** A free-fall or vertical-throw problem set up with upward as positive must keep $g$ negative throughout; switching partway through (often because a later part of the question feels like it's "about falling") is the single most common source of a sign error here.

- **Trap: "returns to the ground" means $s=0$, not $v=0$.** $v=0$ locates the highest point only; a question asking for time of flight or landing speed needs the displacement condition, not the velocity condition.

- **Time budget:** a straightforward equation-of-motion NAT should take under $40$ seconds; a two-part vertical-throw question (height, then time of flight) should take under $90$ seconds once the sign convention is fixed at the start.
