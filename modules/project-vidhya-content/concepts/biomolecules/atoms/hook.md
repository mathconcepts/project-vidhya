Silk, wool, and cotton all feel different against skin, and the chemical reason has nothing to do with how they're woven. Silk and wool are built from long chains of **amino acids** joined end to end — they are proteins. Cotton is built from long chains of **glucose** units joined end to end instead — it is a polysaccharide, cellulose.

Two completely different building blocks, both strung into long chains, giving two completely different materials. Everything in this concept comes down to identifying which small building block — a sugar, an amino acid, a fatty piece, or a nucleotide — a given biomolecule is made from, and how those blocks link up into the chain.
