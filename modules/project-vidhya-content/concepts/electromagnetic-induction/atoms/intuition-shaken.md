Flux is field times area: $\Phi = B \times A$, when the field is straight through the coil's face.

Start: $\Phi_i = 0.6 \times 0.02 = 0.012\text{ Wb}$. End, $0.4$ s later: $\Phi_f = 0.2 \times 0.02 = 0.004\text{ Wb}$.

Change: $\Delta\Phi = 0.004 - 0.012 = -0.008\text{ Wb}$. Rate: $\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{-0.008}{0.4} = -0.02\text{ Wb/s}$.

Faraday's law: $\varepsilon = -\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt} = -(-0.02) = 0.02\text{ V}$ — matches the direct rate-times-area answer from before.

Check the sign by hand: flux went down, so $d\Phi/dt$ is negative, so the minus sign in the formula flips it to a positive emf. That positive emf drives current in the direction that fights the *drop* — meaning it adds to the fading field, not the direction that would simply cancel whatever field remains.
