**"Spread it out, give it up."** A charge that can spread out over several atoms (phenoxide, spread over the ring) forms easily, so the parent compound (phenol) gives up its proton easily. A charge stuck on one atom (alkoxide) resists forming, so the parent compound (an alcohol) holds onto its proton.

**"CHO from chloroform, COOH from carbon dioxide"** — Reimer-Tiemann's chloroform installs $-\text{CHO}$; Kolbe's carbon dioxide installs $-\text{COOH}$. Both land ortho to the $-\text{OH}$, no exceptions.

**Before cleaving an ether, ask: "is a tertiary carbon hiding here?"** If yes, the mechanism is $S_N1$ at that carbon, and the halide ends up there — not on the smaller carbon a first glance suggests.
