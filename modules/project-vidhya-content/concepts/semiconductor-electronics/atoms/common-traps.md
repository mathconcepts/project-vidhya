- **Treating an energy band as one sharp energy level**: a band is millions of extremely closely-spaced allowed energies acting as one continuous range, not a single value like an isolated atom's electron. "The exact energy of an electron in the conduction band" has no single answer — only a range does.

- **Assuming a doped semiconductor is electrically charged overall**: an n-type piece has extra free electrons, but also an equal number of extra fixed positive donor ions holding the crystal neutral. Doping changes which carrier conducts, never the total charge.

- **Confusing which side goes to the higher potential in forward bias**: forward bias connects the **p-side** to the higher potential (and n-side to the lower) — connecting it the other way is reverse bias, which widens the depletion region instead of narrowing it.

- **Inverting the band-gap-to-wavelength relation**: $\lambda_{max}=1240/E_g$ runs backward from how "bigger" feels — a *smaller* band gap gives a *longer* maximum detectable wavelength, reaching further into the infrared, not a shorter one.

- **Assuming a semiconductor behaves like a metal as it heats up**: a metal's conductivity *falls* with rising temperature (more atomic vibration disturbs the free electrons already there); a semiconductor's conductivity *rises* with temperature instead, since heat is what lets more electrons cross the band gap in the first place.
