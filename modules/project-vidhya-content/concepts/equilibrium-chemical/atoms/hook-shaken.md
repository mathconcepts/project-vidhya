For the reaction $A(g) \rightleftharpoons B(g)$, the equilibrium constant is given as $K_c = 4.0$ at a certain temperature.

At one instant (not necessarily at equilibrium), the concentrations are $[A]=2.0$ M and $[B]=3.0$ M.

Compute the reaction quotient using the same expression as $K_c$: $Q = \dfrac{[B]}{[A]} = \dfrac{3.0}{2.0} = 1.5$.

Compare: $Q(1.5)$ is less than $K_c(4.0)$.

When $Q<K_c$, the forward reaction (making more $B$) still has room to run — the reaction shifts forward, consuming $A$ and producing $B$, until $Q$ rises to meet $4.0$ and the system reaches true equilibrium.
