Computing $\Delta=dy/D$ and comparing it to $n\lambda$ says nothing about whether a *stable* fringe pattern exists at that point in the first place — that additionally needs $S_1$ and $S_2$ to be **coherent**, meaning their phase difference stays fixed over time.

Counterexample: replace the two slits with two separate light bulbs of the exact same wavelength, positioned at $S_1$ and $S_2$. The formula $\Delta=dy/D$ still produces a number, and comparing it to $n\lambda$ still classifies that instant as bright or dark — but no lasting pattern ever appears on the screen, because each bulb's light emits in random, independent bursts, so the relative phase between them drifts continuously and the bright/dark classification at any point flips too fast to see. A single source lighting both slits, as in the original setup, is what keeps that phase difference locked — without it, the path-difference formula is correct but describes nothing observable.
