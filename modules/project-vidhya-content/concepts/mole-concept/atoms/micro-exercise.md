How many moles are present in 11 g of carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$? (Molar mass of $\text{CO}_2 = 44$ g/mol.)

A) 0.11 mol
B) 0.25 mol
C) 0.44 mol
D) 4 mol
E) 44 mol

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) 0.25 mol.**

$n = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{molar mass}} = \dfrac{11}{44} = 0.25$ mol.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $0.11$ comes from dividing by $100$ instead of $44$ — a misplaced decimal.
- C) $0.44$ swaps the mass and molar mass roles ($44/100$-style slip).
- D) $4$ comes from computing $44/11$ — molar mass divided by mass, the fraction flipped upside down.
- E) $44$ is just the molar mass itself, restated with no division performed at all.

</details>
