Juggling the three equations of motion and reading slope/area off a $v$-$t$ graph is the easy half. One phrase trips up otherwise-solid students: "distance travelled in the $n$-th second" is *not* the velocity at $t=n$ — it is a displacement, $s_n=s(n)-s(n-1)$, with its own formula, $s_n=u+\dfrac{a}{2}(2n-1)$.

Take a scooter starting from rest ($u=0$) with $a=2$ m/s$^2$. The velocity at $t=3$ s is $v=at=6$ m/s — but the distance travelled *during* the third second (between $t=2$ and $t=3$) is $s(3)-s(2)=9-4=5$ m, using $s=\tfrac{1}{2}at^2$. Two genuinely different numbers, $6$ and $5$, both correct answers to two genuinely different questions. Treating "velocity at $t=n$" and "distance in the $n$-th second" as interchangeable produces an answer off by exactly this gap, and nothing in either formula warns you when it happens.
