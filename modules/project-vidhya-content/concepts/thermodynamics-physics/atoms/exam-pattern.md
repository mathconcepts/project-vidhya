**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: Carnot efficiency or COP from two given temperatures.** These are near-instant once temperatures are confirmed to be in kelvin — compute the ratio, subtract from $1$ (efficiency) or plug into the COP formula directly, without deriving anything from scratch.

- **MCQ: identify the process from a description, not a label.** A stem may describe "a fast compression in an insulated container" without ever writing the word "adiabatic" — recognising $Q=0$ from the description is the actual skill being tested, not memorising the word.

- **Trap: temperature given in Celsius.** Convert to kelvin before touching any thermodynamics formula — an unconverted Celsius value silently produces an "efficiency" that can even exceed $100\%$, a strong warning sign if it happens.

- **Trap: sign convention mismatch.** A question phrased in the chemistry style ("work done on the gas is...") mixed with a physics-style formula sheet is a real source of sign errors — check which convention the specific question is using before substituting.

- **Time budget:** an efficiency or COP NAT should take under $20$ seconds once temperatures are confirmed to be in kelvin. A first-law $\Delta U$ question should take under $30$ seconds — it is one substitution, not a derivation.
