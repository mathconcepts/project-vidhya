Dip a strip of zinc metal into a beaker of copper sulfate solution ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions dissolved in water) and something happens without any wire, switch, or battery involved: the zinc strip slowly dissolves, and a pinkish coating of copper metal builds up on it.

Electrons are moving — from zinc atoms to $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions — even though nothing here looks electrical. $\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$ (zinc losing electrons, called **oxidation**) happens right next to $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}$ (copper gaining them, called **reduction**). Put these two half-reactions in separate containers, connected by a wire, and those same electrons are forced to travel through the wire instead — and now there is a working **electrochemical cell**, turning a chemical reaction directly into electric current.
