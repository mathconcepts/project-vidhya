"Diazonium coupling needs an activated ring" reads as a complete rule — but "activated" is not simply "has a substituent". A ring carrying a strong electron-WITHDRAWING group, like nitrobenzene's $\text{-NO}_2$, is substituted too, yet it couples with a diazonium salt even less readily than plain benzene does.

The direction of electron push is what matters, not the presence of any group at all. $\text{-OH}$ and $\text{-NH}_2$ donate electron density into the ring by resonance, making it a better nucleophile for the diazonium ion's weak electrophile to find. $\text{-NO}_2$ pulls electron density out of the ring instead, leaving it even less reactive toward the same electrophile. "Has a substituent" and "is activated" are not the same claim — only checking which way the electrons move settles it.
