**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **Chord of contact, radical axis, family of circles, and orthogonal intersection sit outside the Tamil Nadu Class 11/12 circle chapter** — Samacheer Kalvi covers the general equation and tangent/normal in full, then stops. Everything else in this concept is genuinely new content here, not a quick refresher, and deserves practice on its own footing.

- **MCQ "which of these is the chord of contact" tests the outside-point condition, not just the algebra.** A wrong option built from an INTERIOR point's $T=0$ line looks structurally identical to a correct one — the giveaway is checking $S_1>0$ first, before trusting the substitution.

- **NAT questions on radical axis usually want one coefficient or a single point on the line, not the derivation.** Subtract the two circle equations, simplify once, and read off exactly what is asked — don't re-derive perpendicularity to the line of centres unless the question specifically wants it verified.

- **Trap: assuming $S_1+\lambda S_2=0$ covers every circle through the two intersection points.** It excludes $S_2=0$ itself, exactly the way $L_1+\lambda L_2=0$ excludes $L_2$ for lines — the same structural gap, one level up.

- **MSQ questions on orthogonal circles often pair the condition $2g_1g_2+2f_1f_2=c_1+c_2$ with a radius or centre fact in the same option set.** Check both halves of an option separately; a distractor frequently gets the orthogonality condition right but the centre or radius wrong, or the reverse.

- **Time budget:** chord of contact from a clean integer point runs $2$ minutes. A full radical-axis-plus-common-chord-length question, done carefully, runs closer to $4$–$5$ minutes — budget for it rather than rushing the perpendicular-distance step at the end.
