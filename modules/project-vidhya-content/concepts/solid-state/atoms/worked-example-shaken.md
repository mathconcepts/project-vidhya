**Problem:** A metal crystallises in a body-centred cubic (bcc) structure. Its density is $7.2\ \text{g/cm}^3$ and its molar mass is $52\ \text{g/mol}$. Find the edge length of its unit cell, in picometres.

---

**Step 1 — Identify $Z$ for bcc.** One corner contribution of $8\times\frac{1}{8}=1$, plus one full body-centred atom: $Z=2$.

---

**Step 2 — Write the density formula and rearrange for $a^3$.** $\rho=\dfrac{ZM}{N_Aa^3} \Rightarrow a^3=\dfrac{ZM}{N_A\rho}$.

---

**Step 3 — Substitute the numbers.** $a^3=\dfrac{2\times52}{(6.022\times10^{23})(7.2)}=\dfrac{104}{4.336\times10^{24}}$.

---

**Step 4 — Divide.** $a^3\approx2.40\times10^{-23}\ \text{cm}^3$.

---

**Step 5 — Take the cube root.** $a\approx2.88\times10^{-8}\ \text{cm}$.

---

**Step 6 — Convert centimetres to picometres.** $1\ \text{cm}=10^{10}\ \text{pm}$, so $a\approx2.88\times10^{-8}\times10^{10}=288\ \text{pm}$.

$$\boxed{a \approx 288\ \text{pm}}$$

---

**Step 7 — Check.** A few hundred picometres is a physically reasonable atomic spacing — the size is right, and the conversion did not leave a stray power of ten behind.
