In an electrolytic cell (electrolysis driven by an external battery), which statement correctly describes the electrode polarities?

A) Anode is negative, cathode is positive — same as a galvanic cell
B) Anode is positive, cathode is negative
C) Both electrodes are positive
D) Both electrodes are negative
E) Polarity cannot be assigned without knowing the electrolyte

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: B) Anode is positive, cathode is negative.**

An external battery forces electrons IN through the cathode (making it negative) and pulls electrons OUT through the anode (making it positive) — the opposite polarity to a galvanic cell, even though the anode is still where oxidation happens and the cathode is still where reduction happens, in both cell types.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) is the galvanic-cell polarity, applied to the wrong cell type — the exact reversal this question is testing.
- C) and D) are impossible: a cell always has one electrode of each sign, since current has to flow in a complete circuit.
- E) is wrong — the polarity is fixed by which terminal the external battery connects to, not by which electrolyte is used.

</details>
