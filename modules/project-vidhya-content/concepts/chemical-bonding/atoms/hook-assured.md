VSEPR, hybridisation, and molecular orbital (MO) theory are not three ways to explain the same fact about water — they answer three genuinely different questions.

VSEPR answers "what **shape**?" — bent, from 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs repelling. Hybridisation answers "what **orbital mixture** on oxygen reproduces that shape?" — $sp^3$, four equivalent hybrid orbitals pointing roughly tetrahedrally. MO theory answers a question VSEPR and hybridisation cannot: "is the resulting bond **stable**, and how many electrons are unpaired?" — via bond order $=\dfrac{\text{bonding} - \text{antibonding electrons}}{2}$.

Treating all three as interchangeable synonyms for "the bonding" is the actual trap: a molecule can have an unambiguous VSEPR shape while its MO-predicted bond order or magnetism is the genuinely new information neither of the other two theories provides.
