**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: equivalent capacitance of a network.** Reduce the network step by step, series pairs first (using $1/C_s=1/C_1+1/C_2$), then combine with any parallel branches — never apply the resistor formulas by habit.

- **MCQ: "two charged capacitors are connected" phrasing.** The moment a question says two *already charged* capacitors are joined, reach for charge conservation ($Q_{\text{total}}$ before = $Q_{\text{total}}$ after), not a voltage average — and check the plate polarity in the diagram before deciding whether charges add or subtract.

- **Trap: dielectric insertion, battery connected vs disconnected.** JEE distractors on "energy stored" after inserting a dielectric are built by silently swapping whether the battery stays connected. Battery connected → energy increases by factor $K$. Battery disconnected (isolated) → energy decreases by factor $K$. Check which case the question describes before picking a direction.

- **Trap: series-parallel formula swap.** A wrong option that looks exactly like the right one but uses the other combination's formula is the single most common distractor type in this concept.

- **Time budget:** a series/parallel reduction NAT should take under a minute for two or three capacitors — reduce one pair at a time rather than trying to combine the whole network in one step.
