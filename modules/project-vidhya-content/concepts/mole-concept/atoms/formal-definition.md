**Mole**: the SI unit for amount of substance. One mole of any entity (atoms, molecules, ions) contains **Avogadro's number** of that entity, $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$.

**Molar mass**: the mass, in grams, of one mole of a substance — numerically equal to the atomic mass (for an element) or molecular mass (for a compound) in amu. $n = \dfrac{\text{given mass}}{\text{molar mass}}$, where $n$ is the number of moles.

**Empirical formula**: the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound, found from percentage composition. **Molecular formula** = (empirical formula)$\times n$, where $n = \dfrac{\text{molecular mass}}{\text{empirical formula mass}}$.

**Stoichiometry**: using a balanced equation's mole ratios to relate the amounts of reactants and products.

**Limiting reagent**: the reactant that is completely used up first, fixing the maximum possible amount of product; every other reactant is left over.

**Molarity** ($M$): moles of solute per litre of *solution*. **Molality** ($m$): moles of solute per kilogram of *solvent* — a different denominator, so the two are not interchangeable.

**Percentage yield**: $\dfrac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100$.
