An AC supply has peak voltage $V_0 = 220\sqrt2\text{ V}$. What is its rms voltage?

A) $110\text{ V}$
B) $155.6\text{ V}$
C) $220\text{ V}$
D) $311\text{ V}$
E) $440\text{ V}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) $220\text{ V}$.**

$V_{rms} = V_0/\sqrt2 = (220\sqrt2)/\sqrt2 = 220\text{ V}$ — the $\sqrt2$ factors cancel directly, which is exactly why Indian household mains voltage is quoted as "$220\text{ V}$": that number already *is* the rms value, with peak voltage the larger, less-often-quoted $220\sqrt2 \approx 311\text{ V}$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) halves $220$ instead of dividing $220\sqrt2$ by $\sqrt2$ — a leftover reflex from an unrelated "half of peak" guess.
- B) multiplies $220$ by $\sqrt2/2$ inconsistently, landing on neither the peak nor the rms value.
- D) $\approx 220\sqrt2$ reports the *peak* value instead of converting it to rms.
- E) doubles $220$, mixing up rms with some unrelated peak-to-peak notion.

</details>
