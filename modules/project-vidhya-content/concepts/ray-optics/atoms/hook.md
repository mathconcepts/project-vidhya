A convex lens of focal length $20\text{ cm}$ sits on an optical bench. An object is placed $30\text{ cm}$ in front of it, on the left. Draw just two rays from the tip of the object: one travels parallel to the axis and bends through the focus on the far side; the other passes straight through the optical centre, undeviated. Where those two rays cross, on the far side of the lens, is the image.

Working this out with the lens formula, $\dfrac{1}{v}-\dfrac{1}{u}=\dfrac{1}{f}$, gives $v=60\text{ cm}$ — a real image, twice as far from the lens as the focal length, and magnified $2\times$, upside down. Every one of those facts — real, inverted, magnified, and exactly where — comes from one formula, provided the distances are fed into it with the right **sign**: measured from the lens, against the incoming light, an object distance is negative. Get that one sign wrong, and every answer after it is wrong too.
