**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: acceleration and tension in a connected-bodies system.** Blocks on a table connected to a hanging block, or two hanging blocks over a pulley (Atwood's machine) — draw one free-body diagram per block, apply Newton's second law to each, then solve the resulting pair of equations together. The system's acceleration is almost always $\dfrac{\text{net driving force}}{\text{total mass being accelerated}}$.

- **MCQ: friction — will it slide, and which direction does friction act.** Given an applied force and $\mu_s$, check whether the applied force exceeds $\mu_sN$ before assuming the object moves at all; friction opposes the *relative sliding tendency*, so its direction depends on which way the object would slide if friction were absent, not simply "opposite to the applied force" in every case.

- **Trap: normal force on an incline, or under an additional push, is not $mg$.** On a frictionless incline at angle $\theta$, $N=mg\cos\theta$, not $mg$ — a question testing friction or normal-force magnitude on an incline is specifically testing whether this adjustment was made.

- **Trap: sign convention must be applied identically to both bodies in a connected system.** JEE distractors for connected-bodies NATs are frequently built by flipping the sign of $a$ or $T$ in exactly one of the two free-body equations — the fix is re-deriving both equations from their own diagrams rather than pattern-matching a remembered formula.

- **Time budget:** a single free-body-diagram NAT (one block, one or two forces) should take under $45$ seconds; a two-body connected system should take under $90$ seconds once both diagrams are drawn correctly.
