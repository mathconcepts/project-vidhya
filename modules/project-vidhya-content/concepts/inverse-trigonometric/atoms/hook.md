$\tan^{-1}(1)$ is $45°$, a positive angle. $\tan^{-1}(2)$ is also positive, close to $63°$. Add them by common sense and you expect something near $108°$ — still positive, still under $180°$. Feed the same two numbers into the standard formula, $\tan^{-1}x+\tan^{-1}y=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x+y}{1-xy}\right)$, and it hands back $\tan^{-1}(-3)$ — a NEGATIVE angle, close to $-72°$. Adding two positive angles should never produce a negative one. The formula did not break; it was used outside the one condition that makes it true, and that condition is exactly where JEE sets its trap.
