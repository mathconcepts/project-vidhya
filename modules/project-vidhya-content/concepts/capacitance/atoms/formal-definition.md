**Capacitance**: $C = Q/V$, the charge stored per unit voltage across a capacitor's two plates.

**Parallel-plate capacitor**: $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$, where $A$ is the area of each plate, $d$ is the separation between them, and $\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space (a fixed constant).

**With a dielectric** (an insulating material with dielectric constant $K>1$, slid fully between the plates): $C' = KC$ — capacitance increases by the factor $K$.

**Series combination**: $\dfrac{1}{C_s} = \dfrac{1}{C_1}+\dfrac{1}{C_2}+\cdots$ — always smaller than the smallest individual capacitance. The charge $Q$ is the same on every capacitor in series.

**Parallel combination**: $C_p = C_1+C_2+\cdots$ — always larger than the largest individual capacitance. The voltage $V$ is the same across every capacitor in parallel.

**Energy stored**: $U = \dfrac{1}{2}CV^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}QV = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$.

**Charge redistribution** (two isolated, pre-charged capacitors connected together): total charge is conserved (added if like plates are joined, subtracted if unlike plates are joined), and the common final voltage is $V=\dfrac{Q_{\text{total}}}{C_1+C_2}$. Some energy is always lost as heat and radiation in this process — the energy after redistribution is never more than the energy before.
