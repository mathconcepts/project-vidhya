Integrate an analytic function from A to B along one curve. Now do it again along a wildly different curve. The answers agree. In real calculus that would be a coincidence; in the complex plane, on a region with no holes in it, it is Cauchy's theorem — and it turns integrals that look impossible into arithmetic. Below, though: put a hole in the region — a single point where $f$ is undefined — and that same guarantee can break entirely.
