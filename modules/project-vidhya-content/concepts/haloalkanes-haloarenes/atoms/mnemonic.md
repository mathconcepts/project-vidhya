**"1 for inversion, 1 for racemisation."** $S_N2$ is a ONE-step process and gives ONE clean, inverted spatial arrangement. $S_N1$ goes through a flat intermediate and gives a mix, since attack from either face is equally likely once the leaving group is already gone.

**"Crowded carbon, halide leaves alone first."** The more alkyl groups crowd the carbon, the harder backside attack becomes, and the more the reaction leans toward the halide simply leaving by itself before anything else happens.

**For elimination, remember: "small base picks the crowd, big base picks the gap."** A small, strong base removes whichever hydrogen gives the more substituted (Zaitsev) alkene — the "crowded," more stable one. A bulky base cannot reach that hydrogen and settles for a less hindered one instead, giving the less substituted (Hofmann) alkene.
