- **Treating VSEPR, hybridisation, and MO theory as interchangeable**: reaching for whichever one is remembered best, regardless of what the question actually asks. VSEPR answers "shape", hybridisation answers "orbital mixture", MO theory answers "bond order and magnetism" — using one to answer a question posed for another gives an answer to the wrong question, even if every number in it is computed correctly.

- **Counting bonding electrons as though each bond contributes only 1 electron in the formal-charge formula**: a single covalent bond has 2 bonding electrons total, and only half of that pair ($1$) is subtracted per bond in the formal charge formula — using the wrong count (2 instead of the correctly halved value, or vice versa) shifts every formal charge by a full unit.

- **Ignoring lone pairs when counting VSEPR's total electron-pair count**: a central atom's molecular *shape* only shows the bonded atoms, but VSEPR's underlying repulsion calculation needs *every* pair — bonding and lone — counted together first.

- **Assuming a higher bond order always means a physically shorter, stronger bond without checking which molecule is actually being compared**: this is generally true *within* a comparison of bonds between the same two elements, but is not a safe cross-element comparison rule on its own.

- **Forgetting that MO theory's antibonding electrons follow Hund's rule too**: filling all of $\text{O}_2$'s antibonding electrons paired up first, instead of one per orbital before pairing, hides the 2 genuinely unpaired electrons that explain its magnetism.
