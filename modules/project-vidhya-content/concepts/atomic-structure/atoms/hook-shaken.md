Use Bohr's formula for a hydrogen atom: $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV.

At $n=1$: $E_1 = -\dfrac{13.6}{1} = -13.6$ eV.

At $n=2$: $E_2 = -\dfrac{13.6}{4} = -3.4$ eV.

An electron falling from $n=2$ to $n=1$ releases energy equal to $E_2 - E_1 = -3.4 - (-13.6) = 10.2$ eV — a fixed, exact number, not a range.

This 10.2 eV comes out as a photon of one specific colour of light every single time this exact fall happens, in every hydrogen atom, because the two energy levels involved are always exactly $-3.4$ eV and $-13.6$ eV — never anything in between. That fixed-value jump, not a smooth slide, is what "quantised" means here.
