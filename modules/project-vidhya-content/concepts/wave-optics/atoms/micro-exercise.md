At a certain point on a YDSE screen, the path difference between the two waves works out to $2.5\lambda$. Is this point bright, dark, or neither?

A) Bright, $n=2$
B) Bright, $n=3$
C) Dark
D) Neither — $2.5\lambda$ is not a valid path difference
E) Cannot be determined without knowing $d$ and $D$ separately

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: C) Dark.**

$2.5\lambda = (2+\tfrac12)\lambda$ — exactly the destructive-interference condition $\Delta=(n+\tfrac12)\lambda$ with $n=2$. This point is dark.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) and B) both misread a half-integer multiple as if it satisfied the whole-number bright condition.
- D) is wrong because $2.5\lambda$ is a perfectly ordinary, valid path difference — it just happens to land on the dark condition rather than the bright one.
- E) is wrong because the classification only ever needs $\Delta$ compared to $\lambda$ — $d$ and $D$ individually are irrelevant once $\Delta$ itself is already known.

</details>
