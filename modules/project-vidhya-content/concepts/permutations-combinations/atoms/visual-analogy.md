Picking $3$ friends out of $5$ for a weekend outing is choosing a group — no one is "first friend" or "second friend", so the order of picking never mattered. That count is a **combination**.

Now take those exact same $3$ friends and decide who drives, who sits in front, and who sits in the back — three specific, distinguishable seats. Suddenly the same $3$ people give $3! = 6$ different seatings, because swapping who sits where genuinely changes the outcome. That count is a **permutation**.

Nothing about the friends changed between the two scenes — only the question changed, from "which group" to "which arrangement of that group". Every permutation-versus-combination confusion on this topic comes down to this single check: does swapping the order of two chosen items produce something the problem treats as different, or the same?
