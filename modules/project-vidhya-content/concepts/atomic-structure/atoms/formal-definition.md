**Bohr model (hydrogen only)**: energy of the $n$-th orbit, $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV; radius of the $n$-th orbit, $r_n \approx 0.529\,n^2$ Å.

**de Broglie relation**: matter has wave-like behaviour, $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$, where $h$ is Planck's constant and $mv$ is momentum.

**Heisenberg uncertainty principle**: position and momentum of a particle cannot both be known exactly at the same time: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \dfrac{h}{4\pi}$.

**Orbital**: a three-dimensional region of space around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is high — a probability region, not a fixed path.

**Quantum numbers**: $n$ (principal — shell, values $1,2,3,\dots$), $l$ (azimuthal — subshell shape, values $0$ to $n-1$, labelled $s,p,d,f$), $m_l$ (magnetic — orbital orientation, values $-l$ to $+l$), $m_s$ (spin, $+\tfrac{1}{2}$ or $-\tfrac{1}{2}$).

**Aufbau principle**: electrons occupy the lowest-energy available orbitals first.

**Pauli exclusion principle**: no two electrons in the same atom can have all four quantum numbers identical; an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spin.

**Hund's rule**: orbitals of equal energy (a subshell) are each filled singly, with parallel spin, before any is doubly occupied.
