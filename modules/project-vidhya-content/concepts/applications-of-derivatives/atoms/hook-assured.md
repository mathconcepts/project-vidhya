The ball's flight $h(t)=20t-5t^2$ returns to $h=0$ at $t=0$ and $t=4$ — equal endpoint heights, so Rolle's Theorem applies directly. Rolle's is not a separate rule to memorise on its own; it is the special case of the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem where the two endpoints happen to share the same value, forcing the secant line's slope to be exactly zero. Change either endpoint height and the guarantee does not vanish — it becomes MVT's more general promise, that some instant matches whatever the secant's slope actually is. Trace the flight below to see exactly where that instant sits.
