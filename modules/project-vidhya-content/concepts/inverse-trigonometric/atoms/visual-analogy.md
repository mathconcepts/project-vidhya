Think of $\tan^{-1}$ as a photograph of a clock hand that only ever shows the hand somewhere between "quarter to" and "quarter past" — a narrow slice of the full dial. If the real hand has actually swept much further around, past that narrow slice, the photograph still only shows you where it would be if you folded the extra distance back into that one slice.

Now imagine adding two separate hand-movements together. Each individual movement, photographed on its own, looks like it fits in the narrow slice. But their COMBINED movement might have swept the real hand well outside it — past "quarter past," into territory the photograph's slice cannot show directly. The photograph does not lie; it reports the position as if the hand had only travelled the extra distance modulo one half-turn. Recovering the real, total movement means recognising when this has happened and adding the missing half-turn back by hand — exactly what the $xy<1$ condition is checking for before you trust the formula's raw output.
