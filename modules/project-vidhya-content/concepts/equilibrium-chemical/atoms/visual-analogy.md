Picture two tanks of water connected by a pipe, water flowing both ways at once. Right after opening the connection, water rushes mostly one direction because the levels differ a lot. As the levels get closer, the *net* flow slows down — until both levels stop changing, even though water is still moving through the pipe both ways, just at equal rates now.

The reaction quotient $Q$ is like "how different the two levels currently are". As a reaction runs, $Q$ moves steadily toward $K_c$ (the level difference the system is heading for), and once $Q=K_c$, the net change stops — dynamic equilibrium, not a dead stop.
