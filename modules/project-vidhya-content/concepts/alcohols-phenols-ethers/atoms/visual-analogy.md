Picture one person holding an entire heavy bag alone versus four people, standing in a connected circle with linked arms, sharing the exact same weight between them. The lone carrier feels the whole load and wants to set it down as soon as possible — that is an alkoxide ion, a full negative charge sitting on one oxygen with nowhere else to go.

The four-person circle barely notices the weight; sharing it out makes each person's share small and comfortable. That is a phenoxide ion, where the ring lets the same charge spread over the oxygen and three ring carbons instead of piling it onto one spot. A comfortable, shared load is easy to pick up in the first place — which is exactly why phenol gives up its proton far more easily than a plain alcohol does.

An electron-withdrawing group at the right ring position is like a fifth person joining that circle specifically where the weight is heaviest, taking on an extra share of it — help that only counts if that person is actually standing next to the load.
