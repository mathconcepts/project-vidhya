"A higher orbit needs more energy, so it needs more speed" sounds obvious and is exactly half right. Total mechanical energy of an orbit ($E=-GMm/2r$) does increase (become less negative) as $r$ grows — but orbital *speed* ($v=\sqrt{GM/r}$) actually falls as $r$ grows.

The two don't move together because most of that extra energy goes into gravitational potential energy, not kinetic energy — climbing to $r=2R$ from $r=R$ costs energy even though the satellite ends up moving slower once it gets there and settles into a circular path. JEE distractors exploit exactly this: "the satellite must speed up to reach the higher orbit" is a trap built by confusing the transfer manoeuvre's energy cost with the final orbit's steady speed.
