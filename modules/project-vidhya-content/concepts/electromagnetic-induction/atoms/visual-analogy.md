Think of a heavily loaded shopping trolley rolling at a fixed speed. Push it to speed it up, and it resists the push — its **inertia** fights the change. Push it (from the front) to slow it down, and it resists that push just as hard, in the opposite sense. The trolley never "prefers" a direction; it only resists whatever *change* is being forced on its current state of motion.

Magnetic flux behaves the same way. Lenz's law is sometimes called the "law of inertia" for flux: a circuit resists a change in the flux passing through it, exactly like the trolley resists a change in its speed — never the field's direction itself. Flux being pushed up gets fought one way; flux being pushed down gets fought the *other* way, because both are the same thing from flux's point of view: something is trying to change what it currently has, and the induced current always leans against that change, whichever way it runs.
