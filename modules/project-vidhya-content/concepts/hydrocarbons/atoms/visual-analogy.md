Picture a roundabout (a traffic circle) with cars going around it smoothly, evenly spaced, none of them stuck. That smooth, unbroken flow is what a delocalised pi system feels like when the electron count fits — the cars (electrons) share the loop comfortably, and the whole circle is calmer than any one lane taken on its own would be.

Now imagine the exact same roundabout, same road, same number of lanes, but with one extra car squeezed in that does not fit the spacing at all. Traffic does not flow any more smoothly than a straight road would — if anything, it is worse, because cars keep bunching up in the same spot every time around. That mismatch, not the roundabout's shape, is what makes a ring **antiaromatic**: the shape (cyclic, flat, connected) is identical to the smooth case, but the electron count no longer fits the loop, and the ring ends up less comfortable than an open road (an open chain) would have been.

The roundabout's shape alone never tells you which case you have — only counting exactly how many cars are going around it does.
