"Charge is conserved when two charged capacitors are joined" is true, but *which* charge adds depends entirely on *how* they're joined — a distinction the hook's positive-to-positive wiring hides.

Join Capacitor A's positive plate to Capacitor B's **negative** plate instead (opposite polarity). The two charges now partly cancel rather than add: $Q_{\text{total}}=|Q_1-Q_2|=|12-48|=36\ \mu\text{C}$, not $60\ \mu\text{C}$. Shared voltage becomes $36/6=6\ \text{V}$, a completely different answer from the same two capacitors. Reading a circuit diagram's polarity correctly, before writing any conservation equation, decides which of these two answers is right.
