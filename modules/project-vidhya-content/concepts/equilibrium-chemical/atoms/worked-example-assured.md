**Problem:** For $A(g) \rightleftharpoons B(g)$, $K_c = 4.0$ at a given temperature. At a certain instant, $[A]=2.0$ M and $[B]=3.0$ M. In which direction, if any, does the reaction proceed?

---

**Step 1 — Compute and compare directly.** $Q = 3.0/2.0 = 1.5 < K_c = 4.0$, so the reaction runs forward.

$$\boxed{\text{Forward, toward } B}$$

---

**The distinction this question is really testing: this is a $Q$-vs-$K$ question, not a Le Chatelier question, even though both involve "which way does it shift".** Le Chatelier answers "which way does an equilibrium move *after an external stress*" — added reactant, removed product, changed volume or temperature. $Q$ vs. $K$ answers "which way does a system *not yet at* equilibrium still need to move", with no stress involved at all — the system given here was simply caught mid-approach.

Confusing the two shows up as reaching for Le Chatelier's qualitative reasoning ("more reactant present should push forward, roughly") on a question that actually wants the precise $Q$ computed and compared to the given $K_c$ — the qualitative Le Chatelier argument happens to agree here, but it would give no numerical answer, and would give the wrong direction entirely on a question phrased in terms of adding a stress to an already-equilibrated system instead.
