**The Seesaw Rule.** Picture $p=1$ as the balance point of a seesaw. At infinity, the BIG-$p$ side goes down (converges): $p>1$ wins. At a point-singularity, the SMALL-$p$ side goes down instead: $p<1$ wins. Same seesaw, opposite side, depending on WHERE the trouble is.

**Worked micro-example.** $p=2$ at infinity: $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}dx=1$, converges (big-$p$ side, correct end). $p=2$ at a point: $\int_0^1 x^{-2}dx$ diverges (big $p$ on the wrong side for a point-singularity).

**Sanity-check reflex.** Before trusting a convergence answer, ask: is the trouble spot at infinity, or at a finite point? Only after answering that does $p>1$ or $p<1$ mean "converges."
