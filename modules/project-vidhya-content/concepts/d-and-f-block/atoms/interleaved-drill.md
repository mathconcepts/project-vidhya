**Cross-concept check: d-and-f-block → coordination-compounds.**

**Question 1 (the same subshell, a new context):** $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ is high-spin ($n=4$, $\mu \approx 4.90$ BM) but $[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-}$ is low-spin ($n=0$, $\mu = 0$). Both are the same $Fe^{2+}$, $3d^6$ ion. What single difference between $H_2O$ and $CN^-$ as ligands causes this?

*Answer:* $CN^-$ is a strong-field ligand — it splits the $d$ orbitals into two energy levels far enough apart that pairing electrons in the lower set costs less energy than promoting one to the upper set. $H_2O$ is a weak-field ligand, splitting the orbitals only slightly, so electrons spread out unpaired across all 5 orbitals first (Hund's rule) rather than pairing up. The metal ion and its $d$-electron count never changed; only the ligand's field strength did.

**Question 2 (predicting colour and magnetism together from one config):** $[Ni(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ has $Ni^{2+}$, configuration $3d^8$. Predict whether it is coloured, and roughly how many unpaired electrons it has.

*Answer:* $3d^8$ has 2 electrons short of a full 10, so it is neither $d^0$ nor $d^{10}$ — coloured, since empty $d$ orbitals remain for an electron to jump into. For the unpaired count: fill 5 orbitals singly first (5 electrons), then pair up the remaining 3 into 3 of those orbitals — 2 orbitals end up with a lone electron each, so $n=2$, giving $\mu = \sqrt{2 \times 4} = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83$ BM.

**Why this drill exists:** d-and-f-block gives you the raw electron-counting tools (configuration, high spin vs low spin, colour and magnetism from one subshell); coordination-compounds is where the ligand identity — the exact thing that decides high spin versus low spin — becomes the object of study in its own right.
