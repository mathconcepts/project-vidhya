**Cross-concept check: chemical-kinetics → equilibrium-chemical.**

**Question 1 (forward and reverse rates at equilibrium):** For a reversible elementary reaction $A \rightleftharpoons B$, with forward rate constant $k_f$ and reverse rate constant $k_r$, what is true about the forward and reverse RATES once the reaction reaches equilibrium?

*Answer:* At equilibrium, the forward rate exactly equals the reverse rate: $k_f[A]_{\text{eq}} = k_r[B]_{\text{eq}}$ — not that either rate has become zero, but that the two opposing rates have become EQUAL, so the concentrations stop changing overall.

**Question 2 (rate constants give the equilibrium constant):** Using the relation above, express the equilibrium constant $K_c$ for $A \rightleftharpoons B$ in terms of $k_f$ and $k_r$.

*Answer:* Rearranging $k_f[A]_{\text{eq}}=k_r[B]_{\text{eq}}$ gives $K_c=\dfrac{[B]_{\text{eq}}}{[A]_{\text{eq}}}=\dfrac{k_f}{k_r}$ — the equilibrium constant is simply the ratio of the two rate constants, for this single-step reversible reaction.

**Why this drill exists:** chemical equilibrium is not a separate, static idea from chemical kinetics — it is exactly the point where the forward and reverse RATES (a kinetics idea) become equal, and the equilibrium constant $K_c=k_f/k_r$ is the direct bridge connecting how fast a reaction runs to where it ends up.
