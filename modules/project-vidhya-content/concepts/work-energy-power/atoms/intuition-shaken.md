Take one block, pushed by $10\text{ N}$ across $4\text{ m}$, with friction of $6\text{ N}$ acting the whole way.

Work by the push: $10 \times 4 = 40\text{ J}$.

Work by friction: friction opposes motion, so its work is negative: $-6 \times 4 = -24\text{ J}$.

Net work: $40 + (-24) = 16\text{ J}$.

By the **work-energy theorem**, $\Delta KE = 16\text{ J}$ exactly — not $40\text{ J}$. The push alone would have given $40\text{ J}$ of gain; friction takes back $24\text{ J}$ of it.

Now check mechanical energy ($KE+PE$): it is conserved only when the net work of non-conservative forces is zero. Here, friction's work is $-24\text{ J}$, not zero — so mechanical energy is *not* conserved for this block. That missing $24\text{ J}$ becomes heat, not extra motion.
