**Amine**: a derivative of ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) with one, two, or three hydrogens replaced by alkyl or aryl groups — primary ($\text{RNH}_2$), secondary ($\text{R}_2\text{NH}$), or tertiary ($\text{R}_3\text{N}$).

**Basicity order, gas phase**: tertiary $>$ secondary $>$ primary $>$ ammonia, for simple alkyl amines. Governed only by how much electron density the alkyl groups donate onto nitrogen — no solvent effect exists to compete with it.

**Basicity order, aqueous phase**: secondary $>$ primary $>$ tertiary $>$ ammonia, for simple alkyl amines. The protonated amine's ammonium ion is stabilised by hydrogen bonding to water through its remaining $\text{N-H}$ bonds; a tertiary ammonium ion has only one such bond and is poorly solvated, pulling its basicity below primary and secondary.

**Aniline's basicity**: lower than ammonia. Nitrogen's lone pair delocalises into the aromatic ring by resonance and is far less available to accept a proton than an aliphatic amine's lone pair.

**Hofmann bromamide degradation**: $\text{RCONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{RNH}_2 + 2\text{NaBr} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Converts an amide into a primary amine with one fewer carbon than the amide, since the carbonyl carbon leaves as carbonate.

**Gabriel phthalimide synthesis**: potassium phthalimide reacts with an alkyl halide $\text{R-X}$ to give N-alkylphthalimide, which is hydrolysed to the pure primary amine $\text{RNH}_2$ — same carbon count as the halide, and no over-alkylation (unlike direct ammonia alkylation). Does not work for preparing aromatic amines.

**Carbylamine test**: a primary amine, warmed with chloroform and alcoholic KOH, gives a foul-smelling isocyanide ($\text{R-NC}$). Secondary and tertiary amines give no such product — this test is specific to primary amines only.

**Hinsberg test**: benzenesulfonyl chloride reacts with a primary amine to give a sulfonamide with an acidic $\text{N-H}$ (soluble in aqueous KOH), with a secondary amine to give a sulfonamide with no $\text{N-H}$ (insoluble in KOH), and does not react with a tertiary amine at all.

**Diazotisation**: a primary aromatic amine plus $\text{NaNO}_2$ and dilute $\text{HCl}$, at $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$, gives the diazonium salt $\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$. The low temperature is necessary because the diazonium salt decomposes above roughly $5^\circ\text{C}$.

**Diazonium coupling**: the diazonium ion is a weak electrophile and reacts (azo coupling, giving $\text{Ar-N=N-Ar}'$) only with a strongly activated aromatic ring — one carrying an electron-donating group such as $\text{-OH}$ or $\text{-NH}_2$ — typically at the position para to that group.
