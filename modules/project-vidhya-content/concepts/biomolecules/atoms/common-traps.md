- **Assuming every disaccharide is non-reducing**: sucrose is non-reducing because its glycosidic bond ties up both anomeric carbons, but maltose (glucose + glucose) leaves one anomeric carbon free and is a reducing sugar. "Disaccharide" alone does not decide the answer — count the free anomeric carbons.

- **Confusing "same building blocks" with "same molecule"**: starch and cellulose are both made entirely of glucose, and glycogen and starch's amylopectin are both branched glucose polymers — but different linkage geometry or branching frequency makes them behave completely differently. Composition alone is never the full picture.

- **Miscounting the number of water molecules released in a peptide chain**: a chain of $n$ amino acids has $n-1$ peptide bonds, not $n$ — the first amino acid's amine group and the last one's acid group are never used up in a bond with each other.

- **Treating primary structure and tertiary structure as the same fact**: primary structure is just the amino acid sequence; tertiary structure is the full three-dimensional folded shape. A protein can have its correct sequence (primary structure intact) while still being non-functional if heat or extreme pH has destroyed its folded shape (denaturation) — sequence and shape are separate levels of description.

- **Assuming DNA and RNA use identical bases**: both use adenine, guanine, and cytosine, but DNA pairs adenine with thymine while RNA uses uracil in thymine's place — writing "thymine" for an RNA base is a common slip.
