Field direction and the symmetry test should feel automatic by now. Here's a pairing that quietly isn't interchangeable: "the field just outside a charged conductor's surface" and "the field of an isolated point charge at the same distance" are computed by the same formula shape, yet they are **not** the same claim.

Just outside any charged conductor, $E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$ ($\sigma$ is surface charge density — charge per unit area), pointing perpendicular to the surface — this holds regardless of the conductor's overall shape, because the conductor's own charges rearrange until the field inside is exactly zero. Treating this as "just Coulomb's law for the total charge divided by area" silently drops the fact that a conductor's charge is **not** uniformly spread unless the conductor happens to be a sphere — on a conductor with sharp points or corners, $\sigma$ (and hence $E$) is far higher near the point than anywhere else on the same object.
