An element's listed atomic mass (like chlorine's, roughly 35.5) is not the mass of any single atom that actually exists — it is a *weighted average* over the different **isotopes** (atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, so different mass).

Think of a large batch of coins where most, but not all, are the standard weight, and a smaller fraction are a slightly heavier minting. The "average coin weight" you would measure is a blend, pulled toward whichever type is more common — never the exact weight of the common type alone, and never the exact weight of the rare type alone.

For chlorine specifically: about 76% of atoms are the lighter isotope (mass number 35) and about 24% are the heavier one (mass number 37). Weighting by these proportions gives an average close to the listed atomic mass of roughly 35.5 — heavier than 35 (since the heavier isotope pulls the average up), but nowhere near 37 (since the heavier isotope is the minority).
