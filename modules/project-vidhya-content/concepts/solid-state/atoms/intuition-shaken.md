A metal crystallises in an fcc (face-centred cubic) structure, with molar mass $M=63.5\ \text{g/mol}$ and edge length $a=361\ \text{pm}$.

For fcc, $Z=4$.

Convert $a$ to centimetres FIRST, since density needs $\text{g/cm}^3$: $a=361\ \text{pm}=361\times10^{-10}\ \text{cm}=3.61\times10^{-8}\ \text{cm}$.

Cube it: $a^3=(3.61\times10^{-8})^3=4.70\times10^{-23}\ \text{cm}^3$.

$\rho=\dfrac{ZM}{N_Aa^3}=\dfrac{4\times63.5}{(6.022\times10^{23})(4.70\times10^{-23})}\approx8.96\ \text{g/cm}^3$.

Check: this matches a genuine metal's real, measured density closely — confirming the fcc structure, the edge length, and the unit conversion were all handled correctly together.
