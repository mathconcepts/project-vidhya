**Problem:** Arrange beryllium, boron and carbon in order of increasing first ionisation enthalpy.

---

**Step 1 — Write down the real numbers first, in kJ/mol.** Be = 899. B = 801. C = 1086. Compare these three numbers directly: $801 < 899 < 1086$.

---

**Step 2 — Read off the order.** So the order is B first, then Be, then C. Notice Be comes AFTER B here, even though Be sits to the left of B in the period — that is the one place the numbers do not follow left-to-right order.

---

**Step 3 — Check why, using electron configuration.** Be: $1s^22s^2$ — the $2s$ subshell is completely full. B: $1s^22s^22p^1$ — the extra electron has moved into a new $2p$ subshell.

---

**Step 4 — Compare stability.** A filled subshell resists losing an electron more than a subshell holding just one new electron in a higher-energy orbital. So B's outer electron comes off more easily than Be's, confirming $IE(B) < IE(Be)$ exactly as the numbers showed.

---

**Step 5 — Final order and check.** $\boxed{IE(B) < IE(Be) < IE(C)}$. Check: $B \to C$ and $Be \to C$ both go the expected direction; only $Be \to B$ flips, and only because that specific step crosses $2s^2$ into a fresh $2p$ subshell.
