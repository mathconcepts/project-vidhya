A vernier caliper (a sliding-scale instrument) reads $a = 2.20$ cm for a cube's side. Its **least count** — the smallest gap it can actually tell apart — is $0.01$ cm, so the true side could be anywhere from $2.19$ cm to $2.21$ cm.

Now compute the volume: $V = a^3 = 2.20 \times 2.20 \times 2.20 = 10.648$ cm$^3$ on a calculator screen.

Check what those digits are worth: $a$ is trustworthy to $3$ digits ($2$, $2$, $0$) and nothing more. Multiplying it by itself twice cannot manufacture extra trustworthy digits out of nowhere — most of $10.648000000000003$ is arithmetic noise, not measured fact.
