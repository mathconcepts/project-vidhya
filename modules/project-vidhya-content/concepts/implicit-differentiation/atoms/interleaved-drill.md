**Cross-concept check: chain-rule → implicit-differentiation.**

**Question 1 (chain-rule):** Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at $x=1$ for $y = (x+1)^3$, differentiated directly.

*Answer:* $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3(x+1)^2$. At $x=1$: $3(2)^2 = 12$.

**Question 2 (implicit-differentiation):** The same relation, written implicitly as $y - (x+1)^3 = 0$, is differentiated implicitly instead. Confirm the slope at $x=1$ still comes out to $12$.

*Answer:* Differentiating both sides: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} - 3(x+1)^2 = 0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3(x+1)^2$ — the identical expression from Question 1. At $x=1$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12$, matching exactly.

**Why this drill exists:** implicit differentiation is not a different technique from the chain rule — it is the chain rule applied inside an equation instead of inside a single formula for $y$. Students sometimes treat the two as separate toolkits and get different (wrong) answers for the same curve depending on which "method" they picked; this checks that both routes must agree whenever $y$ actually could be isolated explicitly.
