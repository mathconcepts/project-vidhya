An exam's scores are said to be "normally distributed around 50, with a spread of 10." That sentence claims something about a curve, not a list — you can't ask "what's the probability of scoring exactly 62" and get a nonzero answer, because a continuous variable assigns zero probability to any single point. What you *can* ask is the probability of landing in a range, and that number is an area under a curve, not a lookup in a table of discrete outcomes. Normal, exponential, uniform, and gamma are four honest shapes that curve can take.
