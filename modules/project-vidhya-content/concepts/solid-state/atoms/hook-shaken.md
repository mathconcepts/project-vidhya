In a simple cubic unit cell, one atom of radius $r$ sits at each of the $8$ corners, each corner atom shared among $8$ neighbouring cells — contributing $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of an atom to THIS cell. With $8$ corners, that's $8\times\dfrac{1}{8}=1$ whole atom per unit cell.

The atoms touch along an edge, so the edge length is $a=2r$.

Packing efficiency $=\dfrac{\text{volume of atoms in the cell}}{\text{volume of the cell}}=\dfrac{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{(2r)^3}=\dfrac{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{8r^3}=\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

Check: $\dfrac{\pi}{6}\approx0.524$, or $52.4\%$ — meaning nearly HALF of a simple cubic cell's volume is empty space, confirming this is the least efficient of the common packing arrangements.
