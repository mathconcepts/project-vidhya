**Problem:** A metal rod of length $0.5\text{ m}$ slides at a constant $2\text{ m/s}$ along two parallel rails, in a uniform field $B = 0.4\text{ T}$ perpendicular to the plane of the rails. The rails are joined at one end by a resistor $R = 2\ \Omega$. Find the induced emf, the current, and the force an external agent must apply to keep the rod moving at constant speed.

---

**Step 1 — Write down what is given.** $L = 0.5\text{ m}$, $v = 2\text{ m/s}$, $B = 0.4\text{ T}$, $R = 2\ \Omega$. All three of $v$, $B$, $L$ are mutually perpendicular.

---

**Step 2 — Apply the motional-emf formula.** $\varepsilon = BLv = 0.4 \times 0.5 \times 2 = 0.4\text{ V}$.

---

**Step 3 — Apply Ohm's law to get the current.** $I = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R} = \dfrac{0.4}{2} = 0.2\text{ A}$.

---

**Step 4 — Apply the force formula.** $F = BIL = 0.4 \times 0.2 \times 0.5 = 0.04\text{ N}$.

---

**Step 5 — Run the check.** Compute mechanical power: $P_{\text{mech}} = Fv = 0.04 \times 2 = 0.08\text{ W}$. Compute electrical power: $P_{\text{elec}} = I^2R = (0.2)^2 \times 2 = 0.08\text{ W}$. Both equal $0.08\text{ W}$ — the answer checks out.

$$\boxed{\varepsilon = 0.4\text{ V}, \quad I = 0.2\text{ A}, \quad F = 0.04\text{ N}}$$
