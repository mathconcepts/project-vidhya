"Double bond opening, no molecule lost" versus "small molecule released at each join" (addition versus condensation) is settled territory by now. Here is the sharper trap: a **copolymer** built from two different monomers is not automatically condensation just because two different starting materials are involved.

Counterexample: Buna-S is a copolymer of two DIFFERENT monomers, buta-1,3-diene and styrene — both of which carry double bonds that simply open and link, exactly like ethylene does. No small molecule is released anywhere in forming Buna-S; it is an **addition** copolymer, despite having two distinct monomers, the same surface feature nylon-6,6 (a genuine condensation polymer) also has. "How many different monomers" tells you nothing about which family a polymer belongs to — only "is anything released at each join" does, whether the monomers are identical or not.
