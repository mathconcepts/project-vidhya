At room temperature ($300$ K), the typical heat energy jiggling every atom is only about $kT\approx0.026$ eV — tiny.

Silicon's **band gap** $E_g\approx1.1$ eV (the gap of forbidden energies between the valence band, mostly full of electrons, and the conduction band, where a free electron can carry current) is about $40$ times bigger than this jiggle — but still small enough that a rare few electrons get lucky and cross it. That is why pure silicon conducts a little, and conducts better as it heats up, since more electrons get lucky.

Diamond's band gap is about $5.5$ eV — exactly $5$ times silicon's gap, over $200$ times the thermal jiggle. Essentially zero electrons ever cross that; diamond is a true insulator.

Both crystals have the same two bands separated by a gap where no electron can sit. The only difference between an insulator and a semiconductor is how big that gap is compared with the thermal energy on offer.
