Picture a wave that never stops repeating — that is what sine and cosine actually are. Two facts about that wave explain every general-solution formula you will ever write.

First: the wave repeats every $2\pi$. Whatever angle solves the equation, adding a whole number of turns, $2n\pi$, gives another solution for free — that is where the "$n\pi$" or "$2n\pi$" in every formula comes from.

Second: sine climbs to its peak and comes back down along a mirror. $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$ — the angle $30°$ and its mirror image $150°$ give the exact same height. Cosine has its own mirror instead: $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, so an angle and its negative give the same value, which is why cosine's general solution uses a plain $\pm$ instead of an alternating sign.

That alternating sign, $(-1)^n$, is just bookkeeping: it flips the correction term back and forth so the formula lands on the reference angle for even $n$ and on its mirror image for odd $n$, without you having to write two separate formulas by hand.
