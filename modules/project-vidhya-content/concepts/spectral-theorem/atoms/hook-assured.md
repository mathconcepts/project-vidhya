$A = Q\Lambda Q^T$ is diagonalization with the extra guarantee that $Q$ is orthogonal — it's what's underneath every $f(A) = Qf(\Lambda)Q^T$ trick, from $\sqrt{A}$ to $e^{At}$ to matrix square roots in Cholesky-adjacent problems.

Where does this construction fail for a non-symmetric matrix, and what's the closest substitute available then?
