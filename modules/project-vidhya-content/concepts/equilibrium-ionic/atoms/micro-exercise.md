A buffer is prepared by mixing equal moles of a weak acid ($\text{p}K_a = 5.0$) with its sodium salt, in the same volume. What is the pH of this buffer?

A) $\text{pH} = 5.0$
B) $\text{pH} = 9.0$
C) $\text{pH} = 2.5$
D) $\text{pH} = 7.0$
E) Cannot be found without knowing the actual concentration

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $\text{pH} = 5.0$.**

Equal moles of acid and its conjugate base, in the same volume, means equal concentrations too — so $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}] = 1$ and $\log_{10}(1) = 0$. Henderson–Hasselbalch then gives $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + 0 = 5.0$.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) $9.0$ comes from computing $\text{p}K_b = 14 - 5 = 9$ and mistakenly reporting it as the pH — mixing up the acid's own $\text{p}K_a$ with its conjugate base's $\text{p}K_b$.
- C) $2.5$ comes from halving $\text{p}K_a$ by mistake, treating it like an average instead of a logarithmic quantity.
- D) $7.0$ assumes any acid-salt mixture must be neutral — true only by coincidence, never by the actual chemistry here.
- E) is wrong because the RATIO, not the absolute concentration, decides the pH here — since the ratio is fixed at $1$ regardless of how concentrated the mixture actually is, the pH is fully determined already.

</details>
