**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ/NAT: IUPAC naming, or the reverse (name to formula).** Work through the fixed order every time: split complex from counter-ion, find the complex's overall charge, subtract known ligand charges to get the metal's oxidation state, name ligands alphabetically with correct endings, add the oxidation state in Roman numerals, and use $-ate$ only for an anionic complex.

- **MCQ: identify the type of isomerism from two given formulas.** Check in this order: do the formulas have the exact same ligand set, just arranged differently (geometrical or optical), or a different SPLIT of the same ions inside versus outside the brackets (ionisation, hydrate, coordination), or the same ligand bonded through a different atom (linkage)? Matching the right category to the right symptom is the whole question.

- **Trap: "which theory explains this property" questions.** A question about colour or magnetic moment is a CFT question; a question about shape or hybridisation is a VBT question. Answering a colour question using only a hybridisation label (or vice versa) answers the wrong theory's question.

- **Assertion-reason: geometry-dependent isomerism.** Expect a claim that a given $MA_2B_2$ or $MA_4B_2$ pattern automatically shows cis-trans isomerism — check the actual geometry (square planar or octahedral, not tetrahedral) before accepting it.

- **Time budget:** a full IUPAC naming question should take under 90 seconds once the fixed order above is automatic — most of the time loss in practice comes from re-deriving the order from scratch each time instead of following it as a checklist.
