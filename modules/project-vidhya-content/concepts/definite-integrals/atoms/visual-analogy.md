Picture the region trapped between the curve $y=x^2$ and the $x$-axis, from $x=0$ to $x=2$. A definite integral is the *signed* size of exactly that trapped region — positive where the curve sits above the axis, negative where it dips below. For this particular curve the whole region sits above the axis, so the signed total and the plain geometric area agree: $\int_0^2 x^2\,dx=\tfrac83$. The moment the curve crosses to the other side, that agreement breaks, and the two numbers — signed integral, geometric area — start telling different stories.
