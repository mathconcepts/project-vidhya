**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **NAT: buffer pH from Henderson–Hasselbalch.** Given $K_a$ (or $\text{p}K_a$) and the moles (or concentrations) of a weak acid and its conjugate-base salt, compute the pH directly from $\text{pH}=\text{p}K_a+\log_{10}([\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}])$ — there is no need to set up a full equilibrium expression from scratch when both species are already present in comparable amounts.

- **MCQ: which mixture is actually a buffer.** A common stem lists several acid-salt combinations and asks which one acts as a buffer. Check that the salt supplies the SAME acid's own conjugate base — a weak acid mixed with an unrelated salt is not a buffer, however similar the setup looks.

- **Trap: solubility-product problems that mix units.** A $K_{sp}$ NAT expects molar solubility, and distractor options are often built by silently switching to a gram-based value partway through — keep solubility in mol/L from the first line of working to the last.

- **Trap: assuming pH scales linearly.** A "how much more acidic" question is really asking for a ratio of $[\text{H}^+]$ values, computed as $10^{\Delta\text{pH}}$ — never a plain subtraction or a percentage of the pH numbers themselves.

- **Time budget:** a direct Henderson–Hasselbalch NAT should take under $45$ seconds — look up or compute $\text{p}K_a$ once, form the ratio, add.
