**Biot-Savart law**: the magnetic field contributed by a small current element $I\,d\vec l$ at a point a distance $r$ away is $d\vec B=\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{I\,d\vec l\times\hat r}{r^2}$, where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space, a fixed constant.

**Field due to a long straight wire**: $B=\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$, at perpendicular distance $r$ from the wire.

**Ampere's circuital law**: $\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$, where $I_{\text{enc}}$ is the current enclosed by the chosen closed loop — useful as a shortcut only when symmetry lets $B$ be pulled out of the integral, just as with Gauss's law for electric fields.

**Force on a moving charge (Lorentz force, magnetic part)**: $\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B$, magnitude $F=qvB\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec v$ and $\vec B$. Direction (positive charge): right-hand rule, curling from $\vec v$ towards $\vec B$; flip for a negative charge.

**Force on a current-carrying wire**: $\vec F=I\vec L\times\vec B$, magnitude $F=BIL\sin\theta$, where $\vec L$ points along the current and $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec L$ and $\vec B$. Direction: Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (thumb = force, first finger = field, middle finger = current).

**Torque on a current loop**: $\tau=NIAB\sin\theta$, where $N$ is the number of turns, $A$ is the loop's area, and $\theta$ is the angle between the loop's **normal** and $\vec B$ — not the angle of the loop's plane itself.

**Moving-coil galvanometer**: a current-carrying coil in a radial magnetic field experiences a torque proportional to the current, balanced against a restoring spring, so deflection is proportional to current.
