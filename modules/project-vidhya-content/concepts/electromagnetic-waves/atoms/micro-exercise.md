An electromagnetic wave in vacuum has frequency $f = 5\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}$. What is its wavelength?

A) $600\text{ nm}$
B) $60\text{ nm}$
C) $1500\text{ nm}$
D) $6\text{ nm}$
E) $6000\text{ nm}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $600\text{ nm}$.**

$c = f\lambda \Rightarrow \lambda = \dfrac{c}{f} = \dfrac{3\times10^8}{5\times10^{14}} = 6\times10^{-7}\text{ m} = 600\text{ nm}$ — squarely in the visible range, as a frequency of this size should be.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) and D) are off by a factor of $10$ and $100$ respectively — a slipped power of ten while dividing $3\times10^8$ by $5\times10^{14}$.
- C) inverts the division, computing $f/c$ scaled up instead of $c/f$.
- E) is off by a factor of $10$ in the other direction from A).

</details>
