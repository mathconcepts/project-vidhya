**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT questions ask for a specific $x$-value or a specific $c$**: the point where a tangent has a given slope, the location of a local maximum, or the value of $c$ guaranteed by Rolle's/LMVT. Set up $f'(x)=0$ or $f'(x)=\text{slope}$, solve, and report the number that lies in the required interval.

- **MCQ "increasing/decreasing on which interval" questions test the sign of $f'$, not the algebra of finding it.** Once $f'(x)$ is factored, the actual skill being tested is reading off where the product is positive versus negative — a step many students rush past after correctly doing the harder differentiation.

- **Trap: closed-interval absolute extrema.** A JEE optimisation problem restricted to $[a,b]$ often has its true maximum or minimum at an endpoint, not at an interior critical point. The distractor is always the value at the interior critical point alone, omitting the endpoint check.

- **Time budget:** identifying which technique applies — tangent/normal formula, sign-of-$f'$ interval test, or Rolle's/LMVT hypothesis check — should take under 20 seconds once the function is differentiated. The differentiation itself, not the theorem, is usually where JEE-level algebra costs the most time; practise the algebra to speed, not the theorem statements.
