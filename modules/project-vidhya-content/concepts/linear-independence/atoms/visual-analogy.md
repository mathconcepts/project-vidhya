Think of a group of people each pulling a rope in a certain direction with a certain force (the vector). If the group is **linearly independent**, then to stop the bundle of ropes from moving, all forces must balance to exactly zero—remove any one person, and the ropes will move in some direction because that person's contribution is irreplaceable. If the group is **linearly dependent**, then at least one person's effort is redundant: their pulling direction and magnitude can be recreated by combining the efforts of the others. In that case, you could remove that person and the ropes would still have the same set of possible outcomes.

Geometrically in 2D: two vectors are independent if they don't lie on the same line through the origin. Three vectors in 3D are independent if they don't all lie in the same plane through the origin. The constraint is: **no vector should live in the span of the others**.
