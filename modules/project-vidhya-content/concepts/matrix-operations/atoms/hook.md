Two shears, $A$ and $B$, multiplied together become one matrix, $AB$, that does both moves in a single step. Push sixteen arrows through $AB=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and watch where they land. Multiply the very same two matrices in the other order, $BA$, and you get a different matrix — the dashed grey arrows show where it sends the same sixteen arrows instead. Order matters: rotate-then-shear is not shear-then-rotate.
