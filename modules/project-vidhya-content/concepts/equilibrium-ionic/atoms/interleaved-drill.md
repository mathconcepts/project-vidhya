**Cross-concept check: equilibrium-ionic → solutions-and-colligative.**

**Question 1 (degree of ionisation feeds the van't Hoff factor):** A weak monobasic acid $\text{HA}$ ionises to the extent $\alpha=0.02$ (i.e. $2\%$) in a dilute aqueous solution. Since $\text{HA}$ produces $2$ particles on full ionisation ($\text{H}^+$ and $\text{A}^-$), find the van't Hoff factor $i$ for this solution.

*Answer:* $i = 1+(n-1)\alpha$, where $n$ is the number of particles one formula unit would give on complete ionisation. Here $n=2$, $\alpha=0.02$: $i = 1+(2-1)(0.02) = 1.02$.

**Question 2 (what this $i$ actually changes):** How does this $i=1.02$ affect a colligative property, such as freezing-point depression, compared with treating $\text{HA}$ as if it never ionised at all?

*Answer:* Every colligative-property formula gets multiplied by $i$: $\Delta T_f = i \cdot K_f \cdot m$. Treating $\text{HA}$ as non-ionising would use $i=1$ and undercount the freezing-point depression by about $2\%$ — a small but genuine correction, since even a weakly ionising acid puts slightly more than one particle into solution per formula unit dissolved.

**Why this drill exists:** ionic equilibrium's degree of ionisation $\alpha$ is not a separate fact from colligative properties — it is exactly the number that turns a simple "$i=1$" or "$i=2$" assumption into the real, slightly-in-between value a partially ionising weak acid or base actually produces.
