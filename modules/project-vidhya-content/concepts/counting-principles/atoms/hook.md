A padlock has 4 dials, each showing digits 0–9. Before you compute a single probability question this topic sets up, answer this: how many codes exist? Multiply the choices per dial — $10\times10\times10\times10=10{,}000$ — and you already have the population every later probability question divides into. Counting principles are not a detour before probability; they are the arithmetic probability quietly assumes you can already do. Get the count wrong here and the fraction you build on top of it is wrong no matter how careful the rest of the argument is.
