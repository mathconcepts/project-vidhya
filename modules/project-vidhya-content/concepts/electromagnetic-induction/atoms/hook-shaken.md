Field drops from $0.6\text{ T}$ to $0.2\text{ T}$ in $0.4$ seconds, through a coil of area $0.02\text{ m}^2$. Rate of drop: $(0.6-0.2)/0.4 = 1\text{ T/s}$.

Size of the induced emf: $\varepsilon = A \times |dB/dt| = 0.02 \times 1 = 0.02\text{ V} = 20\text{ mV}$.

Direction: the field is fading, so the induced current fights the fade — it builds its own field pointing the *same* way the old field did, straight down through the coil. Curl the right hand's fingers so the thumb points down through the coil: the fingers show the current runs clockwise, seen from above.

Next time a field is described as decreasing, do not reach for "current opposes the field" as the rule. It opposes the *drop* — here, that means reinforcing the old field, not cancelling it.
