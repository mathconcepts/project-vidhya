Ethanol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$, and phenol, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$, both carry the exact same $-\text{OH}$ group everyone learns to call "the alcohol group." Add sodium hydroxide solution to each. Ethanol does nothing visible — the $-\text{OH}$ hydrogen stays put. Phenol reacts, forming a clear solution of sodium phenoxide, meaning phenol's hydrogen left as $\text{H}^+$ far more readily.

Same functional group, wildly different willingness to give up that one hydrogen. Whatever decides this is not written in the $-\text{OH}$ itself — it depends entirely on what that oxygen happens to be attached to on the other side.
