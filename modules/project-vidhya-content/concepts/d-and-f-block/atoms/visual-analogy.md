Think of the 5 $d$ orbitals as 5 parking bays in a small lot, and each electron as a car that would rather have a bay to itself. When only a few cars show up (a partly filled subshell), every car parks in its own bay, engine running, radio on — a bay with a lone, "active" car is like an unpaired electron: it can respond to a magnet, and it leaves room for a new arrival to slide in when light energy nudges it, which is what makes the compound coloured.

Once every bay has exactly one car (5 cars, all separate — the half-filled case) or every bay has two cars each, doors closed, engines off (10 cars, the fully filled case), the lot looks calm and settled either way — both the half-filled and fully filled arrangements are notably more stable than an in-between mess, which is exactly why chromium and copper "cheat" the normal filling order to reach one of these two tidy states.
