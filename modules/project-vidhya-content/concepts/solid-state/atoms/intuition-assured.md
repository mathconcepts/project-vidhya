In a close-packed structure, "the number of voids" is often treated as one single number — it is actually two DIFFERENT numbers, and mixing them up is a common source of error.

For $N$ atoms in a close-packed (ccp/hcp) arrangement, there are exactly $N$ **octahedral voids** (larger gaps, surrounded by $6$ neighbouring atoms) and $2N$ **tetrahedral voids** (smaller gaps, surrounded by $4$ neighbouring atoms) — TWICE as many tetrahedral voids as octahedral ones, in every close-packed structure, regardless of what the atoms actually are.

**Knowing a structure is close-packed is necessary to use these ratios — it is nowhere near sufficient to guess which void type a particular ionic compound's smaller ion actually occupies.** That depends on the specific radius ratio of the two ions involved, a separate calculation from simply counting how many voids of each kind exist.
