- **Getting the sign convention itself wrong — the single biggest source of lost marks in this topic.** All distances are measured *from the pole or optical centre*, and distances measured *against the direction the incident light travels* are negative. A real object placed in front of a mirror or lens is measured going backwards along the incoming light's path, so its distance $u$ is always negative — writing $u=+30\text{ cm}$ for an object placed $30\text{ cm}$ away is wrong before a single formula is even applied. Heights above the axis are positive, below are negative, using the exact same rule.

- **Reusing a mirror's magnification sign rule, $m=-v/u$, for a lens.** A lens's magnification is $m=+v/u$, with no minus sign at all. Carrying the mirror's minus sign into a lens problem flips upright answers to inverted and back, silently.

- **Getting concave and convex mixed up on which has a positive $f$.** For mirrors, concave has $f<0$ and convex has $f>0$. For lenses, it flips: convex has $f>0$ and concave has $f<0$. "Concave is always negative" is true for mirrors but false for lenses — check which one the question is even about first.

- **Assuming total internal reflection can happen going either direction between two media.** It requires light travelling from the **denser** medium *into* the rarer one, at an angle past the critical angle. Light entering a denser medium from a rarer one never totally internally reflects, at any angle — it only ever partially reflects and partially refracts.

- **Using the real depth in place of the apparent depth (or the reverse) when viewing through a denser medium.** Looking from air into water, a coin that is really $30\text{ cm}$ deep appears at only $\text{real depth}/n = 30/(4/3) = 22.5\text{ cm}$ — the water makes the coin look shallower than it is, not deeper, and forgetting to divide by $n$ overstates how deep it looks.
