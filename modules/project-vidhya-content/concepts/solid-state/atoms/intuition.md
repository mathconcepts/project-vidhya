A crystal is built from one tiny repeating box, called a **unit cell**, stacked endlessly in every direction. How many atoms actually belong to one unit cell, $Z$, is found by counting SHARES: a corner atom is shared among $8$ cells (contributes $\frac{1}{8}$), a face-centred atom is shared between $2$ cells (contributes $\frac{1}{2}$), an edge atom between $4$ cells (contributes $\frac{1}{4}$), and a body-centred atom belongs entirely to one cell (contributes $1$).

**Simple cubic** has atoms only at the $8$ corners: $Z=1$. **Body-centred cubic (bcc)** adds one atom at the centre: $Z=2$. **Face-centred cubic (fcc)**, also called cubic close-packed, adds one atom at the centre of each of the $6$ faces: $Z=4$.

**Packing efficiency** (the fraction of a unit cell's volume actually filled by atoms) rises with $Z$ and with **coordination number** (how many neighbouring atoms directly touch one atom): $52.4\%$ for simple cubic (coordination number $6$), $68\%$ for bcc (coordination number $8$), and $74\%$ for fcc — the tightest packing spheres of one size can achieve.

**Density** follows directly: $\rho=\dfrac{ZM}{N_Aa^3}$, where $M$ is molar mass, $N_A$ is Avogadro's number, and $a$ is the edge length — every quantity on the right is something a crystal structure fixes exactly.
