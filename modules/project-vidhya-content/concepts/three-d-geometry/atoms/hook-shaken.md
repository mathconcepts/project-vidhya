Picture a drone. It is flying in one straight line, close to a tilted solar panel on a rooftop.

The pilot needs two numbers before it climbs further.

Number one: the angle between the drone's flight line and the flat panel. Is the path cutting across it steeply, or almost sliding along it?

Number two: right now, how far is the drone from the panel's surface? Not along the flight path — straight across, the short way.

Both numbers come from the same two facts: the drone's line, and the panel's equation. This concept teaches exactly how to get both numbers — for one line and one plane, and also for two lines that never touch at all.
