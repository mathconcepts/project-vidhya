**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: basicity-ranking questions almost always specify a medium.** "In aqueous solution, rank..." and "in the gas phase, rank..." are two different questions with two different correct orders — read which medium is named before picking an answer, since the same list of amines can have opposite correct orderings.

- **MCQ: aniline planted in a list with alkyl amines.** A ranking question mixing aniline with methylamine, dimethylamine, and ammonia is testing whether resonance delocalisation into the ring (which drops aniline below ammonia) is remembered separately from the alkyl-substitution rules that govern the other three.

- **Trap: "does this ring couple with a diazonium salt" questions hide the electron-withdrawing option among plausible-looking substituted rings.** A ring bearing $\text{-NO}_2$, $\text{-CHO}$, or a halogen looks "substituted enough" but is not activated in the direction coupling needs — only a strong electron-DONATING group like $\text{-OH}$ or $\text{-NH}_2$ works.

- **NAT/MCQ: diazotisation temperature.** Any question mentioning nitrous acid and a primary aromatic amine is implicitly asking you to recall the $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$ window; a described procedure at room temperature or above is signalling decomposition, not a stable diazonium salt.

- **Time budget:** a Hinsberg-test or carbylamine-test identification question is a direct recall check — under 20 seconds once the reagent name is read, no mechanism needs to be drawn.
