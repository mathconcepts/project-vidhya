$\vec a\cdot\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\cos\theta$ is a shadow. Shine a light straight down onto $\vec b$'s direction, and $\vec a$ casts a shadow of length $|\vec a|\cos\theta$ on it. Multiply that shadow by $|\vec b|$ and you get the dot product. When $\vec a$ points the same way as $\vec b$, the shadow is as long as possible and the dot product is largest. When $\vec a$ is perpendicular to $\vec b$, the shadow shrinks to a point — the dot product is exactly $0$.

$\vec a\times\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta\,\hat n$ is an area. $|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta$ is exactly the area of the parallelogram with $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ as adjacent sides. When the two vectors point the same way, the parallelogram flattens to a line — zero area, zero cross product.

This is also why vectors can prove pure geometry facts with no coordinates. If $\vec a,\vec b$ are the position vectors of two points, their midpoint is $\frac{\vec a+\vec b}{2}$ — one fact, and whole theorems about triangles and parallelograms follow from it in a few lines.
