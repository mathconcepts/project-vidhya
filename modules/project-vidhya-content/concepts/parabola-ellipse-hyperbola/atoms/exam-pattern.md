**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **Tamil Nadu has no standalone Class 11 conics chapter, and the Class 12 chapter stops at definitions and standard equations.** Chord of contact, pole and polar, director circle, and auxiliary circle — everything this concept builds on top of the basics — is genuinely new material here, not revision compressed from two years of exposure the way it might be for some other boards. Treat it as its own topic worth deliberate practice, not a quick recap.

- **MCQ "does this point admit two real tangents" questions are a favourite trap on the hyperbola specifically.** The sign rule flips compared to the ellipse — $S_1<0$ signals two real tangents on a hyperbola, not $S_1>0$. A distractor built by pattern-matching the ellipse's rule is a very common wrong option here.

- **NAT questions on director circle usually want the radius or a single coordinate, not the full derivation.** Read off $a^2+b^2$ (ellipse) or $a^2-b^2$ (hyperbola, only when $a>b$) directly — no need to actually construct two perpendicular tangents unless the question demands it.

- **Trap: assuming a director circle exists for every hyperbola.** When $a\le b$, it does not — $a^2-b^2\le0$ gives no real circle at all.

- **MSQ questions on auxiliary circle test whether you can tell $(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)$ apart from $(a\cos\theta,a\sin\theta)$ at a glance.** One option swaps which point belongs to the ellipse and which to the circle — check which coefficient, $a$ or $b$, multiplies $\sin\theta$ before selecting an option.

- **Time budget:** a chord-of-contact question on a conic with clean integer coefficients runs $2$–$3$ minutes, matching the circle version. A question combining chord of contact with a director-circle or auxiliary-circle fact runs closer to $4$–$5$ minutes; budget the extra time rather than rushing the sign check on a hyperbola.
