**How JEE actually asks this.**

- **MCQ: shape/hybridisation of an interhalogen or noble-gas compound.** Count the central atom's valence electrons, subtract one per bond, halve the remainder into lone pairs, add bonding and lone-pair domains together, and read hybridisation straight off the domain count ($5 \to sp^3d$, $6 \to sp^3d^2$, $7 \to sp^3d^3$) before worrying about the final shape.

- **MCQ/assertion-reason: "which halogen has the strongest/weakest bond, oxide, or acid".** Whenever fluorine or oxygen appears as the SMALLEST member of its group in a comparison, check for the small-size anomaly (weak $F-F$ bond, less negative electron gain enthalpy for $F$ and $O$ than the element right below) before assuming the plain group trend applies.

- **Trap: central atom of an interhalogen.** Distractors routinely put the more electronegative, smaller halogen in the centre. The central atom is always the larger, less electronegative one — check group position, not electronegativity, when picking it.

- **NAT/MCQ: inert pair effect and oxidation state stability.** Given a heavy $p$-block element (Tl, Pb, Bi) and two possible oxidation states, the more stable one is almost always (group $-$ 2), not the full group state — the opposite of what the lighter members of the same group would suggest.

- **Time budget:** a shape/hybridisation question on an interhalogen or xenon compound should take under 45 seconds once the electron-counting method above is automatic — there is no need to draw the full Lewis structure, only to count domains.
