A positive $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ says a reaction is thermodynamically favourable — it says nothing about whether that reaction actually happens at any noticeable speed, and conflating the two is a genuine trap.

Take $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$: combining the half-reactions gives $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.23\ \text{V}$, strongly positive — this reaction is enormously favourable on paper. Yet a jar of hydrogen and oxygen gas can sit on a shelf for years without reacting at all, until a spark or a catalyst is introduced.

**A positive $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ is necessary for a reaction to be spontaneous — nowhere near sufficient for it to be fast.** Whether it actually proceeds, and how quickly, is a separate question about activation energy — a genuinely different idea from the thermodynamic favourability $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ measures.
