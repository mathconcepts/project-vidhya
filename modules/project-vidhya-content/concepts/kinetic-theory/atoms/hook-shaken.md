At room temperature (about 300 K), a single oxygen molecule in the air around you is already moving at roughly 480 metres per second — faster than a rifle bullet leaving its barrel. It cannot travel far before it crashes into a neighbouring molecule and bounces off in a new direction.

Heat that same gas, and every molecule speeds up further. Each one now slams into the container's wall harder, and does so more often every second. Pressure is exactly this: the total push, per square metre of wall, added up over countless such collisions every second. A sealed cylinder left in the sun bursts for precisely this reason — no gas was added, the collisions simply got faster and more frequent.
