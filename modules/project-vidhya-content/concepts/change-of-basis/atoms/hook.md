Write a vector as $(3,2)$ and you have quietly picked a coordinate system — the usual $x$-$y$ axes. Push the two axis arrows, $e_1$ and $e_2$, through $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$ and watch where they land: $e_1$ swings to $(1,-1)$, $e_2$ swings to $(1,2)$. Those two landing arrows are not a side effect — they ARE the new basis, and the matrix's columns are exactly them, always.
