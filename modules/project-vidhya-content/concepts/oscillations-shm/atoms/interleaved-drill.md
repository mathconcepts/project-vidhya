**Cross-concept check: oscillations-shm → waves-mechanical.**

**Question 1 (an SHM source creates a travelling wave):** A tuning fork's prong oscillates in SHM at frequency $f=256$ Hz, driving sound waves through air at speed $v=340$ m/s. Find the wavelength of the sound produced.

*Answer:* $\lambda=v/f=340/256\approx1.33$ m. The oscillation's own frequency, an SHM quantity, fixes the wave's wavelength once the medium's wave speed is known — the wave "inherits" its frequency directly from the source's oscillation.

**Question 2 (what does NOT change the wavelength):** The tuning fork is struck harder, so its oscillation amplitude increases, but its frequency stays exactly $256$ Hz. Does the sound wave's wavelength change?

*Answer:* No. $\lambda=v/f$ depends only on wave speed and frequency, neither of which changed — amplitude affects how loud the sound is, not its wavelength. A larger SHM amplitude at the source does not, by itself, alter the wave it produces beyond making it louder.

**Why this drill exists:** a wave's frequency is nothing but its source's SHM frequency, carried along unchanged — confusing "louder" (an amplitude effect) with "different pitch" (a frequency effect) is a mistake that spans both concepts at once.
