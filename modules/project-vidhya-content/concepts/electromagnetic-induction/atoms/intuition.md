The **magnetic flux** through a surface is $\Phi = B \cdot A \cdot \cos\theta$ — a measure of how much field passes through the coil's face, not just how strong the field is. For the coil from before, the field points straight down and the coil lies flat, so $\theta = 0$ and $\Phi = BA$.

**Faraday's law** says the induced emf equals how fast this flux changes: $\varepsilon = -\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt}$. It does not matter *why* the flux changes — the field weakening, the coil's area being stretched, or the coil rotating so $\theta$ changes — any way flux changes counts equally.

The minus sign is **Lenz's law** written directly into the formula: the induced current always flows in the direction that opposes the *change* in flux, never in the direction that simply cancels whatever field is currently there. As the coil's field fades from $0.6\text{ T}$ to $0.2\text{ T}$, flux is shrinking, so the induced current's own magnetic field points the same way the original field did — reinforcing what is being lost, not fighting the field that still remains.
