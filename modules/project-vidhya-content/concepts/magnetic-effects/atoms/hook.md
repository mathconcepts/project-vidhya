A proton is fired at $2\times10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$ straight down the middle of a long solenoid (a coil that produces a strong, uniform magnetic field along its own axis). The field inside is a hefty $0.5\ \text{T}$ ($\text{T}$ for tesla, the unit of magnetic field strength).

Guess before reading on: does this strong field push the proton off course?

It does not — not even a little. The magnetic force on a moving charge is $F=qvB\sin\theta$, and here $\theta$ (the angle between the proton's velocity and the field) is exactly $0°$, since the proton travels *along* the field direction. $\sin 0°=0$, so $F=0$ regardless of how fast the proton moves or how strong the field is. A charge moving parallel to a magnetic field feels no magnetic force at all.
