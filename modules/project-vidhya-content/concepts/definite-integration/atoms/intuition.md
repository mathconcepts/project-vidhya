A definite integral property turns a hard problem trivial only when the interval and the function actually cooperate — the geometric picture is worth watching once with real numbers before trusting any shortcut on something harder.

The reason this works is not magic: reflecting the interval $[0,\pi]$ about its midpoint $\pi/2$ swaps the first half of the curve with the second, and because $\sin(\pi-x)=\sin x$, that swap lands the curve exactly back on top of itself. Two mirror-image strips of area, cut from the same shape, are equal by construction — the running total simply has no choice but to double.

The lesson generalises well beyond $\sin x$: any time an interval is symmetric about a point, and the integrand maps onto itself (or its exact complement) under that reflection, half the work is free — one integral over half the interval answers the whole question. Skip the check that $f(x)=f(a-x)$ genuinely holds first, though, and the same shortcut silently returns a wrong answer instead of a fast one.
