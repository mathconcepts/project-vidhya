**How JEE Main actually asks this.**

- **NAT: fringe width or path difference at a stated point.** Given $d$, $D$, $\lambda$, and either "find $\beta$" or "find $\Delta$ at $y=\ldots$" — these are two different formulas answering two different questions, even though they share the same three inputs.

- **MCQ: classify a named point as bright, dark, or neither.** Compute $\Delta$, divide by $\lambda$, and read off whether the result is a whole number (bright), a half-integer (dark), or genuinely neither.

- **Trap: single-slit versus double-slit formula swap.** A question naming a slit **width** wants the single-slit dark condition, $a\sin\theta=n\lambda$; a question naming slit **separation** wants the double-slit bright condition, $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$. Read which word the question uses before reaching for a formula.

- **Trap: a thin-film question with no mention of "phase reversal" that still needs it.** If the problem states light reflecting off a film on a denser substrate (like an oil film on water, or a coating on glass), the extra $\lambda/2$ at that one surface is required even when the question never says the words "phase reversal" explicitly.

- **Time budget:** a direct $\beta$ or $\Delta$ NAT should take under $30$ seconds; a bright/dark classification MCQ, once $\Delta$ is known, should take under $15$ seconds.
