A student measures the side of a metal cube with a vernier caliper (a sliding-scale instrument that reads length to the nearest $0.01$ cm) and gets $a = 2.20$ cm. The instrument itself could be off by $\Delta a = 0.01$ cm either way — that is its **least count**, the smallest change it can actually detect.

Now the student needs the cube's volume, $V=a^3$. Punching $2.20^3$ into a calculator gives $10.648000000000003$ — a screen full of digits. Reporting all of them would claim the volume is known far more precisely than the length ever was. The real question this concept answers: given that $a$ is only good to $0.01$ cm, how many digits of $V$ are actually trustworthy, and how does that uncertainty in $a$ carry through to $V$?
