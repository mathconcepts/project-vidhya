The absolute temperature of an ideal gas is increased from $300$ K to $1200$ K, at constant volume and constant amount of gas. By what factor does the rms speed of its molecules increase?

A) $2$
B) $4$
C) $16$
D) $1$ (unchanged)
E) $\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: A) $2$.**

$v_{rms}=\sqrt{3RT/M}$, so $v_{rms}\propto\sqrt{T}$. The temperature ratio is $1200/300=4$. The rms speed ratio is $\sqrt{4}=2$.

**Why the others fail:**
- B) $4$ mistakes $v_{rms}\propto T$ (a straight proportion) for the true relationship $v_{rms}\propto\sqrt{T}$ — this confuses speed with pressure at constant volume, which genuinely does scale as $T$ itself.
- C) $16$ squares the temperature ratio instead of taking its square root.
- D) is wrong because rms speed clearly depends on temperature; it is only *pressure at fixed volume and amount* that would need a separate check.
- E) $\sqrt{2}$ would be correct only if the temperature ratio were $2$, not $4$.

</details>
