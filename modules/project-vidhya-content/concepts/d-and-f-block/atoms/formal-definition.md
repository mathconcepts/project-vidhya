**$d$-block (transition elements)**: Groups 3–12, elements with a partly filled $d$ subshell either as the neutral atom or in at least one common oxidation state. $Zn$, $Cd$ and $Hg$ sit in the $d$-block but keep $d^{10}$ in every common state, so strictly they are not called transition elements.

**$f$-block**: lanthanoids ($Ce$ to $Lu$, filling $4f$) and actinoids ($Th$ to $Lr$, filling $5f$), shown below the main table.

**Aufbau exceptions — chromium and copper**: the order electrons are expected to fill predicts $Cr$: $[Ar]3d^44s^2$ and $Cu$: $[Ar]3d^94s^2$. The real configurations are $Cr$: $[Ar]3d^54s^1$ and $Cu$: $[Ar]3d^{10}4s^1$ — one $4s$ electron shifts into $3d$ in each case, because a half-filled ($d^5$) or completely filled ($d^{10}$) subshell is unusually stable (extra exchange energy between electrons of parallel spin), enough to outweigh the small energy cost of moving one electron out of $4s$.

**Spin-only magnetic moment**: $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ Bohr Magneton (BM), where $n$ is the number of unpaired electrons in the ion.

**Lanthanoid contraction**: atomic and ionic radii shrink steadily across the lanthanoid series ($La$ to $Lu$) because the $4f$ electrons being added shield the nucleus very poorly, letting effective nuclear charge climb almost unchecked. This makes second- and third-row transition metals in the same group (e.g. $Zr$/$Hf$, $Nb$/$Ta$) end up with nearly identical radii, since the lanthanoid contraction cancels out the size increase a new shell would normally give.

**Potassium dichromate and potassium permanganate**: $K_2Cr_2O_7$ (chromium in $+6$, ion $Cr_2O_7^{2-}$, orange) and $KMnO_4$ (manganese in $+7$, ion $MnO_4^-$, purple) are both strong oxidising agents in acidic medium. Both metals are formally $d^0$ in these ions, so their strong colour comes from ligand-to-metal charge transfer, not from a $d$-to-$d$ electron jump.
