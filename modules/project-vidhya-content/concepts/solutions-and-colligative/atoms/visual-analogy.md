A snack table at a party runs out at a rate that depends only on HOW MANY people are in the room — not on who, specifically, those people are. A room of $20$ quiet readers empties the snack bowl exactly as fast as a room of $20$ enthusiastic dancers, because headcount is headcount; personality never enters the calculation.

Colligative properties work exactly the same way. A dissolved particle "crowds" the solvent regardless of whether it is a sugar molecule or a sodium ion — freezing-point depression, boiling-point elevation, and osmotic pressure all respond to the PARTICLE HEADCOUNT alone. This is exactly why the **van't Hoff factor** matters so much: it is nothing but a correction for the fact that one formula unit of a compound does not always mean one particle in solution — a salt that splits into three ions puts three "guests" into the room for the price of one "formula unit invited."
