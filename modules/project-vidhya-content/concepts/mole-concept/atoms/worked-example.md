**Problem:** 4 g of $\text{H}_2$ gas is mixed with 16 g of $\text{O}_2$ gas and ignited: $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Which reactant is the limiting reagent, and what mass of water forms?

---

**Step 1 — Convert both given masses to moles first.** Molar mass of $\text{H}_2 = 2$ g/mol, so moles of $\text{H}_2 = 4 \div 2 = 2$ mol. Molar mass of $\text{O}_2 = 32$ g/mol, so moles of $\text{O}_2 = 16 \div 32 = 0.5$ mol.

---

**Step 2 — Compare using the equation's mole ratio, not the raw masses.** The equation needs 2 mol $\text{H}_2$ for every 1 mol $\text{O}_2$. For the 0.5 mol $\text{O}_2$ available, the amount of $\text{H}_2$ actually required is $0.5 \times 2 = 1$ mol.

---

**Step 3 — Identify the limiting reagent.** 1 mol of $\text{H}_2$ is required, but 2 mol are available — 1 mol of $\text{H}_2$ will be left over unreacted. All 0.5 mol of $\text{O}_2$ gets used up. $\text{O}_2$ is the limiting reagent.

---

**Step 4 — Compute the product from the limiting reagent only.** The equation gives 2 mol $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ for every 1 mol $\text{O}_2$, so moles of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ formed $= 0.5 \times 2 = 1$ mol. Mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ formed $= 1 \times 18 = 18$ g.

$$\boxed{\text{O}_2 \text{ is limiting; } 18 \text{ g of water forms}}$$
