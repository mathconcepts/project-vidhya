$CuSO_4$ solution is a familiar, strong blue. $ZnSO_4$ solution, sitting right next to copper on the periodic table, is completely colourless — same group of the table, same $+2$ charge, same kind of salt, and yet one has colour and the other has none at all.

The difference is not the metal's identity in some vague sense; it is one specific number. $Cu^{2+}$ has the configuration $[Ar]3d^9$ — nine electrons in a ten-electron subshell, one short of full. $Zn^{2+}$ has $[Ar]3d^{10}$ — completely full, with no room left to rearrange. That single difference, a partly filled versus a completely filled $d$ subshell, turns out to be the one structural fact behind colour, magnetism and variable oxidation states across the whole $d$-block — three separate-looking exam topics that are really one idea in three costumes.
