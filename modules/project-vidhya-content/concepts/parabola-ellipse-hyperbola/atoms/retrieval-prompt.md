From memory, before checking: for a hyperbola specifically, is the sign of $S_1=\dfrac{x_1^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y_1^2}{b^2}-1$ that signals "two real tangents exist" the SAME as for an ellipse, or the opposite?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The opposite. For an ellipse or circle, $S_1>0$ means outside, with two real tangents. For a hyperbola, two real tangents exist where $S_1<0$ instead — confirmed directly: $(8,0)$ on $\frac{x^2}{25}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1$ has $S_1>0$ yet zero real tangent lines, while $(4,3)$ has $S_1<0$ and genuinely has two.

</details>
