**Problem:** A satellite moves in a circular orbit at distance $r=2R$ from Earth's centre, where $R=6400\text{ km}$ is Earth's radius and $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ at the surface. Find its orbital speed and time period.

---

**Step 0 — Set up before any formula.** Physical situation: a satellite in a **stable circular orbit**, held there by gravity alone acting as the centripetal force — no engine thrust once the orbit is set. Frame of reference: Earth's centre as the origin, radial direction taken as **positive outward**. Since only the magnitude of the orbit's speed and period are asked, no sign convention beyond "distance and speed are positive" is needed here.

---

**Step 1 — Equate gravity to the required centripetal force.** $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.

---

**Step 2 — Replace $GM$ using surface gravity.** At the surface, $g=GM/R^2$, so $GM=gR^2$. Substituting: $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{gR^2}{r}}$.

---

**Step 3 — Plug in numbers.** $r=2R$, so $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{gR^2}{2R}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{gR}{2}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{(9.8)(6400000)}{2}}=\sqrt{31360000}=5600\text{ m/s}$.

---

**Step 4 — Find the time period.** $T=\dfrac{2\pi r}{v}=\dfrac{2\pi(2\times6400000)}{5600}=\dfrac{2\pi(12800000)}{5600}\approx14362\text{ s}$.

---

**Step 5 — State both answers.** $\boxed{v=5600\text{ m/s}\text{, } T\approx14362\text{ s}\approx3.99\text{ hours}}$ — under four hours to complete one full orbit at this height.
