**Problem:** In a Young's double-slit setup, $d=0.5\text{ mm}$, $D=1\text{ m}$, $\lambda=500\text{ nm}$. Determine whether the point $y=2\text{ mm}$ above the centre is bright or dark, and separately find the fringe width.

---

**Step 1 — Plug into the path-difference formula.** $\Delta = \dfrac{(0.5\times10^{-3})(2\times10^{-3})}{1}$.

---

**Step 2 — Simplify.** $\Delta = 1\times10^{-6}\text{ m} = 1000\text{ nm}$.

---

**Step 3 — Divide by $\lambda$.** $1000/500 = 2$.

---

**Step 4 — Read off the classification.** $2$ is a whole number, so $y=2\text{ mm}$ is **bright**.

---

**Step 5 — Compute the fringe width separately.** $\beta = \dfrac{(500\times10^{-9})(1)}{0.5\times10^{-3}} = 1\text{ mm}$.

$$\boxed{\text{bright } (n=2), \qquad \beta = 1\text{ mm}}$$

---

**Step 6 — Check by a second route.** $y_n = n\beta = 2\times1\text{ mm} = 2\text{ mm}$, matching the given point exactly.
