**Cross-concept check: current-electricity → magnetic-effects.**

**Question 1 (current, still just current):** A long straight wire carries a current of $5\ \text{A}$. What determines whether this counts as a "large" or "small" current, in terms of what you've learned so far?

*Answer:* Nothing about the wire's own resistance or the source's emf decides that on its own — $5\ \text{A}$ is simply the rate of charge flow, $5$ coulombs passing any cross-section of the wire every second, however that current happens to have been driven.

**Question 2 (the same current, now creating a magnetic field):** That same wire carrying $5\ \text{A}$ has a magnetic field at a perpendicular distance of $0.1\ \text{m}$ from it. Using $B=\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$ (with $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \text{T}\cdot\text{m/A}$), find $B$.

*Answer:* $B=\dfrac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times5}{2\pi\times0.1}=\dfrac{2\times10^{-7}\times5}{0.1}=1\times10^{-5}\ \text{T}=10\ \mu\text{T}$.

**Why this drill exists:** current-electricity treats current as a number flowing through a circuit and stops there. The very same current, the moment it flows through a wire in space rather than just around a circuit diagram, also creates a magnetic field around that wire — the exact fact magnetic-effects builds its entire opening chapter on.
