A radioactive sample has a half-life of $10$ days and starts with $N_0=800$ nuclei. How many nuclei remain after $30$ days?

A) $400$
B) $200$
C) $0$
D) $100$
E) $267$

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct answer: D) $100$.**

$30$ days is exactly $3$ half-lives ($30/10=3$). Each half-life halves what is left: $800\to400\to200\to100$. So $N=N_0\left(\tfrac12\right)^3=800\times\tfrac18=100$.

**Why the others fail:**
- A) $400$ is the count after just *one* half-life ($10$ days), not three.
- B) $200$ is the count after *two* half-lives ($20$ days) — one half-life short.
- C) $0$ assumes decay finishes completely after a few half-lives; it never does, however many half-lives pass.
- E) $267$ comes from dividing $800$ by $3$ directly, treating decay as a straight-line loss instead of a repeated halving.

</details>
