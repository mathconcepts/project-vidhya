**Problem:** Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of $Fe^{2+}$ in the octahedral complex $[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$, a weak-field (high-spin) complex.

---

**Step 1 — Find the free atom's configuration.** Iron is $[Ar]3d^64s^2$.

---

**Step 2 — Remove electrons to form $Fe^{2+}$.** Transition-metal ions lose their $4s$ electrons first, before any $3d$ electrons. Removing both $4s$ electrons gives $Fe^{2+}$: $[Ar]3d^6$.

---

**Step 3 — Distribute the 6 $d$ electrons, high-spin.** High-spin means pairing is avoided as long as possible. Fill one electron into each of the 5 orbitals first (Hund's rule): that uses 5 electrons, one per orbital, all unpaired. The 6th electron has no empty orbital left, so it must pair up in one of the five.

---

**Step 4 — Count the unpaired electrons.** 4 orbitals still hold exactly one electron each (unpaired); 1 orbital holds a pair. So $n = 4$ unpaired electrons.

---

**Step 5 — Apply the spin-only formula.** $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{4 \times 6} = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.90$ BM.

---

**Step 6 — State the answer.** $\boxed{\mu(Fe^{2+}) \approx 4.90 \text{ BM}}$ — matching the experimentally observed value for high-spin $Fe^{2+}$ complexes.
