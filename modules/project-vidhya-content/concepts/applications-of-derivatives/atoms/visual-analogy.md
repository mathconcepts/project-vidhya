# The Dashboard Analogy

A car's odometer shows total distance travelled — position, $f(t)$. The speedometer, right beside it, shows something different: how fast that distance is *currently* changing — $f'(t)$, the rate of change, read off in real time rather than computed afterward from two odometer readings. When the speedometer needle sits exactly on zero, the car is momentarily not moving forward at all — a stop, or the exact top of a hill crest before it starts descending on the other side.

The tangent line is what would happen if the steering locked at this exact instant: the car would continue in a dead-straight line matching whichever way the front wheels are pointed right now, no curve. The normal line is the direction a passenger's door would be facing if it swung open at that same instant — square across the car's motion, perpendicular to the tangent, never parallel to it.

Rolle's Theorem and the Mean Value Theorem are both about the speedometer, not the odometer: if the car ends up back where it started ($f(a)=f(b)$), the speedometer must have touched zero somewhere in between — it cannot have driven forward the whole way and still ended up at the start.
