$f'(c)=0$ marks a flat instant on the trail — but flat is necessary for a peak or a valley, nowhere near sufficient for one. $f(x)=x^3$ at $x=0$: $f'(x)=3x^2$, so $f'(0)=0$, exactly as a maximum or minimum would require. Yet $f$ keeps climbing straight through $x=0$ — $f'(x)=3x^2\geq0$ on both sides, never changing sign, so there is no peak and no valley there at all, just a flat instant on an otherwise still-rising trail. The second derivative test agrees it is inconclusive: $f''(0)=0$ too, refusing to call it either way.

What still needs checking: a critical point ($f'(c)=0$) is a candidate, never a verdict. The verdict comes from checking whether the *sign* of $f'$ actually flips around $c$ — trail direction genuinely reversing — not from the mere existence of a flat instant. The tangent and normal at any point exist regardless of whether that point is a critical one; being flat is a special case of "having a tangent," not the other way round.
